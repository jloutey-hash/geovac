\begin{abstract}
The GeoVac label graphs carry Wilson lattice gauge theories, and the two
graphs studied here sit at opposite ends of solvability.  On the scalar
label graph, whose vertices are the labels $(n,l,m)$ of the $S^3$
harmonics, no edge changes $l$, and each $l$-block is a planar
$(n_{\max}-l)\times(2l+1)$ grid.  Taking the plaquettes to be the graph's
4-cycles (the unit squares of these grids), the plaquettes form a basis of
the cycle space at every $n_{\max}$, so after gauge fixing on a spanning
tree the plaquette holonomies are independent Haar variables.
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wilson theory on this graph is therefore the exactly
solvable two-dimensional lattice gauge theory:
$Z(\beta) = [2e^{-\beta} I_1(\beta)/\beta]^{\beta_1}$, and every closed
loop enclosing $A$ plaquettes has
$\langle W \rangle = (I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta))^{A}$, an area law by
factorization that carries no information about confinement.  The
weak-coupling expansion gives the curl form
$(\beta/8)\sum_a \langle A^a, B_2 B_2^{\top} A^a\rangle$, which is
supported on the cycle space and completes the edge Laplacian
$B^{\top}B$ to the full Hodge-1 Laplacian of the plaquette-filled complex;
the cubic term is nonzero, with a vertex proportional to
$\epsilon^{abc}$.  Restricting the links to the maximal torus gives compact
$U(1)$ Wilson theory, with $\langle W\rangle = (I_1/I_0)^A$, and we record
the abelian dictionary between Wilson and graph-Hodge data together with
the exact $n_{\max}=3$ edge-Laplacian spectrum.  On the Dirac Rule-B
graph, whose edges are E1 dipole transitions and all change $l$, the graph
is connected, its 4-cycles outnumber the independent cycles ($44$ against
$11$ at $n_{\max}=2$) and close into 2-cycles of three plaquettes, so
compact $U(1)$ does not factorize.  Seven standard diagnostics are
compatible with compact $U(1)$ and discriminate no dimension: the
monopole density falls roughly exponentially in $\beta$ on a range below
the four-dimensional transition, full Monte Carlo Wilson loops resolve no
area law (the area coefficient of a joint area-plus-perimeter fit is
consistent with zero at $n_{\max}=2$), and the Polyakov loop on the
product graph with a time circle is well defined through one extra
cohomology class, while its expectation vanishes by centre symmetry.  Each
Rule-B link lies in many plaquettes ($8.8$ and $37.5$ on average at
$n_{\max}=2,3$), so the comparison class is mean-field or large-dimension
lattice gauge theory, not a three-dimensional lattice.  The label graphs
have no continuum limit, and nothing here bears on one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===================================================================

The GeoVac framework works on finite graphs whose vertices are quantum
numbers.  The scalar label graph has the labels $(n,l,m)$ of the
unit-three-sphere harmonics as vertices~\cite{paper7}; its edge Laplacian
preserves $l$, splits into $n_{\max}$ disconnected blocks, and does not
converge to the $S^3$ Laplacian (\texttt{tests/test\_graph\_s3\_nonconvergence.py}).
The Dirac Rule-B graph of Paper~29~\cite{paper29} has the Dirac labels
$(n,\kappa,m_j)$ as vertices and the E1 dipole transitions as edges.  Any
oriented graph supports a Wilson lattice gauge
theory~\cite{wilson1974,creutz1983,rothe2012} once a set of plaquettes is
chosen, and this paper works out what that theory is on these two graphs.

The two answers are opposite.  On the scalar graph each $l$-block is a
planar grid, its unit squares are its 4-cycles, and they form a basis of
the cycle space.  The theory is then the exactly solvable two-dimensional
lattice gauge theory~\cite{migdal1975,gross_witten1980,drouffe_zuber1983}:
plaquette holonomies are independent, the partition function and every
Wilson loop are products of one-plaquette integrals, and the area law holds
by factorization at every coupling.  We state this for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
(Section~\ref{sec:su2}), give the weak-coupling kinetic term and its place
in the discrete Hodge decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:weak}), and take the
abelian section by restricting to the maximal torus
(Section~\ref{sec:abelian}).  On the Rule-B graph the 4-cycles are far more
numerous than the independent cycles and satisfy relations, so compact
$U(1)$ is a genuinely interacting theory there; Section~\ref{sec:ruleb}
reports seven standard diagnostics, all of which it passes in the sense of
being compatible with compact $U(1)$, and none of which discriminates a
dimension.

\paragraph*{Scope.}
The constructions are finite, Euclidean, and Haar-normalizable; no
renormalization enters.  This paper does not address a Yang--Mills mass gap,
a continuum limit (the label graphs do not converge to a manifold), or the
fine-structure constant.  The results on the scalar graph are the textbook
two-dimensional results applied to a disjoint union of planar grids; what
the paper adds is the identification of the label graphs' plaquette
complexes and the measured Rule-B diagnostics.  The construction is an
instance of the gauge-network framework of Marcolli and van
Suijlekom~\cite{marcolli_vs2014}, whose spectral action on a graph is a
Wilson lattice gauge theory; its continuum limit is Yang--Mills without a
Higgs field~\cite{perez_sanchez2025}.  For $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Hamiltonian
lattice gauge theory built for quantum simulation from discrete
partitionings of the group, see Refs.~\cite{garofalo2023_su2_s3,jakobs2025_su2_dynamics}.

\subsection{The scalar label graph}
\label{sec:scalar_graph}

The scalar graph at cutoff $n_{\max}$ has vertices $(n,l,m)$ with
$1\le n\le n_{\max}$, $0\le l\le n-1$, $|m|\le l$, so $|V|=\sum_n n^2$.
Its edges are the angular ladders $(n,l,m)\leftrightarrow(n,l,m\pm1)$ and
the radial ladders $(n,l,m)\leftrightarrow(n\pm1,l,m)$.  No edge changes
$l$, so the graph is the disjoint union
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{scalar}} \;=\; \bigsqcup_{l=0}^{n_{\max}-1}
  P_{n_{\max}-l}\times P_{2l+1},
\label{eq:grid_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $P_k$ is the path on $k$ vertices and $\times$ is the Cartesian
product: the $l$-block is a planar grid with rows indexed by
$n=l+1,\ldots,n_{\max}$ and columns by $m$.  A $k_1\times k_2$ grid has
$(k_1-1)(k_2-1)$ unit squares, so
\begin{equation}
\beta_1 \;=\; |E|-|V|+c \;=\; \sum_{l=0}^{n_{\max}-1} 2l\,(n_{\max}-l-1),
\label{eq:betti_scalar}
\end{equation}
with $c=n_{\max}$ components (Table~\ref{tab:scalar_counts}).

\subsection{Plaquettes and the Hodge operators}
\label{sec:hodge}

\begin{definition}[Plaquettes]
\label{def:plaquettes}
The plaquette set $\mathcal{P}$ of a label graph is its set of 4-cycles,
each taken once with a fixed orientation.
\end{definition}

On the scalar graph the 4-cycles are exactly the unit squares of the grids,
and $N_4=\beta_1$ (Table~\ref{tab:scalar_counts}).  Longer closed walks add
nothing to the plaquette set: at $n_{\max}=4$ there are $7$ primitive
non-backtracking closed walks of length $6$ and $18$ of length $8$ (one per
unoriented class), and the signed edge vector of every closed walk lies in
the cycle space, which the 4-cycles span.

Fix an orientation on each edge.  The signed vertex--edge incidence matrix
$B\in\mathbb{Z}^{V\times E}$ has $B_{v,e}=\pm1$ at the two ends of $e$, and
the signed edge--plaquette incidence matrix
$B_2\in\mathbb{Z}^{E\times\mathcal{P}}$ has $(B_2)_{e,P}=\pm1$ according to
whether $P$ traverses $e$ along or against its orientation.  As maps on
cochains, $B^{\top}$ is the coboundary from vertex functions to edge
functions and $B_2^{\top}$ is the coboundary from edge functions to
plaquette functions: $(B_2^{\top}a)_P=\sum_{e\in P}\pm a_e$ is the
circulation of $a$ around $P$.  Since every plaquette is closed,
$BB_2=0$.  The combinatorial Hodge Laplacians~\cite{lim2020,schaub2020}
are
\begin{equation}
L_0 = BB^{\top},\qquad
L_1 = B^{\top}B,\qquad
\Delta_1 = B^{\top}B + B_2B_2^{\top}.
\label{eq:hodge_laplacians}
\end{equation}
$L_0=D-A$ is the graph Laplacian; $L_1$ is the edge Laplacian, whose kernel
is the cycle space $\ker B$, of dimension $\beta_1$; and $\Delta_1$ is the
Hodge-1 Laplacian of the 2-complex obtained by attaching the plaquettes as
faces.  The nonzero spectra of $L_0$ and $L_1$ coincide, being the squared
singular values of $B$.  The kernel of $\Delta_1$ is the first cohomology
of the 2-complex,
\begin{equation}
\dim\ker\Delta_1 \;=\; \beta_1 - \operatorname{rank}B_2,
\label{eq:h1_defect}
\end{equation}
which vanishes exactly when the plaquettes span the cycle space.  When in
addition $\operatorname{rank}B_2<|\mathcal{P}|$, the plaquettes satisfy
relations; the kernel of $B_2$, of dimension
$b_2=|\mathcal{P}|-\operatorname{rank}B_2$, consists of the closed 2-cycles
of the complex.

On the scalar graph $B_2$ has full rank $|\mathcal{P}|=\beta_1$: the
plaquettes are a basis of the cycle space and $b_2=0$
(\texttt{tests/test\_su2\_wilson\_gauge.py}, class
\texttt{TestCertRunCoverageClosures},
\texttt{test\_plaquettes\_form\_cycle\_basis}, checked at
$n_{\max}=3,4,5$).  Every edge of a path-like block ($l=0$, and the
$l=n_{\max}-1$ block) is a bridge, lying in no plaquette.

\subsection{The Dirac Rule-B graph}
\label{sec:ruleb_graph}

The Rule-B graph~\cite{paper29} has vertices $(n,\kappa,m_j)$ with
$1\le n\le n_{\max}$, $\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ encoding
$l=-\kappa-1$ ($\kappa<0$) or $l=\kappa$ ($\kappa>0$) and $j=|\kappa|-\tfrac12$,
and $|m_j|\le j$; there are $2n^2$ vertices in shell $n$.  Two vertices are
adjacent when
\begin{equation}
|\Delta n|\le1,\quad |\Delta l|=1,\quad |\Delta j|\le1,\quad
|\Delta m_j|\le1,
\label{eq:rule_b}
\end{equation}
the E1 dipole selection rule.  Every edge changes $l$ by one, so the graph is
bipartite under $(-1)^l$ and contains no triangles.  It is connected at every
cutoff tested (Table~\ref{tab:ruleb_counts}), with mean degree $2E/V=4.0$,
$7.6$, $10.4$, $12.6$ at $n_{\max}=2$--$5$.  Its 4-cycles outnumber its
independent cycles by a factor that grows with the cutoff, and at
$n_{\max}=2$, where the scalar graph has no cycles at all, Rule B already has
$44$ 4-cycles.  The rank of $B_2$ equals $\beta_1=11$ at $n_{\max}=2$, so the
plaquettes span the cycle space ($\dim\ker\Delta_1=0$) and satisfy
$b_2=44-11=33$ relations.  The smallest closed 2-cycle consists of three
plaquettes, each pair sharing two edges, with the six edges each used twice;
there are $60$ such 2-cycles at $n_{\max}=2$ and $80$ at $n_{\max}=3$.  On the
hypercubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$, $d\ge3$, the smallest closed 2-surface is
the six-plaquette boundary of a cube.

\section{SU(2) Wilson theory on the scalar graph}
\label{sec:su2}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Link variables, action, gauge invariance}

Each oriented edge $u\to v$ carries $U_{u\to v}\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, with
$U_{v\to u}=U_{u\to v}^{\dagger}$.  For a plaquette traversed as
$e_1e_2e_3e_4$ the holonomy is $U_P=U_{e_1}U_{e_2}U_{e_3}U_{e_4}$, and the
Wilson action is
\begin{equation}
S_W[U] = \beta\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\Bigl(1-\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P\Bigr),
\qquad \beta = 4/g^2 .
\label{eq:wilson_action}
\end{equation}
Under $U_{u\to v}\mapsto g_uU_{u\to v}g_v^{\dagger}$ with $g:V\to\mathrm{SU}(2)$
each holonomy is conjugated, so $S_W$ is gauge invariant; with random Haar
links and gauge transformations at $n_{\max}=3$ the change in $S_W$ is at
machine precision (\texttt{tests/test\_su2\_wilson\_gauge.py}, class
\texttt{TestGaugeInvariance} and \texttt{test\_s3\_coulomb\_max\_n3\_endtoend},
which assert $<10^{-13}$).  The partition function is
$Z(\beta)=\int\prod_e dU_e\,e^{-S_W[U]}$ with normalized Haar measure.

\subsection{Character expansion}
\label{sec:characters}

With $\chi_j(U)=\sin((j+1)\theta)/\sin\theta$ the character of the
$(j+1)$-dimensional irreducible representation at
$U=e^{i\theta\hat n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}$, the one-plaquette Boltzmann
factor expands as~\cite{menotti_onofri1981,creutz1983}
\begin{equation}
\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U\Bigr)
=\sum_{j\ge0} d_j(\beta)\,\chi_j(U),\qquad
d_j(\beta)=\frac{2(j+1)}{\beta}\,I_{j+1}(\beta),
\label{eq:character_expansion}
\end{equation}
with $I_k$ the modified Bessel function; $d_0(0)=1$ and $d_j(0)=0$ for
$j\ge1$.

\subsection{Exact factorization}
\label{sec:factorization}

\begin{proposition}[Exact solvability on the scalar graph]
\label{prop:factorization}
At every $n_{\max}$, with the plaquette set of Definition~\ref{def:plaquettes},
\begin{equation}
Z(\beta) = \Bigl[e^{-\beta}\,\frac{2I_1(\beta)}{\beta}\Bigr]^{\beta_1},
\label{eq:Z_exact}
\end{equation}
and every closed loop $C$ in an $l$-block enclosing $A$ unit squares has
\begin{equation}
\langle W_C\rangle \equiv
\Bigl\langle\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,U_C\Bigr\rangle
= \biggl(\frac{I_2(\beta)}{I_1(\beta)}\biggr)^{A}.
\label{eq:W_exact}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument is the standard two-dimensional one~\cite{drouffe_zuber1983,gross_witten1980}.
In each grid, fix the gauge $U_e=I$ on a spanning tree.  The remaining
$\beta_1$ links are in bijection with the unit squares (order the squares so
that each adds one new non-tree edge), and the change of variables from those
links to the plaquette holonomies is a product of left translations, which
preserves Haar measure.  The plaquette holonomies are therefore independent
Haar variables, and $Z$ is the product of $\beta_1$ one-plaquette integrals
$e^{-\beta}d_0(\beta)$.  A loop enclosing $A$ squares has holonomy conjugate
to a product of the $A$ enclosed plaquette holonomies, each conjugated by a
path holonomy; since each is distributed by a class function, Schur
orthogonality gives
$\langle\frac12\mathrm{tr}\,U_C\rangle=\prod\langle\frac12\mathrm{tr}\,U_P\rangle$,
and the one-plaquette expectation is $d_1/(2d_0)=I_2/I_1$.
\end{proof}

The cycle-basis property and the one- and two-plaquette identities are tested
in \texttt{tests/test\_su2\_wilson\_gauge.py}, class
\texttt{TestCertRunCoverageClosures} (the cycle-basis, single-plaquette and
two-plaquette tests).  At $n_{\max}=3$,
$\langle W_{P_A}\rangle=I_2/I_1=0.124$, $0.240$, $0.433$, $0.719$ at
$\beta=0.5$, $1$, $2$, $5$, and the six-edge loop around $P_A\cup P_B$ has
$(I_2/I_1)^2$.  The area law~(\ref{eq:W_exact}) holds at every $\beta$ with
string tension $-\ln(I_2/I_1)$ and no area--perimeter crossover; it is a
consequence of factorization and carries no information about confinement.
A confinement diagnostic with dynamical content needs plaquettes that are not
independent, which the scalar graph does not supply.

\subsection{Weak coupling: the curl form}
\label{sec:weak}

Write $U_e=\exp(\tfrac{i}{2}A_e^a\sigma_a)$ with small real $A_e^a$.  To
second order the plaquette holonomy is
$U_P=I+\tfrac{i}{2}\Phi_P^a\sigma_a-\tfrac18\Phi_P^a\Phi_P^a I+\ldots$, where
$\Phi_P^a=(B_2^{\top}A^a)_P$ is the circulation of $A^a$ around $P$ and the
omitted terms are traceless at second order.  Hence
\begin{equation}
1-\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P
= \tfrac18\sum_a(\Phi_P^a)^2 + O(A^3).
\label{eq:plaquette_quadratic}
\end{equation}
The $O(A^3)$ term does not vanish: the commutators $[A_e,A_{e'}]$ in
$\log U_P$ feed a cubic vertex proportional to $\epsilon^{abc}$.

\begin{proposition}[Kinetic term]
\label{prop:L1_kinetic}
Around the trivial vacuum,
\begin{equation}
S_W[U]=\frac{\beta}{8}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\bigl\langle A^a,\,B_2B_2^{\top}A^a\bigr\rangle
+O(A^3).
\label{eq:L1_kinetic_form}
\end{equation}
The form $B_2B_2^{\top}$ is supported on the cycle space $\ker L_1$
($L_1B_2=0$), and $L_1+B_2B_2^{\top}=\Delta_1$ is the Hodge-1 Laplacian of the
plaquette-filled complex, of full rank $E$ on the scalar graph
(Eq.~(\ref{eq:h1_defect}) with $\operatorname{rank}B_2=\beta_1$).  The three
components $A^a$ decouple at quadratic order, each with coefficient
$1/8$ per plaquette.
\end{proposition}

At $n_{\max}=3$, $\operatorname{rank}B_2B_2^{\top}=2=\beta_1$,
$\operatorname{rank}L_1=11=E-\beta_1$, $L_1B_2=0$, and $L_1+B_2B_2^{\top}$ has
rank $13$; the coefficient $1/8$ is checked against exact
matrix-exponential holonomies on a triangle and on the full $n_{\max}=3$
graph, with relative deviation below $5\times10^{-3}$ at field size
$\epsilon=0.01$ (\texttt{tests/test\_su2\_wilson\_gauge.py}, class
\texttt{TestWeakCouplingKinetic}).  The edge Laplacian $L_1$ is the exact
(longitudinal) half of $\Delta_1$, the half a gauge-fixing term supplies;
the Wilson action supplies only the curl half.  The curl form vanishes on
bridges, so it gives no weight to the edges of the $l=0$ chain.

\begin{remark}[$\mathrm{SU}(N_c)$]
\label{rem:sun_coefficient}
With generators normalized to $\mathrm{tr}(T^aT^b)=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}$ and
the action $\beta\sum_P(1-\frac{1}{N_c}\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P)$, the
per-plaquette, per-component coefficient is $1/(4N_c)$, the standard
normalization of the Wilson action's naive continuum
limit~\cite{rothe2012}.  The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ value $1/12$ is checked on the
Bargmann--Segal graph of Paper~24~\cite{paper24}
(\texttt{tests/test\_su3\_wilson\_s5.py}, class
\texttt{TestWeakCouplingKinetic}).
\end{remark}

\section{The abelian section}
\label{sec:abelian}

\subsection{Maximal-torus reduction}

\begin{proposition}[Maximal-torus reduction]
\label{prop:u1_reduction}
Restricting the links of~(\ref{eq:wilson_action}) to the maximal torus,
$U_e=\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_e},e^{-i\theta_e})$, gives the compact $U(1)$
Wilson action
\begin{equation}
S_{U(1)}[\theta]=\beta\sum_P(1-\cos\theta_P),\qquad
\theta_P=(B_2^{\top}\theta)_P ,
\label{eq:u1_action}
\end{equation}
with gauge transformations $\theta\mapsto\theta+B^{\top}\chi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Products of diagonal matrices are diagonal, so
$U_P=\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_P},e^{-i\theta_P})$ with reversed edges
counted negatively, and $\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P=\cos\theta_P$.
\end{proof}

The reduction is checked on a triangle plaquette and, with random diagonal
links at $\beta=2$, on the full $n_{\max}=3$ graph, to machine precision
(\texttt{tests/test\_su2\_wilson\_gauge.py}, class \texttt{TestU1Reduction}
and \texttt{test\_u1\_reduction\_full\_graph\_n3\_beta2}).  On the torus the
character restricts to $\chi_j=\sum_{k=0}^{j}e^{i(j-2k)\theta}$, and the
expansion~(\ref{eq:character_expansion}) becomes the Jacobi--Anger expansion
$e^{\beta\cos\theta}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I_n(\beta)e^{in\theta}$.  The
reduction is a statement about the action, not about the path integral:
Haar measure on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ does not restrict to Haar measure on the
torus, so compact $U(1)$ is a separate theory with the restricted action,
not a sector of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ theory.  It is solved on the scalar
graph by the same argument: $Z=[e^{-\beta}I_0(\beta)]^{\beta_1}$ and
$\langle\cos\theta_C\rangle=(I_1(\beta)/I_0(\beta))^{A}$.  At weak coupling
$S_{U(1)}=\frac{\beta}{2}\langle\theta,B_2B_2^{\top}\theta\rangle+O(\theta^4)$:
the abelian action is even in $\theta$ and has no cubic term.  The same
restriction for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ on the Bargmann--Segal graph gives the
coupled $U(1)\times U(1)$ character
$\tfrac13(\cos a_P+\cos b_P+\cos(a_P+b_P))$
(\texttt{tests/test\_su3\_wilson\_s5.py}, class \texttt{TestCartanReduction}).

\subsection{Dictionary}

The ingredients of Wilson's construction correspond to graph-Hodge data on
any oriented graph with a chosen plaquette set:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Wilson & Graph Hodge & Role \\
\midrule
Site & vertex $v$ & matter location \\
Link & oriented edge $e$ & gauge-field location \\
Link variable & edge phase $e^{i\theta_e}$, $\theta\in C^1$ & connection \\
Plaquette & 4-cycle $P$, a column of $B_2$ & face \\
Plaquette angle & $\theta_P=(B_2^{\top}\theta)_P$ & curvature \\
Wilson loop & $\exp(i\langle c,\theta\rangle)$, $c\in\ker B$ & gauge-invariant observable \\
Gauge transformation & $\theta\mapsto\theta+B^{\top}\chi$ & coboundary shift \\
Kinetic operator & $B_2B_2^{\top}$ & curl form (Prop.~\ref{prop:L1_kinetic}) \\
Gauge-fixed propagator & $\Delta_1^{-1}=(B^{\top}B+B_2B_2^{\top})^{-1}$ & Feynman-gauge photon \\
Scalar matter operator & $L_0+m^2$, $L_0=BB^{\top}$ & covariant Laplacian at $\theta=0$ \\
Flat connections mod gauge & $\ker B_2^{\top}/\operatorname{im}B^{\top}$ & $H^1$, Eq.~(\ref{eq:h1_defect}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The dictionary is standard and applies to every graph; it is
recorded here because it fixes the notation for Section~\ref{sec:ruleb}.  On
the scalar graph $H^1=0$, so every flat connection is pure gauge.

\subsection{The $n_{\max}=3$ spectra}
\label{sec:spectra}

The edge Laplacian of the scalar graph is an integer matrix, so its
eigenvalues are algebraic.  At $n_{\max}=3$ its nonzero spectrum is the union
of the nonzero Laplacian spectra of the three blocks: the path $P_3$ ($l=0$,
eigenvalues $1,3$), the grid $P_2\times P_3$ ($l=1$, eigenvalues $1,2,3,3,5$),
and the path $P_5$ ($l=2$, eigenvalues $2-2\cos(k\pi/5)$, $k=1,\ldots,4$).
With the two zero modes of the cycle space,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{spec}(L_1)=\Bigl\{0^{(2)},\tfrac{3-\sqrt5}{2},1^{(2)},\tfrac{5-\sqrt5}{2},
2,\tfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2},3^{(3)},\tfrac{5+\sqrt5}{2},5\Bigr\},
\label{eq:L1_spectrum}
\end{equation}
whose characteristic polynomial splits over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$.  Collapsing
each $m$-multiplet at fixed $(n,l)$ to one vertex, with edge weights the
number of crossing edges, gives a weighted graph on the six sectors $(n,l)$
with Laplacian spectrum $\{0,0,0,1,3,6\}$, characteristic polynomial
$x^3(x-1)(x-3)(x-6)$; this is the base graph used in Paper~2~\cite{paper2}.
Both spectra are pinned exactly in \texttt{tests/test\_paper25\_s2\_quotient.py}.

\section{Compact $U(1)$ on the Rule-B graph}
\label{sec:ruleb}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Setting and comparison class}

On the Rule-B graph we take the compact $U(1)$ action~(\ref{eq:u1_action})
with $\mathcal{P}$ the 4-cycles.  The plaquettes span the cycle space but are
not independent ($b_2>0$), so the theory does not factorize, and the
minimal closed 2-cycles of three plaquettes are where monopoles can be
detected.  Two features fix what this theory should be compared with.  Each
link lies in $8.8$ plaquettes on average at $n_{\max}=2$ and $37.5$ at
$n_{\max}=3$, against $2(d-1)$ on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, and the mean degree grows
with the cutoff; and the graph is irregular.  Large plaquette multiplicity is
the regime in which mean-field and large-dimension treatments of lattice
gauge theory become controlled~\cite{drouffe_zuber1983}, and irregular
lattices with plaquettes taken as the smallest loops are the setting of
random-lattice gauge theory~\cite{christ_friedberg_lee1982}.  Those are the
natural comparison classes.  No diagnostic below was run against either, and
none was run against a four-dimensional control; the diagnostics test
compatibility with compact $U(1)$, which in three dimensions confines at
every coupling through a dilute monopole plasma~\cite{polyakov1977,banks_myerson_kogut1977}
and in four dimensions has a Coulomb phase above
$\beta_c\approx1.01$~\cite{guth1980,degrand_toussaint1980}.

The archived drivers and data for this section are in
\texttt{tests/wilson\_rule\_b\_support/}; every number quoted is recomputed or
pinned by \texttt{tests/test\_paper41\_wilson\_witnesses.py}.

\subsection{Seven diagnostics}

\paragraph{(1) Spectral dimension.}
From the heat-kernel return probability of $L_0$, fitted over the same
fixed band $0.1<P(t)-c/V<0.5$ on both graphs, Rule B has $d_s$ of about
$2.1$ to $2.3$ for $n_{\max}=3$--$8$, about $0.45$ above the scalar graph
(about $1.7$ to $1.8$ over the same band), with no trend in $n_{\max}$.  A window that slides with graph
size gives $1.86$, $2.27$, $2.54$ at $n_{\max}=3,4,5$ and passes $3$ at
$n_{\max}=8$; that rise is an artifact of the window.  The graph has growing
degree and is not a lattice approaching a manifold.

\paragraph{(2) Migdal--Kadanoff flow.}
The bond-moving recursion~\cite{migdal1975,kadanoff1976}
$I_1(\beta')/I_0(\beta')=[I_1(\beta)/I_0(\beta)]^{k}$ with
$k=\langle q_e\rangle$ flows to $\beta=0$ with no nontrivial fixed point.
This holds for any $k>1$, because $I_1/I_0$ increases from $0$ to $1$, so it
depends on the graph only through $k$.  The recursion misses the
four-dimensional transition on the hypercubic lattice as well, so the flow
discriminates nothing.

\paragraph{(3) Gaussian Wilson loops.}
At weak coupling,
$\langle W_C\rangle\simeq\exp(-\frac{1}{2\beta}\langle c,(B_2B_2^{\top})^{+}c\rangle)$.
Averaged over primitive closed non-backtracking walks of length
$L=4,6,8$, the exponent scales as $L^{\alpha}$ with
$\alpha\in[0.89,1.18]$ for $n_{\max}=2$--$5$: a perimeter law, as for the
free photon in three or four dimensions.  This sector does not see
compactness.

\paragraph{(4) Strong coupling.}
At leading order in small $\beta$, a loop bounding a surface of $A$
plaquettes has $\langle W\rangle=(I_1/I_0)^{A}+\ldots$, with string tension
$-\ln(I_1/I_0)\simeq\ln(2/\beta)$.  This is a property of the expansion on
any plaquette complex and says nothing outside the strong-coupling regime.

\paragraph{(5) Monopole density.}
With the DeGrand--Toussaint construction~\cite{degrand_toussaint1980} on the
three-plaquette closed 2-cycles ($60$ at $n_{\max}=2$, $80$ at $n_{\max}=3$),
each plaquette angle is split as $\theta_P=\bar\theta_P+2\pi m_P$ with
$\bar\theta_P\in(-\pi,\pi]$, and the integer charge on a 2-cycle $S$ is
$m_S=\sum_{P\in S}\pm m_P$.  Metropolis Monte Carlo of~(\ref{eq:u1_action})
gives a density $\rho_M(\beta)$ that falls roughly exponentially on
$0.05\le\beta\le1.0$; the fit $\rho_M=a\,e^{-c\beta}$ gives $c=9.40$ and
$8.95$ at $n_{\max}=2,3$ ($R^2=0.981$ both).  Above $\beta=1$ the density is
below the detection floor.  Exponential suppression in $\beta$ is generic to
compact $U(1)$, and the measured range lies below the four-dimensional
$\beta_c$, where the four-dimensional theory also confines; in four
dimensions the monopole currents are closed loops that percolate in the
confined phase and become small and sparse in the Coulomb
phase~\cite{drouffe_zuber1983,degrand_toussaint1980}.  The data do not reach
the coupling at which the two would differ.

\paragraph{(6) Full Monte Carlo Wilson loops.}
Loops are built as boundaries of clusters of $A$ plaquettes.  A fit of
$\ln\langle W\rangle$ against $A$ alone gives a positive, decreasing slope
($0.377$, $0.011$, $0.003$ at $\beta=0.1$, $3$, $10$, $n_{\max}=2$), but a
joint fit $\ln W=-\sigma A-\mu L+\mathrm{const}$ gives an area coefficient
$|\sigma|<0.05$ with $\mu>5|\sigma|$ at every $\beta$ at $n_{\max}=2$: the loops are
perimeter dominated and no area law is resolved.  At $n_{\max}=4$ the joint
fit gives $\sigma=-0.080\pm0.002$ at $\beta=0.3$, which we attribute to the
cluster construction, in which the minimal perimeter is not monotone in the
area.  The exponential rate of the area-only slope, $1.196$, matches that of
the perimeter coefficient, $1.242$, so the area-only fit measures the
perimeter term; its disagreement with the dilute-gas prediction
$c/2=4.70$ for a string tension~\cite{polyakov1977} is open.

\paragraph{(7) Polyakov loops.}
On the product graph $G_B\times C_{N_t}$ at $n_{\max}=2$, with spatial and
temporal-rectangle plaquettes, the cohomology defect
$E-V+1-\operatorname{rank}B_2$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:h1_defect}) is $1$ for
$N_t\ge3$ and $0$ for $N_t=2$, where the temporal loop backtracks and
$P\equiv1$.  The extra class is the temporal winding, so the Polyakov loop
$P(v)$ is a well-defined observable not generated by plaquettes.  Shifting
every temporal link of one time slice by $\varphi$ leaves the action
invariant and multiplies $P$ by $e^{i\varphi}$, so $\langle P\rangle=0$ at
every $\beta$ in finite volume and carries no phase information.  The order
parameter $\langle|P_{\mathrm{lat}}|\rangle$, with
$P_{\mathrm{lat}}=V_B^{-1}\sum_vP(v)$, rises from the random-phase value
$0.285$ at $\beta=0.3$ to $0.498$ and $0.833$ at $\beta=1$, $3$ ($N_t=4$;
$0.285$, $0.341$, $0.691$ at $N_t=8$).  On a ten-site spatial graph this
does not settle the phase, and it does not indicate persistent confinement.

\subsection{Verdict}

Compact $U(1)$ on the Rule-B graph is a well-defined, non-factorizing
lattice gauge theory.  Its diagnostics are compatible with compact $U(1)$:
(2)--(4) hold for compact $U(1)$ on any plaquette complex, (1) gives a
fixed-scale separation from the scalar graph rather than a dimension, (5)
stops below the coupling where three and four dimensions differ, (6)
resolves no area law, and (7) supplies a well-defined finite-temperature
observable whose order parameter orders with $\beta$.  No dimension is
discriminated.  The sizes are small ($n_{\max}\le4$ for the Monte Carlo,
$n_{\max}=2$ for the product graph), and the comparisons the graph's high
plaquette multiplicity calls for, against mean-field, large-dimension and
random-lattice results, are not made.

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}
% ===================================================================

\paragraph*{Matter coupling.}  The Rule-B vertices are Dirac labels, so
covariant hopping $\bar\psi_vU_{vv'}\psi_{v'}$ on its dipole edges is the
natural first matter-plus-gauge theory on a label graph; on the scalar graph
matter coupling would only reproduce the factorized two-dimensional
theories.

\paragraph*{Comparison controls for Rule B.}  The diagnostics of
Section~\ref{sec:ruleb} need, in order: mean-field and large-dimension
predictions at the measured plaquette multiplicities; a four-dimensional
hypercubic control run through the same code; monopole data above
$\beta\approx1$; and Wilson loops at fixed perimeter by construction.

\paragraph*{Non-abelian links on Rule B.}  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ links on the
Rule-B graph give a non-factorizing non-abelian theory on a label graph; it
has not been built.

% ===================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===================================================================

On the scalar label graph the 4-cycles are the unit squares of planar grids
and form a cycle basis, so $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $U(1)$ Wilson theories are the
exactly solvable two-dimensional theories,
$\langle W\rangle=(I_2/I_1)^{A}$ and $(I_1/I_0)^{A}$, at every $n_{\max}$;
the weak-coupling kinetic term is the curl form $B_2B_2^{\top}$, which
completes the edge Laplacian to the Hodge-1 Laplacian, with a nonzero cubic
vertex in the non-abelian case.  On the Dirac Rule-B graph the 4-cycles
satisfy relations, compact $U(1)$ does not factorize, and seven standard
diagnostics are compatible with compact $U(1)$ without discriminating a
dimension; the graph's high plaquette multiplicity places it in the
mean-field, large-dimension class, against which it has yet to be tested.
