\begin{abstract}
We compute the electron--photon vertex
$V = \int_{S^3} \langle \psi_1, c(A)\,\psi_2 \rangle$ on the round unit
three-sphere, with $\psi_{1,2}$ Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac eigenspinors,
$A$ a coclosed 1-form eigenform and $c$ Clifford multiplication, and
use it to score eight selection rules.  Representation theory of
$\mathrm{Spin}(4) = \mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ gives the
rule, and two independent exact computations confirm it with no extra
channel and no accidental zero: a group-theoretic route through
Clebsch--Gordan coefficients (896 cells: sign pair, photon component and
levels $n_1, n_2 \le 3$, $q \le 7$) and a coordinate route by exact
monomial integration in $\mathbb{R}^4$ (360 cells), which agree wherever
both run.  For spinors of the same sign of $D$ the vertex keeps the
window $|n_1 - n_2| < q < n_1 + n_2$, $n_1 + n_2 + q$ odd, of the vertex
model used for spectral sums in Paper~28, and adds the channel
$q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$.  For opposite signs it opens channels with
$n_1 + n_2 + q$ even, which the model has none of.  The ground state
couples, also to itself: the diagonal vertex $V(a, a)$ does not vanish.
The Ward--Takahashi identity
$\langle u, c(df)\,v \rangle = (\lambda_u - \lambda_v)\langle u, f v\rangle$
holds exactly.  Each of the eight rules is scored three ways: on the
GeoVac graph (scalar Fock graph 1/8, native Dirac graph 4/8, the latter
by the definition of its edges), in the vertex model, and with the
physical vertex.  With the physical vertex, rules~1 (triangle sparsity)
and~4 ($\Delta m$) hold, rule~6 holds in Ward--Takahashi form, and
rules~2 (ground-state zero), 5 (E1 parity) and~8 ($q \le n_1 + n_2$)
fail; rule~3 holds as a statement of representation theory but not as
the model's channel-count table; for rule~7 the diagonal-vertex zero
fails and Furry's theorem itself is not decided without charge
conjugation and three-vertex traces.  The $1{+}6{+}1$ partition that
the vertex model gives does not survive the physical vertex.  Kept
from the graph and model analyses: the pendant-edge value
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{graph}}(\mathrm{GS}) = 2(n_{\max}-1)/n_{\max}$, the fact
that the edge Laplacian does not block-diagonalize by photon labels
$(q, m_q)$, the diagonal $\alpha_z$ identity of the model, and the
normalization identity $\Sigma_{l=1} = 1/(4\pi)$.  Coupling
normalization, regularization and the gauge sector are owed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===================================================================

Paper~28~\cite{paper28} studies the periods of spectral sums over the
Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac spectrum~\cite{camporesi1996} on $S^3$.  Its
nested sums couple spinor levels $n_1, n_2$ through a photon level $q$
by a vertex model: the triangle rule for scalar harmonics,
$|n_1 - n_2| \le q \le n_1 + n_2$, with $n_1 + n_2 + q$ odd imposed.  This
paper asks what the vertex of QED on round $S^3$ actually allows, and
scores a list of eight selection rules against it.

The eight rules are those of Table~\ref{tab:eight_rules}: five standard
rules of perturbative QED and three stated in terms of the $S^3$
construction.  Each is scored three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{on the graph}: QED built on the finite GeoVac Fock graph,
  with photons as 1-cochains (Sec.~\ref{sec:graph});
\item \emph{in the vertex model}: vector-photon labels $(q, m_q)$ with
  Wigner-$3j$ coupling to scalar harmonics and the imposed parity rule,
  the model behind Paper~28's sums (Sec.~\ref{sec:model});
\item \emph{with the physical vertex}: the Clifford-multiplication
  vertex between Dirac eigenspinors and coclosed 1-forms, computed
  exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}).
\end{enumerate}
The physical vertex is the reference.  It is computed at the level of
selection rules and unnormalized amplitudes; the coupling
normalization, the photon propagator with its gauge sector, and the
flat-space limit are owed (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).

\section{The Physical Vertex on $S^3$}
\label{sec:physical}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Content}

Write $S^3 = \mathrm{SU}(2)$, so that
$\mathrm{Spin}(4) = \mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ acts by
isometries.  The Dirac operator has eigenvalues $\pm(n + 3/2)$ with
$(n+1)(n+2)$ states for each sign~\cite{camporesi1996}; with the
convention used throughout, the sign-$+$ eigenspace at level $n$ carries
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ content $((n+1)/2, n/2)$ and
the sign-$-$ eigenspace $(n/2, (n+1)/2)$.  The coclosed 1-forms at level
$q \ge 1$ have Hodge eigenvalue $(q+1)^2$ and two components,
\begin{equation}
  A: \bigl(\tfrac{q+1}{2}, \tfrac{q-1}{2}\bigr), \qquad
  B: \bigl(\tfrac{q-1}{2}, \tfrac{q+1}{2}\bigr),
  \label{eq:vector_content}
\end{equation}
with $q(q+2)$ states each~\cite{ikeda1978}, on which $*d = \mp(q+1)$.
Clifford multiplication by a 1-form is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$-equivariant,
so $V$ can be nonzero only if
$\rho_1^* \otimes \rho_A \otimes \rho_2$ contains the trivial
representation.  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ tensor products are multiplicity-free,
so each (sign pair, component) carries at most one reduced amplitude.

\subsection{The rule}

\begin{theorem}[Selection rule of the physical vertex]
\label{thm:physical_rule}
Let $d = |n_1 - n_2|$.  The vertex can be nonzero only on the following
channels.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Same sign} ($++$; $--$ is the mirror $A \leftrightarrow B$):
  $n_1 + n_2 + q$ odd, and component $A$ for
  $d + 1 \le q \le n_1 + n_2 + 1$, component $B$ for
  $d + 1 \le q \le n_1 + n_2 - 1$.
\item \emph{Opposite sign} ($+-$; $-+$ is the transpose): $n_1 + n_2 + q$
  even, with component $A$ for
  $|n_1 + 1 - n_2| \le q + 1 \le n_1 + n_2 + 1$ and
  $|n_1 - n_2 - 1| \le q - 1 \le n_1 + n_2 + 1$, and component $B$ for
  $|n_1 + 1 - n_2| \le q - 1 \le n_1 + n_2 + 1$ and
  $|n_1 - n_2 - 1| \le q + 1 \le n_1 + n_2 + 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The triangle and integrality conditions in each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factor
applied to the content above.  For same-sign spinors the left triangle
$((n_1+1)/2, (q\pm1)/2, (n_2+1)/2)$ and the right triangle
$(n_1/2, (q\mp1)/2, n_2/2)$ give the stated ranges, and all four
integrality conditions reduce to $n_1 + n_2 + q$ odd.  For opposite
signs the content of $\psi_2$ is mirrored, which shifts each triangle by
one unit and flips the integrality condition to $n_1 + n_2 + q$ even.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Comparison with the vertex model]
\label{cor:model_comparison}
With $n_1 + n_2 + q$ odd, the model window
$|n_1 - n_2| \le q \le n_1 + n_2$ is $d + 1 \le q \le n_1 + n_2 - 1$.  On
it both same-sign components are open, so the physical same-sign count
equals the model's $W = 2$ there (Paper~28, Sec.~``Inner photon sum'').
The only same-sign channel outside it is $q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$, with one
component.  Every opposite-sign channel is outside the model.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[The ground state couples]
\label{cor:ground_state}
For an external level $n = 0$ the open channels are: same sign at
$q = n_{\mathrm{int}} + 1$ for every $n_{\mathrm{int}} \ge 0$, and
opposite sign at $q = n_{\mathrm{int}}$ for every $n_{\mathrm{int}} \ge 1$
(component $B$ for external sign $+$), one component each.  In
particular the ground state couples to itself at $q = 1$.
\end{corollary}

The theorem is a necessary condition.  That each open channel has a
nonzero reduced amplitude is checked below.

\subsection{Exact computation by two routes}

The rule and the amplitudes were computed in exact arithmetic before
comparison with the predictions, which were registered first
(prediction record
\texttt{docs/predictions/2026-11-07\_physical\_vector\_vertex.md},
with its scored outcome); both routes are ported into the regression
suite as \texttt{tests/test\_paper33\_physical\_vertex.py}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Route 1 (group theory).}  Functions on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ are
  Wigner $D$-functions; spinors and 1-forms are built in the
  left-invariant frame, $D = 2\,\sigma\cdot R + 3/2$ and
  $*d = -2\,S\cdot R - 2$, with $R$ the right-translation generators and
  the constants fixed by the spin connection and by $*d(\mathrm{grad}) = 0$.
  The vertex factorizes into a left Clebsch--Gordan coefficient and a
  frame-side contraction; zero is exact zero in the multi-quadratic
  number field.  Range $n_1, n_2 \le 3$, $q \le 7$, both signs, both
  components, all $m$: 896 cells.
\item \emph{Route 2 (coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^4$).}  Eigenspinors are
  exact null spaces of $D - \lambda$ on polynomial spinors restricted to
  $S^3$, checked against $D$ directly; eigenforms are exact null spaces
  of $*d \mp (q+1)$ on polynomial 4-vectors, with tangency and
  coclosedness verified afterwards, not imposed.  Content is measured by
  the two $\mathrm{su}(2)$ Casimirs.  Vertices are integrated by exact
  monomial moments on $S^3$, with no use of representation theory.
  Range $n_1, n_2 \le 2$, $q \le 5$: 360 cells.  Route~2 imports neither
  route~1 nor the \texttt{geovac} package.
\end{itemize}
Controls: both routes reproduce the Dirac spectrum $\pm(n + 3/2)$ with
multiplicity $(n+1)(n+2)$ per sign and the coclosed spectrum with
$q(q+2)$ states per component; route~1 restricted to the model window
reproduces the channel counts of \texttt{geovac/qed\_anomalous\_moment.py};
and the two routes agree on all 360 common cells, on open/closed and on
the exact value of $\sum_m |V|^2$.  On all 896 cells $\sum_m |V|^2$ equals
the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \sum_m |V|^2 = 6\,(2j_A{+}1)(2j_2{+}1)(2j_1{+}1)(2J_A{+}1)(2J_2{+}1)(2J_1{+}1)
  \begin{Bmatrix} j_A & j_2 & j_1 \\ 1 & \tfrac12 & \tfrac12 \\
  J_A & J_2 & J_1 \end{Bmatrix}^{2},
  \label{eq:ninej}
\end{equation}
where for each field $j$ is the left-translation spin of its scalar
part and $J$ the right-translation spin including the frame index
($J = j \pm 1/2$ for a spinor, $J_A = j_A \pm 1$ for a coclosed 1-form).

\paragraph{Results.}  Of the 896 cells, 168 are open (44 $++$, 44 $--$,
40 $+-$, 40 $-+$); every open cell obeys Theorem~\ref{thm:physical_rule}
and every cell the theorem allows is open.  Of the 88 open same-sign
cells, 32 lie outside the model window, all at $q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$.
Every nonzero amplitude conserves $m$ in each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factor
separately.  Computed with Haar measure of volume one, every amplitude is
algebraic; $\pi$ enters only with the volume $2\pi^2$ of the round
sphere.

\subsection{The diagonal vertex}

\begin{proposition}[The diagonal vertex does not vanish]
\label{prop:diagonal}
For each ground state $a$ ($n = 0$, either sign of $D$),
$\sum_{m_A} |\langle a | c(A) | a \rangle|^2 = 1$ over the $q = 1$
coclosed eigenforms of the open component ($A$ for sign $+$, $B$ for
sign $-$; the other component gives $0$).
\end{proposition}

Both routes give $1$ for every basis state.  The witness is elementary:
a Killing spinor (constant in the left-invariant frame) against a
left-invariant coframe 1-form, a coclosed $q = 1$ eigenform, gives a
Pauli matrix element times the volume.

\subsection{Ward--Takahashi identity}

\begin{proposition}[Ward--Takahashi identity on $S^3$]
\label{prop:ward}
For Dirac eigenspinors $u, v$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_u, \lambda_v$ and
a function $f$,
\begin{equation}
  \langle u, c(df)\,v \rangle = (\lambda_u - \lambda_v)\,\langle u, f v\rangle .
  \label{eq:ward}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$[D, f] = c(df)$ as operators; take matrix elements between
eigenspinors.
\end{proof}

Route~2 checks Eq.~\eqref{eq:ward} exactly on 384 element pairs ($f$ a
harmonic polynomial of degree 1 or 2, spinor levels $n \le 1$), 56 of
them with nonzero left side.  The identity is the vertex-level form of
the Ward--Takahashi identity~\cite{ward1950,takahashi1957}: an exact
(pure-gauge) photon decouples between degenerate states, and in general
its coupling is fixed by the charge vertex.

\subsection{An unnormalized one-loop sum}

With the physical channels and both internal signs, the one-loop sum of
Paper~28's self-energy form at external level $0$, with the squared
amplitudes in place of the channel count (unit coupling, volume-one
measure, photon weight $q(q+2)$ as in Paper~28), is $0.2669$, $0.2702$,
$0.2715$, $0.2719$ at cutoffs $n_{\max} = 3, 5, 8, 12$.  It is nonzero
at every cutoff, where the model's corresponding sum is zero (Paper~28,
Theorem~4).  The number is not a self-energy: normalization,
regularization and the photon's Hodge eigenvalue $(q+1)^2$ in place of
$q(q+2)$ are owed.

\section{The Vertex Model}
\label{sec:model}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Construction}

\begin{definition}[Vertex model]
\label{def:vertex}
Photon modes are $(q, m_q)$, $1 \le q \le q_{\max}$, $|m_q| \le q$, with
propagator $G_\gamma(q) = 1/[q(q+2)]$.  For electron states
$a = (n_a, l_a, m_a)$, $b = (n_b, l_b, m_b)$ the vertex is
\begin{equation}
   V(a, b, q, m_q) = \begin{pmatrix} l_a & q & l_b \\ -m_a & m_q & m_b
   \end{pmatrix} \theta(l_a + l_b + q ~ \text{odd}),
   \label{eq:vertex}
\end{equation}
times the harmonic normalization
$\sqrt{(2l_a+1)(2q+1)(2l_b+1)/(4\pi)}$ and unit radial factor; for Dirac
electrons the photon couples through $\alpha_z$ against the scalar
harmonic $Y_q^{m_q}$.  It is implemented in \texttt{geovac/vector\_qed.py}.
\end{definition}

This is the vertex model of Paper~28 in angular form: its
triangle is the scalar-harmonic one and its parity rule is imposed, so
its ground-state self-energy vanishes for the reason of Paper~28,
Theorem~4.

\subsection{The diagonal $\alpha_z$ identity}

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal $\alpha_z$ vertex vanishes]
\label{thm:alpha_z}
For Dirac spinors $\psi = (f \Omega_\kappa, i g \Omega_{-\kappa})$ with
real radial functions and the model's coupling, $\alpha_z$ against
$Y_q^{m_q}$, the diagonal vertex satisfies $V(a, a, q, m_q) = 0$
identically.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $a = b$ the $m$-rule forces $m_q = 0$, so only $Y_q^0$ and
$\alpha_z = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & \sigma_z \\ \sigma_z & 0
\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ enter.  Then
\[
  \psi^\dagger \alpha_z \psi
  = i fg \bigl[\Omega_\kappa^\dagger \sigma_z \Omega_{-\kappa}
    - \Omega_{-\kappa}^\dagger \sigma_z \Omega_\kappa\bigr]
  = -2 fg\, \Im M, \qquad
  M = \Omega_\kappa^\dagger \sigma_z \Omega_{-\kappa}.
\]
Expanding $\Omega_{j,l,m_j} = \sum_{m_s}
\langle l, m_l; \tfrac12, m_s | j, m_j \rangle Y_l^{m_l} \chi_{m_s}$
with $m_l = m_j - m_s$, $\sigma_z$ is real diagonal and conserves $m_s$,
so
\[
   M = \sum_{m_s = \pm 1/2} (\mathrm{sign}\,m_s)
     \langle l_\kappa, m_l; \tfrac{1}{2}, m_s | j, m_j \rangle
     \langle l_{-\kappa}, m_l; \tfrac{1}{2}, m_s | j, m_j \rangle
     Y_{l_\kappa}^{m_l\,*} Y_{l_{-\kappa}}^{m_l},
\]
with real Clebsch--Gordan coefficients.  The product
$Y_{l_\kappa}^{m_l\,*} Y_{l_{-\kappa}}^{m_l}$ has matched magnetic indices,
so its phases $e^{\pm i m_l \phi}$ cancel and it is a real function of
$\theta$.  Hence $\Im M \equiv 0$, the integrand vanishes pointwise, and
$V(a, a, q, 0) = \int \psi^\dagger \alpha_z \psi\, Y_q^0\, d\Omega = 0$.
\end{proof}

Six cases ($\kappa \in \{-1, +1, -2\}$, $m_j \in \{\pm 1/2, +3/2\}$) are
verified symbolically in \texttt{tests/test\_paper33\_furry\_derivation.py}:
the bilinear vanishes pointwise, $\Im M \equiv 0$ with $M \not\equiv 0$,
and the projected integral is zero.  The mechanism is $m_l$
conservation, not Hermiticity alone: Hermiticity gives
$V_{LS} + V_{SL} = 2i\,\Im V_{LS}$, and $\Im V_{LS} = 0$ needs the
matched magnetic index.

\begin{remark}[Not a property of the physical vertex]
\label{rem:not_C}
Theorem~\ref{thm:alpha_z} concerns the model's coupling.  A physical
vector photon with $m_q = 0$ also has $m = \pm 1$ spherical components,
which couple through $\alpha_\pm$; the diagonal magnetic coupling is
nonzero, as Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal} shows on $S^3$.  Nor would a
diagonal zero be Furry's theorem~\cite{furry1937}: in a closed loop the
intermediate vertices connect different states, so a diagonal identity
removes only the tadpole, while Furry's theorem comes from charge
conjugation of the field.  In \texttt{geovac/vector\_qed.py} the diagonal
zero is imposed by setting $V(a, a) = 0$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The normalization identity}

\begin{proposition}[$\Sigma_{l=1} = 1/(4\pi)$]
\label{prop:1_over_4pi}
In the vertex model at $n_{\max} = 2$ the $l = 1$ self-energy is exactly
$1/(4\pi)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only $q = 1$ and the two $2p$ intermediate states $b$ contribute, with
$4\pi\sum_{m_q}|V|^2 = 9/2$ per $b$ (the Gaunt integral with the
harmonic normalization of Definition~\ref{def:vertex}), $G_e = 1/3$ and
$G_\gamma(1) = 1/3$, so
$\Sigma_{l=1} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{9}{2} \cdot \tfrac19 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4\pi}$.
Verified in \texttt{test\_scalar\_l1\_self\_energy\_is\_one\_over\_4pi}
(\texttt{tests/test\_vector\_qed.py}).
\end{proof}

The factor $1/(4\pi)$ is the normalization of spherical harmonics on the
unit $S^2$, which the model's angular vertex carries.  The physical
vertex of Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}, normalized to volume one on $S^3$,
does not carry it.

\section{QED on the Graph}
\label{sec:graph}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Construction}

On the finite Fock graph (nodes $(n, l, m_l)$, edges the Fock ladders)
photons are 1-cochains, the photon propagator is the pseudo-inverse
$G_\gamma = L_1^+$ of the edge Laplacian $L_1 = B^\top B$ ($B$ the
incidence matrix), and the vertex places the two
electron legs at the endpoints of the photon edge.  The native Dirac
graph replaces the node labels by $(n, \kappa, m_j)$, with edge rule~B
(E1 dipole: $\Delta l = \pm 1$, $|\Delta j| \le 1$, $|\Delta m_j| \le 1$).

\subsection{The pendant-edge value}

\begin{proposition}[Pendant-edge theorem]
\label{prop:pendant_edge}
On the scalar Fock graph at every $n_{\max} \ge 2$, the one-loop
self-energy at the ground state $|1, 0, 0\rangle$ is
\[
   \Sigma_{\mathrm{graph}}(\mathrm{GS}) = \frac{2(n_{\max} - 1)}{n_{\max}}
   \xrightarrow{n_{\max} \to \infty} 2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ground state is a leaf of the Fock graph: its unique edge $e_0$
joins it to $|2, 0, 0\rangle$.  The $s$-wave edges form a path of
$N = n_{\max} - 1$ edges in $L_1$, decoupled from the $l \ge 1$ edges, and
the path-graph Laplacian satisfies $T_N^{-1}[0,0] = N/(N+1)$, so
$G_\gamma[e_0, e_0] = (n_{\max}-1)/n_{\max}$.  All Clebsch--Gordan
coefficients at $l = 0$ equal $1$ and the two intermediate states
$m_j = \pm 1/2$ contribute equally, giving
$\Sigma = 2\,G_\gamma[e_0, e_0]$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Verified in \texttt{tests/test\_graph\_qed\_self\_energy.py}:
exactly at $n_{\max} = 2$ (ground-state block $[[1,1],[1,1]]$) and
numerically to $10^{-10}$ at $n_{\max} = 3, 4$.  The graph value is
nonzero, like the physical vertex's and unlike the model's.

\subsection{Photon labels are not in the edge Laplacian}

\begin{observation}[$L_1$ does not block-diagonalize by $(q, m_q)$]
\label{obs:L1}
Photon labels describe the transition between the endpoint labels of an
edge, not a quantity conserved at a vertex: at a vertex, edges with
different photon labels meet, and $L_1$ couples them.  The cross-sector
Frobenius fraction of $L_1$ in the $(q, m_q)$ grouping is $20\%$ at
$n_{\max} = 3$ on the scalar Fock graph and $57\%$ on the Dirac rule-B
graph.  The only decompositions are by connected components ($l$ on the
Fock graph, $\kappa$ on the Dirac rule-A graph).
\end{observation}

\noindent So vector-photon structure is not a reorganization of the
graph; it has to be added.

\subsection{Graph scores}

On the scalar Fock graph only rule~1 holds: the Fock edges obey the
ladder selection rules, which are Clebsch--Gordan triangle conditions.
On the Dirac rule-B graph rules~1, 4, 5 and a graph analog of rule~7
hold, all by the definition of the edges: $m_j$ is a node label,
$\Delta l = \pm 1$ is the edge rule, and the bipartite structure by
$l$-parity makes the identity-vertex tadpole vanish.  These are
construction-consistency checks, not computed recoveries.  Rule~8 fails
on both graphs because the edges are nearest-neighbour in $n$.  Rule~6
was scored on the graph by a commutator proxy, $\|[\Sigma, H_0]\| /
\|\Sigma\|$, which is not a Ward identity; it fails there.  The
aggregate scores $1/8$ and $4/8$ are asserted in
\texttt{tests/test\_vector\_qed.py} (\texttt{TestCensusAggregates}).

\paragraph{Rule by rule, with the physical vertex.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] Holds in its $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ form: every open cell satisfies
  both $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ triangles.
\item[(2)] Fails: the ground state couples (Corollary~\ref{cor:ground_state}),
  and opposite-sign channels have $n_1 + n_2 + q$ even.  The parity
  clause is a rule for same-sign spinors only.
\item[(3)] Holds as the statement that the vertex vanishes on
  representation-theoretically disallowed cells; fails as the model's
  table, which misses $q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$ and every opposite-sign channel.
\item[(4)] Holds, in each $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factor separately.
\item[(5)] Fails in its literal form, since a state couples to itself
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal}).  The antipodal element of
  $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ gives photon parity $(-1)^{q+1}$, not $-1$; the
  selection content it carries is the integrality condition already in
  rules~1 and~2.
\item[(6)] Holds as the Ward--Takahashi identity, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ward}.
\item[(7)] The diagonal-vertex zero fails; Furry's theorem is not
  decided here.
\item[(8)] Fails at every $q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the model's tally was, and why it does not carry over.}
In the vertex model the rules grouped as $1{+}6{+}1$: rule~1 from the
graph, rules 2--6 and~8 from adding the photon labels, and rule~7 from
the $\alpha_z$ identity (Dirac electrons).  With the physical vertex
that partition does not survive: three of the six rules the photon
labels were credited with fail (2, 5, 8), the channel count holds only
as representation theory, rule~6 holds in a form the model cannot
state, and the mechanism behind rule~7 fails.  What does carry over is
that rules~1 and~4 are properties of the coupling coefficients in every
construction.

\section{Open Questions}
\label{sec:open}
% ===================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Normalization.}  Fix the coupling and the photon field
  normalization: the transverse propagator on $S^3$ in a stated gauge,
  with the coexact mode measure and Hodge eigenvalue $(q+1)^2$, and the
  relation of the volume-one amplitudes to the radius.
\item \emph{Regularization and the flat-space limit.}  The large-level
  asymptotics of the reduced amplitudes, from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ninej}, are
  needed to define the one-loop sums and to take $R \to \infty$ at fixed
  physical momentum.
\item \emph{Gauge sector.}  Include the exact and longitudinal modes and
  the ghosts (Feynman gauge), with Eq.~\eqref{eq:ward} as input.
\item \emph{Furry's theorem.}  Construct charge conjugation on $S^3$
  spinors and compute three-vertex traces to decide rule~7.
\item \emph{Periods.}  Which periods do the chain sums of Paper~28 take
  when built on the physical window and amplitudes?
\end{enumerate}

% ===================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===================================================================

The vertex of QED on round $S^3$, computed exactly by two independent
routes, keeps the vertex model's rule for same-sign spinors inside the
model's window and goes beyond it in three ways: the channel
$q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$, opposite-sign channels with $n_1 + n_2 + q$ even,
and a nonzero diagonal vertex, so that the ground state couples.  It
satisfies the Ward--Takahashi identity exactly.  Scored against it,
two of the eight rules hold as stated (triangle sparsity, $\Delta m$),
one holds in Ward--Takahashi form, one holds only as representation
theory, three fail, and Furry's theorem is left undecided.  The graph
scores ($1/8$ and $4/8$) and the model's $1{+}6{+}1$ tally describe those
constructions, not the physical vertex.

The stage-A computation is recorded in the prediction record named in
Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}; a regression test porting its checks to
\texttt{tests/} is owed.
