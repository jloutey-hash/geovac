\title{A Convergent Discrete Replica Derivative on the Spinor Cone, \\
       with the Two-Term Camporesi--Higuchi Spectral Action \\
       on $S^3 \times S^1$}
\author{J.~Loutey}
\date{November 7, 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove one new theorem and record the two continuum results it serves.
The theorem concerns a finite-difference discretization of the
two-dimensional cone of apex angle $2\pi\alpha$ carrying an
anti-periodic (spinor) field: its heat trace $K_n(\alpha, t)$ converges
to the continuum heat trace in $C^1$ in $\alpha$ near $\alpha = 1$, so the
replica derivative $dK_n/d\alpha|_{\alpha=1}$, whose mode sum carries an
extra weight $(j+\tfrac12)$, converges, and that weight costs nothing in
the rate (Theorem~\ref{thm:L6}). The proof dominates the mode series and
its $\alpha$-derivative by one summable Gaussian sequence. Numerically, the
replica combination converges to the continuum value $1/6$, at order
$O(a)$ in the radial spacing $a$ on the production grid. The continuum
results are these. (i)~The Chamseddine--Connes spectral action of the
Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac operator on $\sthree_R$ and on
$\sthree_R \times S^1_\beta$ has exactly two power-law terms, up to
exponentially small corrections. This is Chamseddine and Connes's result
(by Poisson summation); we give an alternative proof through the
Bernoulli identity $B_{2k+1}(3/2) = (2k{+}1)/4^k$, which makes the
spectral zeta function vanish at every non-positive integer. The scalar
and transverse-traceless tensor heat traces on $\sthree$ do not
truncate. (ii)~Traced over the rank-4 spinor bundle, the action's
Einstein--Hilbert coefficient gives $G = 3\pi/(\phi(1)\Lambda^2)$ for a
cutoff with Mellin moments $\phi(s)$. The replica derivative of the
black-hole cigar's tip term, with the Dirac cone coefficient
$-(1/12)(1/\alpha - \alpha)$ and the fermionic sign of the effective
action, gives $S_{\BH} = A\Lambda^2\phi(1)/(12\pi) = A/(4G)$, positive and
with the same $G$. That the two routes agree is the induced-gravity
statement of Susskind--Uglum and Frolov--Fursaev, specialised to this
spectrum. The absence of higher-curvature terms holds on the conformally
flat $\sthree \times S^1$; the cigar is not conformally flat, and there
the two routes agree at leading order in $\Lambda$. We also show that the
second variation of the action on the $(1,1)$ sector of $SO(4)$ cannot
distinguish the diagonal-$SU(2)$ spins $J = 0, 1, 2$, so it cannot by
itself isolate a graviton. None of these results uses the GeoVac label
lattice: they concern the continuum Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum and a
finite-difference cone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

The spectral action $\mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2)$ of
Chamseddine and Connes~\cite{chamseddine_connes1997, chamseddine_connes2010}
turns the spectrum of a Dirac operator into a gravitational action. This
paper records three results about it on the round three-sphere and on the
Euclidean black-hole cigar.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A convergent discrete replica derivative (new).} The
        Bekenstein--Hawking entropy is obtained by the replica method as
        a derivative with respect to the apex angle $2\pi\alpha$ of a
        cone. On a discretized cone, the derivative weights each angular
        mode by $(j+\tfrac12)$, so its convergence does not follow from
        that of the heat trace. Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} shows that it does
        converge, in $C^1$ near $\alpha = 1$, and at the heat-trace rate.
        (INTERNAL THEOREM, with one standard ingredient argued rather than
        proved, \S\ref{sec:L6};
        \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_L6\_full.py}.)
  \item \textbf{Two-term exactness on $\sthree$ and $\sthree \times S^1$.}
        The spectral zeta function of the Camporesi--Higuchi
        spectrum~\cite{camporesi_higuchi1996} vanishes at every
        non-positive integer (Theorem~\ref{thm:zeta_unit_neg_k}), so the
        spectral action has exactly two power-law terms. The result is
        Chamseddine and Connes's~\cite{chamseddine_connes2008_uncanny};
        the Bernoulli route is an alternative proof. (SYMBOLIC PROOF;
        \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_zeta\_unit\_neg\_k.py}.)
  \item \textbf{One Newton constant from two routes.} The
        Einstein--Hilbert coefficient of the action and the replica
        derivative of the cigar tip give the same
        $G = 3\pi/(\phi(1)\Lambda^2)$ (\S\ref{sec:g4}), once the action is
        traced over the rank-4 spinor bundle and the entropy is taken with
        the fermionic sign. (INTERNAL THEOREM, continuum-level;
        \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_replica\_sbh.py},
        \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_cutoff\_mellin.py}.)
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} No result in this paper uses the GeoVac label lattice
(the finite set of $(n, l, m)$ labels of the $\sthree$ harmonics,
\cite{paper7}). The continuum results use only the Camporesi--Higuchi
spectrum $|\lambda_n| = n + 3/2$ with multiplicity $2(n+1)(n+2)$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} concerns a finite-difference discretization of a flat
cone, which is not the label lattice. The results are offered as spectral
geometry of the round three-sphere and the cone, not as consequences of
the lattice. Two further limits apply throughout. First, ``two terms''
and ``no higher-curvature corrections'' are statements about the
conformally flat backgrounds $\sthree$ and $\sthree \times S^1$
(\S\ref{sec:g2}), not about the spectral action in general. Second, the
cutoff function $f$ is external input, as in the Chamseddine--Connes
programme; the paper fixes how each prediction depends on it
(\S\ref{sec:g8}) but not its values.

\paragraph{Prior art.} The two-term result is Chamseddine and
Connes's~\cite{chamseddine_connes2008_uncanny}. The spinor cone coefficient
is Kabat's~\cite{kabat1995} and Fursaev and Miele's~\cite{fursaev_miele1996}.
The agreement of the action-route and entropy-route Newton constants for
minimally coupled fields is the induced-gravity statement of Susskind and
Uglum~\cite{susskind_uglum1994} and of Frolov and
Fursaev~\cite{frolov_fursaev1997}; Solodukhin's review records it for
scalars and Dirac fermions~\cite{solodukhin2011_review}. What is new here
is Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} and the bookkeeping that carries these statements
through the Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum and the rank-4 bundle.

\section{The spinor cone: continuum and discrete}
\label{sec:cone}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Continuum.} Let $C_\alpha$ be the flat two-dimensional cone
of apex angle $2\pi\alpha$, cut off at radius $R$ with a Dirichlet
condition. Its heat trace has the small-$t$ form
\begin{equation}
  K(\alpha, t) = \frac{\alpha\,\pi R^2}{4\pi t}\,c_0
               - (\text{perimeter term} \propto \alpha)
               + c(\alpha) + O(t^{1/2}),
\end{equation}
with $c_0$ the number of field components. The area and perimeter terms
are linear in $\alpha$. The apex term $c(\alpha)$ is the
Sommerfeld--Cheeger coefficient~\cite{sommerfeld1894, dowker1977,
cheeger1983}. For a real scalar it is $+\tfrac{1}{12}(1/\alpha - \alpha)$.
For a two-component Dirac field, with the trace taken over the spinor
index, it is the negative of the scalar value~\cite{kabat1995,
fursaev_miele1996}:
\begin{equation}
  c_{1/2}(\alpha) = -\frac{1}{12}\!\left(\frac{1}{\alpha} - \alpha\right).
  \label{eq:spinor_sc_tip}
\end{equation}
(Fursaev and Miele state that the spin-$\tfrac12$ cone coefficient ``is
just the minus of the same coefficient of the scalar operator'', their
Eq.~(4.13), in agreement with Kabat.) Because the area and perimeter terms
are linear in $\alpha$, the replica combination
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{tip}(t) = \frac{dK}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=1} - K(1, t)
  \label{eq:tip_def}
\end{equation}
keeps only the apex term, and for the spinor it equals
$c_{1/2}'(1) = +1/6$ at small $t/R^2$ (to $2\times10^{-8}$ for
$t/R^2 \le 0.05$; at $t/R^2 = 0.1$ the finite-disk value is $0.166520$).

\paragraph{Discrete.} The discrete cone used here has radial sites
$\rho_i = ia$, $i = 1, \dots, N_\rho$, with $R = N_\rho a$ and a Dirichlet
wall, and a Hermitian finite-difference polar Laplacian obtained from
the substitution $u = \sqrt{\rho}\, f$. The spinor's azimuthal momenta
are $m = (j + \tfrac12)/\alpha$ (anti-periodic boundary condition, taken
either exactly or by an azimuthal finite difference with $N_\phi$ sites),
and each mode sees the radial operator
$L(m) = -\partial_\rho^2 + (m^2 - \tfrac14)/\rho^2$. The discrete heat
trace on the wedge is denoted $K^{\rm Dirac}_{\rm wedge}(\alpha, t)$, and
the disk ($\alpha = 1$) heat trace $K^{\rm Dirac}_{\rm disk}(t)$.

\paragraph{The discrete tip coefficient.} On this substrate the
subtraction
$\Delta_K(\alpha, t) = K^{\rm Dirac}_{\rm wedge}(\alpha, t) - \alpha\,
K^{\rm Dirac}_{\rm disk}(t)$ recovers Eq.~\eqref{eq:spinor_sc_tip} on the
deficit side $\alpha < 1$. At $R = 10$, $a = 0.05$, $N_\rho = 200$, the
mean recovery over $\alpha \in \{2/5, 1/2, 3/5, 2/3\}$ at $t = 1$ is
$99.4\%$, and the single best cell is $\alpha = 2/5$, $t = 2$, at
$1.000021$; that cell is where a sweep crosses the target, not a converged
value. (MEASURED;
\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_g44\_headlines.py}.) A periodic scalar on
the same substrate recovers its coefficient
$+\tfrac{1}{12}(1/\alpha - \alpha)$ at only $66.3\%$ on the same four
$\alpha$ values and $t = 1$. The scalar has an $m = 0$ mode with an
attractive $-1/(4\rho^2)$ term in the $u$-representation, the spinor
has no $m = 0$ mode, and that difference is why the spinor extraction is
clean. (An earlier scalar test on a different estimator --- the fitted
slope of $\Delta_K$ against $(1/\alpha - \alpha)$ over
$\alpha \in \{1/3, \dots, 3\}$ with both branches --- found $28\%$ of the
target at $t = 0.005$, rising to $53\%$ at $t = 1$; the ``about one
quarter'' quoted for it in earlier versions is the $t = 0.005$ value. The
three figures are different measurements, not one measurement quoted
three ways.)

\paragraph{Excess angle.} At $\alpha > 1$ and fixed $a = 0.05$ the
recovery plateaus near $67.9\%$, independent of the azimuthal count.
Refining the radial mesh removes it: at $\alpha = 2$, $t = 1$, the
recovery rises as $N_\rho = 200 \to 3200$ ($0.675, 0.769, 0.837, 0.885,
0.919$), with $1 - \text{recovery} \propto \sqrt{a}$ (successive error
ratios $1.4085, 1.4143, 1.4164$, near $\sqrt{2}$). The continuum
excess-angle coefficient is therefore the plain continuation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:spinor_sc_tip}, and the fixed-$a$ plateau is a
discretization artifact (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_owed\_guards.py}).

\section{Theorem L6: the replica weight is harmless}
\label{sec:L6}
% =====================================================================

The replica method needs the derivative of the heat trace with respect
to $\alpha$, and the question for the discrete cone is whether the
discrete derivative converges to the continuum one, and at what rate.
On the discrete substrate (exact azimuthal momenta
$m_{\rm eff}=(k+\tfrac12)/\alpha$),
$K(\alpha,t)=4\sum_{j\ge0}h((j+\tfrac12)/\alpha,t)$ with $h(m,t)$ the radial
heat trace, and
$\frac{dK}{d\alpha}|_1 = -4\sum_j(j+\tfrac12)h'(j+\tfrac12)$. The per-mode
\emph{replica contribution} $C_j=-4(j+\tfrac12)h'(j+\tfrac12)>0$ carries the
$(j+\tfrac12)$ \emph{replica weight}, heavier than the bare heat trace.

Write $n=(N_\phi, N_\rho, a)$ for the discrete truncation ($N_\phi$ azimuthal
modes, $N_\rho$ radial sites, spacing $a$, $R=N_\rho a$ fixed), and
$g_j^{(n)}(\alpha) := 4\,h_n\!\big((j+\tfrac12)/\alpha,\,t\big)$, so that
$K_n(\alpha,t)=\sum_{j\ge0} g_j^{(n)}(\alpha)$ and the per-mode replica
contribution is $C_j(\alpha)=-g_j^{(n)\prime}(\alpha)$. The continuum objects
$g_j$, $K$ are the $n\to\infty$ limits.

\begin{theorem}[L6: replica-weight-harmless convergence --- INTERNAL
THEOREM, Lemma~\ref{lem:L6_rate}(b) argued; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_L6\_full.py}]
\label{thm:L6}
Fix $t>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$. As $n\to\infty$ the maps
$\alpha\mapsto K_n(\alpha,t)$ converge to $\alpha\mapsto K(\alpha,t)$ in
$C^1([1-\delta,1+\delta])$. In particular
\begin{equation}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{dK_n}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=1}
=\frac{dK}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=1}
=\frac{d}{d\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=1}\lim_{n\to\infty}K_n,
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{tip}_n(t) \to \mathrm{tip}(t) = 1/6$. The convergence rate of
the replica derivative is the per-mode rate of the radial heat trace and
its $m$-derivative: the replica weight $(j+\tfrac12)$ adds only a
super-polynomially small tail and does not degrade it.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses Lemmas~\ref{lem:L6_rate} and~\ref{lem:L6_harmless}; the
second is the new content. Lemma~\ref{lem:L6_backbone} concerns
geometric faithfulness of the truncation, is \textbf{[OPEN]}, and is not
used.

\begin{lemma}[Propinquity backbone; \textbf{[OPEN]}, not used]
\label{lem:L6_backbone}
For each fixed $\alpha$ near $1$, the truncated constant-warp metric spectral
triples $\mathcal{T}_{n,\alpha}$ converge to the continuum tip triple
$\mathcal{T}_\alpha$ in the van Suijlekom state-space Gromov--Hausdorff
distance, $d_{\mathrm{GH}}(\mathcal{T}_{n,\alpha},\mathcal{T}_\alpha)
\le C_3\,\gamma_n\to0$, with $C_3$ truncation-independent.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The azimuthal circle and the horizon sphere have known
reconstruction maps (the Toeplitz circle of Connes and van Suijlekom,
\cite{paper44}; Berezin--Toeplitz quantization of $S^2$), but the
disk-with-cone factor does not: its $1/\rho^2$ term couples the radial and
azimuthal parts, it is a manifold with boundary, and it is not a group.
Paper~53~\cite{paper53} proves state-space convergence on the flat plane
by a positive squared-window reconstruction and translation invariance,
and leaves the disk and the cone open; the cone $\alpha\ne1$ has no
translation group. The compressed disk operator system has propagation
number $2$ at the truncations tested (MEASURED). The lemma
would certify faithfulness at each $\alpha$; it says nothing about the
$\alpha$-derivative, which is what Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} needs.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise heat-trace convergence]
\label{lem:L6_rate}
(a) For each fixed $(\alpha,t)$, $K_n(\alpha,t)\to K(\alpha,t)$ as
$n\to\infty$, with error bounded by a radial discretisation term $O(a^{p})$
and super-polynomially small azimuthal and radial truncation terms. The
radial operator $L(m)=-\partial_\rho^2+(m^2-\tfrac14)/\rho^2$ on $(0,R]$ has,
for every $m=(j+\tfrac12)/\alpha>0$, eigenfunctions $\sim\rho^{|m|+1/2}$
vanishing at the apex, so $\rho=0$ needs no boundary condition and the only
boundary datum is the Dirichlet wall at $\rho=R$.
(b) For each fixed $j$, $g_j^{(n)}$ and $g_j^{(n)\prime}$ converge to $g_j$
and $g_j'$ uniformly on $[1-\delta,1+\delta]$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\emph{Status.} Part (a) is finite-difference convergence for a
regular Sturm--Liouville problem: the spinor has no $m = 0$ mode, so the
apex is centrifugally screened. Part (b) follows from the convergence of
the discrete eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $L(m)$, locally uniformly in
$m$, together with the Hellmann--Feynman formula
$\partial_m\lambda_k=2m\langle\rho^{-2}\rangle_k$. We argue it here; we do
not prove it in detail. The exponent is measured (B1 below): $p=1$ on the
production grid $\rho_i=ia$, and $p=2$ on a boundary-corrected grid.
(Part (a) is the bounded-interval norm-resolvent statement recorded in the
archived Paper~47.)

\begin{lemma}[The replica weight is harmless --- new]
\label{lem:L6_harmless}
There is a constant $c(\delta)=t/((1+\delta)^2 R^2)>0$ and, for each $(t,\delta)$,
a summable Gaussian-dominating sequence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L6_dominating}
G_j \;=\; C(t,\delta)\,(j+\tfrac12)^2\,e^{-c(\delta)(j+\frac12)^2},
\qquad \sum_{j\ge0}G_j<\infty,
\end{equation}
such that $\big|g_j^{(n)}(\alpha)\big|\le G_j$ and
$\big|g_j^{(n)\prime}(\alpha)\big|\le G_j$ for all $\alpha\in[1-\delta,1+\delta]$
and all $n$ large. Hence $\sum_j g_j^{(n)}$ and $\sum_j g_j^{(n)\prime}$ converge
uniformly on $[1-\delta,1+\delta]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The centrifugal floor obeys $\lambda_0(m)\ge m^2/R^2$ (continuum Bessel-zero
bound $j_{m,1}\ge m$; on the lattice the floor is at least as steep,
$\lambda_0(m)\ge c_a\,m^2/R^2$ with $c_a\to1^+$ as $a\to0$, confirmed
numerically below at $c_a\approx1.16$). The excited gaps
$\lambda_k(m)-\lambda_0(m)$ grow like $k^2\pi^2/R^2$ uniformly in $m$ (the high
modes see the box, not the centrifugal barrier), so the relative partition
function $Z(t)=\sum_k e^{-t(\lambda_k-\lambda_0)}$ is bounded uniformly in $m$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
h(m,t)\le Z(t)\,e^{-t m^2/R^2},\qquad
|h'(m,t)|\le t\,C'\,(m/R^2)\,Z(t)\,e^{-t m^2/R^2},
\end{equation}
the second from $|\partial_m\lambda_k|=2m\langle\rho^{-2}\rangle_k\le C'm/R^2$
(Hellmann--Feynman; the screened modes concentrate near $\rho\sim R$ so
$\langle\rho^{-2}\rangle=O(R^{-2})$). For $\alpha\in[1-\delta,1+\delta]$ and
$m_j=(j+\tfrac12)/\alpha$ one has $m_j\ge(j+\tfrac12)/(1+\delta)$, hence
$e^{-t m_j^2/R^2}\le e^{-c(\delta)(j+\frac12)^2}$, while
$|g_j^{(n)\prime}|=4|h'(m_j)|(j+\tfrac12)/\alpha^2$ carries a polynomial factor
$\le (j+\tfrac12)^2/(1-\delta)^2$. Both $|g_j^{(n)}|$ and $|g_j^{(n)\prime}|$ are
thus bounded by~\eqref{eq:L6_dominating}, which is summable because a Gaussian
beats any polynomial. The Weierstrass $M$-test gives uniform convergence of both
series.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:L6}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:L6_rate}(b) each term $g_j^{(n)\prime}$ converges to
$g_j'$ uniformly on $[1-\delta,1+\delta]$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:L6_harmless} every term is bounded by $G_j$, uniformly in
$n$ and $\alpha$, with $\sum_j G_j<\infty$. Dominated convergence for series
(Tannery's theorem) then gives $\sum_j g_j^{(n)\prime}\to\sum_j g_j'$
uniformly in $\alpha$, and likewise for $\sum_j g_j^{(n)}$. Since each
$K_n(\cdot,t)$ is $C^1$, the uniform limit of derivatives is the derivative
of the limit: $K(\cdot,t)\in C^1([1-\delta,1+\delta])$, $K_n\to K$ in $C^1$,
and evaluating at $\alpha=1$ exchanges $\lim_n$ with $d/d\alpha$. For the
rate, split the series at $J$: the terms $j<J$ converge at the per-mode rate,
and the tail $\sum_{j\ge J}G_j$ is super-polynomially small in $J$, so the
weight $(j+\tfrac12)$ enters only through a tail that is negligible at any
polynomial rate. The tip $\mathrm{tip}_n=dK_n/d\alpha|_1-K_n(1,t)$ inherits
the convergence.
\end{proof}

\noindent The dominating exponent $c(\delta)\to t/R^2$ as $\delta\to0$ is
the envelope slope measured below.

\paragraph{Numerical verification.} Unless stated, $R=10$ and $t=1$; a
lean tridiagonal solver is cross-checked bit-exact against the production
class \texttt{DiscreteDiskDiracSpectral} of
\texttt{geovac/gravity/warped\_dirac.py}.
\emph{(i) Gaussian envelope.} $C_j\sim\exp(\text{slope}\cdot(j+\tfrac12)^2)$,
measured slope $-0.01129$ vs predicted $-t/R^2=-0.01000$; the replica weight is
crushed, $C_j/C_0$ falling from $4.7$ at $j{=}4$ to $1.6\times10^{-20}$ at
$j{=}64$, and the azimuthal partial sum reaches the total to machine precision
by $K_{\rm az}\approx48$ (tail/total $=4\times10^{-13}$).
\emph{(ii) Uniformity.} The envelope slope across $\alpha\in[0.9,1.1]$ lies in
$[-0.01134,-0.01123]$, so the domination is uniform in $\alpha$. The ratio
$(K_{\rm fine}-K_{\rm coarse})/K_{\rm coarse}$ at $(a,a')=(0.05,0.025)$ and
$t=10$ varies by $1.3\%$ (coefficient of variation; $0.23\%$ at $t=1$) across
$\alpha\in\{0.90,0.95,1.00,1.05,1.10\}$.
\emph{(iii) Derivative consistency.} At a single truncation the per-mode
analytic $dK_n/d\alpha = 41.501652$ and a finite difference of $K_n$,
$41.501681$, agree to $2.9\times10^{-5}$. This checks the derivative
implementation at fixed $n$; it does not test the interchange of
$\lim_n$ with $d/d\alpha$, which is what the theorem proves.

\paragraph{The rate and the target (B1, B2).} \emph{(B1, rate.)} The
discrete lowest eigenvalue $\lambda_0(m;a)$ against the exact continuum value
$(j_{m,1}/R)^2$ (Bessel zero) converges at order $q=1.00$ at every
$m\in\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52\}$, and the tip itself at order
$p=0.97$, so $p=1$ on the production grid. The cause is the placement of
the Dirichlet wall, not the centrifugal term: the grid $\rho_i=ia$ puts the

\section{Spectral action on $\sthree_R$: two-term exactness}
\label{sec:g1}
% =====================================================================

The Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac operator on the unit $\sthree$ has spectrum
\begin{equation}
  |\lambda_n^{CH}| = n + 3/2, \qquad g_n = 2(n+1)(n+2), \qquad n \ge 0,
  \label{eq:CH_spec}
\end{equation}
where $g_n$ counts both signs of the eigenvalue~\cite{camporesi_higuchi1996}.
The spectral action of a spectral triple is
\begin{equation}
  S[D] = \mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2)
       = \sum_n g_n\, f(\lambda_n^2 / \Lambda^2),
\end{equation}
where $f$ is a smooth positive cutoff function and $\Lambda$ is a
mass scale. On $\sthree_R$ (radius $R$),
$\lambda_n = (n + 3/2)/R$, so $\lambda_n^2/\Lambda^2 = (n + 3/2)^2 /
(R\Lambda)^2$, and the action depends only on the dimensionless
combination $u = R \Lambda$. We work with the Gaussian cutoff
$f(x) = e^{-x}$ until Section~\ref{sec:g8}, where we generalize to
arbitrary $f$. For the Gaussian cutoff, $\phi(s) :=
\int_0^\infty f(x) x^{s-1} dx = \Gamma(s)$, so $\phi(k) = (k-1)!$ for
integer $k$ and $\phi(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$, $\phi(3/2) = \sqrt{\pi}/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral zeta identity at non-positive integers ---
SYMBOLIC PROOF; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_zeta\_unit\_neg\_k.py}]
\label{thm:zeta_unit_neg_k}
For the Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum on unit $\sthree$, the spectral
zeta function
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_{\rm unit}(s) := \sum_{n \geq 0} g_n (n + 3/2)^{-2s}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\zeta_{\rm unit}(-k) = 0$ for every integer $k \geq 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\zeta_{\rm unit}(s) = 2 \sum_{n \geq 0} (n+1)(n+2) (n + 3/2)^{-2s}$,
which equals $2 \sum_{n \geq 0} [(n+3/2)^2 - 1/4] (n + 3/2)^{-2s}$.
This separates as $\zeta_{\rm unit}(s) = 2 \zeta_H(2s - 2, 3/2) -
\frac{1}{2} \zeta_H(2s, 3/2)$, where $\zeta_H$ is the Hurwitz zeta.
By standard Hurwitz functional equations, $\zeta_H(-m, a) =
-B_{m+1}(a)/(m+1)$ for non-negative integer $m$. At $s = -k$:
\begin{align*}
\zeta_{\rm unit}(-k) &= -\frac{2 B_{2k+3}(3/2)}{2k+3}
  + \frac{B_{2k+1}(3/2)}{2(2k+1)}.
\end{align*}
From the shift formula $B_n(x+1) = B_n(x) + nx^{n-1}$ and the
identity $B_{2k+1}(1/2) = 0$ (standard for all $k \geq 0$), one
obtains $B_{2k+1}(3/2) = (2k{+}1)/4^k$. Substituting:
the first term gives $-2(2k{+}3)/[4^{k+1}(2k{+}3)] = -1/(2 \cdot 4^k)$
and the second gives $(2k{+}1)/[4^k \cdot 2(2k{+}1)] = 1/(2 \cdot 4^k)$.
Their sum is zero at every $k \geq 0$. \qed
\end{proof}

The identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:zeta_unit_neg_k} forces the
Mellin-transform expansion of $\mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2)$ on
$\sthree_R$ to truncate at low order.

\begin{corollary}[Two-term exactness of the spectral action ---
SYMBOLIC PROOF; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_zeta\_unit\_neg\_k.py}]
\label{cor:two_term}
For Gaussian cutoff on $\sthree_R$, the spectral action equals
\begin{equation}
  S(R, \Lambda) = \phi(3/2) (\Lambda R)^3
                - \frac{1}{4} \phi(1/2) (\Lambda R)
                + O(e^{-\Lambda^2 R^2}).
  \label{eq:two_term_action}
\end{equation}
On the round $\sthree_R$ there are no further power-law terms at any
order.
\end{corollary}

The corresponding extremum equation is
$\frac{d}{du}[\phi(3/2) u^3 - \tfrac{1}{4} \phi(1/2) u] = 0$, giving
$3 \phi(3/2) u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4} \phi(1/2)$, or
$u_{\rm crit}^2 = \phi(1/2)/(12 \phi(3/2))$. For Gaussian cutoff this
gives $u_{\rm crit}^2 = (\sqrt{\pi})/(12 \cdot \sqrt{\pi}/2) = 1/6$,
so $\Rcrit \Lambda = 1/\sqrt{6}$. This extremum belongs to the two-term
form; the exact mode sum $\mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2)$ is monotonic in
$R$.

\begin{remark}[Relation to Chamseddine--Connes]
\label{rem:cc_uncanny}
Chamseddine and Connes~\cite{chamseddine_connes2008_uncanny} showed, by
Poisson summation over the Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum, that the spectral
action on $\sthree$ and on $\sthree \times S^1$ is given by two terms up to
rapidly decaying corrections, so that all higher Seeley--DeWitt
coefficients vanish, and checked $a_4$ and $a_6$ by a local heat-kernel
computation. The Bernoulli argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:zeta_unit_neg_k} is
an alternative algebraic proof of the same all-order vanishing: the
identity $B_{2k+1}(3/2) = (2k{+}1)/4^k$ forces exact pairwise cancellation
in the Hurwitz decomposition at every non-positive integer. Under the
standard volume normalization the two Dirac coefficients are
$a_0^{D^2} = 4\pi^2$ and $a_1^{D^2} = -2\pi^2$; with the scalar series
$a_k^{\Delta} = 2\pi^2/k!$ of Section~\ref{sec:g3}, all lie in
$\bigoplus_k \pi^{2k}\cdot\mathbb{Q}$, consistent with the period
statements of Fathizadeh and Marcolli for Robertson--Walker
spectral actions~\cite{fathizadeh_marcolli2016} (Paper~32~\cite{paper32},
\S~VIII).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Odd spheres]
\label{rem:two_term_uniqueness}
The identity $\zeta_{\rm unit}(-k) = 0$ also holds for the
Camporesi--Higuchi spectra of $S^5$ and $S^7$, checked symbolically
through $k = 7$ (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_owed\_guards.py}). The mechanism is the same:
the degeneracy polynomials (degree 4 and 6) are even in the shifted
eigenvalue $n + d/2$, and the Bernoulli multi-step formula
$B_{2m+1}(p + 1/2) = (2m{+}1)\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}(l+1/2)^{2m}$ produces the
cancellation. On $S^{2m+1}$ the spectral action then has exactly $m+1$
power-law terms, $\Lambda^{2m+1}, \Lambda^{2m-1}, \dots, \Lambda$; on
$\sthree$ these are the volume and scalar-curvature terms. On a round
sphere every local curvature invariant is a power of the constant scalar
curvature, so the term count does not by itself say which invariants
(curvature squared, Weyl squared, Gauss--Bonnet) a general background would
carry; Section~\ref{sec:g2} states what can be said.
\end{remark}

\section{The product $\sthree \times S^{1}_{\beta}$ and its $\beta \to \infty$ limit}
\label{sec:g2}
% =====================================================================

The Euclidean thermal product geometry $\sthree \times S^{1}_{\beta}$
has Dirac operator $D = D_{\sthree} \otimes \sigma_1 + I \otimes
\sigma_2\,(-i \partial_\tau)$ with $\tau \in [0, \beta)$, acting on
the rank-4 bundle $\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$ (two-component
$\sthree$ spinors tensored with $\mathbb{C}^2$; odd-dimensional
$\sthree$ has no chirality operator, so a rank-2 product is not a
Dirac operator). Then $D^2 = D_{\sthree}^2 \otimes I - \partial_\tau^2$
and the heat kernel factorizes with a factor $2$:
\begin{equation}
  K_{\sthree \times S^1}(t) = 2\,K_{\sthree}(t) \cdot K_{S^1}(t)
                            = 2\,K_{\sthree}(t) \cdot \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[4D spectral action with exact two-term structure ---
INTERNAL THEOREM]
\label{cor:zeta_4D_neg_k}
The Chamseddine--Connes spectral action on $\sthree_R \times S^1_\beta$
for Gaussian cutoff equals
\begin{equation}
  S(R, \beta, \Lambda) = \frac{\beta R^3}{2} \Lambda^4
                       - \frac{\beta R}{4} \Lambda^2
                       + O(\text{exp small}).
  \label{eq:4D_action}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{What ``no higher-curvature terms'' means here.} The Weyl tensor
of $\sthree \times S^1$ vanishes, as does the Gauss--Bonnet density
there~\cite{chamseddine_connes2008_uncanny}; the background is conformally
flat. In general the fourth Seeley--DeWitt coefficient of the spectral
action contains the square of the Weyl curvature and a topological
term~\cite{chamseddine_connes2008_uncanny}. So
Corollary~\ref{cor:zeta_4D_neg_k} says that on this background the action
is the Einstein--Hilbert term plus a cosmological term, with nothing else.
It does not say that the spectral action is pure Einstein gravity on other
backgrounds. In particular the Euclidean black-hole cigar of
Section~\ref{sec:g4} is not conformally flat, and there the Weyl-squared
term is present (\S\ref{sec:g4}, Wald paragraph).

\paragraph{The $\beta \to \infty$ limit.} Sending $S^1_\beta \to
\mathbb{R}_\tau$ replaces $K_{S^1}$ by the density $K_{\mathbb{R}}(t) =
1/(2\sqrt{\pi t})$, and the action density per unit Euclidean time is
\begin{equation}
  s(R, \Lambda) = \frac{R^3}{2} \Lambda^4 - \frac{R}{4} \Lambda^2,
\end{equation}
on $\sthree \times \mathbb{R}$, the Euclidean Einstein static universe. Its
two-term form has the same formal extremum $\Rcrit\Lambda = 1/\sqrt{6}$ as
on $\sthree$, where it takes the value
\begin{equation}
  s_{\min} = -\frac{\Lambda}{6 \sqrt{6}}.
  \label{thm:dS_vacuum}
\end{equation}
(SYMBOLIC; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_dS\_vacuum.py}, which computes the
density from the spectrum on the rank-4 bundle.) This is an observation
about the two-term density, not a vacuum-selection result: the exact mode
sum is monotonic in $R$.

\section{The truncation is specific to the spinor bundle}
\label{sec:g3}
% =====================================================================

The two-term exactness of Sections~\ref{sec:g1} and~\ref{sec:g2}
is specific to the Camporesi--Higuchi spinor bundle on
$\sthree$. We exhibit the heat traces of three sectors: the spinor sector
(Dirac), the scalar Laplacian, and transverse-traceless (TT) symmetric
tensors.

\paragraph{Dirac sector (spinor bundle).}
\begin{equation}
  K_{D^2}(t) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} t^{-3/2} - \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4} t^{-1/2}
             + O(e^{-\pi^2/t}),
\end{equation}
where the exponentially-small correction comes from Jacobi $\vartheta_2$
modular transformation on the half-integer-shifted Camporesi--Higuchi
spectrum.

\paragraph{Scalar sector.} The bosonic Laplacian on unit $\sthree$
has spectrum $\lambda_n^{\Delta} = n(n+2)$ with multiplicity $(n+1)^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form Seeley--DeWitt coefficients for scalar
                Laplacian on unit $\sthree$ --- INTERNAL THEOREM
                (classical $\theta_3$/Poisson asymptotics; algebra
                symbolic, closed form matched at 50~dps);
                \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_scalar\_ak.py}]
\label{thm:scalar_ak}
The heat trace satisfies
\begin{equation}
  K_{\Delta}(t) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\, \frac{e^t}{t^{3/2}}
                + O(e^{-\pi^2/t})
              = \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k^{\Delta}\, t^{k - 3/2} \cdot (4\pi)^{-3/2},
\end{equation}
with $a_k^{\Delta} = 2\pi^2/k!$ for all $k \geq 0$.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the Jacobi $\vartheta_3$ modular transformation on
the integer-spectrum bosonic sum, exploiting the structural
difference between half-integer (Dirac) and integer (Laplacian)
spectra.

\paragraph{TT-tensor sector.} The rough Laplacian $\nabla^*\nabla$ on
transverse-traceless symmetric 2-tensors on unit $\sthree$ has
spectrum $(n+1)(n+3) - 2$, $n \ge 1$ (the Lichnerowicz operator is
$\nabla^*\nabla + 6$ on this sector). Writing the spectrum as $m^2 - 3$
($m \ge 3$) with multiplicity $2(m^2-4)$, the heat trace is
\begin{align}
  K_{TT}(t) &= e^{3t}\!\left[\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2} t^{-3/2}
                              - 4\sqrt{\pi} t^{-1/2} + 4\right]
               + 6 e^{2t} + O(e^{-\pi^2/t}),
\end{align}
which is pure integer $\vartheta_3$ content: the full-lattice sum is a
$\vartheta_3$ series, and the $+4$ and $6e^{2t}$ terms are the excluded
$m = 1, 2$ modes. (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_tt\_heat\_trace.py}.)

\begin{remark}[Structural distinction]
The two-term exactness holds only for the Dirac sector (half-integer
spectrum, $\vartheta_2$ content). The scalar Laplacian and the TT sector
(integer spectra, $\vartheta_3$ content) have infinite Seeley--DeWitt
series, $a_k^{\Delta} = 2\pi^2/k!$ for the scalar. The two-term
Einstein--Hilbert plus cosmological form of Sections~\ref{sec:g1}
and~\ref{sec:g2} therefore belongs to the spinor sector; a scalar or tensor
field on the same background carries the full tower.
\end{remark}

\section{The Newton constant by two routes}
\label{sec:g4}

\subsection{Action route}
\label{sec:g7}

Matching the spectral action~\eqref{eq:4D_action} to Einstein--Hilbert
plus a cosmological constant,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm EH} = -\frac{1}{16 \pi G_{\rm eff}} \int R_{\rm scalar} \sqrt{g}
              + \frac{\Lcc}{8 \pi G_{\rm eff}} \int \sqrt{g},
\end{equation}
with the volume forms on $\sthree_R \times S^1_\beta$ giving
$\mathrm{Vol} = 2 \pi^2 R^3 \beta$ and
$\int R_{\rm scalar} \sqrt{g} = 12 \pi^2 R \beta$, the coefficient
matching gives
\begin{align}
  \Lambda^2 \text{ term}: &\quad \Geff = 3 \pi / \Lambda^2, \\
  \Lambda^4 \text{ term}: &\quad \Lcc = 6 \Lambda^2.
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Newton constant and cosmological constant ---
INTERNAL THEOREM]
\label{prop:newton_cc}
The spectral action on $\sthree_R \times S^1_\beta$, traced over the rank-4
bundle with Gaussian cutoff, produces Newton constant
$\Geff = 3 \pi / \Lambda^2$ (Planck-scale) and cosmological constant
$\Lcc = 6 \Lambda^2$. (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_cutoff\_mellin.py},
which derives both from the spectral sum.)
\end{proposition}

Setting $\Lambda = M_{\rm Planck}$ gives $\Lcc / M_{\rm Planck}^2 = 6$,
against an observed value of order $10^{-120}$. This is the standard
cosmological-constant problem of the Chamseddine--Connes
programme~\cite{chamseddine_connes2010}; the present construction neither
adds to it nor resolves it.

\subsection{Entropy route}
\label{subsec:entropy_route}

\paragraph{Sign conventions.} We use one set throughout, that of
Solodukhin's review~\cite{solodukhin2011_review}. The Euclidean effective
action is $W(\alpha) = -\ln Z(\alpha)$ on the cone of apex angle
$2\pi\alpha$, and the replica entropy is
\begin{equation}
  S = \Big(\alpha\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha} - 1\Big)
      W(\alpha)\Big|_{\alpha = 1}.
  \label{eq:replica_formula}
\end{equation}
For a Dirac field $Z = \det D$, so
$W = -\tfrac12\ln\det D^2 = +\tfrac12\int (ds/s)\,\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-sD^2}$
(regulated): the heat trace enters with a positive weight, the opposite of
the bosonic sign~\cite{kabat1995, solodukhin2011_review}. The spectral
action $\mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2) = \int_0^\infty g(s)\,
K(s/\Lambda^2)\, ds$, with $f$ the Laplace transform of $g \ge 0$, enters
the same way, and we take $W = I_E := \mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2)$.
With this sign the negative $\Lambda^2 R$ term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:4D_action} gives $G > 0$, as above; Solodukhin notes that the
negative sign of the Dirac curvature coefficient and the fermionic sign of
the effective action together give a positive contribution to $1/G$.

\paragraph{The cigar.} The Euclidean Schwarzschild cigar is
\begin{equation}
  ds^2 = d\rho^2 + \rho^2 d\phi^2 + r(\rho)^2 d\Omega_2^2,
\end{equation}
with $\phi$-period $2\pi$ for a smooth tip and $2\pi\alpha$ off-shell, and
horizon sphere of radius $r(0) = r_h$. Near the tip, at constant warp
$r = r_h$, the squared Dirac operator is
$D_{C_\alpha}^2 \otimes I + I \otimes D_{S^2}^2/r_h^2$ on a rank-4 bundle
(two-component cone spinors tensored with two-component $S^2$ spinors), and
the heat trace factorizes. The $S^2$ Dirac spectrum is
$|\lambda_n| = (n+1)/r_h$ with multiplicity $4(n+1)$, $n \ge 0$, so
\begin{equation}
  K_{S^2}(t) = \sum_{n \geq 0} 4(n+1) e^{-(n+1)^2 t/r_h^2}
             = \frac{2 r_h^2}{t} - \frac13 + O(t).
  \label{eq:KS2}
\end{equation}
This is an infinite Seeley--DeWitt series (integer spectrum,
$\vartheta_3$ content), with first curvature coefficient
$a_1^{S^2} = -4\pi/3$ in the $(4\pi t)^{-1}$ normalization. The cone tip
contributes $c_{1/2}(\alpha)\,K_{S^2}(t)$ to the heat trace,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:spinor_sc_tip}.

\paragraph{The entropy.} Integrating the tip term against the cutoff
(Proposition~\ref{thm:sector_mellin} below) gives
\begin{equation}
  I_E^{\rm tip}(\alpha)
    = c_{1/2}(\alpha)\left[2 r_h^2 \Lambda^2\,\phi(1) - \tfrac13 f(0)\right]
    = -\frac{r_h^2 \Lambda^2\,\phi(1)}{6}\!\left(\frac{1}{\alpha} - \alpha\right)
      + O(\Lambda^0).
\end{equation}
The tip term vanishes at $\alpha = 1$ and the bulk terms are linear in
$\alpha$, so only $\partial_\alpha I_E^{\rm tip}$ survives in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:replica_formula}, and with $c_{1/2}'(1) = +1/6$,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\BH} = \frac{r_h^2 \Lambda^2\,\phi(1)}{3} - \frac{f(0)}{18}
          = \frac{A \Lambda^2\,\phi(1)}{12 \pi} + O(\Lambda^0),
  \label{eq:S_BH}
\end{equation}
with horizon area $A = 4 \pi r_h^2$. The leading term is positive. Matching
$S_{\BH} = A/(4G)$ gives
\begin{equation}
  G = \frac{3 \pi}{\phi(1)\,\Lambda^2},
\end{equation}
the action-route value of Proposition~\ref{prop:newton_cc} and
Proposition~\ref{thm:cutoff_dep}. (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_replica\_sbh.py}
computes both routes from their spectra at the Gaussian cutoff and checks
that they agree.) The two inputs to the agreement are the rank-4 trace of
Section~\ref{sec:g2} and the total-trace cone coefficient
$\tfrac{1}{12}|1/\alpha - \alpha|$ for the two-component spinor; a trace
over a rank-2 bundle, or a per-component reading of the cone coefficient,
would each introduce a factor of $2$.

\paragraph{Why the routes agree, and where.} For a minimally coupled field,
the one-loop entropy and the one-loop renormalization of $1/G$ come from
the same heat-kernel coefficient: this is Susskind and Uglum's
observation~\cite{susskind_uglum1994}, and it underlies the induced-gravity
account of black-hole entropy~\cite{frolov_fursaev1997}. Solodukhin's review
states that each minimally coupled field, bosonic or fermionic, contributes
positively and proportionally to both~\cite{solodukhin2011_review}. The
agreement above is that statement for the Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum. Wald's
Noether-charge entropy~\cite{wald1993} gives the same conclusion for a pure
Einstein--Hilbert Lagrangian, but the cigar is not conformally flat, and
there the spectral action carries a Weyl-squared term at order $\Lambda^0$
(\S\ref{sec:g2}). That term changes the Wald entropy at order $\Lambda^0$,
so the identification of the two $G$'s holds at the leading order
$\Lambda^2$, not to all orders. Nonminimally coupled fields (gauge fields,
gravitons) break the proportionality~\cite{kabat1995,
solodukhin2011_review}; the statement here is for the Dirac field only.

\subsection{Cutoff dependence}
\label{sec:g8}

For an arbitrary cutoff $f$, define the Mellin moments
\begin{equation}
  \phi(s) := \int_0^\infty f(x)\, x^{s-1}\, dx .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Cutoff-dependent gravity predictions; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_cutoff\_mellin.py}]
\label{thm:cutoff_dep}
For arbitrary smooth positive cutoff $f$ with finite Mellin moments
$\phi(1), \phi(2)$, the spectral action on $\sthree_R \times
S^1_\beta$ produces
\begin{align}
  \Geff(f, \Lambda) &= \frac{3 \pi}{\phi(1)\, \Lambda^2}, \\
  \Lcc(f, \Lambda) &= \frac{6\, \phi(2)}{\phi(1)}\, \Lambda^2, \\
  \Rcrit\, \Lambda &= \sqrt{\frac{\phi(1)}{6\, \phi(2)}}.
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

No combination of these is independent of the cutoff:
$\Geff \Lambda^2 = 3\pi/\phi(1)$,
$\Lcc / \Lambda^2 = 6\, \phi(2)/\phi(1)$,
$\Geff \Lcc = 18 \pi\, \phi(2)/\phi(1)^2$, and
$\Rcrit \Lambda = \sqrt{\phi(1)/(6\, \phi(2))}$. The construction fixes
which moment each quantity carries; the cutoff function, and hence the
values, are external input, as in the Chamseddine--Connes
programme~\cite{chamseddine_connes1997, chamseddine_connes2010}. In these
terms the cosmological-constant problem is the requirement
$\phi(2)/\phi(1)^2 \approx 10^{-124}$ with $\phi(1) = O(1)$, which no
natural cutoff meets.

\begin{proposition}[Sector-wise Mellin moment map; \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_cutoff\_mellin.py}]
\label{thm:sector_mellin}
Let $f$ be a cutoff function with regulated Mellin moments
$\phi(s) = \int_0^\infty f(x)\, x^{s-1}\, dx$, and let the
Chamseddine--Connes spectral action on the Euclidean Schwarzschild
cigar be decomposed into bulk and topological-tip sectors via the
replica method. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\emph{(i)}] The cosmological-constant sector (proportional to
    $a_0 \sim t^{-d/2}$) scales with $\phi(d/2) = \phi(2)$ in $d=4$.
  \item[\emph{(ii)}] The Einstein--Hilbert sector (proportional to
    $a_2 \sim t^{-(d-2)/2}$) scales with $\phi((d-2)/2) = \phi(1)$ in $d=4$.
  \item[\emph{(iii)}] The topological tip sector (the
    Sommerfeld--Cheeger contribution).  On the bare 2D cone the tip
    term is $t$-independent, so in the spectral action it contributes
    $\tfrac16 f(0)$, independent of the Mellin moments.  On the 4D cigar
    it multiplies the Dirac transverse kernel
    $K_{S^2}(t) = 2r_h^2/t - 1/3 + O(t)$, so its leading contribution to
    $S_{\BH}$ is $(r_h^2\Lambda^2/3)\,\phi(1)$, the Einstein--Hilbert
    moment, and the subleading piece is $-f(0)/18$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the cutoff as a Laplace transform, $f(x) = \int_0^\infty
e^{-sx} g(s)\,ds$, so that $S = \mathrm{Tr}\, f(D^2/\Lambda^2) =
\int_0^\infty g(s)\, K(s/\Lambda^2)\, ds$ with $K(t) = \mathrm{Tr}\,
e^{-tD^2}$.  A heat-trace term $c\,t^{-\beta}$ with $\beta > 0$
contributes $c\,\Lambda^{2\beta} \int_0^\infty g(s) s^{-\beta} ds
= c\,\Lambda^{2\beta}\phi(\beta)/\Gamma(\beta)$, and a $t^0$ term
contributes $c\,f(0)$.  The Seeley--DeWitt expansion
$K(t) \sim \sum_n a_n\, t^{(n-d)/2}$ then gives (i) and (ii) for the
$n=0$ and $n=2$ bulk coefficients.

For the replica tip sector: the Sommerfeld--Cheeger contribution to the
heat trace on a cone of apex angle $2\pi\alpha$ is
$\Delta K^{\rm SC}(\alpha, t) = c(\alpha) \cdot K_{S^{d-2}}(t)$,
where $c(\alpha) = -(1/12)(1/\alpha - \alpha)$ for spinors
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:spinor_sc_tip}) and
$K_{S^{d-2}}(t)$ is the transverse-sphere heat kernel; its replica
derivative is the constant $1/6$.  On the bare 2D cone the tip term is
$t^0$ and contributes $f(0)/6$.  On the 4D cigar,
$\tfrac16 (2r_h^2/t - 1/3)$ contributes
$\tfrac13 r_h^2\Lambda^2\phi(1) - f(0)/18$; the leading term is the
$A\Lambda^2\phi(1)/(12\pi)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:S_BH}. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The proper-time estimator orders the cutoffs differently]
\label{cor:phi0_ratio}
The discrete measurements of Section~\ref{sec:discrete} weighted the
substrate's tip with the proper-time functional
$\int (dt/t)\, f(t\Lambda^2)\,\mathrm{tip}(t)$, which for a constant tip is
a log-regulated $\phi(0)$ and orders the cutoffs sharp $>$ polynomial $>$
Gaussian (the ordering is pinned in
\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_cutoff\_mellin.py}). That is a property of the
estimator, not of the spectral action, in which the bare 2D tip carries
$f(0)$ and the 4D leading tip carries $\phi(1)$. A naive expectation that
the entropy would carry $\phi(2)$ is rejected by the same measurement.
\end{remark}

\section{The second variation on the $(1,1)$ sector}
\label{sec:fierz_pauli}
% =====================================================================

This section asks what the spectral action alone says about
spin-two perturbations of the round $\sthree$ Dirac operator. The answer is
a necessary condition and a limit.

\subsection{Positive kinetic modes}
\label{sec:g6}

The Camporesi--Higuchi eigenspaces carry the $SO(4) = SU(2)_L \times
SU(2)_R$ irreps $((n+1)/2, n/2)$ and $(n/2, (n+1)/2)$, and the products of
an eigenspace with itself contain the irrep $(j_L, j_R) = (1,1)$ (dimension
9) for $n \geq 1$. For a perturbation $\delta D = X$ of the Dirac operator
$D_0$ supported within one eigenspace of eigenvalue $\lambda$ (a
\emph{within-sector} mode), the Gaussian spectral action's second variation
is
\begin{equation}
  A_\lambda = a_\lambda\!\left(\frac{4 \lambda^2}{\Lambda^4} - \frac{2}{\Lambda^2}\right),
  \qquad a_\lambda = e^{-\lambda^2/\Lambda^2}.
\end{equation}
Modes connecting different eigenspaces (\emph{cross-sector} modes) include
the inner-automorphism tangents $V = i[X, D_0]$, whose entries
$(\lambda_b - \lambda_a) X_{ab}$ vanish within a sector; so the inner gauge
acts only across sectors.

\begin{proposition}[Positive within-sector modes --- MEASURED, $n_{\max} \le 3$]
\label{prop:graviton_physical}
$A_\lambda > 0$ exactly when $\lambda^2 > \Lambda^2/2$. At $\Lambda^2 = 6$
the within-sector $(1,1)$ modes at $|\lambda| = 5/2, 7/2, 9/2$ have
$A_\lambda = +0.127, +0.133, +0.066$, and a five-point finite-difference
stencil reproduces the formula. For $\Lambda^2 > 2\lambda_{\min}^2 = 12.5$
the lowest block is negative. Positivity is a necessary condition for
spin-two dynamics, and it holds only on the infrared side
$\Lambda^2 < 2\lambda_{\min}^2$. (\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_j\_blindness.py}
checks the formula against the stencil.)
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom count.} In the continuum the nine components
of $(1,1)$ reduce to two transverse-traceless polarizations once
diffeomorphism gauge (four) and constraints (three) are removed. At finite
truncation this reduction does not occur inside a within-sector block,
because the inner gauge acts only across sectors (zero within-sector gauge
entries, checked at $n_{\max} = 1, 2, 3$). The reduction can only appear in
a limit in which the cross-sector directions assemble into continuum
diffeomorphisms; no such limit is established here.

\subsection{$J$-blindness}

Under the diagonal $SU(2) \subset SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R$,
\begin{equation}
  (1, 1) = J{=}0 \;\oplus\; J{=}1 \;\oplus\; J{=}2
  \label{eq:j_decomp}
\end{equation}
with dimensions $1, 3, 5$. If $(1,1)$ is read as the symmetric traceless
four-tensors, these are the $h_{00}$ component, the $h_{0i}$ components and
the traceless spatial $h_{ij}$; that reading requires a dictionary between
$\delta D$ and $\delta g_{\mu\nu}$, which this paper does not supply.

\begin{proposition}[$J$-blindness of $S^{(2)}$ --- \texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_j\_blindness.py}]
\label{thm:j_blindness}
The Gaussian spectral-action second variation $S^{(2)}$ restricted to
the $(1, 1)$ subspace of $\mathrm{Herm}(H_{n_{\max}})$ has identical
eigenvalue spectra across $J = 0, 1, 2$. For cross-sector modes,
eigenvalue multiplicities scale as $\dim(J) = 1{:}3{:}5$; for
within-sector modes, after Hermitianization, as $1{:}1{:}3$.
The same argument applies to every irrep in a block, so the spectral action
assigns the helicity-two irreps $(2,0)\oplus(0,2)$ the same eigenvalues as
$(1,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The spectral action $S = \mathrm{Tr}(f(D^2/\Lambda^2))$ is invariant
under unitary conjugation $D \mapsto UDU^*$, hence under the
$SO(4)$ symmetry of $D_0$ and in particular under the diagonal
$SU(2)$. The second variation $S^{(2)}$ is therefore equivariant
under diagonal $SU(2)$. By Schur's lemma, $S^{(2)}$ is proportional
to the identity on each irreducible $J$-component within a given
sector pair $(a, b)$, with proportionality constant
\begin{equation}
  w(\lambda_a, \lambda_b) =
  \begin{cases}
    e^{-\lambda_a^2/\Lambda^2}
    (4\lambda_a^2/\Lambda^4 - 2/\Lambda^2)
    & \lambda_a = \lambda_b, \\[4pt]
    \dfrac{(\lambda_a + \lambda_b)^2}{\Lambda^4}\,
    \dfrac{\Lambda^2(a_a - a_b)}{\lambda_b^2 - \lambda_a^2}
    - \dfrac{a_a + a_b}{\Lambda^2}
    & \lambda_a \neq \lambda_b,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:s2_weight}
\end{equation}
where $a_k = e^{-\lambda_k^2/\Lambda^2}$. This weight depends only on
the eigenvalues $\lambda_a, \lambda_b$ of $D_0$, not on the angular
quantum numbers or on $J$.
\end{proof}

The test checks the eigenvalue-set and multiplicity identities at
$n_{\max} = 1, 2$, and the weight~\eqref{eq:s2_weight} against a five-point
stencil at the $10^{-4}$ level at $n_{\max} = 1$ (stencil noise sets that
floor).

\paragraph{Which irrep carries the graviton.} The $(1,1)$ irrep has
$|j_L - j_R| = 0$: it is a helicity-zero sector. The transverse-traceless
tensor harmonics on $\sthree$ lie in $(j{+}2, j) \oplus (j, j{+}2)$, lowest
$(2,0)\oplus(0,2)$, with $|j_L - j_R| = 2$ (their multiplicity
$2(m^2 - 4)$ is that of Section~\ref{sec:g3}). The within-sector blocks do
contain $(2,0)\oplus(0,2)$ (checked at $n = 1, \dots, 4$), and by the
Proposition they carry the same $A_\lambda$ as $(1,1)$, so the positivity
of Proposition~\ref{prop:graviton_physical} extends to them under the same
condition on $\Lambda$. What the Proposition rules out is any separation:
the spectral action alone cannot distinguish the transverse-traceless
modes from the trace or longitudinal ones, nor any $J$ from another. That
separation needs the identification of $\delta D$ with a metric
perturbation (the Chamseddine--Connes dictionary), which breaks $SO(4)$ to
the $SO(3)$ that distinguishes them; whether such an identification exists
on a truncation is open. (The local polarization space is $(1,1)$ while
the global transverse-traceless harmonics are $|\Delta j| = 2$, and the
dictionary may move irrep assignments; that is why the question is open
rather than settled negatively.)

\section{Discrete-substrate measurements}
\label{sec:discrete}
% =====================================================================

The continuum statements of Sections~\ref{sec:g1}--\ref{sec:g8} were
re-measured on a discretized cigar. These are sprint-scale measurements,
and the end-to-end discrete entropy is not converged.

\paragraph{Substrate.} The near-horizon cigar is discretized as the cone
of Section~\ref{sec:cone} tensored with the $S^2$ Dirac spectrum truncated
at $l \le l_{\max}$, with warp $r(\rho) = r_h$ (constant) or
$r_h\sqrt{1 + (\rho/r_h)^2}$ (smooth tip). The continuum Dirac operator on
the warped cigar is
\begin{equation}
  D_{\rm cigar}
  = D_{D^2} \otimes I_{S^2}
  + \gamma^5_{D^2} \otimes \frac{D_{S^2}}{r(\rho)}
  + \frac{r'(\rho)}{r(\rho)}\, \gamma^\rho \otimes I_{S^2}.
  \label{eq:cigar_dirac}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Checks that pass.} (All MEASURED;
\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_L6\_full.py} F1--F7 and
\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_g44\_headlines.py}.)
\begin{itemize}
  \item At constant warp the heat trace factorizes,
        $K_{\rm cigar} = K_{D^2}\cdot K_{S^2}$, to machine precision;
        the variable-warp operator reduces to the constant-warp one at
        constant warp to $\sim 10^{-16}$; the chirality grading
        anticommutes with the disk Dirac operator.
  \item The spinor-to-scalar heat-trace ratio is $1.9998$ (rank two).
  \item At $t = 0.1$, $N_\phi = 192$, $N_\rho = 200$, $a = 0.05$, the
        leading coefficient is $a_0^{\rm Dirac} = 1.992$. Against the bulk
        value $2.0$ this omits the Dirichlet perimeter term; against the
        continuum value of the same estimator with the perimeter term
        ($1.888$ at $R = 10$, $t = 0.1$) the substrate is $5.5\%$ high,
        and its $a \to 0$ sweep has not reached it. The value $1.992$ is a
        crossing of the bulk value, not a recovery.
  \item As $r_h \to 0$ the variable-warp heat trace tends to the
        singular-tip cone ($r(\rho) = \rho$) heat trace, to $6\times10^{-8}$
        relative at $r_h = 10^{-3}$, quadratically in $r_h$; this is by
        construction, since the lattice excludes $\rho = 0$. It is not the
        naive ceiling $4(l_{\max}+1)(l_{\max}+2)\,K_{\rm disk}$, because
        the lattice spacing fixes a finite $S^2$ mass at the apex: the
        substrate has two scales, $a$ and $r_h$.
  \item At smooth-tip warp the factorization fails by a positive amount
        that grows as $r_h$ shrinks.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The tip coefficient and the replica derivative.} The spinor
tip coefficient and its excess-angle behaviour are those of
Section~\ref{sec:cone}; the replica derivative is Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} with
the measurements B1 and B2.

\paragraph{What is not converged.} Integrating the discrete replica
derivative against a cutoff to obtain $S_{\BH}$ on the substrate gives
values that depend strongly on $\Lambda$ and on the $t$-grid; at one cell
($r_h = 2$, $\Lambda = 2$) an 8-point grid gives $0.85$ of the continuum
$r_h^2\Lambda^2/3$ and a 13-point grid gives $1.96$. The cause is that the
substrate's tip profile peaks at a $t$ fixed by $a$ and $r_h$, not by
$\Lambda$; the cure is radial refinement, not a different cutoff. The
end-to-end discrete $S_{\BH}$ is therefore not established, and this paper
claims only the continuum Eq.~\eqref{eq:S_BH} and the convergence of the
replica derivative at fixed $t$.

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Q1: the discrete entropy.} Theorem~\ref{thm:L6} controls the
replica derivative at each fixed $t$. An end-to-end discrete $S_{\BH}$
needs the convergence uniform over the $t$-window of the cutoff integral,
the joint variable-warp and conical Dirac operator in the continuum limit,
and a converged extraction (Section~\ref{sec:discrete}). The propinquity
backbone, Lemma~\ref{lem:L6_backbone}, is open.

\paragraph{Q2: the graviton dictionary.} Proposition~\ref{thm:j_blindness}
shows the spectral action cannot by itself separate the spin-two modes.
Whether a truncation supports an identification of $\delta D$ with
$\delta g_{\mu\nu}$, and whether the $9 \to 2$ reduction appears in a
limit, are open.

\paragraph{Q3: the cutoff.} The cutoff function is external input. No
mechanism examined here selects one, and none produces the
$\phi(2)/\phi(1)^2 \approx 10^{-124}$ that the observed cosmological
constant would need.

\paragraph{Tests.} B1, B2, B4, the continuum tip and $a_0$, the
odd-sphere identity, the rank-4 trace, the total-trace cone coefficient,
the coefficient of variation of the discretization error across $\alpha$
($1.3\%$ at $t=10$, $0.23\%$ at $t=1$) and propagation number $2$ of the
compressed disk operator system are backed by
\texttt{tests/test\_paper51\_owed\_guards.py}.
