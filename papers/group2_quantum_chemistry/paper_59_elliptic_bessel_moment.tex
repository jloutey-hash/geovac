\begin{abstract}
Two-center electron-repulsion integrals over Slater-type orbitals close
in elementary functions plus the exponential integral, the logarithm, and
Euler's~$\gamma$---a genus-zero transcendence class---which is why the
two-center problem has been solved since Roothaan and Ruedenberg.  The
genuine three-center integral has resisted a closed form for seventy years,
and the standard verdict is that it is simply hard: reduce it to an infinite
series or a one-dimensional quadrature and be done.  We give it a structural
reading instead.  Evaluating the two-electron three-center integral
$(XY|XZ)$ over $1s$ orbitals in momentum space---the Fock projection under
which Coulomb--Sturmians become hyperspherical harmonics, the setting native
to the Averys' generalized-Sturmian program---the three centers enter as
three \emph{phases} rather than three foci, so the coordinate obstruction
(prolate spheroidal coordinates admit exactly two foci) dissolves.
\textbf{[MEASURED]} The resulting momentum integral reproduces the exact
integral (agreement $1.9\times10^{-14}$ with McMurchie--Davidson on the same
Gaussian fits; with the true Slater density, $4.3\times10^{-7}$ against the
fitted reference, which itself sits $7\times10^{-8}$ above the Slater value), and
its angular part collapses to a single spherical Bessel kernel.
\textbf{[MEASURED / classical]} What remains---the radial content---is a
two-scale Bessel moment whose period is a \emph{complete elliptic integral}
(verified to 31 digits), living on the genus-one curve
$y^{2}=(c_{1}k^{2}+1)(c_{2}k^{2}+1)$; it degenerates to the genus-zero
two-center case exactly on the diagonal $c_{1}=c_{2}$.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC +
MEASURED]} The one-mass slice is the Laplace transform of the elliptic
differential---an elliptic deformation of the Bessel $K_{0}$, reducing to
$K_{0}(D)$ when one scale decouples ($\rho\to0$) and to the complete elliptic
integral $K$ at zero separation---and satisfies an explicit fourth-order
Picard--Fuchs equation with polynomial coefficients (residual
$\sim\!10^{-24}$), whose characteristic exponents are the four branch points
of the curve.  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} This places a molecular integral inside
the modern theory of Bessel moments and two-mass elliptic Feynman integrals;
a targeted literature search finds that connection unmade in either field.
\textbf{[OPEN]} We do \emph{not} give the finite closed form, and we show the
natural route to it is obstructed: the elliptic-dilogarithm (unequal-mass
sunrise) mechanism does not engage, because the modulus Picard--Fuchs source
is not an elementary subtopology but lies in the integral's own Gauss--Manin
$D$-module---the branch-point boundary that would supply the sunrise's tadpole
vanishes identically.  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The object is therefore an
apparently unclaimed elliptic transcendent one level \emph{above} the
elliptic-dilogarithm tower, a period of a rank-four irregular connection.
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} That connection is \emph{irreducible}---it is the
Fourier--Laplace transform of an irreducible connection, and its monodromy admits
no invariant coordinate subspace---so no closed form arises through factorization;
\textbf{[OPEN]} whether the irreducible object admits a transcendental closed form
we leave open.  The message is not a solution
but a relocation: the third center does not make the radial integral messier
within the two-center class, it raises the transcendence one genus, past the
reach of the sunrise's elliptic-dilogarithm mechanism.
\textbf{[MEASURED]} Two pre-registered extensions (Sec.~\ref{sec:beyond})
probe the rung variable:\ a fourth distinct center adds no genus,
consistent with the class counting the distinct two-center densities a
kernel couples and not the nuclei, while a three-electron chain over two-center
densities with two Coulomb kernels at one electron lives on a K3 surface
whose holomorphic period is a three-scale Bessel moment, a sum of
products of two complete elliptic integrals.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]}
A third extension finds that the holomorphic period of any tree of linked
densities depends only on how many densities it links, and that four linked
densities give the first period not built from elliptic ones:\ its equation
is an irreducible fourth-order operator of Calabi--Yau type, with no
quadratic relation among its solutions, an entry of the published
Calabi--Yau tables;\ for two to five densities the tree ladder is the banana ladder of
Feynman integrals under a Hadamard product with the Legendre family.
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} A fourth finds that a Coulomb ring of three densities does not climb:\ its
operator factors at the fibre into elementary, algebraic and elliptic
pieces, one rung below the three-density chain, and at one center it
carries a dilogarithm where the chain is rational.  With exponential
geminals $e^{-\gamma r}$ on two edges, the correlated triangle of explicitly
correlated methods sits with the chain on the K3 rung at $\gamma=1$;\
\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} the drop is read as the uniqueness of the Coulomb loop, which
factors it into pairwise inverse distances.
\end{abstract}

\section{The two-center class, and the wall beyond it}
%% ========================================================================

The electron-repulsion integral over Slater-type orbitals on two centers is
one of the oldest solved problems in molecular quantum
mechanics~\cite{harris_michels1967}.  In prolate
spheroidal coordinates, with the two nuclei at the foci, the Neumann
expansion of $1/r_{12}$ terminates against the orbital angular momenta, and
every integral reduces to elementary functions together with the exponential
integral $E_{1}$, the logarithm, and Euler's constant~$\gamma$.  In the
transcendence bookkeeping of the Geometric Vacuum program (Paper~18~\cite{loutey_paper18}), these
are \emph{embedding-tier} constants---the cost of the continuum two-center
geometry rather than content of the discrete graph---and, decisively, they
sit at transcendence \emph{weight one}, and no dilogarithm appears, even
though the ordered radial integral has the simplex shape that generically
produces one.  In the direct-Coulomb $(AA|BB)$ class---and there alone---even
the prefactor $\pi$ cancels against the Coulomb $4\pi$, leaving a rational
combination of $\{1,\,e^{-\lambda R}\}$ whose zeros Lindemann--Weierstrass
separates;\ that is what lets \emph{that block} be \emph{decided} rather than
merely counted (Paper~58~\cite{loutey_paper58}).  It does not extend to the
three genuinely cross classes, which carry $E_1$, $\ln$ and $\gamma$ seeds and
remain counted---weight one is not the same as decidable.

The genuine three-center integral has no such story.  Prolate spheroidal
coordinates are built from exactly two foci; a third nucleus has nowhere to
sit.  Gaussians escape this because two Gaussians on different centers
multiply into a single Gaussian on a third point~\cite{boys1950}---Slater
orbitals have no product theorem---and this, not any Geometric-Vacuum-specific
limitation, is the seventy-year-old reason Gaussian bases displaced Slater
orbitals for polyatomic quantum chemistry.  The modern state of the art for three-center
Slater repulsion integrals (Ref.~\cite{ozdogan2012}) reduces them to a single
infinite expansion or a one-dimensional numerical quadrature; the one
celebrated closed form in correlated three-electron theory, Fromm and Hill's
analytic evaluation~\cite{fromm_hill1987}, is a \emph{dilogarithm}---a
genus-zero object---and belongs to a different integral topology (the
one-center, explicitly correlated master integral), which lacks the structure
we are about to exhibit.

This paper asks what the three-center integral \emph{is}, transcendentally,
and answers: it is an elliptic object.  The two-center weight-one closure is
a genus-zero fact and it stops at two centers.  The third center raises the
transcendence one genus, to the elliptic (genus-one) world of Bessel moments
and two-mass sunrise Feynman integrals.  We establish this constructively,
validate every step numerically, place the object in the literature, and mark
the finite closed form as open.

\emph{Lineage and scope.}  The momentum-space route is not new machinery.
Molecular integrals over exponential-type orbitals have long been evaluated by
Fourier transformation, molecular orbitals in momentum space going back to
Shibuya and Wulfman~\cite{shibuya1965}:\ the transform of a two-center orbital
product is classical~\cite{trivedi_steinborn1983}, its reduction to
four-dimensional (Fock)
hyperspherical harmonics goes back to Niukkanen~\cite{niukkanen1984}, and the
general one- and two-electron multicenter integrals close, via the
Fourier-transform method together with Feynman's identity, to low-dimensional
numerical quadrature~\cite{grotendorst_steinborn1988}.  Momentum-space
Coulomb--Sturmians are hyperspherical harmonics under Fock's conformal
projection~\cite{fock1935}, the framework the Averys built into the
generalized-Sturmian method~\cite{avery1989,avery2006,avery_avery2015} and
applied to Coulomb--Sturmian repulsion
integrals~\cite{avery_avery_4center}.  The closest prior art to the present
calculation is Avery's momentum-space route~\cite{avery2013}---the same
Fock-projection method on the same objects, and the one place the elliptic
structure was nearest to visible; a targeted search finds it, like all prior
work, carrying the evaluation to expansion and numerics without drawing the
genus reading, though this is a search-negative inferred from the published
abstracts, not a verified absence.  What is new here is the reading: that the residual radial object is a
specific elliptic Bessel moment, and hence that a molecular integral is an
instance of the elliptic-Feynman-integral class.  We work throughout with $1s$ orbitals at a
common exponent; the generalization to higher $(n,\ell,m)$ multiplies the
integrand by solid harmonics and shifts exponents but does not change the
genus, and we return to this in Sec.~\ref{sec:scope}.

\section{The momentum-space reduction}
\label{sec:momentum}
%% ========================================================================

Write the chemist integral for two densities
$\rho_{1}=\chi_{X}\chi_{Y}$ and $\rho_{2}=\chi_{X}\chi_{Z}$, two two-center
$1s$ densities that share the center $X$ on different axes.  With the Fourier
representation of the kernel,
$1/|\bm r_{1}-\bm r_{2}| = (2\pi^{2})^{-1}\!\int d^{3}k\,
e^{i\bm k\cdot(\bm r_{1}-\bm r_{2})}/k^{2}$, the integral becomes
%
\begin{equation}
  (XY|XZ) = \frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int \frac{d^{3}k}{k^{2}}\,
  \tilde\rho_{1}(\bm k)\,\overline{\tilde\rho_{2}(\bm k)},
  \qquad
  \tilde\rho(\bm k)=\!\int\! \rho(\bm r)\,e^{i\bm k\cdot\bm r}\,d^{3}r .
  \label{eq:momentum}
\end{equation}
%
Each center now appears only through a phase $e^{i\bm k\cdot\bm R}$: three
centers are three phases, not three foci, and there is nothing to
diagonalize.  Equation~\eqref{eq:momentum} is exact; the only content is the
density Fourier transform $\tilde\rho$ and the $\bm k$ integral.

We verify Eq.~\eqref{eq:momentum} two independent ways on the reference
geometry $X=(0,0,0)$, $Y=(0,0,2)$, $Z=(1.5,0,0.5)$ (bohr), $\zeta=1$, for
which a McMurchie--Davidson evaluation over $8$-Gaussian $1s$ fits gives the
reference value $(XY|XZ)=\gvq{three_center_eri_truth}{0.204941722}$.  That
value carries the fit error:\ as the number of Gaussians grows ($6$ to $16$)
the evaluation converges to the Slater value
$\gvq{three_center_eri_slater}{0.2049416525}$, $7\times10^{-8}$ lower, which an
exact expansion in angular channels about the shared center reproduces to
$3\times10^{-10}$ (\texttt{tests/test\_pairwise\_three\_center.py}).  \textbf{[MEASURED]} Using the closed-form
Fourier transform of the Gaussian-fitted density (a product of Gaussians is a
Gaussian) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:momentum} reproduces the McMurchie--Davidson value
on the \emph{same} fits to $1.9\times10^{-14}$: the momentum formula, the
Fourier and Coulomb conventions, and the $\bm k$-quadrature are correct.
Independently, computing the \emph{true} Slater density transform (no Gaussian
fit) by a one-parameter reduction of the two-center Yukawa product reproduces
that reference to $4.3\times10^{-7}$.  Both are pinned in the regression
suite~\cite{test_routeC}.

\emph{The angular integral closes to a spherical Bessel kernel.}  Writing
each Slater factor through $e^{-\zeta r}=-\partial_{\zeta}(e^{-\zeta r}/r)$
and the Feynman parametrization of the resulting Yukawa product, the three
phases combine into a \emph{single} phase $e^{i\bm k\cdot\bm W}$ with
$\bm W(s,t)=(t-s)\bm X+s\bm Y-t\bm Z$ ($s,t$ the two Feynman parameters), so
the angular integral is elementary,
%
\begin{equation}
  \int d\Omega_{\bm k}\, e^{i\bm k\cdot\bm W}
  = 4\pi\, j_{0}(k|\bm W|)
  = 4\pi\,\frac{\sin(k|\bm W|)}{k|\bm W|}.
  \label{eq:angular}
\end{equation}
%
The whole integral reduces to a two-dimensional Feynman integral over a
one-dimensional radial ($k$) integral,
%
\begin{align}
  (XY|XZ) = \frac{8}{\pi}\,
  &\partial_{\zeta_{a}}\partial_{\zeta_{b}}
   \partial_{\zeta_{c}}\partial_{\zeta_{d}}
   \!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!ds\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!dt\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!dk\;
   j_{0}(k|\bm W|)\notag\\[-2pt]
  &\times\frac{e^{-D_{1}\Delta_{1}}}{\Delta_{1}}\,
          \frac{e^{-D_{2}\Delta_{2}}}{\Delta_{2}}
  \;\Bigg|_{\zeta=1},
  \label{eq:reduced}
\end{align}
%
with $\Delta_{1}=\sqrt{c_{1}k^{2}+1}$, $\Delta_{2}=\sqrt{c_{2}k^{2}+1}$,
$c_{1}=s(1-s)$, $c_{2}=t(1-t)$, and $D_{1}=|X-Y|$, $D_{2}=|X-Z|$.  The
$\partial_{\zeta}$'s (a bookkeeping device restoring the Slater from the
Yukawa) and the $(s,t)$ integrations are elementary operations; the
transcendental content is entirely in the radial factor.
Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced} reproduces the $8$-Gaussian reference to
$1.8\times10^{-6}$ \textbf{[MEASURED]}, the residual being the finite-order
angular and Feynman quadrature.

\section{The transcendence jump: genus zero to genus one}
\label{sec:genus}
%% ========================================================================

The radial factor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:reduced} carries two dispersion factors
$\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}$ with \emph{different} scales $c_{1}\neq c_{2}$.  A
single such factor is benign.  Under Fock's substitution
$k\sqrt{c}=m\sinh\theta$ it linearizes, and \textbf{[MEASURED, $6\times
10^{-18}$]}
%
\begin{equation}
  \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\cos(kb)\,
  \frac{e^{-D\sqrt{c\,k^{2}+m^{2}}}}{\sqrt{c\,k^{2}+m^{2}}}\,dk
  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\,
    K_{0}\!\Big(\tfrac{m}{\sqrt{c}}\sqrt{c\,D^{2}+b^{2}}\Big),
  \label{eq:K0}
\end{equation}
%
a Bessel $K_{0}$: the momentum-space Coulomb--Sturmian object, living on a
rational (genus-zero) curve.  This is why the two-center engine is weight one.

Two different scales cannot be linearized together.  Their product is
governed by the algebraic curve
%
\begin{equation}
  y^{2} = (c_{1}k^{2}+1)(c_{2}k^{2}+1),
  \label{eq:curve}
\end{equation}
%
a quartic with four distinct branch points $k=\pm i/\sqrt{c_{1}},\,
\pm i/\sqrt{c_{2}}$ whenever $c_{1}\neq c_{2}$: an \emph{elliptic curve},
genus one.  When $c_{1}=c_{2}$ the branch points collide and the quartic
becomes a perfect square---rational, genus zero.  The cleanest witness is the
period.  At $D_{1}=D_{2}=0$ the radial integral is exactly a complete
elliptic integral \textbf{[MEASURED, 31 digits; classical]},
%
\begin{equation}
  \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{dk}{\sqrt{(c_{1}k^{2}+1)(c_{2}k^{2}+1)}}
  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{\max}}}\,K(m),
  \label{eq:period}
\end{equation}
%
with modulus $m=1-c_{\min}/c_{\max}$
(nondegenerate, $0<m<1$;\ prefactor verified for both orderings of
$c_{1},c_{2}$ in \texttt{test\_paper59\_euclidean\_dictionary.py}), while on the diagonal $c_{1}=c_{2}=c$ it collapses
to the elementary $\pi/(2\sqrt{c})$.  Since $c_{1}=s(1-s)$ and $c_{2}=t(1-t)$
range independently over $[0,\tfrac14]$, the full integral~\eqref{eq:reduced}
is an integral over a \emph{family} of elliptic curves, of modulus
$m(s,t)=1-c_{\min}/c_{\max}$, degenerate only on the measure-zero locus
$s=t$ or $s=1-t$.  Every term generated by the $\partial_{\zeta}$'s is a
meromorphic function on the same curve~\eqref{eq:curve}, so the whole integral
is a period of this elliptic family.

The structural statement is that the embedding tier of the transcendence
taxonomy (Papers~18,~34~\cite{loutey_paper18,loutey_paper34}) is \emph{genus-graded}: genus zero
($\{E_{1},\ln,\gamma\}$) at two centers, genus one (elliptic) at three.
The two two-center negatives---no $\pi$, no dilogarithm---are genus-zero
properties; the third center does not climb to a higher rung of the
polylogarithm weight tower but leaves that tower for the elliptic one.  This
is the transcendence-level form of ``no coordinate system for three foci,''
and it also explains, after the fact, why an attempt to identify the three-center
value against dilogarithm constants was structurally doomed: the constants are
in the wrong class.

\section{The reduction: an elliptic Bessel moment}
\label{sec:reduction}
%% ========================================================================

The radial factor can be named precisely.  Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:K0} to each
factor and Parseval's theorem, the radial core (at fixed Feynman parameters,
before the elementary $j_{0}$ and $\partial_{\zeta}$ wrapping) is a two-mass
Bessel moment \textbf{[MEASURED, 30 digits]},
%
\begin{equation}
  M = \frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{c_{1}c_{2}}}
  \int_{0}^{\infty}\!\! db\;
  K_{0}\!\big(a_{1}\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+b^{2}}\big)\,
  K_{0}\!\big(a_{2}\sqrt{p_{2}^{2}+b^{2}}\big),
  \label{eq:besselmoment}
\end{equation}
%
with $a_{i}=1/\sqrt{c_{i}}$ and $p_{i}=D_{i}\sqrt{c_{i}}$.  This is a
shifted-argument, two-scale member of the Bessel-moment family; it is not one
of the single-scale moments $\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}K_{0}^{n}(t)\,dt$
catalogued in closed form by Bailey, Borwein, Broadhurst and
Glasser~\cite{bbbg2008}.

A change of variable $x=\sqrt{c_{1}k^{2}+1}$ on the one-mass slice
$N(D)\equiv M(D,0)$ puts it in the cleanest canonical form: the Laplace
transform of the holomorphic elliptic differential,
%
\begin{equation}
  N(D) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{1}}}
  \int_{1}^{\infty}\!
  \frac{e^{-Dx}\,dx}{\sqrt{(x^{2}-1)(\rho\,x^{2}+1-\rho)}},
  \qquad \rho=\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}.
  \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
%
This is a single explicit one-dimensional integral of an elementary
integrand.  A low-dimensional reduction is the expected output of the
Fourier-transform method---the same machinery reduces multicenter Slater
integrals to two- or three-dimensional quadrature~\cite{grotendorst_steinborn1988}
and three-center integrals to a semi-infinite oscillatory Bessel
integral~\cite{safouhi2001}---so what is new is not the reduction itself but the
\emph{elliptic reading} of the integrand it produces, which makes the genus-one
structure manifest.
It is an \emph{elliptic deformation of the Bessel} $K_{0}$: as $\rho\to0$
(the one-mass limit, one scale decoupling) $N\to K_{0}(D)$, and at $D=0$ it is the complete elliptic
integral $K$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}.

Because $1/\sqrt{Q}$, $Q(x)=(x^{2}-1)(\rho x^{2}+1-\rho)$, satisfies the
first-order relation $Q f' + \tfrac12 Q' f = 0$, and the boundary at the
branch point $x=1$ vanishes ($Q(1)^{1/2}=0$), integration by parts against
$e^{-Dx}$ (using $\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{n}e^{-Dx}f\,dx=(-1)^{n}L^{(n)}(D)$,
$L\equiv\sqrt{c_{1}}\,N$) yields an exact fourth-order Picard--Fuchs equation
with polynomial coefficients \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF; MEASURED residual
$\sim\!10^{-24}$]},
%
\begin{equation}
  D\rho\,L'''' + 2\rho\,L''' + D(1{-}2\rho)L'' + (1{-}2\rho)L'
  - D(1{-}\rho)L = 0.
  \label{eq:pf}
\end{equation}
%
Its characteristic polynomial factors as
$\rho(s^{2}-1)\big(s^{2}+(1-\rho)/\rho\big)$, whose roots $s=\pm1$ and
$s=\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}$ are exactly the four branch points of the
curve~\eqref{eq:curve}: the $K_{0}/I_{0}$ sector from the $(x^{2}-1)$ pair,
the oscillatory $J_{0}/Y_{0}$ sector from the other.  (A differential-equation
method for molecular two-electron integrals is itself established:\
Pachucki~\cite{pachucki2009} evaluates the \emph{two}-center integral from a
second-order equation in the internuclear distance, closing it in elementary
functions;\ the genus jump appears here as the step from that second-order,
genus-zero equation to the fourth-order Picard--Fuchs equation of an elliptic
curve.)  A subordination
identity casts the same object as a two-scale sunrise integral,
$\propto\int\!\!\int ds_{1}ds_{2}\,(s_{1}s_{2})^{-1/2}
e^{-D_{1}^{2}/4s_{1}-s_{1}-D_{2}^{2}/4s_{2}-s_{2}}/\sqrt{s_{1}c_{1}+s_{2}c_{2}}$,
whose second-Symanzik-type $\sqrt{\text{linear form}}$ is the genus-one
signature and separates only when $c_{1}=c_{2}$.

One numerical fact pins the object's class beyond the period.  $N(D)$ is
\emph{non-analytic in $D$ at $D=0$}: it carries a $D\ln D$ term
\textbf{[MEASURED]}, so it is not a pure elliptic integral ($K$/$E$ are
analytic in their parameters).  The elliptic $D=0$ period together with the
$D\ln D$ nonanalyticity are the two fingerprints of an
\emph{elliptic ${\oplus}$ logarithmic} object---the inhomogeneous
(``regulator'') term of the Picard--Fuchs solution associated with the elliptic
dilogarithm~\cite{blochvanhove2015,adamsbognerweinzierl2014,broedel_sunrise2018}---a
correspondence whose reach we test, and bound, in
Sec.~\ref{sec:obstruction}.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} \emph{The kinematics.} Equation~\eqref{eq:K0} is the frequency
representation of the two-dimensional Euclidean propagator: with
$G_{a}(\tau,x)=(2\pi)^{-1}K_{0}(a\sqrt{\tau^{2}+x^{2}})$ the Green's function of
$-\Delta+a^{2}$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$, its left-hand side is exactly
$(2\pi/\sqrt{c})\,G_{a}(p,b)$ with mass $a=\zeta/\sqrt{c}$, Euclidean time $p=D\sqrt{c}$ and
space $b$, and the fibre~\eqref{eq:besselmoment} is exactly the two-propagator correlator
$M=4\pi a_{1}a_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}db\,G_{a_{1}}(p_{1},b)\,G_{a_{2}}(p_{2},b)$: two Euclidean
packets of unequal mass, evolved from a point source for Euclidean times $p_{1},p_{2}$ and
overlapped on one spatial slice \textbf{[MEASURED, $10^{-30}$ and $10^{-31}$ at five parameter
points each;\ \texttt{test\_paper59\_euclidean\_dictionary.py}]}. Three things become visible.
First, the Feynman parameters are
K\"all\'en--Lehmann spectral variables: $c=s(1-s)$ forces $a=\zeta/\sqrt{c}\ge2\zeta$, so each
two-centre density is dispersed over its own two-particle cut and the elliptic modulus
$\rho=c_{2}/c_{1}=(a_{1}/a_{2})^{2}$ is the squared ratio of the two spectral masses---the
genus jump is a mass-splitting phenomenon, and the three cusps are equal masses ($\rho=1$)
and one mass at the heavy end of its cut ($\rho\to0,\infty$). Second, the exponential twist
is $a_{i}p_{i}=\zeta D_{i}$, \emph{independent of the Feynman parameter}: the family is swept
at fixed mass$\times$time, and the physical point is one Compton wavelength of Euclidean-time
displacement, the $u=D$ endpoint of the source-time superposition
$e^{-D\Delta}\Delta^{-n}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D}^{\infty}(u-D)^{n-1}e^{-u\Delta}\,du$ that
converts the Yukawa into the Slater. Third, $j_{0}(k|\bm W|)$ is a uniform smearing of the
shared spatial coordinate over the composite separation, so the third centre enters
\emph{only} through space while the bond lengths set the two Euclidean-time offsets
\textbf{[MEASURED, $1.8\times10^{-10}$ for the full position-space fibre]}.

\section{The elliptic-dilogarithm route is obstructed}
\label{sec:obstruction}
%% ========================================================================

The unequal-mass sunrise is closed by variation of parameters: a second-order
Picard--Fuchs operator in the external variable annihilates the two periods,
its inhomogeneity is an elementary subtopology (a tadpole), and the particular
solution---an iterated integral of the periods against that elementary
source---is the elliptic dilogarithm~\cite{adamsbognerweinzierl2014,
blochvanhove2015}.  (The full unequal-mass sunrise has since been closed to all
orders in $\varepsilon$ as iterated integrals on $\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,3}$
by Bogner, M\"uller-Stach and Weinzierl~\cite{bognermsw2019}---a \emph{Fuchsian}
object; the point below is that $N(D)$ is its irregular Laplace dual and so lies
outside that technology.)  Running the same machinery on $N(D)$ is the natural
attempt.  It does not close, and the reason is structural.

The curve $y^{2}=Q(x,\rho)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:curve} moves with the modulus
$\rho$, not with the Laplace variable $D$.  Accordingly the two periods
$K(\rho),K(1-\rho)$ are the homogeneous solutions of the second-order modulus
operator \textbf{[MEASURED: both annihilated to $10^{-25}$;
$L(0,\rho)=K(1-\rho)$ to $19$ digits]}
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}_{\rho}
  = \rho(1-\rho)\,\partial_{\rho}^{2} + (1-2\rho)\,\partial_{\rho}
    - \tfrac14 ,
  \label{eq:modpf}
\end{equation}
%
and they are \emph{not} solutions of the fourth-order operator in
$D$: a constant gives $\mathcal{O}[1]=-D(1-\rho)\neq0$, and the single
Bessel functions of both sectors, $K_{0}(D),I_{0}(D)$ and
$J_{0}(\omega D),Y_{0}(\omega D)$ with $\omega=\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}$, are
likewise not $D$-solutions \textbf{[MEASURED]} (they are coupled, which is why
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf} does not factor into elementary Bessel operators).  The
elliptic-dilogarithm construction lives on the periods, so it must be run in
the modulus $\rho$.

There it meets the obstruction.  Acting with $\mathcal{M}_{\rho}$ under the
integral sign is an exact total derivative \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF; exact
residual $0$]},
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}_{\rho}\big[Q^{-1/2}\big]
  = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\,g(x)\,Q^{-1/2}\right],
  \quad
  g(x)=\frac{-x(x^{2}-1)}{4(\rho x^{2}+1-\rho)},
  \quad g(1)=0,
  \label{eq:exactform}
\end{equation}
%
so for $D\neq0$ the modulus source is
$S(D,\rho)\equiv\mathcal{M}_{\rho}[L]=D\!\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-Dx}g\,Q^{-1/2}dx$.
Because $g$ \emph{vanishes at the branch point} $x=1$, differentiating in the
modulus produces no branch-point boundary term: the analogue of the sunrise's
pinched propagator---the elementary tadpole that would be the
inhomogeneity---is absent, and the source stays on the elliptic curve.
Quantitatively \textbf{[MEASURED]}, $S$ closes exactly and
finitely only inside the integral's own Gauss--Manin $D$-module,
%
\begin{equation}
  S(D,\rho)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}P_{k}(D,\rho)\,\partial_{D}^{k}L,
  \qquad \deg_{D}P_{k}=2,
  \label{eq:inmodule}
\end{equation}
%
with held-out residual saturating flat under increase of the fit degree
(below $10^{-25}$ in the regression witness, $5\times10^{-44}$ at driver
precision; numerically an exact finite identity); whereas the genuine
variation-of-parameters shape---period level $\{L,\partial_{D}L\}$ plus an
elementary Bessel subtopology $\{K_{0},K_{1}\}(D)$, $\{J_{0},Y_{0}\}(\omega
D)$---only \emph{approximates} it (residual $2\times10^{-14}$ falling
geometrically to $3\times10^{-30}$ across fit degrees, never saturating).  The
inhomogeneity is in-module, not a simpler subtopology, and variation of
parameters against the periods is therefore circular.

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} $N(D)$ is thus one transcendence level above the
sunrise---above the \emph{fully solved} sunrise, not merely its leading elliptic
dilogarithm:\ the all-orders unequal-mass sunrise is now closed as iterated
integrals on $\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,3}$~\cite{bognermsw2019}, a period of a
\emph{Fuchsian} connection, whereas $N(D)$ is not.  It is not an elliptic
dilogarithm of elementary or period-level arguments; it is a period of the
rank-four irregular connection defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf}, for which the
sunrise mechanism supplies no reduction to the elliptic-polylogarithm
tower~\cite{bonisch2021}.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} The irregularity is explicit at
the operator level:\ at $D=\infty$ the operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf} admits four
formal exponential solutions $e^{\lambda D}$ with
$\lambda\in\{\pm1,\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}\}$---its leading Newton edge is a
full-width slope-one segment, so it is irregular of Poincar\'e rank one, the
four $\lambda$ being exactly the branch points (the $K_{0}/I_{0}$ and
$J_{0}/Y_{0}$ Bessel sectors), whereas the sunrise/Legendre period operator is
regular singular at infinity ($\lambda=0$ only).  The two operators therefore
sit on opposite sides of the Laplace transform, and the Fuchsian
elliptic-polylogarithm machinery of~\cite{bognermsw2019} cannot reach $N(D)$.
Whether $N(D)$ admits a closed form of any kind is a differential-Galois
question---whether the fourth-order operator factors over the differential
field generated by the periods $K(\rho),K(1-\rho)$---settled below, negatively, at the
reducibility level (the operator does not factor);\ what remains open is only whether a
\emph{transcendental} closed form exists at the irreducible level.

\textbf{[MEASURED]} The same irregularity is visible in $N(D)$ itself, as a
\emph{resurgent} structure.  Expanded about the Bessel point $\rho=0$
($N\to K_{0}(D)$), the one-mass slice has the explicit elementary term series
%
\begin{equation}
  N(D) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{1}}}\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^{n}
  \frac{\big((2n-1)!!\big)^{2}}{2^{n}\,n!}\,\rho^{n}\,\frac{K_{n}(D)}{D^{n}},
\end{equation}
%
each coefficient a rational multiple of an \emph{integer-order} Bessel
function---the transcendence does not escalate---but the series is
\emph{asymptotic}, diverging factorially (optimal truncation at
$n\!\sim\!1$--$3$, a few digits, then unbounded).  Equivalently the
$D\to\infty$ expansion $N(D)\sim e^{-D}\sum_{k}b_{k}D^{-(k+1/2)}$ is
Gevrey-one, and its Borel transform is the algebraic function
$\psi(\zeta)=[(\zeta+2)(\rho(1+\zeta)^{2}+1-\rho)]^{-1/2}$ on the very curve
\eqref{eq:curve}, singular exactly at the three non-dominant branch points
$\zeta\in\{-2,\,-1\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}\}$---the Stokes actions linking the
$e^{-D}$ sector to the $e^{+D}$ and $e^{\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}\,D}$ ones.  A
Borel--Pad\'e resummation confirms these locations (at $\rho=\tfrac15$:\
$\zeta=-2$ and $-1\pm2i$, recovered to $\sim\!1\%$).  So the residual
transcendence of $N(D)$ is not exotic:\ it is the resurgence of an elementary
Bessel series, its Stokes data pinned to the branch points of the elliptic
curve.

\textbf{[MEASURED]} The resurgence, moreover, \emph{closes with algebraic Stokes
data}.  The Borel transform $\psi(\zeta)$ carries only square-root singularities,
so its amplitude at each is algebraic:\ $a_{*}=1$ at $\zeta=-2$, and
$a_{*}^{2}=-\tfrac12\mp\tfrac{i}{2}\sqrt{\rho/(1-\rho)}$ at
$\zeta=-1\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}$ (matched to the closed-form residue to
$10^{-37}$, and confirmed to be the large-order growth constants by singularity
analysis; the dominant Stokes ray switches from the real to the complex pair at
$\rho=\tfrac14$).  These amplitudes \emph{are} the Stokes constants connecting the
four exponential sectors, so the entire trans-series of $N(D)$ over
$\{e^{\pm D},\,e^{\pm i\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}\,D}\}$ has \emph{algebraic} connection
data.  The one genuinely elliptic ingredient enters only at the boundary:\ at $D=0$
the open Laplace thimbles close into the cycles of the curve and
$\sqrt{c_{1}}\,N(0)=K(1-\rho)$ exactly ($14$ digits).  So the closed form at the
irregular level is explicit:\ $N(D)$ is the Borel--Laplace transform of the algebraic
differential $\psi$, with algebraic Stokes data and an elliptic-period
$K(1-\rho)$ normalization---as far as a genuinely irregular, genus-one object
reduces.  What remains transcendental is the elliptic period at the boundary and the
standard Borel--Laplace resummation, not any new constant.

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} Structurally this is the genus-one analogue of the classical
resurgence of the modified Bessel function $K_{0}$---the archetypal rank-one
irregular equation, whose large-argument series has an algebraic Borel transform and
elementary Stokes data~\cite{dingle1973,costin2008}.  The natural home for the
algebraic-Stokes-versus-transcendental-period dichotomy seen here is Sabbah's theory
of Stokes structures for Fourier--Laplace transforms of algebraic
connections~\cite{sabbah_stokes2013}, in which the Stokes data is local at the
irregular point (hence algebraic) while transcendental periods enter through the
regular points (hence $K(1-\rho)$ at $D=0$).  The closest worked instance is the
rank-two, genus-\emph{zero} Bessel/Kloosterman connection treated via irregular Hodge
theory~\cite{fresansabbahyu2023}; and the only resurgence analysis of the
sunrise/banana family we found operates in the modular parameter $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$
(the $|q|\to1$ limit), not the Laplace-dual $D$~\cite{broadhurst_dorigoni2026}.  The
present genus-one, Laplace-dual reading appears unmade;\ an absence from a targeted
search, not a proof.

\emph{A concrete route from the solved dual.}  Because $N(D)$ is the Laplace
transform of the sunrise elliptic differential and the unequal-mass sunrise is
now known in full closed form~\cite{bognermsw2019}, one concrete---if not
obviously tractable---route to $N(D)$ is to Laplace-transform that solution
directly, an operation that necessarily leaves the elliptic-polylogarithm class
for the Bessel-moment/resurgence one, consistent with the irregular character
just established.  We have not carried this out.

\emph{The rank-four label is not vacuous: a base-field reducibility scan.}
That $N(D)$ is a period of a genuinely rank-four object---rather than one
secretly carrying lower-rank content---is itself a reducibility statement, and
it can be tested directly.  Two structural facts constrain
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf}.  First, the operator is \emph{formally self-adjoint},
$L^{*}=L$ \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]}, so its differential Galois group lies in
the symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{C})$---the signature of a
polarized period.  Second, its indicial exponents at the regular singular point
$D=0$ are $\{0,1,1,2\}$ \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]}; the \emph{double} exponent at
$1$ is the local-monodromy source of the $D\ln D$ nonanalyticity noted above.
A search over the base field $\mathbb{Q}(\rho)(D)$ then returns \emph{no} factor
of order one or two---hence, by self-adjointness, none of order three---with
polar locus at the true singularities $\{0,\infty\}$
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC, positive-control validated]}: there is no hyperexponential
solution $e^{\lambda D}Q(D)$ for any $\lambda\in\{\pm1,\pm i\omega,0\}$, and the
only two order-two factorizations the symbol $(s^{2}-1)(s^{2}+\omega^{2})$
admits over $\mathbb{Q}(\rho)$---the two Bessel sectors---both fail.  The two
natural elementary structures are excluded by the exponents alone: $L$ is
neither the symmetric product of its Bessel sectors (which would carry exponents
$\{0,0,0,0\}$) nor a symmetric cube.  This is strong evidence
that Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf} is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}(\rho)(D)$, and hence that
the rank-four label is earned.  The eigenring sharpens it to a single residual
target.  The endomorphism algebra of the solution space is trivial,
$E(L)=\mathbb{C}$ \textbf{[computed at $\rho=1/5$; robust across pole windows]}, so
$L$ is indecomposable; and the symplectic form pairs the exponential solution
$y_{\lambda}$ at $D=\infty$ with $y_{-\lambda}$.  A non-degenerate invariant
subspace would split $V=W\oplus W^{\perp}$ and enlarge the eigenring, so every
invariant subspace is \emph{isotropic}; the exponential torus $T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}$
confines invariant subspaces to spans of the four lines
$\{y_{\pm1},y_{\pm i\omega}\}$, whose two symplectic pairs (the Bessel sectors)
are non-degenerate.  A one-dimensional isotropic invariant subspace is a
hyperexponential solution, already excluded.  Hence $L$ is
reducible over $\mathbb{C}(D)$ \emph{iff} one of the four ``mixed'' Lagrangian
planes $\mathrm{span}\{y_{\pm1},y_{\pm i\omega}\}$ is invariant---equivalently,
iff the Stokes matrices at $D=\infty$~\cite{sabbah_stokes2013} fail to couple the real
exponential pair $\{\pm1\}$ to the imaginary pair $\{\pm i\omega\}$.  This is
decidable, and it is settled twice over---once in principle, once explicitly.

\emph{In principle.}  The reduction is an instance of the Fourier--Laplace
transform of $D$-modules, where irreducibility is automatic.  Let $\mathcal{L}$ be
the rank-one meromorphic connection on $\mathbb{P}^{1}$ whose flat sections are
$Q^{-1/2}$: it is regular singular, with local monodromy $-1$ at each of the four
branch points $x\in\{\pm1,\pm i\omega\}$ and trivial monodromy at $\infty$ (there
$Q^{-1/2}\sim x^{-2}$).  Having rank one, $\mathcal{L}$ is irreducible, so its
minimal (intermediate) extension $j_{!*}\mathcal{L}$ to $\mathbb{A}^{1}$ is a
\emph{simple} holonomic $D$-module.  (Because every finite local monodromy is
$-1\neq1$ and hence has no invariant, $j_!\mathcal{L}=j_{!*}\mathcal{L}=j_*\mathcal{L}$,
so ``the $D$-module generated by $Q^{-1/2}$'' is unambiguous.)  The Laplace kernel
$e^{-Dx}$ makes the radial integral the Fourier--Laplace transform
$\mathrm{FL}(j_{!*}\mathcal{L})$, and $\mathrm{FL}$ is an exact auto-equivalence of
the category of holonomic $D$-modules on the affine line (with its dual
coordinate)~\cite{katz1990}; an equivalence sends simple objects to simple
objects, so $\mathrm{FL}(j_{!*}\mathcal{L})$ is simple.  Its generic rank
is the total drop of $\mathcal{L}$---one unit at each branch point, where the
monodromy $-1$ leaves no invariant, and none at the regular point $\infty$, hence
\emph{four}, matching the order of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf}, of which $N(D)$ is the
defining (cyclic) solution.  A simple holonomic $D$-module of generic rank four
restricts to an irreducible connection over the function field, so \textbf{the
operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pf} is irreducible} over $\mathbb{C}(D)$, hence over
$\mathbb{Q}(\rho)(D)$.  Simplicity is a property of the connection at the generic
point, so it forbids a factor of \emph{any} order---apparent singularities and
all; the monodromy computation next verifies it by hand.

\emph{Explicitly.}  The same conclusion is exhibited, and the mechanism made
visible, by the monodromy.  The four exponentials are the branch points
$x\in\{\pm1,\pm i\omega\}$ of $y^{2}=Q$ in the Borel (Laplace-dual) plane, and the
monodromy of $L$ around $D=0$, computed in the exponential-line basis of
Lefschetz-thimble solutions $\int e^{-Dx}\,dx/\sqrt{Q}$, is \textbf{[MEASURED;
exact-integer certificate]} the integer unipotent matrix
%
\begin{equation*}
  M_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&2&2\\-2&3&2&2\\-2&2&3&2\\2&-2&-2&-1\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
%
the \emph{same} for $\rho=\tfrac15$ and $\rho=\tfrac13$ (a topological invariant
of the branch-point configuration), with a single $2\times2$ Jordan block---the
lone $D\ln D$ log---and \emph{no} invariant coordinate subspace.  The Stokes
matrices therefore \emph{do} couple the real and imaginary exponential pairs, no
mixed plane survives, and the reducibility criterion above returns
\emph{irreducible}---confirming the structural argument concretely.
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} Consequently $N(D)$ is a period of an \emph{irreducible}
rank-four connection: it admits no closed form through any factorization of $L$,
and the sole remaining route to a closed form is a genuinely transcendental one at
the full rank-four level---the elliptic-polylogarithm / $\Gamma(2)$
multiple-modular-value structure developed in the companion (Paper~61~\cite{loutey_paper61}).  The differential-%
Galois \emph{reducibility} question is thereby settled (negatively: $L$ is
irreducible); the transcendental closed form is the one problem that remains
open~\cite{test_routeC}.

\section{Placement in the literature}
\label{sec:literature}

The object of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:besselmoment}--\eqref{eq:pf} sits inside a
fully developed body of mathematics, applied so far only to problems far from
chemistry.  Two-mass elliptic Feynman integrals---the sunrise/banana
family---are evaluated in terms of elliptic dilogarithms as inhomogeneous
solutions of exactly this kind of Picard--Fuchs equation
\cite{blochvanhove2015,adamsbognerweinzierl2014,blochkerrvanhove2015}; their
periods are the complete elliptic integrals of Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}, and at
special (complex-multiplication) scale ratios their values reach critical
$L$-values of modular
forms~\cite{broadhurst2016,fresansabbahyu2023,zhou_wick2018,zhou_wronskian2018}.  The
closest published analog to Eq.~\eqref{eq:besselmoment} is a doubled-argument
Bessel moment that Broadhurst evaluated as a product of complete elliptic
integrals via a lattice-Green-function contour~\cite{broadhurst2008}: the
same ``two-scale moment $\to$ product of $K$'s'' mechanism we expect at
rational $\rho$.

On the chemistry side the treatment is uniformly numerical: three-center
Slater repulsion integrals are reduced to infinite series or one-dimensional
quadrature~\cite{ozdogan2012,barnett_coulson1951}, and the Averys'
momentum-space Coulomb--Sturmian route~\cite{avery_avery_4center,avery2013}, closest in
spirit to ours, likewise carries the evaluation to expansion and numerics with no
genus reading our search could find.  We could find no
paper drawing the bridge in either direction: no chemistry reference names an
electron-repulsion integral as a Bessel moment or an elliptic Feynman
integral, and no amplitudes reference applies the elliptic machinery to a
molecular integral.  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The identification---that the
three-center Slater integral, via the Fock projection, is a two-scale Bessel
moment on the sunrise elliptic curve---appears to be unmade.  This is an
absence from a targeted search, not a proof of absence; the residual risk is
an obscure single source.  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} A second placement datum is
arithmetic:\ the $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ content this family carries (the theta
series $\theta_{3}^{2}$ of the companion (Paper~61);\ $G=\beta(2)=
L(2,\chi_{-4})$) does not appear in the established
Bessel-moment corpus, whose forms sit at levels $3, 6, 8,
15$~\cite{broadhurst2016,fresansabbahyu2023,zhou_wick2018} and, in the twisted
moments of Lim, Tu and Yu~\cite{lim_tu_yu2026}, which are the moments
$c_{n,0}$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:four_density}, $24$ and $48$:\ a targeted search
finds no Catalan-constant appearance in any of these, and $\chi_{-4}$ enters
$c_{3,0}$ as a twist:\ the form attached to it is the newform of level $12$
twisted by $\chi_{-4}$, whose complex multiplication stays by
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, not $\mathbb{Q}(i)$~\cite{lmfdb};\ the nearest other $\chi_{-4}$
appearance is the topological-string sector of the Broadhurst--Dorigoni
programme~\cite{broadhurst_dorigoni2026}.  The family thus occupies an
apparently unclaimed arithmetic slot ($\mathbb{Z}[i]$, complex multiplication by $\mathbb{Q}(i)$) in that literature, of a
piece with the unmade bridge above.  (Reconciliation with the value-level searches of

$D\to0$ shadow;\ the physical integrated value itself shows no measured conductor-$4$

The modular and complex-multiplication structure of this elliptic family, its

Paper~61~\cite{loutey_paper61}, which takes the present reduction as its

\section{Explicitly-correlated integrals are native to this representation}
\label{sec:f12}
%% ========================================================================

The momentum reduction of Sec.~\ref{sec:momentum} is \emph{kernel-agnostic}.  A
two-electron matrix element of \emph{any} operator $f(r_{12})$ between orbital products
is, by the convolution theorem,
%
\begin{equation}
\langle \phi_a\phi_b\,|\,f(r_{12})\,|\,\phi_c\phi_d\rangle
=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\!\int\! d^3k\;
\widetilde{\rho}_{ac}(\bm k)^{*}\,\widetilde f(\bm k)\,\widetilde{\rho}_{bd}(\bm k),
\qquad \rho_{ac}=\phi_a\phi_c,
\label{eq:kernelswap}
\end{equation}
%
the identical object as the electron-repulsion integral with the Coulomb kernel
$\widetilde{(1/r_{12})}=4\pi/k^{2}$ replaced by $\widetilde f$, and the angular closure to
the spherical Bessel kernel of Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular} applies unchanged.
Explicitly-correlated (F12) methods take $f$ to be a correlation factor; the standard
choice is the Slater geminal $f=e^{-\gamma r_{12}}$, whose transform
$\widetilde f(k)=8\pi\gamma/(k^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{2}$ is exact, elementary, and---unlike the
Coulomb kernel---\emph{regular} at $k=0$.  In this representation the geminal is therefore
a native object: no expansion of $e^{-\gamma r_{12}}$ in Gaussians (the first workaround of
Gaussian-basis F12) is required.  \textbf{[MEASURED]} The momentum
form~\eqref{eq:kernelswap} reproduces a direct position-space quadrature to $10^{-14}$, and
the Coulomb self-repulsion of a $1s$ density to its exact value $5a/8$
(\texttt{test\_f12\_kernel\_swap\_and\_native\_geminal}).

Whether the integral closes in elementary functions is governed \emph{not} by the operator
but by the \emph{density topology}.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} When both transition densities are
single-center the radial integrand is rational and the pair integral is genus zero: e.g.\
$\int_{0}^{\infty}[k^{2}/(k^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{2}]\,j_{0}(kR)\,dk=(\pi/4\gamma)\,e^{-\gamma R}$
(\texttt{test\_f12\_geminal\_pair\_is\_elementary}).  A \emph{two}-center transition density,
however, contributes a Fock dispersion factor $\sqrt{c\,k^{2}+1}$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reduction}), and two of them build the very curve of Eq.~\eqref{eq:curve},
$y^{2}=(c_{1}k^{2}+1)(c_{2}k^{2}+1)$, whose period is a complete elliptic integral
(\texttt{test\_f12\_multicenter\_shares\_eri\_elliptic\_genus}).  A rational kernel---Coulomb
$4\pi/k^{2}$ or geminal $8\pi\gamma/(k^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{2}$ alike---is a rational function on
this curve, so $\widetilde f\,dk/y$ is again an elliptic integral: the genus is
\emph{unchanged}, the geminal merely trading an elliptic integral of the first kind (the
Coulomb period) for one of the third kind (its poles at $k^{2}=-\gamma^{2}$).  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} On every
instance tested, the transcendence class is set by the number of two-center transition densities on distinct
center pairs---zero or one gives genus zero, two gives genus one, while two densities on the
same pair (the two-center exchange class) build the same fibre family yet close in the
two-center seed set (Paper~18~\cite{loutey_paper18}), so the fibre genus alone does not
decide the class---exactly as for the repulsion integral.

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The consequence is a \emph{unification}, not an escape.  Explicitly-correlated integrals are
native to the Fock momentum representation and cost the same as the corresponding repulsion
integrals, because here the difficulty of a two-electron integral lives entirely in the
densities, never in the operator between them.  In particular the elliptic obstruction of
this paper---the genuine three-center, two-two-center-density integral---gates
explicitly-correlated \emph{evaluation} as well: the corresponding closed form (the
kernel changes the kind of period, not the curve) that would evaluate the
three-center repulsion integral would evaluate the multi-center F12 correction.  What
correlation buys is not a cheaper integral but a far more compact basis---a single
$r_{12}$ factor recovers the electron--electron cusp that orbital products approach only as
$\ell\to\infty$---so the route trades many repulsion integrals for few correlated ones drawn
from the same transcendence classes.  We do not here treat the many-electron
(resolution-of-identity) machinery of a full F12 method, nor a resource comparison with tuned
Gaussian F12; the statement is about the two-electron integrals, whose momentum-space
transcendence we have mapped.

\subsection{Four linked densities:\ the first rung beyond elliptic}
\label{sec:four_density}

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} The holomorphic period of a tree of linked
densities depends only on how many densities the kernels link, not on the
pattern of the links;\ a Coulomb ring of three behaves differently
(Sec.~\ref{sec:density_ring}).  Take $n$ two-center $1s$ densities joined into a tree by $n-1$
rational kernels, and write the dispersion root of density $v$ at the
fingerprint fibre as $\Delta_v=\sqrt{c_vL_v^2+1}$, with $L_v$ the signed
sum of the momenta on the kernels at $v$.  Fourier-transforming each
$1/\Delta_v$ to $K_0(|y|/\sqrt{c_v})/(\pi\sqrt{c_v})$ turns every momentum
integration into a delta function that identifies the two ends of its
kernel, and a tree is connected, so
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} d^{n-1}k\,\prod_{v=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\Delta_v(L_v(k))}
  = \frac{2^{n}}{\pi\sqrt{c_1\cdots c_n}}
    \int_0^\infty \prod_{v=1}^{n} K_0(a_v x)\,dx,
  \qquad a_v = c_v^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:tree}
\end{equation}
for a star and a chain alike.  At $n=3$ this is the $\bar V_3$ reduction
of Sec.~\ref{sec:three_electron};\ at $n=2$ it is the elliptic period of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}.  A three-dimensional evaluation of four-density
stars and chains, with the faster-decaying roots $\Delta_v^{-3}$ to which
the same argument applies, matches the Bessel-moment side to between
$4\times10^{-9}$ and $2\times10^{-7}$, the grid's own convergence.  That
check tests the identity's bookkeeping;\ the real three-dimensional
integral reduces to moments of the same family with other weights in $x$,
the step assumed below.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} To read the rung, vary one scale:\
$F_n(t)=\int_0^\infty K_0(x)^{n-1}K_0(tx)\,dx$.  The Mellin transform of
$K_0^{\,n-1}$ obeys the recurrence of the $(n-1)$-th symmetric power of the
Bessel operator, and that of $K_0(tx)$ is $2^{s-2}\Gamma(s/2)^2$, so $F_n$
satisfies an explicit linear equation in $t$ of order $n$;\
\textbf{[MEASURED]} each one is checked against direct quadrature of $F_n$
to $10^{-28}$.  For $n=2$ it is the Legendre equation of $K$.  For $n=3$
it is the equation of ${}_3F_2(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12;1,1;t^2/4)$,
Clausen's square of an elliptic period, as Bailey's formula requires.
For $n=4$, in the variable $x=1/(4t^2)$, it is
\begin{equation}
  \theta^4 - x(2\theta+1)^2(10\theta^2+10\theta+3)
  + 9x^2(2\theta+1)^2(2\theta+3)^2,
  \qquad \theta = x\,\frac{d}{dx},
  \label{eq:four_density_op}
\end{equation}
which has maximally unipotent monodromy at $x=0$ and conifold points at
$x=1/36$ and $x=1/4$, with local exponents $(0,1,1,2)$.  Its holomorphic
solution is $\sum_m B_m (x/4)^m$ with integer
$B_m=\binom{2m}{m}^2\sum_k\binom{m}{k}^2\binom{2k}{k}$, the term-by-term
(Hadamard) product of $\binom{2m}{m}^2$ with the Ap\'ery-like sequence of
the equal-mass sunrise, and its mirror map has integer coefficients.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} Whether a family is built from elliptic periods shows
in the quadratic relations among the solutions of its equation:\ for
monodromy of these types, an elliptic family has none, the square of
one elliptic family or the tensor product of two has one, and the cube of
one has three.  Counted
exactly on Taylor bases at two rational points, the numbers are
$0,1,0,1$ for $n=2,3,4,5$.  For $n=4$ the operator is irreducible, with
no bound on degrees:\ a right factor of order $k$ would carry, at each
singular point, $k$ of the operator's local exponents, and Fuchs' relation
then fails for every $k<4$, however many apparent singularities the factor
adds.  The same count shows the operators for $n=2,3,5$ irreducible.  Its
Yukawa coupling,
$1+16q+1152q^2+99520q^3+\cdots$, is not constant, as it would be for a
symmetric cube.  An irreducible fourth-order local system with no invariant
quadric that is not a symmetric cube has differential Galois group $\mathrm{Sp}_4$ or $\mathrm{SL}_4$,
and neither is a quotient of a product of copies of $\mathrm{SL}_2$.  So
$F_4$ is not a polynomial in elliptic periods with algebraic moduli, and
neither is the generic four-scale moment, which restricts to it.  Two
linked densities give an elliptic period, three give a K3 whose period is
elliptic squared, and four give the first period of a new kind.  At $n=5$
the single relation marks an orthogonal structure of rank five, not the
fourth power of an elliptic period, which would carry six.  That is the
structure of the exterior square of a fourth-order Calabi--Yau operator,
and the tables of Ref.~\cite{aesz2005} print the operator of $F_5$
verbatim, attached to entry \#39, a different operator from the \#58 of
$n=4$.  So five linked densities give no new type of period:\ the type is
that of four, in a different family.

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} Every electron-repulsion integral couples exactly
two densities, so the integrals of a molecular Hamiltonian stay on the
elliptic rung however large the molecule.  The class climbs only in
correlated integrals that link more densities, such as the four-electron
bridge integrals of explicitly correlated methods taken over two-center
densities.  Two caps apply.  These are statements about the fibre and
its holomorphic period, so the integrated value may sit lower, as the
two-center exchange class does (Sec.~\ref{sec:f12}).  The real
three-dimensional four-density integral is assumed, not shown, to reduce
into the one-dimensional Bessel-moment family of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tree};\ the
analogous step is checked only at $n=3$.  Guards:\
\texttt{tests/test\_paper59\_four\_density\_rung.py}.

\textbf{[PRIOR ART]} Most of this structure is known in the mathematics of
Bessel moments;\ what is new here is the route into it from a molecular
integral.  The operator~\eqref{eq:four_density_op} is entry \#58 of the
Calabi--Yau tables of Almkvist, van Enckevort, van Straten and
Zudilin~\cite{aesz2005} (entry 2.9 of the Calabi--Yau operator
database~\cite{cydb}), listed there as a Hadamard product, with the same
holomorphic coefficients;\ Gegelia and van
Straten~\cite{gegelia_vanstraten2024} list it among their conjectural
sources of paramodular forms, at conductors $2145$ and $2465$.  For every
$n$ from $2$ to $5$ the holomorphic solution of the equation of $F_n$ at
$t=\infty$ is $t^{-1}\sum_m\binom{2m}{m}^2W_{n-1}(2m)\,(16t^2)^{-m}$, the
Hadamard product of $\binom{2m}{m}^2$ with the even moments $W_{n-1}(2m)$
of the $(n-1)$-step planar random walk.  The Hadamard construction, and the
tables that place its $n=4$ and $n=5$ cases at entries \#58 and \#39, are
Almkvist and Zudilin's~\cite{almkvist_zudilin2007};\ the links of $W_N$ to
Bessel moments, random walks and Calabi--Yau equations are
Broadhurst's~\cite{broadhurst2009walks};\ Vanhove's operators for the
equal-mass two-dimensional banana integral with $N$
propagators~\cite{vanhove2014} annihilate $\sum_k W_N(2k)s^{-k-1}$, checked
here in exact arithmetic for $N=2$ to $5$, so that series is the banana's
holomorphic period at large $s$.  The
alternation of the monodromy, symplectic for $n$ even and orthogonal for
$n$ odd, matches the self-duality of the symmetric-power Kloosterman
motives of Fres\'an, Sabbah and Yu~\cite{fresansabbahyu2023}, whose
pairing is symplectic when the number of Bessel factors is even and
orthogonal when it is odd.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} So the tree ladder is tied to the banana ladder,
where an $l$-loop equal-mass banana integral carries a Calabi--Yau
$(l-1)$-fold~\cite{bonisch2021}, by one exact operation:\ with
$y=1/(16t^2)$ and $F_n=t^{-1}g(y)$, the operator of $g$ is the Hadamard
product of the Legendre operator with the operator of the banana with $n-1$
propagators, checked in exact arithmetic for $n=2$ to $5$.  Since
$\int_0^\infty J_0(px)\,dp=1/x$, the same fact reads
$F_n(t)=\int_0^\infty dp\int_0^\infty x\,J_0(px)\,K_0(tx)K_0(x)^{n-1}dx$:\
$F_n$ is the $n$-propagator banana, with masses $(t,1,\dots,1)$, integrated
over a line of external momentum.  It sits on the rung of the banana with
$n+1$ propagators but is not that family:\ its singular values of $t^2$,
$\{1\},\{4\},\{1,9\},\{4,16\}$ for $n=2,\dots,5$, are the thresholds of the
banana with $n-1$ propagators.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} At $t=1$ the ladder is the Ising class of
Bessel moments, $F_n(1)=c_{n,0}=\int_0^\infty K_0(x)^n\,dx$~\cite{bbbg2008}.
The singular values of $t^2$ are the thresholds $(n-1-2j)^2$ for every $n$
up to $8$, with no other finite nonzero singular point, so $t=1$ is a singular point
of $F_n$'s operator exactly when $n$ is even;\ the operators for $n=6$, $7$
and $8$ are irreducible by the exponent argument above, so these are the
singular points of $F_n$'s minimal operator.  There one local exponent is
doubled, while at the thresholds of odd $n$ one is a half-integer.  Since
$F_n$ is analytic for $t>0$, at a threshold it lies among the solutions
without the logarithm or the branch.  A prediction registered and committed before the
computation (\texttt{docs/predictions/2026-10-17\_ising\_class.md}) read
this as a rule for values:\ odd moments stay on their rung and even ones
drop.  The registered value cases hold, with moments for $n=2$ to $8$ to
286 digits by two routes and every informative integer-relation search
controlled;\ the registered attachment of $c_{4,0}$ to the conifold fibre
was not tested.  $c_{3,0}$ is a complex-multiplication elliptic value;\
$c_{5,0}$ and $c_{7,0}$ fit no tested lower basis, at coefficient bounds
of $10^{14}$ to $10^{17}$ and $10^{7}$ to $10^{9}$;\
$c_{4,0}=4\pi^2L(f,2)$ with $f=\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(4\tau)^4$ of weight four
and level eight, checked to $5\times10^{-261}$;\ and
$c_{6,0}=108\pi^2L(g,4)$ with $g$ of weight six and level 24, to the same
precision.  But $c_{8,0}$ fits no single $L$-value tried, with no Tate
relation to coefficient bound $10^7$ and none with an $L$-value to
$10^{12}$, so the $n=8$ search gives parity no support as a rule for values.  \textbf{[PRIOR ART]} The $L$-value forms of $c_{3,0}$ and $c_{4,0}$ are
collected by Lim, Tu and Yu~\cite{lim_tu_yu2026}, who credit them to earlier
work, through the hypergeometric forms of Ref.~\cite{bbbg2008};\ the
numerical form of $c_{6,0}$ is theirs, and in their account of the
underlying Kloosterman motives the moments at $n=5$, $7$ and $8$ enter
only through $2\times2$ determinants of periods.  The singular points themselves are the
thresholds and pseudo-thresholds of the equal-mass banana operator
(Ref.~\cite{bonisch2021}, Eq.~(3.7));\ the threshold reading of the
values is what the ladder adds.  In the bookkeeping of Papers~18
and~34~\cite{loutey_paper18,loutey_paper34} these values lie beyond the
genus-graded embedding tier:\ $c_{3,0}$ is a rational multiple of $2^{-2/3}\Gamma(1/3)^6/\pi$, a
complex-multiplication period, and $c_{4,0}$ and, numerically, $c_{6,0}$
are $\pi^2$ times critical $L$-values of modular forms, periods of the
Kloosterman motives.  The nearest
physics analogue of a ceiling set by the calculation rather than the
system is the bound Bourjaily, McLeod, von Hippel and Wilhelm argue for on the
rigidity of massless four-dimensional Feynman integrals, $2(L-1)$ at $L$
loops~\cite{bourjaily2019}:\ there the loop order caps the geometry, here
the number of densities one integral links.  On the chemistry side,
one-center three- and four-electron correlated integrals have closed
forms~\cite{fromm_hill1987,padhy2017}, consistent with the rung needing
two-center densities.  A targeted search found no source placing a
molecular integral on these operators;\ that is an absence from a search,
not a proof of absence.

\subsection{A ring of three densities:\ the loop does not climb}
\label{sec:density_ring}

Every rung above is a tree.  An integral sits on a rung when the minimal
annihilator of its fibre has no composition factor above that rung.  A
ring of kernels leaves one momentum that the delta functions behind
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tree} do not remove, and one might expect a loop to raise the
class faster than a leg does.  The test case is the Coulomb triangle
$T=\langle\rho_a(1)\rho_b(2)\rho_c(3)/(r_{12}r_{13}r_{23})\rangle$, three
densities with every pair joined by a kernel.  Its explicitly correlated
relative $\langle f_{ik}f_{jk}/r_{ij}\rangle$ is the one three-body class of
the LiH correlated engine that does not separate.  The prediction, written
and committed on 2026-10-17 before the computation
(\texttt{docs/predictions/2026-10-17\_density\_loop.md}), was that the loop
does not climb.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} The loop integrates out exactly.  In three
dimensions three massless propagators of unit index are unique, and the
uniqueness (star-triangle) relation gives
\begin{equation}
  \int\frac{d^3l}{|l-x_1|^2\,|l-x_2|^2\,|l-x_3|^2}
  = \frac{\pi^3}{|x_1-x_2|\,|x_2-x_3|\,|x_3-x_1|},
  \label{eq:star_triangle}
\end{equation}
which a Feynman-parameter evaluation confirms at five random triples to
$1.2\times10^{-12}$.  The triangle becomes an integral over the same two momenta and
three densities as the three-electron chain.  For spherical densities the
magnitude measure cancels the weight, and
\begin{equation}
  T = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|p-q|\le w\le p+q}
      \tilde\rho_a(p)\,\tilde\rho_b(q)\,\tilde\rho_c(w)\,dp\,dq\,dw .
  \label{eq:ring_reduced}
\end{equation}

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} At one center, with $1s$ Slater densities,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ring_reduced} reproduces Remiddi's closed
form~\cite{remiddi1991}, as written in Ref.~\cite{ppr2004} and
differentiated in the exponents, to $2\times10^{-21}$.  Where the chain is
rational (Sec.~\ref{sec:three_electron}), the triangle carries a
dilogarithm.  At exponents $(1,1,1)$
\begin{equation}
  T = \frac{11}{3}-\frac{\pi^2}{8}-\frac32\,\mathrm{Li}_2\!\left(-\tfrac12\right)
      -\frac{46}{9}\ln 2-\frac34\ln^2 2+\frac32\ln 2\ln 3 ,
  \label{eq:ring_one_center}
\end{equation}
and at $(1,2,3)$ a 100-digit integer-relation search finds a closed form in
$\mathrm{Li}_2(1/3)$, $\mathrm{Li}_2(1/5)$, $\mathrm{Li}_2(1/6)$, $\pi^2$
and products of logarithms.  The ring raises the weight from zero to two.
In the bookkeeping of Papers~18 and~34 these one-center values are
embedding-tier constants of weight two, one step above the weight-one
two-center seed set;\ the $\pi^3$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:star_triangle} is the
loop's normalization.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} At the fingerprint fibre,
$\tilde\rho_v=\Delta_v^{-3}$ with scales $(1,1,t)$, the triangle is a moment
of three Struve functions,
\begin{equation}
  G(t)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,t^2\int_0^\infty x^2\,\psi(x)^2\,\psi(tx)\,dx,
  \qquad \psi=\mathbf{L}_{-1}-I_1 ,
  \label{eq:ring_struve}
\end{equation}
where $\psi$ is one density with its kernel in position space and solves
$(\theta^2-1-x^2)\psi=-2/\pi$.  The Mellin route of
Sec.~\ref{sec:four_density} gives
\begin{equation}
  L_G = -4\theta(\theta-3)^2 + t^2\,(5\theta^3-7\theta^2-5\theta+3)
        - t^4\,\theta(\theta+2)(\theta+3),
  \qquad L_G\,G = -3t^2 ,
  \label{eq:ring_op}
\end{equation}
with $\theta=t\,d/dt$, checked by quadrature of the Struve form at six
values of $t$ to 18 digits, and independently on the momentum form, with the
$t$-derivatives taken analytically.  The source term is the operator applied to a constant:\ $L_G\,1=3t^2$,
so $L_G(G+1)=0$, and the constant is what the regularized Mellin transform
cannot see.  This operator factors.
The solution of $L_G$ regular at $t=0$ is algebraic,
$(1-t^2/4)^{-3/2}$.  Write $L_G=M\,(\theta-\lambda_G)$ with $\lambda_G=3t^2/(4-t^2)$;\ the
division is exact.  The quotient $M$ is equivalent to the Legendre equation
$\theta^2-t^2(\theta+1)^2$ of $F_2$, the two-density period:\ the map
$f\mapsto t^3(1+\theta)f/(4-t^2)$ sends every solution of the Legendre
equation to a solution of $M$, an identity checked in exact rational
arithmetic.  And $G$ needs the elliptic piece:\ $(\theta-\lambda_G)(G+1)$ is a
nonzero solution of $M$, equal to $-0.161$ at $t=0.5$ and $-2.33$ at
$t=1.3$.  Neither side has a relation to the K3 family of $F_3$.
So $G$ is built from an elementary constant, an algebraic function and an elliptic
period integrated once against an algebraic kernel.  The
triangle sits on the elliptic rung of a single repulsion integral.  That is
one rung below the three-density chain.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} The chain $C(t)$ at the same fibre, treated the same
way, is the control.  The Mellin route gives it an operator of order seven,
which divides exactly as $N_2\,R_3\,R_1\,(\theta-2)$.  The two lowest pieces
are elementary:\ $\theta-2$ has the solution $t^2$, and
$R_1=(\theta-3)-t^2(\theta+1)$ has the solution $t^3/(1-t^2)^2$.  The piece
$R_3$, of order three, is equivalent to the operator
$4\theta^3-t^2(\theta+1)^3$ of $F_3$:\ the map
\begin{equation}
  f\;\mapsto\;\frac{t^3(t^4-18t^2-4)\,f+t^3(2t^4-43t^2-40)\,\theta f
  +t(t^6-27t^4-96t^2+32)\,\theta^2 f}{(t^2-4)^2}
  \label{eq:chain_k3_map}
\end{equation}
sends every solution of $F_3$'s operator to a solution of $R_3$, an identity
checked in exact rational arithmetic.  The top piece $N_2$, of order two, is
not needed:\ $R_3R_1(\theta-2)$ already annihilates the chain, with a
residual of at most $2\times10^{-31}$ at 32 digits and $4\times10^{-19}$
at 20 digits at four values of $t$ between $0.5$ and $3.5$, while changing
one coefficient of $R_3$ gives residuals from 1.9 to 604.  The chain's only piece beyond the elementary ones is therefore
the K3 family of $F_3$.  Finer still, $R_3R_1$ splits exactly, so the order-five
operator is a left multiple of an operator $P$ of order four, and the chain
satisfies $P$:\ $PC$, divided by the solution $t^3/(1-t^2)^2$ of $R_1$, is
at most $10^{-23}$ at 20 digits and $10^{-33}$ at 30 digits at four values
of $t$, and $9\times10^{-45}$ at 40 digits at one, while a $1\%$ change in
one coefficient gives $0.02$ to $0.07$.  The second solution of
$R_1(\theta-2)$, which carries $\operatorname{artanh} t$, is absent.  So
$C=c\,t^2+t^2\int^t s^{-3}(B'f)(s)\,ds$ with $f$ a solution of $F_3$'s
operator and $B'$ a rational map.  That integral cannot be done inside the
K3 family:\ no rational map splits the K3 piece off $t^2$.  This is proved
by bounding the poles and degree of any such map from the local exponents
and searching that bound exhaustively in exact arithmetic, a search that
finds every planted split.  Because $F_3$'s operator is irreducible, the
chain is therefore not a rational combination of $F_3$ and its derivatives
plus any function without a K3 piece:\ it is a K3 function integrated once
against an elementary kernel.  So the
Coulomb ring lowers the rung at the fibre and raises the weight at one
center.  In neither limit does it climb the period ladder.

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]} The same holds for the physical weights.
With the Slater densities of Sec.~\ref{sec:three_electron}, the chain's
fibre at zero separation, on the slice with scales $(t;1,1)$ and $t$ on
the central density, is
\begin{equation}
  G(t)=\int_0^\infty x\,K_1(tx)\,J(x)^2\,dx,\qquad
  J=3\int_0^x K_0-3xK_0-x^2K_1 .
  \label{eq:chain_slater_slice}
\end{equation}
The Mellin route gives an operator of order seven, which divides exactly
as $N_2\,R_3\,R_1\,(\theta+2)$.  The two lowest pieces are elementary:\
$\theta+2$ has the solution $t^{-2}$, and $R_1=(1-t^2)\theta-(1+7t^2)$ has
the solution $t/(1-t^2)^4$.  The piece $R_3$, of order three, is
equivalent to $F_3$'s operator $4\theta^3-t^2(\theta+1)^3$:\ an explicit
order-two map, rational in $t$ with poles at $t=0,\pm2$, sends every
solution of $F_3$'s operator to a solution of $N_2R_3$, and $R_3$ is the
annihilator of the image, both checked in exact rational arithmetic.
Again $N_2$ is not needed:\ $R_3R_1(\theta+2)$ annihilates $G$ with a
residual of at most $2\times10^{-31}$ at 32 digits at five values of $t$
between $0.5$ and $3.5$, while changing one coefficient of $R_3$ by one part
in 198 gives residuals from $10^3$ to $4\times10^4$.  And $G$ needs the K3
piece:\ $R_1(\theta+2)G$ is nonzero, $191$ at $t=0.5$ and $8.9$ at $t=3.5$.
A search modulo a prime finds no rational map that splits the K3 piece
off, in a box larger than the bound set by the leading terms of the local
exponents, and the same call finds a planted split.  So $G$ is an
elementary function plus a K3 function integrated once against an
elementary kernel, here one that carries $\operatorname{artanh}t$;\ whether
that term drops, as it does for the chain at the fingerprint fibre, was not
tested.  The operator is singular at $t=1$, the equal-scale point where
the fibre collapses (Sec.~\ref{sec:three_electron});\ its exponent $-3$
there comes from $R_1$, so the singularity is not apparent.  Caps:\ the

\section{Scope, and the open frontier}
\label{sec:scope}
%% ========================================================================

\emph{Scope.}  The demonstration is for $1s$ orbitals at a common exponent.
Higher $(n,\ell,m)$ multiply the integrand of Eq.~\eqref{eq:reduced} by
solid-harmonic polynomials in $\bm k$ and shift the exponents; these change
the weights and arguments but not the genus of the curve~\eqref{eq:curve},
which is set by the two distinct scales.  The result is a statement about the
transcendence \emph{class} of the three-center integral, established on its
simplest genuine instance.  It does not by itself deliver a polyatomic energy:
a molecular Hamiltonian requires the full three-center tensor, and a value
that is part closed-form and part numerical is neither more accurate (the
Gaussian fits are already exact to fit quality) nor decidable.  What the
reduction buys is an accurate, fast, geometry-native evaluator for the
three-center integral and---the point of this paper---a precise diagnosis of
why it is hard.

\emph{The wall is closed-form, not computational.}  \textbf{[MEASURED]} The
infinite series that the standard verdict falls back on is, concretely, the
expansion of both densities in multipoles about the shared center (the
Barnett--Coulson / L\"owdin $\alpha$-function route~\cite{barnett_coulson1951};
a recent analytic treatment of the same integrals, for linear
geometries, is Ref.~\cite{ozdogan2012}; both are the numerical treatments
named in Sec.~\ref{sec:literature}).  Each channel is a product of two
one-pair factors, and the third center enters only through the relative
orientation of the two pairs.  On the water geometry
($R_{\rm OH} = 1.809$, $104.5^\circ$) with O $1s/2s/2p$ and H $1s$ Slater
orbitals, seven three-center integrals of both shared-O and shared-H type
converge to $\sim\!10^{-9}$ by $L \approx 12$.  Gaussian-fit sequences
converge onto every value; for the O $1s$ core the Gaussians approach it from
below and are still $6\times10^{-8}$ short at $18$ Gaussians, while the
channel sum is stable to thirteen digits
(\texttt{tests/test\_pairwise\_general.py},
\texttt{tests/test\_pairwise\_three\_center.py}).  The elliptic content of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:curve} is spread over the infinite channel sum.  The radial core is a period of an irreducible connection
(Sec.~\ref{sec:obstruction}, \textbf{[SYMBOLIC + MEASURED]}), so it has no
closed form through any factorization;\ that does not bar an accurate
evaluation, and whether the integrated three-center integral has a finite
closed form is the open problem below.

\emph{The open problem.}  As shown in Sec.~\ref{sec:obstruction}, the
elliptic-dilogarithm route is obstructed: $N(D)$ is not an elliptic
dilogarithm of elementary or period-level data but a period of the rank-four
irregular connection~\eqref{eq:pf}.  We settle the differential-Galois
\emph{reducibility}: the fourth-order operator is irreducible over
$\mathbb{C}(D)$---hence over $\mathbb{Q}(\rho)(D)$---because it is the
Fourier--Laplace transform of an irreducible connection, confirmed concretely by
its exact-integer monodromy (Sec.~\ref{sec:obstruction}), so $N(D)$ admits no
closed form through any factorization of the operator.  The
one remaining question is whether the irreducible connection nonetheless carries
a \emph{transcendental} closed form, which we do not settle.
The companion (Paper~61) localizes it: the object is an Eisenstein /
complex-multiplication Bessel-moment period---not a cusp-form $L$-value and not an
elliptic dilogarithm---whose master family obeys the quadratic period relations
$B=\pi\,\Omega$, $\Omega$ the canonical symplectic intersection form now identified in
closed form (developed in the companion, Paper~61), preserved by the integral monodromy; the
open piece is the explicit finite reduction of the observable itself to $\pi$ and the
CM-fibre $\Gamma$-values.
(This tempers a literature-scout expectation that the object would sit at the
tractable elliptic-dilogarithm level because it has only two propagator
factors; that pattern-match from the sunrise is overridden by the in-module
computation of Sec.~\ref{sec:obstruction}.)  Because it continues the Averys'
momentum-space Sturmian program and requires elliptic-Feynman-integral
technique one level beyond the sunrise, it is the natural place for
collaboration.

\section{Conclusion}
%% ========================================================================

The three-center electron-repulsion integral is not merely a harder version
of the two-center one; it is an object of higher genus.  Two centers close at
genus zero over $\{E_{1},\ln,\gamma\}$; three centers, read in momentum space
where the coordinate obstruction dissolves, reduce to a two-scale Bessel
moment on an elliptic curve, an elliptic deformation of the Bessel $K_{0}$
with an explicit fourth-order Picard--Fuchs equation and a period that is a
complete elliptic integral.  The seventy-year difficulty of polyatomic Slater
integrals is, at the level of transcendence, a genus jump; and a classical
molecular integral turns out to be an instance of the modern theory of
elliptic Feynman integrals---a bridge that, to our knowledge, had not been
drawn.  The finite closed form remains open---and, we have shown, past the
reach of the sunrise's elliptic-dilogarithm mechanism, whose inhomogeneity is
absent here: the object is a period of a rank-four irregular connection which we
prove irreducible---it is the Fourier--Laplace transform of an irreducible
connection, and its monodromy admits no invariant coordinate subspace---so it does not
close by any factorization.  Whether it closes at the irreducible level---as an Eisenstein/CM Bessel-moment
period, a finite reduction to $\pi$ and the CM-fibre $\Gamma$-values
(companion, Paper~61)---is the one frontier that remains, posed here as
it deserves to be.  Two pre-registered extensions (Sec.~\ref{sec:beyond})
probe the rung variable:\ a fourth center adds nothing, consistent with the class
counting the distinct two-center densities a kernel couples and not the nuclei, while
a second Coulomb kernel at one electron, over two-center densities, climbs
to a K3 whose period is elliptic squared.  A third extension shows the rung of a tree's holomorphic period is
set by how many densities it links:\ four linked densities give the
first period not built from elliptic ones, five give no new type, and the
integrals of a molecular Hamiltonian, which link two, stay elliptic at the fibre for any
molecule;\ the operators for two to five densities are known, tied to the banana ladder by a Hadamard
product, and what is new is the route from a molecular integral.  A fourth shows
that the count orders trees, not rings:\ a Coulomb triangle of three
densities sits on the elliptic rung at the fibre, below the chain of three,
while the correlated triangle with exponential geminals sits with the chain at
$\gamma=1$;\ we read the difference as uniqueness, which factors the Coulomb loop
and not the geminal one.
