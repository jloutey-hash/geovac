\begin{abstract}
We study a discrete labelling of the hydrogen atom's bound-state space. The states $|n,\ell,m\rangle$ are indexed by a label lattice, drawn as a paraboloid (a visualization in which the depth coordinate $-1/n^2$ is placed by hand, not an output), on which the $SU(2)\times SU(1,1)$ ladder operators act as sparse matrices. This single-particle model separates two kinds of content: \textbf{(i) Algebraic exactness:} \textbf{[MEASURED]} Transition operators $T_\pm, L_\pm$ reproduce the Rydberg quantum numbers from operator eigenvalues, to floating-point tolerance ($10^{-9}$):\ the $SU(1,1)$ element $N = -2[T_+, T_-]$ returns $n$ on the interior shells $n < n_{\max}$ (not on the truncated top shell), and $L^2$ returns $\ell(\ell+1)$ on every shell;\ on every shell, including the top one, the $SO(4)$ Casimir $\mathbf{L}^2 + \mathbf{A}^2 = n^2 - 1$ ($\mathbf{A}$ the energy-normalized Runge--Lenz vector) is exact on the labels.  The energies $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ are an imposed map from $n$ (Fock's energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$, with $p_0 = Z/n$ fixed by Fock's kernel eigenvalue, Paper~7), not an output of the ladder operators;\ once Fock's kernel fixes the function, on the labels they are $E_n = -1/(2(C+1))$ of the Casimir $C = n^2-1$ (i.e.\ $H = -\tfrac12(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$ at $Z = 1$). \textbf{(ii) Geometric artifacts:} \textbf{[MEASURED]} A node-amplitude proxy for the $s/p$ gap is nonzero at finite lattice cutoff ($37\%$ at $n_{\max}=5$, oscillating through $15.6\%$ at $8$ and $1.7\%$ at $10$ to $0.39\%$ at $30$ on the binary production lattice; see the \S III QA note).  \textbf{[INTERNAL THEOREM]} Its size tracks the residue of $n_{\max}$ modulo $3$ --- exactly closed form on $n_{\max} \equiv 0$, and generically larger off it (the off-/on-branch ratio grows asymptotically as $n_{\max}/(\sqrt{3}\,\pi)$;\ what was retracted is two earlier \emph{precision} claims about that ratio [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-ratio-false-precision], read off three sampled cutoffs) --- so the quoted endpoint sits on a favourable branch and the sequence is not evidence of convergence (2026-09-03;\ the earlier wording, ``the graph Laplacian lifts $s/p$ degeneracy \ldots\ through differential weighted connectivity'', both contradicted the clause below and named the weighted construction's mechanism for binary-lattice numbers, where every edge weight is~$1$). The cutoff sweep from $n_{\max}=5$ to 30 shows this is a discretization artifact---a residue-controlled truncation effect, \emph{not} spectral aliasing [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism] (that reading was withdrawn 2026-09-03: the $l$-blocks are disconnected and carry no degeneracy to alias)---that oscillates and reads $0.39\%$ at $n_{\max}=30$ on the $n_{\max}\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ branch ($1.65\%$ and $2.58\%$ at $28$ and $29$): a proxy value, not evidence of recovering the exact Coulomb degeneracy [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism], which belongs to the continuum operator (the graph's $l$-blocks are disconnected, so the graph has no $s/p$ degeneracy of its own). \textbf{(iii) Geometric phase structure:} \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} The Berry phase vanishes identically for the real-valued transition operators; on the optional topological-weight lattice ($w = 1/(n_1 n_2)$) the edge-weight log-holonomy $\Theta(n) = -2\ln((n{+}1)/n) \sim n^{-1}$ measures the variation (a discrete gradient) of that weight function (on the binary production lattice and the CG-magnitude construction $\Theta \equiv 0$). \textbf{This model describes only the electron's state space:}\ it contains no electromagnetic coupling, and we find no trace of the fine structure constant $\alpha$ in it (a companion paper~\cite{companion_alpha} records a separate numerical Observation about $\alpha$, not a coupling mechanism).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Texture of the Vacuum}

Quantum mechanics is ordinarily formulated on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with continuous operators. The bound-state sector of hydrogen, however, is also completely described by a discrete set of labels $(n, l, m)$ together with the operators that act on them; the continuous and discrete descriptions are dual descriptions of the same quantum-number structure, and we assign neither ontological priority.

We study a discrete labelling of the hydrogen state space. The hydrogen state space is indexed by a \textbf{graph}: nodes are the quantum-number labels of the basis states, edges connect labels related by the ladder operators $T_\pm, L_\pm$, and those operators act as sparse matrices on the labels.

This discrete structure can be drawn with a natural geometry. The hydrogen atom's $SO(4)$ symmetry~\cite{fock1935}, extended to the $SO(4,2)$ dynamical group~\cite{malkin_manko1965,fronsdal1967,barut1967}, motivates the \textbf{paraboloid lattice} picture, in which the labels $(n, l, m)$ are placed at coordinates on a curved 3D surface. The picture is a visualization, not a derivation:\ only the $SU(2)\times SU(1,1)$ subalgebra is used below, and the depth coordinate $z = -1/n^2$ is placed by hand from the known Rydberg formula (it is not an output of the lattice).

The label set carries two kinds of content, which we keep apart:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Algebraic Skeleton:} Transition operators $T_\pm$ (radial) and $L_\pm$ (angular) generate the quantum numbers. The number operator $N = -2[T_+,T_-]$ and the Casimir $L^2$ built from them reproduce $n$ exactly on the interior shells and $\ell(\ell+1)$ on every shell (on every shell, including the top one, the $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2-1$ is exact on the labels); the formula $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ is then an imposed map from $n$ through Fock's energy shell, with $p_0 = Z/n$ fixed by Fock's kernel eigenvalue (Paper~7), not an output of the ladder operators (once Fock's kernel fixes the function, it is $E_n = -1/(2(C+1))$ of the Casimir $C = n^2-1$ on the labels).
\item \textbf{The Geometric Flesh:} The lattice \textit{topology}---the pattern of connections---produces finite-truncation artifacts, not physical corrections:\ the structure of the graph Laplacian is intrinsic to its disconnected two-dimensional grid blocks. Node degrees differ between angular-momentum sectors at finite truncation --- on the binary lattice they depend on $m$ and on which shell neighbours exist, not monotonically on $l$ (read before 2026-09-03 as differential weighted connectivity [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism] lifting the $s/p$ degeneracy;\ withdrawn --- the $l$-blocks are disconnected components). On the optional topological-weight lattice the edge-weight log-holonomy measures the variation (a discrete gradient) of the weight function and decays as $n^{-1}$ (it vanishes identically on the binary production lattice).
\end{itemize}

In this paper, we present computational evidence for this dual framework. Section~\ref{sec:algebra} demonstrates exactness of the quantum numbers on interior shells. Section~\ref{sec:geometry} analyzes the $s/p$ node-amplitude proxy, showing it is a discretization artifact whose size tracks the residue of $n_{\max}$ modulo $3$ --- not a graph degeneracy (the $\ell$-blocks are disconnected) and not a continuum-convergence signal. Section~\ref{sec:relativity} analyzes the geometric phase structure, showing that the Berry phase vanishes while the log-holonomy decays as $n^{-1}$. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the separation between the algebraic and graph-geometric content. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes our findings.


\section{The Algebraic Skeleton: Exact Quantum Numbers}
\label{sec:algebra}

The paraboloid lattice is generated by two sets of ladder operators forming the hydrogen atom's dynamical algebra $SU(2) \otimes SU(1,1)$:
\begin{align}
\text{Angular:} \quad & L_z, L_\pm \quad (\text{standard angular momentum}), \\
\text{Radial:} \quad & T_0, T_\pm \quad (\text{energy shell transitions}).
\end{align}
These operators act on basis states $|n, l, m\rangle$ with selection rules:
\begin{align}
L_\pm |n, l, m\rangle &\propto |n, l, m \pm 1\rangle, \\
T_\pm |n, l, m\rangle &\propto |n \pm 1, l, m\rangle.
\end{align}
The operator weights are the standard $SU(2)$ ladder matrix elements \cite{biedenharn1981} and the noncompact $SU(1,1)$ ladder matrix elements \cite{barut1967}.

\subsection{Quantum-Number Reproduction}

The critical test of this algebra is spectroscopic: do the operators reproduce the quantum numbers that label the Rydberg formula? \textbf{[MEASURED]} They do, on interior shells:\ the operators return $n$ ($n < n_{\max}$) and $\ell(\ell+1)$ to $10^{-9}$ in floating point, and the Rydberg energies
\begin{equation}
E_n = -\frac{1}{2n^2} \quad (\text{Hartree atomic units}),
\label{eq:rydberg}
\end{equation}
are then the imposed map from $n$ given by Fock's energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$, with $p_0 = Z/n$ fixed by Fock's kernel eigenvalue (Paper~7), not an eigenvalue output of the lattice.  (The matched kinetic scale $\kappa = -1/16$ plays no role here:\ it matches only the graph Laplacian's extremal eigenvalue, a band edge, to $-\tfrac12$, and its coincidence with the Fock Jacobian factor is an Observation, Paper~7.) No fitting parameters are required; the algebra reproduces the quantum-number labels (with $n$ entering through the chosen $T_\pm$ weights).

This result validates the lattice as a faithful discrete \emph{labelling} of the (truncated) hydrogen Hilbert space. The nodes are not a coarse-graining---they label the quantum states. The edges connect labels related by the ladder operators $T_\pm, L_\pm$;\ they are not dipole-allowed physical transitions. The quantum-number structure emerges from operator eigenvalues, not from solving differential equations; the energies are the imposed energy-shell map of $n$. (Verified in \texttt{tests/test\_paper1\_rydberg.py}: the $SU(1,1)$ number operator $N = -2[T_+, T_-]$, assembled purely from the ladder generators, has integer spectrum $\{n\}$ on the \emph{interior} shells $n < n_{\max}$ and the $SU(2)$ Casimir $L^2$ gives $l(l+1)$, both to $10^{-9}$ in floating point;\ $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ is then imposed as a function of $n$, not output by the algebra. This is the dynamical-symmetry construction, and it has three limits stated here so that it is not over-read: its $n$-dependence enters through the off-diagonal $T_\pm$ weights rather than through a diagonal operator;\ it is not exact on the top shell, where the truncated commutator makes $N$ $l$-dependent and non-positive (the test checks interior states only);\ and $SU(1,1)$ is noncompact, so its truncation is not a sub-representation. The exact \emph{compact} discrete operator on these labels is the $SO(4)$ Casimir $\mathbf{L}^2 + \mathbf{A}^2 = n^2 - 1$ ($\mathbf{A}$ the energy-normalized Runge--Lenz vector), diagonal with multiplicity $n^2$ on every shell including the top one, since each shell is a complete irreducible representation (\texttt{tests/test\_graph\_s3\_nonconvergence.py});\ it is $-\Delta_{S^3}$, the $S^3$ Laplace--Beltrami operator up to sign, acting on the label set. The discrete graph Laplacian $L = D - A$ is a separate object and does \emph{not} reproduce this spectrum in any limit:\ it preserves $l$, splits into $n_{\max}$ disconnected blocks, and does not converge to $\Delta_{S^3}$ (Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7};\ corrected 2026-09-25 from ``reproduces the spectrum only in the continuum limit'').)


\section{The Geometric Flesh: Topological Artifacts of Finite Truncation}
\label{sec:geometry}

While the algebraic operators yield the exact quantum numbers (on interior shells), the \textit{geometric structure} of the lattice---its connectivity pattern---introduces systematic artifacts when the lattice is truncated at finite $n_{\max}$. We demonstrate this by constructing the graph Laplacian and analyzing its finite-truncation properties as a function of lattice size.

\subsection{The Graph Laplacian Hamiltonian}

In graph theory, the kinetic energy of a particle hopping on a graph's vertices is described by the \textbf{graph Laplacian}~\cite{chung1997}; here the vertices are quantum-number labels, not positions, so this is a statement about the graph, not about the electron:
\begin{equation}
L = D - A,
\label{eq:laplacian}
\end{equation}
where $A_{ij}$ is the adjacency matrix (edge weights) and $D$ is the weighted degree matrix\footnote{The weighted degree $D_{ii} = \sum_j |A_{ij}|$ represents the total connectivity strength of node $i$. For transition matrices with complex coefficients, we use absolute values to obtain undirected edge weights, explaining the non-integer values in Table~\ref{tab:degrees}.}. This Laplacian is a discrete analog of $-\nabla^2$ on each $l$-block (a two-dimensional grid in $n$ and $m$);\ it is not a discretization of the $S^3$ Laplacian, to which it does not converge (Paper~7).

For the paraboloid lattice, we construct $A$ from the transition operators:
\begin{equation}
A = |T_+| + |T_-| + |L_+| + |L_-|,
\end{equation}
where $|\cdot|$ denotes element-wise absolute value (undirected edges). The Hamiltonian becomes
\begin{equation}
H = \beta L + V = \beta (D - A) + V,
\label{eq:ham_graph}
\end{equation}
where $V_{ii} = -1/n_i^2$ is a diagonal Coulomb node weight and $\beta$
a scaling factor.  \textbf{Eq.~\eqref{eq:ham_graph} is a general form, not the
object measured below and not the production object} (clarified 2026-09-03):\
the splitting measurements of \S\ref{sec:geometry} are taken on the bare
graph Laplacian $L = D - A$, and the labelled legacy graph model
\texttt{AtomicSolver(hamiltonian='graph')} builds
$H = K_{\mathrm{vac}} L$ with node weights never applied (the solver's
default is now the physical diagonal $H = \mathrm{diag}(-Z^2/2n^2)$ on
the labels;\ the splitting numbers below are measured on the bare
lattice and are unaffected).  Under
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ham_graph} with $V_{ii} = -1/n_i^2$ and $\beta = 1$, the first six
of the twelve cutoffs of \S III ($n_{\max} = 5$--$10$) give
$44.7$, $2.2$, $27.5$, $10.6$, $31.5$ and $620.9\%$ --- a different
quantity entirely (the values depend on $\beta$;\ $\beta = \pm 1/16$ gives
different figures, and none of them is pinned by a test).

\subsection{Differential Connectivity by Angular Momentum}

The degree matrix $D$ is \textit{not rotationally invariant}. The weighted degrees originally printed for states with $n=2$ were
\begin{align}
\text{Pole (2s):} \quad & D_{(2,0,0)} = 0.854, \\
\text{Equator (2p):} \quad & D_{(2,1,0)} = 3.416,
\end{align}
\emph{historical values that reproduce under no current construction} (flagged inline 2026-09-28;\ the binary production lattice gives $D_{2s} = 2$, $D_{2p} = 3$, the CG-magnitude rebuild $1.93$ and $3.83$ --- see the \S III QA note and Table~\ref{tab:degrees}).
\textbf{[MEASURED]} On the binary production lattice the degree is \emph{not} monotone in $l$ (nor is it on the CG construction of \S III once $m \ne 0$ nodes are counted:\ $3d_{m=\pm2}$ $3.23 < 3p_{m=0}$ $5.41$). A node's degree counts its $L_\pm$ neighbours (two for interior $m$, one at $|m| = l$, none for $l = 0$) plus its $T_\pm$ neighbours $(n \pm 1, l, m)$ that exist; so $D_{2p,\,m=0} = 3 > D_{2s} = 2 = D_{2p,\,m=\pm1}$, while in an interior shell the maximum degree rises from $l = 0$, holds at its plateau value from $l = 1$ to $l = n-2$, and drops at $l = n-1$ ($n = 3$ at $n_{\max} = 4$:\ $l = 0, 1, 2 \to 2,\ 3$--$4,\ 2$--$3$;\ $n = 5$ at $n_{\max} = 6$:\ maxima $2, 4, 4, 4, 3$), and the top shell is lowered by truncation. A higher degree means a higher on-site entry in the Laplacian formulation;\ it does not couple the $l$-blocks, which are disconnected components, so it is not a mechanism for the $s/p$ proxy (that reading withdrawn 2026-09-03;\ \S III).

\subsection{Convergence Analysis}

The key diagnostic is the behavior of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{rel}}$ as
$n_{\max} \to \infty$. \textbf{[MEASURED]} We swept the lattice cutoff from $n_{\max} = 5$
(55 vertices) through $n_{\max} = 30$ (9{,}455 vertices) and tracked
the 2s/2p splitting at each resolution:

\begin{itemize}
  \item At $n_{\max} = 5$: $\Delta E_{\mathrm{rel}} \approx 37\%$ (the
    maximum on the grid)
  \item At $n_{\max} = 6, 7$: $12.7\%$, $0.65\%$
  \item At $n_{\max} = 8$: $15.6\%$ (a secondary peak); by
    $n_{\max} = 10$ it has fallen to $\approx 1.7\%$
  \item At $n_{\max} = 15, 18$: $1.7\%$, $1.1\%$
  \item At $n_{\max} = 20$: $\Delta E_{\mathrm{rel}} \approx 4.1\%$ (a
    third excursion)
  \item At $n_{\max} = 25, 30$: $1.8\%$, $0.39\%$
\end{itemize}
(binary production lattice, basis-free maximum-projection identification ---
see the conditioning caveat above;
\texttt{tests/test\_paper1\_block\_spectrum.py} pins the on-branch closed
form, the $28$/$29$/$30$ values, the conditioning and the
CG-construction non-decay;\ the off-branch values at $n_{\max} = 5$--$10$,
$20$ and $25$, and every value on this list, are now pinned by
\texttt{tests/test\_trunk\_qa\_splitting.py}, rewritten 2026-09-28 to the
basis-free $\|P_\lambda e_{\mathrm{node}}\|^2$ rule (invariant under a
rotation of the eigenbasis inside a degenerate eigenspace, unlike the
raw-eigenvector argmax it replaces) and so counted as backing; the list
printed before 2026-09-03---$13\%$, $16\%$, $0.3\%$, $0.005\%$---did not
reproduce at $n_{\max} = 5$, $20$ or $30$.)

The splitting does not decay monotonically; it \emph{oscillates} at
intermediate resolutions.  \textbf{[INTERNAL THEOREM]} \emph{The oscillation
is the residue of $n_{\max}$ modulo $3$, and the sequence is closed form}
(2026-09-03).  The $\ell = 0$ block is the path $P_{n_{\max}}$, whose
eigenvalues are $2 - 2\cos(j\pi/n_{\max})$;\ the mode selected by maximal
overlap with the $2s$ node has $j = \operatorname{round}(2n_{\max}/3)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{2s} = 3 \ \text{\emph{exactly}} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  n_{\max} \equiv 0 \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),
  \label{eq:lambda2s_residue}
\end{equation}
and on that branch the reported ratio is exactly
$\bigl(2 - 2\cos\frac{\pi}{n_{\max}-1}\bigr)/3$ --- $2.7005$, $1.6715$,
$1.1351$, $0.8208$, $0.6209$, $0.4861$ and $0.3908\%$ at
$n_{\max} = 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30$, matching the measured values to every
digit printed.  \emph{At neighbouring cutoffs} the off-branch value is generically larger --- asymptotically by $n_{\max}/(\sqrt{3}\,\pi)$ (the asymptotic itself stands;\ only two earlier \emph{precision} claims about it were retracted [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-ratio-false-precision], see below), but only roughly at small cutoff: $4.2\times$ and $6.6\times$ at $n_{\max} = 28, 29$ against $30$ ($1.65\%$, $2.58\%$ vs $0.39\%$), where the formula gives $5.5$;\ $20$--$23\times$ at $118, 119$ against $120$ ($22.1$);\ $550$--$552\times$ at $2998, 2999$ against $3000$ ($551.3$). Below $n_{\max} \approx 12$ the ordering can invert ($0.65\%$ at $7$ against $12.7\%$ at $6$). (Two earlier statements of this ratio --- ``three to six times'' and ``tracking $n_{\max}/(\sqrt{3}\,\pi)$ to within $1\%$'' [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-ratio-false-precision] --- were each read off three sampled cutoffs and stated as theorems;\ both are withdrawn.)  \textbf{The endpoint quoted throughout
this paper, $0.39\%$ at $n_{\max} = 30$, therefore lies on the favourable
branch}, a selection this paper did not disclose before 2026-09-03.

The decay is consequently $O(n_{\max}^{-2})$ along $n_{\max} \equiv 0$ and
$O(n_{\max}^{-1})$ generically --- a proved rate, replacing the earlier
statement that none was proven.  \emph{Proof sketch of the generic rate:}\
off the branch $|j - 2n_{\max}/3| = 1/3$, so
$\lambda_{2s} - 3 = \pm 2\sin(2\pi/3)\,\pi/(3n_{\max}) + O(n_{\max}^{-2})
= \pm\pi/(\sqrt{3}\,n_{\max}) + O(n_{\max}^{-2})$, which dominates the
$O(n_{\max}^{-2})$ $2p$-side term;\ dividing by the on-branch value gives
the $n_{\max}/(\sqrt{3}\,\pi)$ ratio.  (The on-branch closed form is pinned by
a test;\ this generic $O(n_{\max}^{-1})$ rate currently has no dedicated test.)  \emph{The mechanism previously printed here
--- ``spectral aliasing\ldots\ standing-wave reflections that differentially [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism]
shift eigenvalues in distinct angular momentum sectors'' --- is withdrawn:}
the $\ell$-blocks are disconnected components
(\texttt{tests/test\_paper1\_block\_spectrum.py}), so there is no $s/p$
degeneracy present to alias and no cross-sector reflection to shift it.  Both
selected eigenvalues approach $3 = 2 - 2\cos(2\pi/3)$ because the target node
is vertex~$2$ of a path in each block.  As $n_{\max}$ grows the two proxy
eigenvalues coincide (a path-graph coincidence), which does \emph{not}

\section{Geometric Phase Structure of the Lattice}
\label{sec:relativity}

The lattice carries a natural notion of parallel transport around closed loops
(plaquettes).  We examine whether this transport produces a nontrivial
geometric phase---a Berry holonomy~\cite{berry1984}---and find that the answer is negative for
the real-valued transition operators used here.  We then characterize the
\emph{log-holonomy}, which measures variation (a discrete gradient) of the edge-weight function,
and discuss what would be required for a genuine geometric phase.

\subsection{Flat Connection for Real Operators}

A \textbf{rectangular plaquette} on the lattice is constructed via radial
($T_\pm$) and azimuthal ($L_\pm$) operators:
\begin{equation}
|n, l, m\rangle \xrightarrow{T_+} |n+1, l, m\rangle \xrightarrow{L_+}
|n+1, l, m+1\rangle \xrightarrow{T_-} |n, l, m+1\rangle \xrightarrow{L_-}
|n, l, m\rangle.
\label{eq:plaquette}
\end{equation}
The Berry phase for this plaquette is the argument of the holonomy product:
\begin{equation}
\theta = \arg\!\bigl(
  \langle n\!+\!1,l,m|T_+|n,l,m\rangle\,
  \langle n\!+\!1,l,m\!+\!1|L_+|n\!+\!1,l,m\rangle\,
  \langle n,l,m\!+\!1|T_-|n\!+\!1,l,m\!+\!1\rangle\,
  \langle n,l,m|L_-|n,l,m\!+\!1\rangle
\bigr).
\label{eq:berry_arg}
\end{equation}
Each matrix element is a Biedenharn--Louck coupling coefficient.  For the
$SU(2)$ angular-momentum operators,
$\langle l,m\!+\!1|L_+|l,m\rangle = \sqrt{(l-m)(l+m+1)}$,
and for the $SU(1,1)$ radial operators,
$\langle n\!+\!1,l|T_+|n,l\rangle = \sqrt{(n-l)(n+l+1)/4}$.
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} All four factors are \textbf{real and positive}.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\theta = \arg(\text{positive real number}) = 0
\qquad\text{for every plaquette at every $(n,l,m)$.}
\label{eq:berry_zero}
\end{equation}
The lattice connection defined by these operators is \emph{flat}: it carries
zero Berry curvature.

\textbf{Erratum.}  An earlier version of this paper reported a fitted
exponent $k = 2.113 \pm 0.015$ for the Berry phase scaling.  That value was
a theoretical prediction placed as a placeholder pending numerical
validation; the accompanying figure was never generated (it remained a
\texttt{[Placeholder]}).  Subsequent computation confirms
$\theta = 0$ identically for all plaquettes, as shown above.  The claimed
$k = 2.113$ is therefore \textbf{retracted}.

\subsection{Log-Holonomy of Edge Weights}

Although the Berry phase vanishes, the lattice edge weights carry a
well-defined \emph{log-holonomy}---the logarithmic circulation around a
plaquette:
\begin{equation}
\Theta(n) = \ln w_{12} + \ln w_{23} - \ln w_{34} - \ln w_{41},
\label{eq:log_holonomy}
\end{equation}
where $w_{ij}$ are the edge weights of the \emph{topological-weight}
lattice (\texttt{GeometricLattice(topological\_weights=True)}); on the
binary production lattice and on the CG-magnitude construction of \S III
the $T$-edge weight is $m$-independent and the $L$-edge weight is
$n$-independent, so $\Theta \equiv 0$ there (2026-09-03).  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} For the
topological weights $w = 1/(n_1 n_2)$, the $T_\pm$ edges share the factor
$1/\bigl(n(n+1)\bigr)$ and cancel, leaving
\begin{equation}
\Theta(n) = \ln\!\frac{1}{(n+1)^2} - \ln\!\frac{1}{n^2}
           = -2\ln\!\frac{n+1}{n}
           \;\sim\; -\frac{2}{n}
           \qquad (n \gg 1).
\label{eq:log_holonomy_exact}
\end{equation}
Both results---$\theta = 0$ on every plaquette (with a phased-plaquette
control confirming the detector is not vacuous) and the exact
$\Theta(n)$ closed form with its $n^{-1}$ decay---are pinned by
\texttt{tests/test\_paper1\_geometric\_phase.py} (added 2026-09-01,
closing a coverage gap: the prior Berry-phase artifact was archived
with the retracted $k = 2.113$ claim).
The per-plaquette log-holonomy $\Theta(n) = -2\ln((n+1)/n)$
decays as $n^{-1}$ asymptotically (a closed form, so no fitted exponent or
$R^2$ is involved).  This measures the \textbf{variation of the weight function} (a first difference of its logarithm)
---how edge weights vary across the lattice---not a genuine geometric phase
in the Berry sense.

\subsection{Toward a Genuine Geometric Phase}

A nonzero Berry phase requires complex-valued transition amplitudes.
Possible extensions include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Not} a change of phase convention. Departing from the
  Condon--Shortley convention (which makes the $L_\pm$ matrix elements
  real and non-negative, as used above), e.g.\ by omitting its $(-1)^m$
  factor on $m \ge 0$, flips the sign of every $L_\pm$ matrix element
  between two $m \ge 0$ states (of every $L_\pm$ element, if the factor is
  dropped for all $m$);\ but such a change is a diagonal $\pm 1$ rephasing
  of the basis, and every closed loop visits each of its nodes twice
  (in and out), so every plaquette holonomy is unchanged and stays at
  $\theta = 0$. A convention change cannot produce a geometric phase
  (\emph{corrected 2026-09-28;} the item previously claimed discrete
  $\{0,\pi\}$ phases). Genuine routes are:
\item A $U(1)$ gauge extension---attaching phases $e^{i\theta_e}$ to
  the \emph{edges} (the transition operators) with nonzero net flux
  $\sum_{e \in \partial P} \theta_e \neq 0$ through some plaquette
  $P$---which would produce continuously varying curvature (phases
  that depend only on the node labels, $e^{i f(n,l,m)}$ at each
  endpoint, are a rephasing of the basis and give zero holonomy, as in
  the item above);
\item An adiabatic Berry connection from eigenstates of a
  parameter-dependent Hamiltonian (as in the hyperspherical $P_{\mu\nu}(R)$
  connection of Ref.~\cite{paper13}).
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{[OPEN]} Whether any such extension recovers a scaling exponent near $k \approx 2$
(the velocity-dependent kinematic factor $v^2 \propto n^{-2}$) remains an
open question for future work.


\section{Discussion: Algebraic versus Graph-Geometric Content}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results separate two kinds of content carried by the label lattice.

\subsection{The Two Kinds of Content}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Algebraic structure:} The operators $T_\pm, L_\pm$ and the number operator and Casimir built from them reproduce the \textit{unperturbed} hydrogen quantum-number structure exactly on interior shells (and the $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2-1$ is exact on every shell); the energies (Eq.~\ref{eq:rydberg}) are the imposed energy-shell map of $n$, not an output of the ladder operators (once Fock's kernel fixes the function, they are $E_n = -1/(2(C+1))$ of the Casimir $C = n^2-1$ on the labels). This is the exact aspect, and it acts on the discrete labels.

\item \textbf{Graph-geometric structure:} The graph Laplacian $L = D - A$ carries finite-truncation structure that is intrinsic to its disconnected two-dimensional grid blocks;\ it supplies no physical correction or perturbation to the hydrogen levels. The weighted degree matrix $D$ differs between angular momentum sectors at finite truncation (read before 2026-09-03 as differential connectivity [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism] lifting the $s/p$ degeneracy;\ withdrawn --- the sectors are disconnected components). On the optional topological-weight lattice the edge-weight log-holonomy decays as $n^{-1}$ (Section~\ref{sec:relativity}), measuring the variation (a discrete gradient) of that weight function;\ on the binary production lattice it vanishes.
\end{enumerate}

The separation is the point:\ the algebraic operators provide the exact quantum-number structure, while the graph topology introduces finite-size artifacts that must be controlled.

\subsection{Why Calibration Fails}

When we attempt to match the graph Laplacian's eigenvalues directly to physical energies, calibration fails. The eigenvalues are non-negative (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), but that alone is not the obstruction --- a negative scale such as $\kappa = -1/16$ fixes the sign and matches the extremal eigenvalue to $-\tfrac12$.

The obstruction is structural:\ the Laplacian's spectrum is the band structure of its disconnected two-dimensional grid blocks, lying in $[0, 8]$, and it carries neither absolute energies nor physical splittings between hydrogen levels (Paper~7).  The algebraic operators provide the quantum numbers, and the energies are the imposed energy-shell map of $n$;\ the graph Laplacian supplies no correction to them.

\subsection{The Absence of Fine Structure}

\textbf{[MEASURED]} A computational search (three checks) for the fine structure constant $\alpha \approx 1/137$ within the lattice geometry yields a null result. We examined:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Operator ratios:} \textbf{[PANEL-VERIFIED]} The spectral-norm gearing ratio
$\|L_+\|/\|T_+\| = 2.0$ \emph{exactly}---at every lattice cutoff
tested (and for every $N \geq 2$, since $L_+$ and $T_+$ are weighted shifts whose largest weights are $\sqrt{(N-1)N}$ and $\sqrt{(N-1)N}/2$;\ this argument is not separately test-backed)---while the Frobenius-norm ratio
converges to $2\sqrt{6}/3 = 1.632993\ldots$, its exact limit (algebraic closed form;\ the frozen \texttt{test\_trunk\_qa\_gearing.py} panel verifies only the monotone approach to $n_{\max}=30$, ratio $1.701$, not the limit) from
$\|L_+\|_F^2/\|T_+\|_F^2 = 4(N+2)(2N+1)/(3N^2-2) \to 8/3$ (corrected
2026-09-03 from ``drifts through $\approx 1.77$ without converging'':\ the
sequence is monotone decreasing and $1.77$ is a finite-$n_{\max}$ value, not a
non-limit)
(\texttt{tests/test\_trunk\_qa\_gearing.py}).  Neither encodes a
physical constant such as $137$ or $1/\alpha$: the spectral value is $2$,
and the Frobenius limit is the algebraic number $2\sqrt{6}/3$.  The commutator
$[T_+, L_+] = 0$, however, \emph{is} bit-exact.
\item \textbf{Commutator defects:} \textbf{[PANEL-VERIFIED]} The operators commute exactly, $[T_+, L_+] = 0$ (the radial and angular ladders act on independent labels), leaving no commutator defect in which a coupling constant could sit.
\item \textbf{Holonomy defects:} \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} The Berry phase is identically zero for real operators (Section~\ref{sec:relativity}); on the topological-weight lattice the log-holonomy decays as $n^{-1}$, reflecting the asymptotic flatness of that weight function.
\end{enumerate}

This null result is \textit{physically correct}. The fine structure constant $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar c)$ measures the strength of electromagnetic interactions---specifically, the coupling between electron and photon fields. Our lattice describes the \textit{kinematic} structure of the electron state space without electromagnetic interactions. The hydrogen spectrum $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ is independent of $\alpha$; fine structure appears only through:
\begin{align}
\text{Relativistic corrections:} \quad & \Delta E_{\text{rel}} \sim \alpha^2 \cdot E_n / n, \\
\text{Spin-orbit coupling:} \quad & \Delta E_{\text{so}} \sim \alpha^2 \cdot E_n \cdot l(l+1) / n^3, \\
\text{Lamb shift (QED):} \quad & \Delta E_{\text{Lamb}} \sim \alpha^5 \cdot mc^2.
\end{align}
These require photon degrees of freedom, which are absent from our single-particle lattice.

This clarifies the role of geometry: the lattice encodes \textit{kinematic} constraints (quantum-number labels, angular momentum structure) but not \textit{dynamical} couplings. \textbf{[OPEN]} (Speculation, untested:)\ fine structure would require additional photon degrees of freedom coupled to the electron labels, and in such a construction $\alpha$ would enter as a coupling strength supplied from outside the electron lattice;\ no such construction is built here, and the one attempt to derive $\alpha$ from a lattice ratio is recorded in \S\ref{sec:limitations} as superseded.

\subsection{Artifact vs.\ Physics: Drawing the Line}

The cutoff sweep separates discretization artifacts from the
structural (non-physical) content of the graph Laplacian, without being
evidence of convergence to any continuum operator. The $s/p$ splitting is
firmly in the artifact category:\ on the binary lattice it decays as
$n_{\max} \to \infty$ (not on the CG-weighted construction of \S III),
oscillating with the residue of $n_{\max}$ modulo $3$ (no aliasing
mechanism is involved;\ that reading was withdrawn 2026-09-03).

By contrast, the spectral bound $\lambda_{\max} \to 2 d_{\max} = 8$
(hence the extremal eigenvalue $\kappa\lambda_{\max} \to -1/2$; the graph
spectrum is \emph{not} a level-by-level Rydberg ladder,
\texttt{tests/test\_paper7\_graph\_convergence.py}) is a \emph{convergent}
property of the graph as $n_{\max} \to \infty$;\ the log-holonomy
$\Theta(n)$ of the topological-weight lattice (Section~\ref{sec:relativity})
is a closed-form function of the shell index $n$, not of $n_{\max}$, and
decays as $n^{-1}$.
They are structural content of the graph itself, not of $S^3$:\ the
bound $8 = 2 d_{\max}$ is the band edge of any large two-dimensional grid,
and the graph Laplacian does not converge to $\Delta_{S^3}$ (Paper~7;\
the ``survive the continuum limit'' reading printed here until
2026-09-25 is withdrawn).

This distinction is essential: the graph Laplacian does not generate
electromagnetic forces or the Lamb shift, and it does not carry the
\textit{kinematic} structure of the electron state space either. That
structure lives in the label set $(n,\ell,m)$ and its ladder operators
$T_\pm, L_\pm$ (with the $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2-1$ diagonal on the
labels); the graph Laplacian carries no $S^3$ or hydrogen spectral
content, only the structure of its disconnected two-dimensional grid
blocks. Finite
lattice effects must be understood as numerical artifacts, not
new physics.

\subsection{The Wavefunction as Distribution}

If quantum states are labelled by discrete nodes, what is the wavefunction $\psi(r)$? In this framework it is the standard object:\ a probability amplitude expanded in the basis states the nodes label, each node carrying one coefficient. A reading in which $\psi$ is a statistical distribution over lattice sites and the Schr\"odinger equation becomes a diffusion equation on the graph is \emph{not} available here:\ the graph Laplacian does not converge to $\Delta_{S^3}$ and its spectrum does not carry the Rydberg ladder (Paper~7;\ \S III), so diffusion on the graph is not hydrogen dynamics (reading withdrawn 2026-09-28).


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that the hydrogen state space, when labelled on a discrete lattice, carries two separable kinds of content:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Algebraic exactness:} \textbf{[MEASURED]} Transition operators reproduce the hydrogen quantum numbers on interior shells ($n < n_{\max}$;\ to $10^{-9}$ in floating point) and $\ell(\ell+1)$ on every shell;\ the $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2-1$ is exact on every shell;\ the energies are the imposed energy-shell map of $n$, with $p_0 = Z/n$ fixed by Fock's kernel eigenvalue (Paper~7), not an output of the ladder operators (once Fock's kernel fixes the function, it is $E_n = -1/(2(C+1))$ of the Casimir $C = n^2-1$ on the labels).
\item \textbf{Understood discretization artifacts:} \textbf{[INTERNAL THEOREM]} The graph Laplacian's node-amplitude proxy for the $s/p$ gap is closed form on $n_{\max} \equiv 0 \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)$ and is set by that residue rather than by proximity to a continuum limit (2026-09-03).  \textbf{[MEASURED]} It is large at finite $n_{\max}$ (up to $37\%$ at $n_{\max}=5$ on the binary production lattice, $15.6\%$ at $8$, $1.7\%$ at $10$), and is not spectral aliasing [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism] (withdrawn 2026-09-03: the $l$-blocks are disconnected and carry no degeneracy to alias) nor an emergent physical force. The proxy oscillates ($4.1\%$ at $n_{\max}=20$) and reads $0.39\%$ at $n_{\max}=30$ on the $n_{\max}\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ branch ($1.65\%$, $2.58\%$ at $28$, $29$) --- a proxy value, not a recovery of the exact Coulomb degeneracy [retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-aliasing-mechanism], which belongs to the continuum operator; on the CG-weighted construction of \S III the lift does \emph{not} decay ($129$, $68$, $84$ and $139\%$ at $n_{\max} = 8, 10, 15, 20$; \texttt{tests/test\_paper1\_block\_spectrum.py}), so the artifact-decay claim is scoped to the binary lattice, and both figures carry the conditioning caveat of \S III.
\item \textbf{Geometric phase structure:} \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} The Berry phase vanishes identically for the real-valued $SU(2)/SU(1,1)$ operators; on the optional topological-weight lattice the edge-weight log-holonomy decays as $\Theta \propto n^{-1}$, measuring the variation (a discrete gradient) of that weight function (it vanishes identically on the binary production lattice). Whether a $U(1)$ gauge extension can produce a genuine geometric phase with relativistic scaling remains an open question.
\item \textbf{Kinematic completeness:} \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The label set and its ladder operators encode the $\alpha$-independent single-electron quantum-number structure---the Coulomb quantum numbers and the angular momentum structure (the Berry phase being zero)---while cleanly separating finite-truncation artifacts from physical content.
\end{itemize}

These results support a discrete label-set reformulation of the hydrogen state space in which the algebraic operators carry the exact quantum-number structure on interior shells (the energies being the imposed energy-shell map of $n$) and the graph Laplacian introduces controlled, quantitatively understood finite-size artifacts. Critically, the graph Laplacian does not replace electromagnetic interactions or generate the Lamb shift; the kinematic structure of the electron lives in the label set and its ladder operators, while the graph Laplacian carries no $S^3$ or hydrogen spectral content.


\section{Limitations and Next Steps: Missing Photon Degrees of Freedom}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{What This Model Does Not Include:}

The paraboloid lattice presented here is explicitly a \textit{single-particle} model describing only the electron's quantum state manifold. It does not include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Photon degrees of freedom:} The electromagnetic field ($U(1)$ gauge symmetry) is absent.
\item \textbf{Electron-photon coupling:} There is no mechanism for radiative transitions or electromagnetic interactions.
\item \textbf{The fine structure constant $\alpha$:} As a pure electron-only model, $\alpha$ does not appear in it (the null search of \S\ref{sec:discussion}); $\alpha$ measures the strength of electron-photon coupling, which this model does not contain.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What would have to be added:}

Incorporating electromagnetic interactions would require photon degrees of freedom and electron--photon coupling rules on top of the label lattice;\ no such construction is built or tested in this paper.

\textbf{An earlier route, superseded.}  An earlier version of this section conjectured that $\alpha$ emerges as a ``symplectic impedance'' --- a ratio of action densities between the electron paraboloid and a helical photon fiber.  That route was tried:\ on the paraboloid lattice it reached $6.2\times10^{-5}$ relative error only with a \emph{fitted} helical pitch, and it has been superseded (Paper~2~\cite{companion_alpha} records the attempt and its supersession);\ it is not an open, unexplored route. \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The companion paper~\cite{companion_alpha} records a different, parameter-free numerical observation:\ $\alpha$ approximately satisfies (to $8.8\times10^{-8}$ relative;\ an Observation, not a law) the cubic
$\alpha^3 - K\alpha + 1 = 0$ with $K = \pi(B + F - \Delta)$ built from three
spectral quantities on $S^3$.  The combination rule is an Observation only, with no first-principles derivation;\ the companion paper's working hypothesis reads $\alpha$ as a projection constant, not derivable from any single principle.

\textbf{Outlook:}

This framework invites generalization. The hydrogen labels are those of $SO(4)$;\ what label sets describe molecules, nuclei, or quantum fields? The answer may lie in higher-dimensional lattices---label sets of larger symmetry groups.

\section{Mathematical Definitions}
\label{app:berry}

\subsection{Weighted Degree Matrix}

The weighted degree matrix $D$ is a diagonal matrix where each diagonal element represents the total connection strength of a node:
\begin{equation}
D_{ii} = \sum_{j} |A_{ij}|,
\end{equation}
where $A_{ij}$ is the adjacency matrix constructed from transition operators. For complex-valued operators, the absolute value ensures symmetric (undirected) edge weights. On the CG-magnitude construction the degrees are therefore non-integer (weighted sums of transition matrix elements);\ on the binary production lattice every edge weight is $1$ and the degrees are integer edge counts (Table~\ref{tab:degrees} caption).

\subsection{Berry Phase and Log-Holonomy}

For a plaquette (Eq.~\ref{eq:plaquette}), the Berry phase is
$\theta = \arg(\prod_i \langle s_i | \hat{U}_i | s_{i+1}\rangle)$.
As shown in Section~\ref{sec:relativity}, all four matrix elements are real
and positive, so $\theta = 0$ identically.

The log-holonomy (Eq.~\ref{eq:log_holonomy}) provides a nonzero measure of
weight-function variation.  For topological weights $w = 1/(n_1 n_2)$, the
exact result is $\Theta(n) = -2\ln\bigl((n+1)/n\bigr)$, decaying as
$n^{-1}$.


\section{Computational Details}

\subsection{Lattice Construction}

The lattice was constructed for $1 \leq n \leq n_{\max}$ with $n_{\max}$ from $5$ to $30$ (at $n_{\max} = 10$, 385 quantum states $|n, l, m\rangle$ with $0 \leq l < n$ and $-l \leq m \leq l$). Transition operators $T_\pm$ and $L_\pm$ were built from the standard $SU(2)$ and $SU(1,1)$ ladder matrix elements.

\subsection{Graph Laplacian Diagonalization}

The adjacency matrix $A$ was computed as the sum of absolute values of all transition operators:
\begin{equation}
A = |T_+| + |T_-| + |L_+| + |L_-|.
\end{equation}
This CG-magnitude construction is used only for the \S III comparison with the weighted lattice;\ its weighted degree matrix $D$ ranges from $0.71$ to $19.9$ with mean degree $12.0$ at $n_{\max} = 10$ (faithful rebuild, 2026-09-03; the figures printed earlier, $0.854$--$20.624$ and $12.42$, belong to the non-reproducing table of \S III).
