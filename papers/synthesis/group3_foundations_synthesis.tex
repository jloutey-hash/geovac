\begin{abstract}
The Geometric Vacuum (GeoVac) framework is a discretization scheme for
non-relativistic quantum mechanics on natural geometries, built on a
packed lattice of quantum-number labels.  For hydrogen the lattice's
nodes are exactly the labels of the unit-$S^3$ harmonics, on which
(minus) the continuous Laplace--Beltrami operator acts as the diagonal
$SO(4)$ Casimir at every cutoff;\ the lattice's edge Laplacian does \emph{not}
replace or converge to that operator (it splits into $n_{\max}$
disconnected $l$-blocks;\ corrected 2026-09-25).  This synthesis paper assembles the framework's foundational
arc---the packing construction, the discrete
graph and its dynamics, the conformal equivalence with the unit $S^3$,
the exchange constant taxonomy, the angular sparsity theorem, the
Bargmann--Segal lattice, and the universal/Coulomb partition, together
with the \emph{reconvergence} node where these threads cohere (two-body
selection rules from the tensor-product triple, the period
census, the Tannakian substrate and cosmic-Galois comparison homomorphism, and
the forced/free seam)---into a single coherent narrative.  The arc has a
dependency structure worth naming:\ a single taproot (the packing
construction, made physical by the Fock $S^3$ projection) fans out into
the framework's machinery, then reconverges at the periods, Tannakian,
and forced/free nodes, where the scattered structural findings cohere
into one statement about what the framework forces and what it consumes.  Four headline structural results frame the
program: \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} (a) the conformal chain \emph{from the
continuum operator onward} --- the unit-$S^3$ Laplace--Beltrami operator, its
integer spectrum (\textbf{[PRIOR ART]}, checked for $n \le 4$), and the energy-shell projection, under which the hydrogen
bound-state Hamiltonian is $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$ --- audited by a suite of 18
symbolic checks, with the non-definitional energy step (the $S^3$
Coulomb kernel's eigenvalue $2\pi^2/n$ forcing $p_0 = Z/n$) carried by
the exact kernel-eigenvalue test of Paper~7 ($n \le 4$;\ general $n$ is
Fock's);\ \textbf{[WALL]} the discrete Fock graph's edge
Laplacian does \emph{not} converge to that operator (it splits into
$n_{\max}$ disconnected $l$-blocks with an $n_{\max}$-dimensional
kernel);\ what is exact is its label set, on which the operator is the
diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir (the 18 checks
do not touch the graph-side step) (ten of the eighteen limiting-case,
definitional or non-discriminating rather than logically independent,
Paper~7); \textbf{[PRIOR ART mechanism;\ the explicit densities are
new]} (b) the angular sparsity of two-body matrix elements
depends only on $l_{\max}$ and is bit-identical across five distinct
central potentials, qualifying as a potential-independent theorem
about spherical-fermion bases; \textbf{[PANEL-VERIFIED]} (c) the three-dimensional isotropic
harmonic oscillator admits a discrete graph encoding on the Hardy
space $H^2(S^5)$ that is bit-exactly $\pi$-free in exact rational
arithmetic at every finite cutoff; \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} (d) the framework's results split
cleanly along an A/D partition---an algebra-only universal sector that
transfers free across central potentials, and a Dirac-operator-specific
sector containing the spectrum, the projection, and the
candidate fine-structure-constant combination of Paper~2~\cite{loutey_paper2_alpha}.  \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} A structural reading we make explicit in this synthesis is that
the framework's substrate-level scale-freedom---the packing diameter
$d_{0}$ cancelling out of every spectral quantity, and physical
scales entering only through the Fock-projection focal length
$p_{0}$---is the geometric expression of the same principle that
conformal field theory embodies at
the physics level (cf.\ the F-theorem of~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011}).  GeoVac provides a scale-free mathematical
substrate; CFT is a scale-free physics theory living on such a
substrate.  This is not a claim that GeoVac is a CFT; it is a
structural reflection consistent with (not evidenced by) Paper~50's
closed-form re-derivation of the continuum CFT-on-sphere free energies,
in which the truncation never enters (operator-algebra arc, outside the
foundations scope of this synthesis).  We present
GeoVac as a discretization framework that exploits natural geometry,
not as a new foundation for quantum mechanics: the discrete label
lattice and the continuous Laplace--Beltrami operator are dual descriptions
linked by an exact Peter--Weyl label identification, with Fock's
conformal projection linking the operator to the Schr\"odinger equation
(the bound-state Hamiltonian is $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$),
and the interpretive question of
which description is more fundamental is left open.  The framework's
concrete computational advantages---$O(V)$ sparsity, angular momentum
selection rules baked into the basis, bit-exactly rational squared
matrix elements on the Bargmann--Segal lattice, and the universal sparsity
bound---are independent of that interpretive question. (The integer
spectrum $n^2 - 1$ is a property of the continuum operator, not of the
graph, whose closed-form spectrum is bounded in $[0, 8]$.)

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ========================================================================

The standard formulation of non-relativistic quantum mechanics
presupposes a continuous configuration space and treats the
Schr\"odinger operator $-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^2 + V(r)$ as the
fundamental object.  For the hydrogen atom in particular, Fock showed
in 1935~\cite{fock1935} that the momentum-space Schr\"odinger equation
maps by stereographic projection onto the unit three-sphere $S^3$:\ the
bound states become the hyperspherical harmonics (the eigenfunctions of
the Laplace--Beltrami operator $\Delta_{S^3}$), and the bound-state
Hamiltonian becomes the function $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$ of that operator.  This observation has been developed extensively
by Bargmann~\cite{bargmann1936}, Bander and Itzykson~\cite{bander_itzykson1966},
Avery~\cite{avery_book1989}, Aquilanti and
co-workers~\cite{aquilanti_caligiana2002}, and others working in the
hyperspherical-momentum-space tradition, which uses it both for
analytic work and as a computational method (Sturmian and
hyperspherical-harmonic bases for atoms, molecules and reactive
scattering).  In that tradition the $S^3$ structure is a reformulation
of a known continuum problem, not a foundation in its own right.

The Geometric Vacuum (GeoVac) framework
\cite{loutey_paper0,loutey_paper1,loutey_paper7} takes a complementary
computational stance.  Starting from a packing construction on an
isotropic sphere, it builds a finite directed graph whose nodes are
quantum-number labels $(n, l, m)$ and whose edges are transition
amplitudes generated by the $SU(2) \otimes SU(1,1)$ ladder algebra of
hydrogen.  The label set is dimensionless and scale-invariant;
physical energies enter only through Fock's energy-shell constraint
$p_0^2 = -2E$, which acts as a stereographic focal length, with
$p_0 = Z/n$ forced by the eigenvalue of Fock's $S^3$ Coulomb kernel on
the shell labelled $n$ (not through the graph Laplacian, whose spectrum is a
grid band structure).  \textbf{[WALL]} This discrete construction does
not converge to the unit-$S^3$ Laplace--Beltrami operator:\ the graph Laplacian has no $l$-changing edges, so it splits into
$n_{\max}$ disconnected blocks, its saturation $\lambda_{\max} \to 8$ is
the band edge of two-dimensional grids, and the $s/p$ proxy decay
[retracted 2026-09-04: sp-splitting-as-convergence-evidence] tracks cutoff
divisibility ($\lambda_{2s} = 3$ exactly iff $n_{\max} \equiv 0 \bmod 3$;\
Paper~1 \S III).  What is exact is the label set:\ ``degeneracy recovery''
legitimately means that the nodes, $n^2$ per shell, are the
Gel'fand--Tsetlin labels of the Peter--Weyl truncation of $L^2(S^3)$,
on which $-\Delta_{S^3}$ is the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2 - 1$.  The
discrete labels and the continuous operator are linked by this exact
Peter--Weyl label identification, with Fock's conformal
projection linking the operator to the Schr\"odinger equation
($H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$), and we present them throughout as dual descriptions of
the same physics; the interpretive question of ontological priority is
explicitly left to the reader.

GeoVac differs from three nearby families of discretization schemes
in ways worth naming at the outset.  Lattice gauge theory discretizes
the spacetime continuum on a hypercubic grid; GeoVac discretizes the
quantum-number space and inherits the natural geometry of separable
quantum systems.  Tensor-network methods choose a basis adapted to
entanglement structure; GeoVac chooses a basis adapted to angular
momentum and the Fock conformal projection.  Algebraic quantum field
theory works at the level of operator algebras over Minkowski
spacetime; GeoVac is non-relativistic and operates on compact
Euclidean-signature manifolds.  The rhetoric rule that governs the
GeoVac papers (see the project's positioning statement) applies here:
GeoVac is not a new foundation for quantum mechanics, and the
synthesis presented in this paper does not assert ontological priority
of either the discrete or the continuum description.

\section{The Packing Construction and the Graph}
\label{sec:packing}
%% ========================================================================

GeoVac's discrete lattice is constructed by a packing argument that is
prior to the Schr\"odinger equation~\cite{loutey_paper0}.  Two axioms
suffice.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Axiom 1 (binary distinguishability).}  A minimum of
two distinguishable states is required to define a metric on an
information-bearing manifold.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Axiom 2 (geometric indifference).}  In the absence of
external constraints, the distribution of states on a homogeneous
surface maximizes entropy and is therefore isotropic.

\medskip

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} Given concentric isotropic shells in two dimensions with linearly
spaced radii and area-proportional state counting (choices the axioms
motivate but do not force, Paper~0), the angular state count on
shell~$k$ is $2k - 1$.
The shell capacities $\{1, 3, 5, 7, \ldots\}$ are isomorphic to the
spherical harmonic basis $Y_{lm}$ under the identification $l = k - 1$
and $m = -l, \ldots, +l$.  The angular quantum numbers $(l, m)$ and
the per-shell degeneracy $2l + 1$ thus emerge from the packing
geometry and these counting choices, without invoking the Schr\"odinger equation or the
angular momentum operators $\hat{L}^2$, $\hat{L}_z$.

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The principal quantum number~$n$ enters as a cumulative grouping of
the first $n$ shells, producing the count
$\sum_{k=1}^{n} (2k - 1) = n^2$.  Paper~0 is careful to flag that this
grouping is natural but not uniquely forced by the two axioms.
Similarly, the factor of two in the per-shell count, set by the binary
initialization (with the orientability of the compactified packing
manifold $S^2$ as a heuristic reading), corresponds in counting to spin
multiplicity, which the paper presents as a structural correspondence
rather than a derivation.  The strong claim is the angular sector $(l, m)$; the
other identifications are framed as structural correspondences.

\subsection*{From the packing to the graph}

The packed lattice acquires a graph structure when the
$SU(2) \otimes SU(1,1)$ ladder operators of the hydrogen dynamical
algebra~\cite{barut1967,biedenharn1981} are imposed as edges:
\begin{align}
L_\pm |n, l, m\rangle &\propto |n, l, m \pm 1\rangle, \\
T_\pm |n, l, m\rangle &\propto |n \pm 1, l, m\rangle.
\end{align}
The angular operators $L_\pm$ connect states within an $n$-shell along
$m$; the radial operators $T_\pm$ connect states between adjacent
$n$-shells at fixed $l$.  \textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} Paper~1~\cite{loutey_paper1} shows
that the operators built from the ladder generators reproduce the
Rydberg quantum numbers exactly on interior shells:\ the $SU(1,1)$
element $N = -2[T_+, T_-]$ returns $n$ for $n < n_{\max}$ (not on the
truncated top shell), and the $SU(2)$ Casimir returns $l(l+1)$;\ on
every shell the $SO(4)$ Casimir $\mathbf{L}^2 + \mathbf{A}^2 = n^2 - 1$
is exact on the labels.  The energies $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ are not an
output of the ladder operators or of the graph (Paper~1 imposes them as a
map from $n$);\ the Casimir yields them only through the function
$E_n = -Z^2/(2(C+1))$, $C = n^2-1$, that Fock's kernel fixes (Paper~7);\ in Paper~7's continuum chain the eigenvalue $2\pi^2/n$
of Fock's $S^3$ Coulomb kernel forces $p_0 = Z/n$, and the energy shell
$p_0^2 = -2E$ then gives $E_n$.  At the algebraic level the discrete and continuous
descriptions therefore agree on the quantum-number skeleton, while the
graph Laplacian's node-amplitude proxy for the $s/p$ gap is large at finite
cutoff (up to $37\%$ at $n_{\max} = 5$ on the binary lattice;\ $0.39\%$ at
$n_{\max} = 30$, which sits on the $n_{\max} \equiv 0 \bmod 3$ branch, against
$1.65\%$ and $2.58\%$ at $28$ and $29$; the $\ell$-blocks are disconnected,
so this is a proxy and not a spectral gap --- Paper~1 \S III).

\subsection*{The kinetic constant $\kappa = -1/16$}

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} A second constant attached to the packing graph is the kinetic
constant $\kappa = -1/16$.  Its role is narrow:\ it matches the graph's
extremal eigenvalue (a band edge, $\lambda_{\max} \to 8$) to the
hydrogen ground state $-\tfrac12$.  It is not the energy unit of the
Rydberg ladder and does not map the graph spectrum onto it:\ the
Rydberg levels come from Fock's continuum projection, with no role for
$\kappa$:\ the eigenvalue $2\pi^2/n$ of the $S^3$ Coulomb kernel on the
shell labelled by $n$ forces the focal length $p_0 = Z/n$, and the
energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$ then gives $E_n$ (Paper~7;\ the Casimir
$C = n^2 - 1$ alone does not fix the function, but once Fock's kernel
fixes it, $E_n = -Z^2/(2(C+1))$ on the labels).  Recent analysis~\cite{loutey_paper18, loutey_paper7} has
clarified its status:\ the value $\kappa = -1/16$ \emph{coincides} with
a geometric quantity computed independently of any energy calibration.
The squared coupling between adjacent $n$-shells in the Gegenbauer
eigenbasis is $c^2(n, l) = (1/4)[1 - l(l+1)/(n(n+1))]$ (corrected
2026-09-03 from a printed $1/16$ prefactor; the Chebyshev amplitude is
$1/2$), giving $c^2(n, 0) = 1/4$ for $l = 0$; the quantity that
coincides with $1/16$ is the fourth power of the inverse conformal factor, $1/\Omega^4(0)$,
equivalently $c^2(n,0)/4$.  That the energy-calibration constant
$\kappa$ \emph{equals} this geometric $1/16$ is recorded as an
\emph{Observation}, not a derivation:\ the geometric value is rigid,
but no mechanism is known that derives $\kappa$'s calibration role from
it (Paper~7~\S III).  Paper~18 accordingly lists $\kappa$ beside the
\emph{conformal} tier's energy shell $p_0$ only as a matched scale:\ it
is the energy shell, not $\kappa$, that carries the spectrum into atomic
units.

\subsection*{Status of the packing-versus-physics question}

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The discrete lattice is built by the packing
construction, before any reference to the Coulomb potential or the
Schr\"odinger equation, though its radius and counting choices are
motivated rather than forced (Paper~0).  It matches known atomic
structure---the Rydberg quantum-number labels and the chemical
periodicity of multi-electron atoms (Paper~16~\cite{loutey_paper16},
outside the foundations group).  We present this as the
\emph{structural fit} between the packing construction and known
atomic structure.  The synthesis paper does not argue for the
ontological priority of either description; the dual-description
framing of Paper~7 governs the discussion.

\section{The $S^3$ Conformal Equivalence}
\label{sec:s3}
%% ========================================================================

The theoretical foundation of GeoVac is the conformal equivalence,
established in Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7}, between the \emph{continuum}
unit-$S^3$ Laplace--Beltrami operator and the hydrogen Schr\"odinger
problem, carried by the Fock projection (the bound-state Hamiltonian is
$H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$).  The
discrete Fock graph's role is its label set, on which minus that
operator is the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir exactly;\ that the graph's edge Laplacian
has that operator as its continuum limit is \emph{false} (it splits
into $n_{\max}$ disconnected $l$-blocks;\ corrected 2026-09-04 from
``established'' and 2026-09-25 from ``unestablished'').  This section
states the result and explains its operational role.

\subsection*{The Fock projection and the chordal distance identity}

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} Fock's 1935 construction~\cite{fock1935} writes the momentum-space
hydrogenic (nuclear charge $Z$) Schr\"odinger equation
\begin{equation}
\left[ \frac{p^2}{2} - E \right] \varphi(\mathbf{p})
- \frac{Z}{2\pi^2} \int \frac{\varphi(\mathbf{p}')}{|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}'|^2}\,
\mathrm{d}^3 p' = 0
\end{equation}
and applies the stereographic projection
\begin{equation}
u_i = \frac{2 p_0 \, p_i}{p^2 + p_0^2}, \quad
u_4 = \frac{p^2 - p_0^2}{p^2 + p_0^2}, \quad
p_0 = \sqrt{-2 E},
\end{equation}
which maps $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ onto the unit three-sphere
$\mathbf{u} \in S^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$.  The Coulomb kernel
$1/|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}'|^2$ becomes the inverse-square chordal
distance on $S^3$, and with $\varphi = \Omega^2\Psi$ the integral equation
reduces to the $S^3$ integral equation
\begin{equation}
p_0\, \Psi(\boldsymbol{\omega}) = \frac{Z}{2\pi^2}\int_{S^3}
\frac{\Psi(\boldsymbol{\omega}')}{|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}'|^2}\,
\mathrm{d}\Omega'
\end{equation}
(Paper~7).  The kernel is zonal, so it is diagonal on the $S^3$ harmonics;
on the degree-$(n-1)$ harmonics its eigenvalue is $2\pi^2/n$ (Fock 1935,
an instance of Funk--Hecke; tested exactly for $n \le 4$ in Paper~7), and
this is what forces the focal length $p_0 = Z/n$ and hence
$E_n = -Z^2/(2n^2)$.  The Laplace--Beltrami operator carries the same
content in operator form:\ the degree-$(n-1)$ harmonics are the
eigenfunctions of $-\Delta_{S^3}$ with eigenvalue $C = n^2 - 1$ (the
$SO(4)$ Casimir;\ an integer spectrum fixed by the round unit-sphere
geometry, the metric and not merely the topology, Paper~7), so the
kernel acts as $2\pi^2(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1/2}$ and the bound-state
Hamiltonian is the function $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$,
$E_n = -Z^2/(2(C+1))$.  This is the precise sense in which Fock's
projection makes $\Delta_{S^3}$ equivalent to the Schr\"odinger problem
(the standard ``free motion on $S^3$'' reading).  The kernel is not the
Green's function of $\Delta_{S^3}$ (that behaves like $1/d$, with
eigenvalues $\propto 1/(n^2-1)$);\ it is the restriction to $S^3$ of the
Green's function of the $\mathbb{R}^4$ Laplacian (Paper~7).

\subsection*{The 18 symbolic checks}

\textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7} verifies the conformal-equivalence chain
through a suite of 18 symbolic checks implemented in \texttt{sympy}
(Paper~7 flags ten of them as limiting-case, definitional or
non-discriminating rather than logically independent theorems).
The checks cover the stereographic projection formulas, the unit
sphere constraint, the chordal distance identity, the conformal factor
identity, the conformal Laplacian transformation law, the integer
eigenvalue spectrum, and the (definitional) eigenvalue-to-energy
bookkeeping of the energy-shell projection from $S^3$ to
$\mathbb{R}^3$.  These tests live in
\texttt{tests/test\_fock\_projection.py} and
\texttt{tests/test\_fock\_laplacian.py} in the GeoVac repository and
are run as topological integrity checks before any release.  The
non-definitional part of the energy step is carried separately by
\texttt{tests/test\_paper7\_fock\_kernel.py}:\ in exact arithmetic, the
eigenvalue $2\pi^2/n$ of Fock's $S^3$ Coulomb kernel on the
degree-$(n-1)$ harmonics (every $l < n$ for $n \le 4$, one real
harmonic per $l$;\ \textbf{[PRIOR ART]} the eigenvalue holds for every
$n$, Fock 1935 / Funk--Hecke), the resulting $p_0 = Z/n$ with $E_n$
never an input, and the identification of the Fock harmonics with the

\subsection*{Scale-freedom of the substrate and its reflection in conformal field theory}
\label{sec:cft_reflection}

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} The separation between dimensionless topology and dimensional projection
described above is more than a computational convenience.  It is the
geometric expression of a principle that physics encounters in another
guise:\ conformal field theory.

A conformal field theory is a quantum field theory whose action and
correlators are invariant under rescaling of the underlying coordinates,
together with the larger conformal group $SO(d, 2)$ in $d$ spacetime
dimensions.  Operationally:\ a CFT has no preferred length scale, and
its observables transform predictably under conformal
transformations.  The standard examples include free massless scalars
and fermions on round $S^d$, where the partition functions
$Z_{S^d}^{\text{CFT}}$ are computable in closed form and reproduce
universal coefficients (e.g.\ the F-theorem constant
$F_{S^3}$~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011}).

GeoVac's substrate exhibits an analogous property at the
\emph{geometric / mathematical} layer, prior to physics.  The packing
construction (Paper~0~\cite{loutey_paper0}) takes a circle of diameter
$d_0$ (the fundamental scale, with no specific physical value
assigned) and outputs a graph whose nodes carry the labels of the eigenspaces of the
continuum $S^3$ operator (the integer eigenvalues $n^2 - 1$ belong
to that continuum operator, carried on the labels as the diagonal
$SO(4)$ Casimir; the graph spectrum is not a level-by-level
copy of it, Paper~7), the
multiplicities are integers, the Wigner-symbol couplings are exact
square roots of rationals (their squares rational), and the packing
diameter $d_0$ \emph{cancels out} of every
spectral quantity.  Shrink $d_0$ to the Planck length, grow it to a
galactic radius, set it to anything in between:\ the substrate is the
same.  The labels that come out of the packing are atomic quantum
numbers in interpretation, but they are not atomic-scale in
construction.  The packing produces a scale-free structural skeleton.

The Fock projection of Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7} is a
\emph{conformal map}:\ stereographic projection from $S^3$ to
$\mathbb{R}^3$ preserves angles but rescales lengths by a
position-dependent conformal factor $\Omega(\mathbf{p}) = 2 p_0 /
(p^2 + p_0^2)$.  Physical scales enter the framework through exactly
this factor, in the form of the focal-length parameter $p_0$.  The
scale-free substrate becomes scale-bearing physics only when a specific
$p_0$ is supplied (e.g.\ $p_0 = Z/n$ for hydrogenic atomic levels).
Different choices of $p_0$ correspond to different physical
interpretations of the same combinatorial skeleton; the substrate is
agnostic to the choice.

This is structurally the same principle that CFT scale-invariance
embodies.  A CFT is a physics theory with no preferred length scale at
the level of its action and observables; GeoVac's label set, with the
matrix elements built on it, is a mathematical object with no preferred
length scale.  CFT scale-invariance is the physics side of the
principle; the dimensionless label set is the substrate side.
The two converge structurally in that they realise the same
property---scale-freedom---at different layers of description, and the
Fock projection that connects them is the conformal map that
implements the scale-free-to-scale-bearing transition.

This reading is not a claim that GeoVac \emph{is} a CFT, nor that the
discrete graph derives CFT scale-invariance.  Those would overshoot:\
GeoVac provides a geometric / spectral substrate, and a CFT is a
quantum field theory living on a substrate.  Paper~50 (operator-algebra
arc, outside the foundations scope of this synthesis) evaluates the
standard zeta-regularised CFT-on-sphere free energies of the conformally
coupled scalar and Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac on $S^3$ and $S^5$ in closed
Hurwitz form from the full continuum spectra --- a re-derivation of known
continuum values in which the discrete truncation never enters, so it is
not evidence from the discrete substrate.  It is merely consistent with
the substrate-side scale-freedom established here; no mechanism is claimed.
The substrate is the right kind of mathematical object to host
scale-free physics, which is a reading of the parallel, not an
explanation of it.

\section{The Universal/Coulomb Partition}
\label{sec:partition}

\textbf{[OBSERVATION]} Paper~31~\cite{loutey_paper31} states the cleanest single
articulation of what GeoVac claims as universal versus what it claims
as potential-tied.  The framework is read as a discrete spectral
triple $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{H}, D)$ in the sense of
Connes~\cite{connes_book1994} and the graph-spectral-triple lineage of
Marcolli and van Suijlekom~\cite{marcolli_vs2014, perez_sanchez2024}:
$\mathcal{A}$ is an algebra of functions on the Fock-projected
discrete graph, $\mathcal{H}$ is a Hilbert space of scalar and spinor
states, and $D$ is a Dirac operator carrying the
Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum on $S^3$~\cite{camporesi_higuchi1996}.
One caution on which $D$:\ the diagonal spectral form $D_{\mathrm{GV}}$
(the Camporesi--Higuchi eigenvalues placed on the diagonal of the
$|n, \kappa, m_j\rangle$ basis) commutes with $\pi(\mathcal{A})$, which is
diagonal in the same basis, so $[D_{\mathrm{GV}}, \pi(f)] = 0$ and that
triple carries no metric.  The metric, and with it the ``Dirac-dependent
sector'' below, rests on the truthful, non-diagonal Camporesi--Higuchi
Dirac operator of Paper~38~\cite{loutey_paper38}, not on the diagonal
form.  (Paper~38's convergence theorem itself
is metrized by the translation seminorm;\ convergence in the Dirac
seminorm is open there.)

\subsection*{The A/D split}

The structural decomposition reads:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Universal sector ($\mathcal{A}$-only).}  Results that
depend only on the algebra $\mathcal{A}$ and the angular structure of
the basis, and \emph{not} on the specific choice of Dirac operator.
These transfer free across central potentials.  Examples:
\begin{itemize}
\item Angular sparsity (Paper~22): ERI density $D(l_{\max})$ depends
only on the spherical-harmonic angular factorization.
\item Composed scaling (Paper~14, group~4): the
exactly $Q$-linear Pauli-term count of composed-geometry qubit
encodings ($N_{\mathrm{Pauli}} = \gvq{composed_coeff}{27.90} \times Q$ for main-group molecules at fixed basis)
depends on the composed block decomposition and, within blocks, the
Gaunt selection rules.
\item Selection rules: $\Delta l = \pm 1$, $\Delta m = 0, \pm 1$ for
dipole transitions; the $3j$-triangle inequality at every vertex of
the two-body integral.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Potential-specific sector ($D$-dependent).}  Results
that depend on the specific spectrum and matrix elements of the Dirac
operator $D$, and therefore on the specific central potential.  These
require per-system rederivation.  Examples:
\begin{itemize}
\item The spectrum itself: $n^2 - 1$ for Coulomb, $N + 3/2$ for HO.
\item The matched scale $\kappa = -1/16$ (Coulomb-specific; no analog
on the Bargmann lattice;\ it matches only the graph's band edge, the
energies entering through the energy shell).
\item Transcendental content: $\pi$ for Coulomb via the $k = 0$
volume-slot measure, $\pi$-free for
HO.
\item Rigidity theorems: Fock rigidity (Paper~23) pins the Coulomb
geometry to $S^3$; HO rigidity (Paper~24;\ conditional on the imported
$SU(3)$-closure converse) pins the HO geometry to
$H^2(S^5)$ with $(N, 0)$ symmetric irreps.
\item The candidate fine-structure-constant combination
$K = \pi(B + F - \Delta)$ of Paper~2 (an Observation):\ lives in the
Coulomb-specific sector through the volume-slot $\pi$ on $S^3$
(Paper~31);\ of its three ingredients only $\Delta$ uses the Dirac
degeneracy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Why the partition matters operationally}

The partition organizes Papers 22, 23, 24, and 25 into a single
conceptual structure.  Paper~22's angular sparsity theorem lives in
the universal sector and transfers free between hydrogen and the
deuteron; Paper~23's nuclear shell model qubit Hamiltonians inherit
the angular sparsity automatically but require a fresh per-potential
analysis of the Dirac sector.  Paper~24's HO rigidity and the
$\pi$-free certificate live in the potential-specific sector and
explicitly do \emph{not} transfer to the Coulomb side---the
Bargmann-style first-order operator has no analog on $S^3$.  Paper~25
(group~5, QED/gauge) on the SU(2) Wilson lattice gauge structure of the
Hopf graph also lives in the potential-specific sector as far as Wilson
gauge \emph{with natural matter coupling} is concerned: that depends on
the parallelizability of $S^3 = SU(2)$, which has no analog on $S^5$
(pure-gauge $SU(3)$ is well defined on $S^5$;\ natural $(N,0)$ matter
there needs a Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner).

The operational utility of the partition is that anyone porting GeoVac
to a new system can read off, from the universal sector, what
structural properties they inherit for free, and from the
potential-specific sector, what calibrations they need to derive
afresh.  For nuclear shell models, gauge-theoretic extensions, and
oscillator-based quantum dots, the universal sector provides angular
sparsity and composed scaling automatically; the potential-specific
sector has to be redone for each system.

\subsection*{Status as a spectral-triple-language reformulation}

Paper~31 is careful to frame the partition as a structural
reformulation, not a new theorem.  The angular sparsity theorem

of NCG and spectral triples because that language has the structural
vocabulary to articulate the divide; the underlying physics content
is unchanged.

\section{Open Questions and Outlook}
\label{sec:open}
%% ========================================================================

The foundations arc as catalogued here leaves five named structural
questions open.

\subsection*{The $\alpha$ combination rule}

Paper~2~\cite{loutey_paper2_alpha} (group~5, QED/gauge) presents the
candidate combination
\begin{equation}
K = \pi (B + F - \Delta), \qquad K \stackrel{?}{=} \alpha^{-1},
\end{equation}
where $B = 42$ is a finite Casimir trace at the truncation $n_{\max} = 3$,
$F = \zeta_R(2) = \pi^2/6$ is the Fock-degeneracy Dirichlet series at
the packing exponent $d_{\max} = 4$, and $\Delta = 1/40$ is the
inverse Dirac mode count $g_3^{\text{Dirac}}(S^3) = 2(n+1)(n+2)|_{n=3}$.
Each ingredient now has a structurally identified spectral home;
twelve mechanisms have been eliminated as candidate single-principle
derivations of the sum~\cite{loutey_paper2_alpha}.  The combination rule
itself remains an \emph{Observation} at the rule level (never a
conjecture or derivation, per the project's hard rule):\ there is no
first-principles derivation explaining why the additive combination of
three objects with spectral homes of distinct type approximately equals
$\alpha^{-1}$ (the plain sum $K$ matches $\alpha^{-1}$ to
$4.8\times10^{-7}$ relative; the $8.8 \times 10^{-8}$ agreement is that
of Paper~2's self-consistency root $\alpha^{-1}+\alpha^{2}=K$).  The synthesis treats this as an open structural
question and does not assert priority for the observed identification.

\subsection*{The second packing axiom (W3)}

\textbf{[OPEN]} The packing construction of Paper~0 yields the angular sector
$(l, m)$ and provides natural correspondences for $n$ and spin
multiplicity, but it does not produce calibration data such as Yukawa
couplings or the number of fermion generations.  These appear in the
exchange-constant taxonomy as Layer-2 inner-factor input data: the
framework consumes them rather than deriving them.  The open question
labeled W3 in the project's internal taxonomy is whether a second
packing-axiom-like construction generates calibration data from a
similarly minimal principle.  A 2026 sprint surveying candidates in the
spectral-zeta / master-Mellin-engine ring returned uniformly negative
results across PMNS angles, lepton masses, and a $\sim 21{,}000$-form
mechanical basis~\cite{loutey_paper34}.  The second-packing-axiom
question is open and is structurally distinct from ``find a packing on
a different geometry''---the latter has been answered for the HO via
the Bargmann--Segal lattice without addressing W3.

\subsection*{Cross-manifold extensions}

The Coulomb and HO discretizations live on $S^3$ and the Hardy sector
of $S^5$ respectively.  \textbf{[OPEN]} A natural question is whether a coupled system
(electronic plus nuclear, or two distinct potential regimes within one
molecule) admits a tensor-product spectral triple of the form
$\mathcal{T}_{S^3} \otimes \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Hardy}(S^5)}$.  Within
the foundations arc, Paper~24's Coulomb/HO asymmetry (five standing layers)
documents structural obstructions to such a construction at the
NCG-framework level.  The operator-algebra arc (Group~1) has formally
named this as a frontier-of-field open problem; we are not aware of a
construction that supports this cross-manifold tensor product.

\subsection*{The multi-focal composition wall}

\textbf{[OPEN]} The framework's composition rules for multiple Fock-style projections
constitute an active research direction.  The internal taxonomy
labels three sub-walls: W1 (within-framework sub-walls:
cross-register coordinate operators, magnetization-distribution
operators, frozen-core cross-center screening), W2 (multi-loop UV/IR
composition and cross-manifold extensions), and W3 (inner-factor
calibration data, discussed above).  W1c has been built without
closing the chemistry wall it targeted (the W1d/W1e residual, Paper~32);\
W1a/b are architectural gaps; W2a (multi-loop renormalization) remains open at the
field-theory frontier of the operator-algebra arc.  None of these
walls falsify the foundations as presented in this synthesis; they
sharpen the open-question boundary of the framework.

\subsection*{The elliptic layer of the cosmic Galois}

\textbf{[OPEN]} Paper~56's cosmic-Galois comparison homomorphism lands in the mixed-Tate (genus-zero) group
$\mathcal{G}_4$;\ the elliptic (genus-one) layer it reaches for---the
Hain--Brown mixed-elliptic-motive target over $M_{1,1}$---came back negative,
because GeoVac's periods are $\mathbb{Q}(i)$-CM-pure rather than generically
modular.  Paper~59 now instantiates that layer with a concrete, irreducible
genus-one period (the three-center integral's elliptic Bessel moment on the
Legendre/$\Gamma(2)$ family, descending to $X_0(2)$), carrying the same
$\mathbb{Q}(i)$ arithmetic at the \emph{family} level ($\theta_3^2$, the
$\mathbb{Z}[i]$ theta series) by level-forcing rather than spin-forcing---%
convergent with the Dirac route, not common-cause.  The open question is whether
any GeoVac observable reaches the \emph{generic} (non-CM, full-$SL_2$)
Hain--Brown locus---a lift that would unify the genus-zero and genus-one layers
(the Hain--Brown mixed-elliptic completion).  On the physical contour the two
layers demonstrably meet at the pure-Tate cusp;\ no natural comparison map has
been exhibited, and the measured seam test ($\beta(2)$ in the physical value)
is negative at clean heights---a lift beyond convergence is unestablished.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ========================================================================

The Group~3 foundations arc of GeoVac establishes a small set of
structural results that together provide a coherent discretization of
non-relativistic quantum mechanics on natural geometries.
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC PROOF]} The conformal chain from the unit-$S^3$
Laplace--Beltrami operator onward is audited symbolically
(Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7}:\ Fock's projection makes the bound-state
Hamiltonian $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$, its non-definitional
kernel-eigenvalue step tested exactly for $n \le 4$, general $n$
Fock's); \textbf{[WALL]} the discrete Fock
graph's edge Laplacian does \emph{not} converge to that operator (it
splits into $n_{\max}$ disconnected $l$-blocks with an
$n_{\max}$-dimensional kernel), while the label set is exact, carrying
$-\Delta_{S^3}$ as the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2 - 1$;\ and
\textbf{[INTERNAL THEOREM]} the state-space GH convergence of the spinor
spectral truncations---the commutative $S^3$ factor, on the true
Camporesi--Higuchi window (the almost-commutative extension is
\textbf{[OPEN]})---is proved in Paper~38~\cite{loutey_paper38}:\ the
bound and $\gamma_{n_{\max}} \to 0$ are proved (Paper~38, main theorem:\ the
Fej\'er moment is $(4/\pi)\log n_{\max}/n_{\max}$, \textbf{[INTERNAL THEOREM +
MEASURED]};\ a tapered amplitude gives a \textbf{[MEASURED]} $O(1/n_{\max})$); \textbf{[PRIOR ART mechanism;\ explicit
densities new]} the angular sparsity of two-body matrix elements is potential-independent
(Paper~22~\cite{loutey_paper22}); the 3D harmonic oscillator admits a
bit-exactly $\pi$-free discrete encoding on $H^2(S^5)$
(Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}); \textbf{[OBSERVATION]} the framework's results decompose
cleanly into an algebra-only universal sector and a Dirac-dependent
potential-specific sector (Paper~31~\cite{loutey_paper31}); the
exchange-constant taxonomy of Paper~18~\cite{loutey_paper18} catalogues
where transcendentals enter physical observables and identifies a
\textbf{[CONDITIONAL]} master Mellin engine at three operator orders as the generator of the
$\pi$-content of the projection chains case-checked so far (Paper~32~\cite{loutey_paper32}'s
case-exhaustion theorem, over Paper~34 \S III.1--15, with its
(i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) leg resting on empirical verification); the
packing axiom of
Paper~0~\cite{loutey_paper0} yields the angular sector geometrically
(its structural choices motivated, not forced);
the spectral graph theory of Paper~1~\cite{loutey_paper1} and the
sparse $O(V)$ time propagation of Paper~6~\cite{loutey_paper6}
(archived; demonstrated on the graph model, not physical hydrogen)
provide the computational machinery.

The framework's concrete advantages are independent of the interpretive
question of which description---discrete or continuous---is more
fundamental.  $O(V)$ sparsity, angular momentum selection rules baked
into the basis, bit-exactly rational squared matrix elements on the
Bargmann--Segal lattice, and the universal sparsity bound at fixed
$l_{\max}$ are statements about the discrete label set and the basis it
indexes, not about the graph Laplacian (the integer spectrum $n^2 - 1$
belongs to the continuum operator, carried exactly on the labels as the
diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir, while the graph Laplacian's own spectrum is
closed form, bounded in $[0, 8]$, and does not converge to it ---
Paper~0~\S VI, Paper~7); their continuum counterparts are theorems about
the Laplace--Beltrami operator or the Euler operator on the corresponding
natural manifold, and consistency between the two is guaranteed by the
label-set/Casimir correspondence together with Fock's conformal
equivalence.
The discrete label lattice and the continuous operator are dual
descriptions linked by an exact Peter--Weyl label identification, with
Fock's conformal projection linking the operator to the
Schr\"odinger equation ($H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$), and the interpretive question is left open.

The open questions catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:open} are
substantive but do not destabilize the structural results of the
foundations arc.  The $\alpha$ combination rule remains an Observation;
the second-packing-axiom question is open; no construction for the
cross-manifold extensions is known at the NCG-framework level; the multi-focal composition
wall is an active research frontier; and the elliptic (genus-one) layer of the
cosmic Galois is instantiated but not lifted---the generic Hain--Brown locus
stays open.  Of the composition walls, W2a and W2b sit at the frontier
of the spectral-action program, while W1a--W1c are GeoVac-internal
(Paper~32~\cite{loutey_paper32});\ none is a foundational concern.
