\begin{abstract}
The Geometric Vacuum (GeoVac) framework labels the hydrogen bound
states by the harmonics of the unit three-sphere $S^3$ via Fock's
conformal projection.  This synthesis assembles the five Group~5
papers---gauge structure, the vector-photon vertex and the periods of
Dirac spectral sums on $S^3$,
the fine-structure-constant observation,
and a spectral-action gravity sketch---into one narrative around a single
organizing distinction:\ \emph{$U(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wilson
structures can be built on the graph, the choice of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ being
natural because $S^3 = \mathrm{SU}(2)$, while the couplings are
calibration/observation, not derived}.  On the scalar label graph the
plaquettes (the 4-cycles) form a cycle basis, so $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wilson
theory and its maximal-torus restriction, compact $U(1)$, are the exactly
solvable two-dimensional theories, whose area law holds by factorization
and carries no confinement information; on the cross-$\ell$ Dirac Rule-B
graph compact $U(1)$ does not factorize and is compatible with compact
electrodynamics under seven standard diagnostics, none of which
discriminates a dimension~\cite{loutey_paper30}; the natural gauge reach is
$U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ (an organizing reading,
not a derivation), with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
entering only as a pure-gauge theory whose natural matter coupling is
obstructed.  Of eight QED selection rules the scalar graph keeps one
(triangle sparsity);\ the physical vector-photon vertex, computed exactly
from $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ representation theory, keeps triangle sparsity
and $\Delta m$, satisfies the Ward--Takahashi identity, couples the
ground state, and does not preserve the $1{+}6{+}1$ partition of the
vertex model~\cite{loutey_paper33};\ the continuum $S^3$ Dirac spectral
sums carry odd zeta values ($\zeta(3)$, $\zeta(5)$), the level-4 values
Catalan~$G$ and $\beta(4)$ when the spectrum is restricted to one parity
class, and, in the vertex model's three-loop chain sum, the depth-2
period $\zeta(5,3)$, through a depth-filtered period
tower~\cite{loutey_paper28}.  \gvc{k-combination-rule}{The fine-structure constant approximately satisfies (to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ relative; an Observation) the
cubic $\alpha^3-K\alpha+1=0$ with $K=\pi(B+F-\Delta)$ assembled from
three spectral data of the unit $S^3$, each with an independently
established spectral home; this
is recorded strictly as an \textbf{Observation} (agreement
$8.8\times10^{-8}$, twelve single-mechanism derivations eliminated),
never as a derivation~\cite{loutey_paper2}}; in a look-elsewhere search
of the same kind the expected chance count at this precision is about
ten.  The gravity paper gives a convergence theorem for a discrete
replica derivative on the spinor cone (one standard ingredient argued
rather than proved), and records the two-term spectral action of the
round sphere (Chamseddine--Connes; an alternative proof there) and a
Newton constant obtained by two routes, whose agreement (at leading
order in $\Lambda$ on the black-hole cigar) is the induced-gravity statement of Susskind--Uglum and
Frolov--Fursaev~\cite{loutey_paper51}.  The discipline throughout matches the
rest of the corpus:\ every load-bearing claim wears its tier, the
structural results are foregrounded, and the couplings are positioned as
the free, calibration side of the construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: natural structure, free couplings}
%% ========================================================================

The GeoVac program builds its discrete hydrogen lattice on the labels
$(n,l,m)$ of the Laplace--Beltrami operator on the unit
three-sphere~$S^3$, on which that operator acts as the diagonal $SO(4)$
Casimir;\ the operator is related to the Schr\"odinger equation by
Fock's 1935 conformal projection~\cite{loutey_paper7}.  (\gvc{graph-s3-wall}{The lattice's
graph Laplacian is not equivalent to it and does not converge to it, Paper~7}.)  The Group~5 arc
asks what the \emph{same} spectral geometry says about the rest of
electromagnetism, the gauge sector, and---most speculatively---gravity.
A single distinction organizes the answer, and it is the through-line of
this synthesis.

\emph{The $U(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge structures are constructions the geometry makes natural, not consequences it forces;\ the $U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ saturation below is an organizing reading, not a derivation.}  The three-sphere
carries a canonical principal-bundle structure, the Hopf fibration
$S^1\!\to\!S^3\!\to\!S^2$, and $S^3$ \emph{is} the group manifold
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$.  These two facts are not GeoVac inputs; they are
geometry.  Reading the GeoVac graph through its incidence matrix, one
can build an abelian and then a non-abelian Wilson lattice gauge theory
on the same nodes and edges (a Wilson theory can be placed on any graph;
what the geometry supplies is the natural choice of group), whose labels carry the Coulomb spectrum
(\S\ref{sec:gauge}; \gvc{graph-s3-wall}{the spectrum sits on the labels as the diagonal
$SO(4)$ Casimir, not in the edge Laplacian}).  The natural gauge reach saturates at
$U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and where it stops is
itself structural:\ $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ admits only a pure-gauge Wilson
theory, its natural matter coupling obstructed at the Clebsch--Gordan
intertwiner level (a sharpening of the Coulomb/harmonic-oscillator
asymmetry of Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}).

\emph{The couplings are not.}  The fine-structure constant, the QED
multi-loop constants, and the cutoff function of the gravity sector are
all on the other side of the ledger:\ calibration or observation, in the
two-layer language of Paper~34~\cite{loutey_paper34}.  The framework's
most precise number---\gvc{k-match-precision}{the cubic's root matches $\alpha$ to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ ($K$ itself to $4.8\times10^{-7}$)---is held
explicitly at \textbf{Observation} tier}, with no claim of derivation
(\S\ref{sec:alpha}).  This is the same discipline that runs through the
quantum-chemistry and quantum-computing arcs:\ lead with the structural
result, name the calibration boundary, and let the tier of each claim be
visible in the prose.

Following the framework's positioning convention, the discrete graph
topology and the continuous Hopf/spectral-action geometry are treated as
\emph{dual descriptions} connected by proven maps;\ no ontological
priority is asserted for either, and the synthesis leads with concrete
structural content rather than interpretive claims about the nature of
electromagnetism or gravity.

The five papers form a loose dependency order.  Paper~30 builds the
gauge structure on the scalar and Dirac Rule-B label graphs.  Paper~33
computes the physical vector-photon vertex on $S^3$ and scores eight
selection rules on the graph, in a vertex model and with the physical
vertex;\ Paper~28 determines the periods of the Dirac spectral sums,
whose nested sums use that vertex model.  Paper~2 reports
the $\alpha$ observation.  Paper~51 proves a convergence theorem for a
discrete replica derivative and records the continuum spectral-action
gravity results it serves, together with what did not work.  The
hydrogen Lamb-shift paper (Paper~36~\cite{loutey_paper36}) uses no $S^3$
input in its number and is part of the precision group (Group~6).

\section{Wilson gauge theory on the label graphs}\label{sec:gauge}
%% ========================================================================

\paragraph{The Hodge/Wilson dictionary on the graph (Paper~30 \S4.2).}
With $B$ the node--edge incidence matrix of the scalar label graph, the
node Laplacian $L_0=BB^{\top}$ is the scalar matter operator of the lattice
gauge theory (\gvc{graph-s3-wall}{it does \emph{not} converge to $-\Delta_{S^3}$:\ it splits
into $n_{\max}$ disconnected $\ell$-blocks, and the Coulomb spectrum
lives on the labels instead~\cite{loutey_paper7}}), the edge Laplacian
$L_1=B^{\top}B$ has the cycle space as its kernel, and with the plaquette
incidence $B_2$ the gauge-fixed (Feynman-gauge) photon propagator is the
inverse of the Hodge-1 Laplacian $B^{\top}B+B_2B_2^{\top}$.  The
dictionary is standard and holds on every graph.  Collapsing each
$m_\ell$-multiplet at fixed $(n,\ell)$ to one vertex gives a weighted graph
on the six sectors $(n,\ell)$ with Laplacian spectrum $\{0,0,0,1,3,6\}$
(Paper~30 \S4.3).  Grouping each shell by the chiral $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factor
whose torus is the Hopf fibre instead of by $\ell$ halves the Casimir trace
of the $\alpha$ observation, so that trace belongs to the rotation
labelling, not to the fibration (Paper~2, scope section).  None of the
underlying mathematics is new (graph Hodge theory, Wilson lattice gauge
theory, the Marcolli--van~Suijlekom gauge-network spectral
triple~\cite{marcolli_vs}); what Paper~30 adds is the identification of the
label graphs' plaquette complexes and the measured Rule-B diagnostics.  The
edge Laplacian is an integer matrix, so its spectrum is algebraic
($\pi$-free; at $n_{\max}=3$ its characteristic polynomial splits over
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$), and no transcendental can hide in the gauge
1-forms themselves.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ because $S^3=\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Paper~30 \S3).}
The rank-1 non-abelian case is special:\ the manifold \emph{is} the gauge
group.  With links $U_e\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, plaquettes the graph's
4-cycles (the unit squares of the planar $\ell$-blocks), and Wilson action
$S_W=\beta\sum_P\bigl(1-\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P\bigr)$,
the construction is a gauge-invariant, Haar-normalizable finite theory.
The plaquettes form a basis of the cycle space at every $n_{\max}$, so after
gauge fixing the plaquette holonomies are independent Haar variables:\
$Z(\beta)=[2e^{-\beta}I_1(\beta)/\beta]^{\beta_1}$ and every loop enclosing
$A$ plaquettes has $\langle W\rangle = (I_2/I_1)^{A}$ exactly, an area law
by factorization that carries no information about confinement
(Paper~30 Prop.~1).  The second-order weak-coupling expansion produces
the curl plaquette form $B_2B_2^{\top}$ --- \emph{not} a
multiple of $L_1$; it is the missing half that completes the edge
Laplacian $L_1=B^{\top}B$ to the full discrete Hodge-1 Laplacian (Paper~30
Prop.~2) --- and the cubic term is nonzero, with a vertex proportional to
$\epsilon^{abc}$.  The kinetic normalization is the standard one of the
Wilson action's naive continuum limit,
\begin{equation}
\text{coeff} = \frac{1}{4N_c}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\tfrac18\ (\mathrm{SU}(2)),\quad
\tfrac1{12}\ (\mathrm{SU}(3)),
\end{equation}
checked numerically (Paper~30 Remark~1).  Restricting the links to the
maximal torus gives the compact $U(1)$ Wilson action (Paper~30 Prop.~3,
checked to machine precision); this is a statement about the action, not
the path integral, since Haar measure on $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ does not restrict
to Haar measure on the torus, so compact $U(1)$ is a separate theory, solved
on the scalar graph by the same factorization,
$\langle\cos\theta_C\rangle=(I_1/I_0)^{A}$.  The scope is explicitly
bounded:\ this is a compact-graph construction, \emph{not} a Yang--Mills
mass-gap or confinement claim on $\mathbb{R}^4$, and the label graphs have
no continuum limit.

\paragraph{Where the gauge reach stops.}
The non-abelian story does not continue to $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a
\emph{natural} matter-coupled theory.  On the $S^5$ Bargmann--Segal
lattice (the harmonic-oscillator analog of $S^3$) a pure-gauge Wilson
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is well defined (the same $1/(4N_c)=1/12$ coefficient and
a Cartan reduction to a coupled $U(1)\times U(1)$ character are checked,
Paper~30 Remark~1 and \S4.1), but the natural matter sector cannot be coupled
in the strict Wilson sense:\ the Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner obstruction
re-appears at the matter level, and the $m_\ell$-quotient is not a spectral
discretization of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (Paper~24).  This is the gauge-side face of the Coulomb/HO asymmetry
catalogued in Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}:\ $U(1)$ transfers
universally, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the natural choice because
$S^3=\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ admits a pure-gauge Wilson theory
but no natural matter coupling.  The cumulative
reading is that the natural gauge content of the construction saturates
at $U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$---the structural reach,
not a derivation of the Standard-Model gauge group, and consistent with
the spectral-triple gauge analysis of Paper~32~\cite{loutey_paper32}.

\paragraph{Compact $U(1)$ on the Dirac Rule-B graph (Paper~30 \S5).}
On the scalar graph the gauge theory factorizes into disjoint per-$\ell$
planar grids, so it is the exactly solvable two-dimensional theory.  The
cross-$\ell$ Dirac ``Rule~B'' graph (the $E1$ dipole adjacency,
$|\Delta\ell|=1$) is different:\ every edge changes $\ell$, the graph is
connected, and its 4-cycles outnumber the independent cycles ($44$ against
$11$ at $n_{\max}=2$) and close into 2-cycles of three plaquettes, so compact
$U(1)$ does not factorize.  At fixed diffusion scale its spectral dimension
sits about $0.45$ above the scalar graph's.  Compact $U(1)$ on Rule~B is
tested against seven diagnostics (spectral dimension, Migdal--Kadanoff
flow, Gaussian perimeter law, strong-coupling area law, a
DeGrand--Toussaint monopole density falling roughly exponentially in
$\beta$, full Monte-Carlo Wilson loops, and Polyakov loops on
$G_B\times C_{N_t}$).  All are compatible with compact $U(1)$ and none
discriminates a dimension: no four-dimensional control was run, the
monopole data end below the four-dimensional transition, the joint-fit
area coefficient is consistent with zero at $n_{\max}=2$, and the Polyakov
loop is well defined through one extra cohomology class while its
expectation vanishes by centre symmetry.  Each link lies in many plaquettes
($8.8$ and $37.5$ on average at $n_{\max}=2,3$), so the comparison class is
mean-field or large-dimension lattice gauge theory, not a
three-dimensional lattice.

The QED arc asks how much of perturbative quantum electrodynamics is

\paragraph{The selection-rule census (Paper~33).}
On the scalar Fock graph with a scalar ($1$-cochain) photon exactly one of
the eight rules survives---the Clebsch--Gordan triangle sparsity of the
discrete edge set.  The physical vertex
$\int_{S^3}\langle\psi_1, c(A)\,\psi_2\rangle$, Clifford multiplication
by a coclosed 1-form between Camporesi--Higuchi eigenspinors, is fixed by
$\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ representation theory and confirmed by two exact
routes.  For same-sign spinors it keeps the vertex model's window and
adds $q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$;\ for opposite signs it opens channels with
$n_1 + n_2 + q$ even;\ the ground state couples, also to itself, and the
Ward--Takahashi identity holds exactly.  Scored against it, triangle
sparsity and $\Delta m$ hold, the Ward identity holds in
Ward--Takahashi form, the channel count holds as representation theory,
the ground-state zero, E1 parity and the level triangle fail, and Furry's
theorem is undecided.  The vertex model's $1{+}6{+}1$ partition, whose
Dirac Furry score rests on a diagonal $\alpha_z$ identity, does not
survive the physical vertex.

\paragraph{The perturbative period tower (Paper~28).}
The $S^3$ Dirac spectral sums (Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum
$|\lambda_n|=n+\tfrac32$, Hodge-1 photon, the vertex model's selection
rule) produce odd zeta values and the level-4 content $G$,
$\beta(4)$ through a spectral mechanism organized by operator order and
a parity restriction of the spectrum.  The spectral-zeta identities are statements about the
sums as defined and stand independently of the vertex model:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T9 (two-term theorem).}  The $D^2$ spectral zeta is a
two-term $\pi^{\text{even}}$ polynomial with rational coefficients at
every integer $s$---no odd-zeta content there (the one-loop determinant
$\zeta'_{D^2}(0) = 3\zeta_R(3)/(4\pi^2) + \tfrac12\ln 2$ does carry it); operator order is the primary
transcendental discriminant, conditional on the period conjecture.
\item \textbf{$\chi_{-4}$ bridge (Theorem~3).}  A spectral-zeta-to-Dirichlet-$L$
identity, $D_{\text{even}}(s)-D_{\text{odd}}(s)=2^{\,s-1}\bigl(\beta(s)-\beta(s-2)\bigr)$
with $\beta(s)=L(s,\chi_{-4})$, in which the parity of $n$ acts as the
Dirichlet character (that the vertex model's parity clause acts this way
on internal lines is a reading of the model); proven, and verified
symbolically and by PSLQ to 100 digits.
\item \textbf{Realized-depth tower.}  Loop order bounds the multiple-zeta
depth but does not automatically mint new irreducibles; the realized
depth grows strictly more slowly, $\le k-1$.  The two-loop sunset
constant reduces entirely,
$S_{\min}\in\mathbb{Q}[\pi^2,\ln2,\zeta(3),\zeta(5)]$ (depth $\le1$);
the three-loop chain $S^{(3)}=40.6554\ldots$ first introduces a depth-2
period, $\zeta(5,3)$, as its only constant beyond the single-zeta ring
(its irreducibility to single zetas is conjectural).
\end{itemize}
On the finite graph these quantities are $\pi$-free rationals or
algebraic numbers---the transcendentals enter only through the infinite
spectral sums of the continuum projection.  The vertex model's
ground-state self-energy zero is not reproduced on the graph (the
pendant-edge proposition gives
$\Sigma_{\text{graph}}(\text{GS})=2(n_{\max}-1)/n_{\max}\to2$, Paper~33).
A one-loop magnetic-moment ratio of $1.084$ in the vertex model divides a
coupling-free sum by the CODATA $\alpha/(2\pi)$; its closeness to
$1 + 2/25$ is a numerical coincidence (Paper~28).

\section{The fine-structure-constant observation}\label{sec:alpha}
%% ========================================================================

The arc's most precise---and most carefully bounded---result is
Paper~2's.  The fine-structure constant approximately satisfies (to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ relative; an Observation) the depressed cubic
\begin{equation}
\alpha^3-K\alpha+1=0,\qquad
K=\pi\!\left(B+F-\Delta\right)
 =\pi\!\left(42+\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{1}{40}\right),
\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
where the three coefficients are spectral data of the unit $S^3$:\
$B=42$ is the degeneracy-weighted trace of the $SO(3)$ Casimir $l(l+1)$
over the scalar $S^3$ harmonics of the first three shells (an $S^3$
quantity, not an $S^2$ one:\ the $S^2$ trace over $l\le2$ is $36$; the
criterion $B/N=\dim S^3=3$ is chosen and, given it, fixes the cutoff
$n_{\max}=3$ uniquely), $F=\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$ is the Dirichlet series of the
shell degeneracy $n^2$ at $s=4$, the maximum degree $d_{\max}$ of the
packing~\cite{loutey_paper0} (equivalently half the spectral zeta of the
circle at $s = 1$, its $k > 0$ modes), and $\Delta=1/40$ is the reciprocal
of the single-level Dirac degeneracy
$g_3^{\text{Dirac}}=2(k{+}1)(k{+}2)|_{k=3}=40$ (Camporesi--Higuchi index).
\gvc{k-match-precision}{The physical root gives $\alpha^{-1}=137.036011$, matching CODATA to a
relative error of $8.8\times10^{-8}$; the weights, signs, prefactor and
the cubic are chosen}.  In a
combinatorial search over $1.92\times10^9$ comparable formulas ten match
more closely (the formula ranks eleventh; $p = 5.2\times10^{-9}$ per
formula), so the expected chance count is $Np\approx10$ and within that
search the formula is not unusual; the search was run after the formula
was found and does not account for that.

The status of Eq.~\eqref{eq:K} must be stated exactly, and it is a
\textbf{hard prohibition} of the framework that it be stated no more
strongly.  \gvc{k-combination-rule}{The combination rule $K=\pi(B+F-\Delta)$ is an
\textbf{Observation}:\ an empirical numerical coincidence with structural
support}, \emph{never} a derivation, conjecture, prediction, or theorem.
Each ingredient has an \emph{identified} spectral home---Paper~2's
``Three homes'' Proposition records that $B$, $F$, $\Delta$ live in two
distinct sectors of $S^3$ (scalar and spinor) and are three different object
types (finite trace, infinite Dirichlet series, single-level degeneracy);\
\gvc{three-homes-negative}{no single construction generating all three has been exhibited (an
empirical record, not part of the Proposition)}---but their \emph{combination} is not derived.  A multi-phase
structural-decomposition program eliminated twelve single-mechanism
derivations of the rule, and no derivation of the combination rule
exists.  The synthesis
inherits this verdict verbatim:\ what is structural here is each spectral
home; \gvc{k-match-precision}{what is observational is that they sum approximately to
$\alpha^{-1}/\pi$ (to $4.8\times10^{-7}$ relative;\ $8.8\times10^{-8}$
through the cubic's root)}.

\section{The spectral-action gravity sketch}\label{sec:gravity}
%% ========================================================================

Paper~51 records one new theorem and the continuum spectral-action
results of Chamseddine and Connes~\cite{chamseddine_connes} that it serves,
on the round $S^3$ and on the Euclidean black-hole cigar.  None of its
results uses the GeoVac label lattice:\ they concern the continuum
Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum and a finite-difference cone.

The new result is a convergence theorem (Paper~51, Theorem~3.1; one
standard ingredient argued rather than proved):\ on a finite-difference
discretization of the two-dimensional spinor cone of apex angle
$2\pi\alpha$, the heat trace converges in $C^1$ in $\alpha$ near $\alpha=1$,
so the replica derivative, whose mode sum carries an extra weight
$(j+\tfrac12)$, converges at the heat-trace rate.  Numerically the replica
combination converges to the continuum spinor tip value $1/6$, at order
$O(a)$ in the radial spacing.

The continuum results are these.  The spectral action on the round $S^3$
(and $S^3\times S^1$) is two-term up to exponentially small
corrections---Einstein--Hilbert plus a cosmological constant---and its
content is Chamseddine and Connes's; Paper~51 gives an alternative proof
through the spectral-zeta identity $\zeta_{\text{unit}}(-k)=0$ for all
$k\ge0$ (via the Bernoulli identity $B_{2k+1}(3/2)=(2k+1)/4^k$;
Paper~51, Theorem~4.1 and Cor.~4.2).  It is spinor-bundle-specific (the
scalar and transverse-traceless heat traces do not truncate;
Theorem~6.1), and the absence of higher-curvature terms is a statement
about the conformally flat $S^3\times S^1$.  Traced over the rank-4 spinor
bundle, the action gives $G=3\pi/(\phi(1)\Lambda^2)$ for a cutoff with
Mellin moments $\phi(s)$; the replica derivative of the cigar's tip term,
with the Dirac cone coefficient and the fermionic sign of the effective
action, gives $S_{\text{BH}}=A\Lambda^2\phi(1)/(12\pi)=A/(4G)$ with the same
$G$.  The cigar is not conformally flat, and there the two routes agree at
leading order in $\Lambda$; their agreement is the induced-gravity
statement of Susskind--Uglum and Frolov--Fursaev, specialised to this
spectrum, not a new result.  The second variation of the action on the
$(1,1)$ sector of $SO(4)$ cannot distinguish the diagonal-$\mathrm{SU}(2)$
spins $J=0,1,2$ (Paper~51, Prop.~8.2), so it cannot by itself isolate a
graviton.  On $S^3\times\mathbb{R}$, the Euclidean Einstein static
universe, the two-term density has a formal extremum; the exact mode sum
is monotonic in the radius.

The negatives are stated plainly:
\begin{itemize}
\item the continuum spinor cone tip term is $t$-independent ($1/6$), and
the discrete per-$t$ tip recovery is a crossing of that value at the
lattice scale, not a converged extraction;
\item at excess angle $\alpha>1$ the fixed-spacing recovery plateau is a
discretization artifact:\ as the radial mesh refines, the recovery climbs
toward the plain continuation of the cone coefficient;
\item the Newton-constant ``factor of~2'' between the action and entropy
routes came from tracing over a rank-2 bundle (or reading the cone
coefficient per component); the rank-4 trace and the total-trace
coefficient make the two agree;
\item the end-to-end discrete $S_{\text{BH}}$ on the substrate is not
converged:\ it depends strongly on $\Lambda$ and on the $t$-grid, because
the substrate's tip profile peaks at a $t$ fixed by the lattice, not by the
cutoff.
\end{itemize}
The overall verdict is that the round-sphere spectral action supplies a
two-term Einstein--Hilbert skeleton (Chamseddine--Connes; an alternative
proof here), the cutoff function remains external calibration data, and
the full discrete-substrate re-derivation of $S_{\text{BH}}$ is an
explicitly open research program.

\section{Honest scope and positioning}
%% ========================================================================

\paragraph{What is robust.}
The load-bearing results are structural and proven.  Wilson loops on the
scalar graph factorize exactly (the plaquettes form a cycle basis), the
weak-coupling kinetic term is the curl form that completes the edge
Laplacian to the Hodge-1 Laplacian, and the maximal-torus restriction of
the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ action is the compact $U(1)$ action to machine
precision (Paper~30).  The perturbative period results---the T9
two-term theorem, the $\chi_{-4}$ Dirichlet-$L$ bridge, and the
realized-depth tower with $\zeta(5,3)$ as the first depth-2 period
(conjecturally irreducible)---are theorems (T9, $\chi_{-4}$) and a
Proposition (the realized-depth tower, PSLQ-identified) about the sums as
defined.  The spectral-action two-term exactness on $S^3$ is a theorem
(Chamseddine--Connes; alternative proof in Paper~51).  The physical
vector-photon vertex rule is fixed by $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ representation

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Read as a whole, the Group~5 arc is a structural map of how far the
spectral geometry of $S^3$ reaches into electromagnetism, the gauge
sector, and gravity, with the boundary between forced structure and free
coupling drawn explicitly at every step.  The natural gauge
constructions, the QED period tower of the spectral sums, and the
two-term round-sphere action are the structural assets;\ the fine-structure constant, the QED
multi-loop constants, and the gravity cutoff are the calibration side.
The discrete and continuous descriptions are dual, with no ontological
priority asserted for either.  The arc is not a derivation of $\alpha$,
not a derivation of the Standard-Model gauge group, and not a theory of
quantum gravity;\ it is a disciplined account of which structures the
framework forces and which numbers it must be told.
