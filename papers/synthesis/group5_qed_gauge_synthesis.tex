\begin{abstract}
The Geometric Vacuum (GeoVac) framework labels the hydrogen bound
states by the harmonics of the unit three-sphere $S^3$ via Fock's
conformal projection.  This synthesis assembles the eight Group~5
papers---gauge structure, QED spectral sums on $S^3$ and the graph,
precision bound-state QED,
and a spectral-action gravity sketch---into one narrative around a single
organizing distinction:\ \emph{$U(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wilson
structures can be built on the graph, the choice of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ being
natural because $S^3 = \mathrm{SU}(2)$, while the couplings are
calibration/observation, not derived}.  The graph carries a $U(1)$
Wilson/Hodge lattice gauge structure~\cite{loutey_paper25}, a non-abelian
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Wilson theory because $S^3=\mathrm{SU}(2)$ as a
manifold~\cite{loutey_paper30}, and---on the cross-$\ell$ Dirac
graph---a $U(1)$ theory compatible with compact electrodynamics, with no
3D-versus-4D discrimination~\cite{loutey_paper41}; the natural gauge reach is
$U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ (an organizing reading,
not a derivation), with $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
entering only as a pure-gauge theory whose natural matter coupling is
obstructed.  Only one of eight selection rules (Gaunt sparsity) is
graph-native; the rest are scored in a vertex model with vector-photon
labels, whose ground-state rule is a scalar-triangle zero and whose
eighth rule rests on an $\alpha_z$ identity that a physical vector
photon does not satisfy~\cite{loutey_paper33};\ the period content of the
model's spectral sums enters through the continuum $S^3$ Dirac sums, which
produce the odd zeta values of flat-space QED ($\zeta(3)$, $\zeta(5)$),
plus $S^3$ vertex-parity content (Catalan~$G$, $\beta(4)$) and the
depth-2 period $\zeta(5,3)$, through a depth-filtered period
tower~\cite{loutey_paper28}.  \gvc{k-combination-rule}{The fine-structure constant approximately satisfies (to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ relative; an Observation) the
cubic $\alpha^3-K\alpha+1=0$ with $K=\pi(B+F-\Delta)$ assembled from
three spectral invariants of the Hopf bundle, each with an independently
established spectral home; this
is recorded strictly as an \textbf{Observation} (agreement
$8.8\times10^{-8}$, twelve single-mechanism derivations eliminated),

\section{Introduction: natural structure, free couplings}
%% ========================================================================

The GeoVac program builds its discrete hydrogen lattice on the labels
$(n,l,m)$ of the Laplace--Beltrami operator on the unit
three-sphere~$S^3$, on which that operator acts as the diagonal $SO(4)$
Casimir;\ the operator is related to the Schr\"odinger equation by
Fock's 1935 conformal projection~\cite{loutey_paper7}.  (\gvc{graph-s3-wall}{The lattice's
graph Laplacian is not equivalent to it and does not converge to it, Paper~7}.)  The Group~5 arc
asks what the \emph{same} spectral geometry says about the rest of
electromagnetism, the gauge sector, and---most speculatively---gravity.
A single distinction organizes the answer, and it is the through-line of
this synthesis.

\emph{The $U(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gauge structures are constructions the geometry makes natural, not consequences it forces;\ the $U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ saturation below is an organizing reading, not a derivation.}  The three-sphere
carries a canonical principal-bundle structure, the Hopf fibration
$S^1\!\to\!S^3\!\to\!S^2$, and $S^3$ \emph{is} the group manifold
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$.  These two facts are not GeoVac inputs; they are
geometry.  Reading the GeoVac graph through its incidence matrix, one
can build an abelian and then a non-abelian Wilson lattice gauge theory
on the same nodes and edges (a Wilson theory can be placed on any graph;
what the geometry supplies is the natural choice of group), whose labels carry the Coulomb spectrum
(\S\ref{sec:gauge}; \gvc{graph-s3-wall}{the spectrum sits on the labels as the diagonal
$SO(4)$ Casimir, not in the edge Laplacian}).  The natural gauge reach saturates at
$U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$, and where it stops is
itself structural:\ $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ admits only a pure-gauge Wilson
theory, its natural matter coupling obstructed at the Clebsch--Gordan
intertwiner level (a sharpening of the Coulomb/harmonic-oscillator
asymmetry of Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}).

\emph{The couplings are not.}  The fine-structure constant, the QED
multi-loop constants, and the cutoff function of the gravity sector are
all on the other side of the ledger:\ calibration or observation, in the
two-layer language of Paper~34~\cite{loutey_paper34}.  The framework's
most precise number---\gvc{k-match-precision}{the cubic's root matches $\alpha$ to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ ($K$ itself to $4.8\times10^{-7}$)---is held
explicitly at \textbf{Observation} tier}, with no claim of derivation
(\S\ref{sec:alpha}).  This is the same discipline that runs through the
quantum-chemistry and quantum-computing arcs:\ lead with the structural
result, name the calibration boundary, and let the tier of each claim be
visible in the prose.

Following the framework's positioning convention, the discrete graph
topology and the continuous Hopf/spectral-action geometry are treated as
\emph{dual descriptions} connected by proven maps;\ no ontological
priority is asserted for either, and the synthesis leads with concrete
structural content rather than interpretive claims about the nature of
electromagnetism or gravity.

The eight papers form a loose dependency order.  Papers~25, 30, and~41
build the gauge structure.  Papers~33 and~28 develop QED spectral sums
on $S^3$ and the graph in a vertex model:\ its selection-rule census and
its perturbative period content.  Paper~2 reports
the $\alpha$ observation.  Paper~36 turns the Dirac-$S^3$ machinery onto
precision bound-state observables.  Paper~51 sketches the spectral-action
gravity sector and---characteristically---records what did not work.

\section{Gauge structure from the Hopf bundle}\label{sec:gauge}
%% ========================================================================

\paragraph{The Hodge/Wilson dictionary on the graph (Paper~25).}
With $B$ the node--edge incidence matrix of the GeoVac graph, the node
Laplacian $L_0=BB^{\top}$ is the matter propagator of the lattice
gauge theory (\gvc{graph-s3-wall}{it does \emph{not} converge to $-\Delta_{S^3}$:\ it splits
into $n_{\max}$ disconnected $\ell$-blocks, and the Coulomb spectrum
lives on the labels instead~\cite{loutey_paper7}}) and the edge Laplacian
$L_1=B^{\top}B$ is the discrete Hodge-1 Laplacian whose kernel counts
cycles---the photon propagator in temporal gauge.  Assigning a $U(1)$
connection $U_e=e^{i\theta_e}$ to each edge and collapsing the magnetic
label $m_\ell$ over its $2\ell+1$ rotation multiplet, a stand-in for the
Hopf fibre (whose own labels mix $\ell$ within a shell), gives the $S^2$
base graph; grouping by the fibre's own chiral factor instead (measured,
pre-registered) gives one connected base with an irrational spectrum,
so the three components belong to the rotation grouping.  None of the underlying mathematics is new (graph Hodge
theory, Wilson lattice gauge theory, the Marcolli--van~Suijlekom
gauge-network spectral triple~\cite{marcolli_vs}); what Paper~25 claims is
the \emph{synthesis}---one physically motivated discretization on which
matter, an abelian connection, and the Hopf base coexist.  The paper is
explicit about its tier:\ ``an observational synthesis paper. No new
experimental or computational prediction is derived,'' and the
edge-Laplacian spectrum of a finite Hopf graph is necessarily
algebraic ($\pi$-free), so no transcendental can hide in the gauge
1-forms themselves.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ because $S^3=\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Paper~30).}
The rank-1 non-abelian case is special:\ the manifold \emph{is} the gauge
group.  With links $U_e\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, plaquettes the primitive
non-backtracking (Ihara) cycles, and Wilson action
$S_W=\beta\sum_P\bigl(1-\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_P\bigr)$,
the construction is a gauge-invariant, Haar-normalizable finite theory.
Two results carry real weight.  The maximal-torus (Cartan) reduction
reproduces Paper~25's $U(1)$ action to machine precision
($|S_W-S_{U(1)}|<10^{-12}$)---stated as a \textbf{proposition with a
proof}---so Paper~25 is exactly the abelian sub-sector of a genuinely
non-abelian theory.  The second-order weak-coupling expansion produces
the co-exact (curl) plaquette form $B_2B_2^{\top}$ --- \emph{not} a
multiple of $L_1$; it is the missing half that completes Paper~25's
$L_1=B^{\top}B$ to the full discrete Hodge-1 Laplacian (Paper~30
Prop.~2, corrected 2026-07-03) --- with non-abelian couplings first
entering at order $A^4$.  The kinetic normalization is
universal,
\begin{equation}
\text{coeff} = \frac{1}{4N_c}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\tfrac18\ (\mathrm{SU}(2)),\quad
\tfrac1{12}\ (\mathrm{SU}(3)),
\end{equation}
verified symbolically and numerically.  The scope is explicitly bounded:\
this is a compact-graph construction, \emph{not} a Yang--Mills mass-gap
or confinement claim on $\mathbb{R}^4$; on this graph the plaquettes form
a cycle basis of each planar $l$-block, so Wilson loops factorize and
$\langle W\rangle = (I_2/I_1)^{\mathrm{Area}}$ exactly, which long Monte
Carlo chains reproduce.

\paragraph{Where the gauge reach stops.}
The non-abelian story does not continue to $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a
\emph{natural} matter-coupled theory.  On the $S^5$ Bargmann--Segal
lattice (the harmonic-oscillator analog of $S^3$) a pure-gauge Wilson
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is well defined and machine-precision verified (the same
$1/(4N_c)=1/12$ coefficient, Cartan reduction over $7{,}765$ plaquettes
to $<1.5\times10^{-11}$), but the natural matter sector cannot be coupled
in the strict Wilson sense:\ the Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner obstruction
re-appears at the matter level, and a least-squares fit of the
$m_\ell$-quotient to $\mathbb{CP}^2$ fails uniformly ($40.8\%$ least-squares
residual, with a convention-robust $\geq 33\%$ floor; Paper~25 \S VII).  This is the gauge-side face of the Coulomb/HO asymmetry
catalogued in Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}:\ $U(1)$ transfers
universally, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the natural choice because
$S^3=\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ admits a pure-gauge Wilson theory
but no natural matter coupling.  The cumulative
reading is that the natural gauge content of the construction saturates
at $U(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$---the structural reach,
not a derivation of the Standard-Model gauge group, and consistent with
the spectral-triple gauge analysis of Paper~32~\cite{loutey_paper32}.

\paragraph{The Dirac Rule-B graph and 3D compact $U(1)$ (Paper~41).}
Paper~25's scalar Wilson $U(1)$ has a structural limitation:\ on the
scalar Fock graph the gauge theory factorizes into disjoint per-$\ell$
two-dimensional grids, so it is really 2D compact $U(1)$ with no genuine
three-dimensional phase structure.  The cross-$\ell$ Dirac ``Rule~B''
graph (the $E1$ dipole adjacency, $|\Delta\ell|=1$) repairs this:\ every
edge flips $\ell$-parity, the graph is a single connected component, and
at fixed diffusion scale its spectral dimension sits about $0.45$ above
the scalar graph's (an earlier ``climb toward 3'' was an estimator effect).
Wilson $U(1)$ on Rule~B is then tested against seven diagnostics (spectral
dimension, Migdal--Kadanoff RG flow, Gaussian perimeter law,
strong-coupling area law, a DeGrand--Toussaint monopole density decaying
over 3.7 decades, full Monte-Carlo Wilson loops, and finite-$T$ Polyakov
loops on $G_B\times C_{N_t}$).  The verdict is compatibility with compact
$U(1)$ and no 3D-versus-4D discrimination: no 4D control was run, the
monopole data end below the 4D transition, the full-MC area coefficient
is consistent with zero, and the Polyakov-loop confinement reading is
withdrawn ($\langle P\rangle = 0$ is forced by centre symmetry).

The QED arc asks how much of perturbative quantum electrodynamics is

\paragraph{The selection-rule census (Paper~33).}
On the scalar Fock graph with a scalar ($1$-cochain) photon exactly one of
the eight rules survives---the Gaunt/Clebsch--Gordan angular sparsity of
the discrete edge set.  Promoting the photon to a vector harmonic with
labels $(q,m_q)$ and Wigner-$3j$ coupling scores six more, with the vertex
normalization contributing $1/(4\pi)$ per loop (read as the $S^2$ Weyl
exchange constant, an Observation).  These scores are within a vertex
model:\ the ground-state rule is the zero of a scalar-harmonic triangle
rule, which the physical vector-photon vertex (allowing
$q = n_1 + n_2 + 1$) does not enforce, and the eighth rule for Dirac
electrons rests on a diagonal $\alpha_z$ identity that fails for a
physical vector photon, whose diagonal magnetic coupling is nonzero.  The
census with the physical vertex is open.

\paragraph{The perturbative period tower (Paper~28).}
The $S^3$ Dirac spectral sums (Camporesi--Higuchi spectrum
$|\lambda_n|=n+\tfrac32$, Hodge-1 photon, the vertex model's selection
rule) produce odd zeta values and the $S^3$ vertex-parity content $G$,
$\beta(4)$ through a spectral mechanism organized by operator order and
vertex topology.  The spectral-zeta identities are statements about the
sums as defined and stand independently of the vertex model:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T9 (two-term theorem).}  The $D^2$ spectral zeta is a
two-term $\pi^{\text{even}}$ polynomial with rational coefficients at
every integer $s$---no odd-zeta content there (the one-loop determinant
$\zeta'_{D^2}(0) = 3\zeta_R(3)/(4\pi^2) + \tfrac12\ln 2$ does carry it); operator order is the primary
transcendental discriminant, conditional on the period conjecture.
\item \textbf{$\chi_{-4}$ bridge (Theorem~3).}  A spectral-zeta-to-Dirichlet-$L$
identity, $D_{\text{even}}(s)-D_{\text{odd}}(s)=2^{\,s-1}\bigl(\beta(s)-\beta(s-2)\bigr)$
with $\beta(s)=L(s,\chi_{-4})$, in which vertex parity acts as the
Dirichlet character; proven, and verified symbolically and by PSLQ to
100 digits.  It is consistent with but does not enforce the Riemann
hypothesis for $\beta$.
\item \textbf{Realized-depth tower.}  Loop order bounds the multiple-zeta
depth but does not automatically mint new irreducibles; the realized
depth grows strictly more slowly, $\le k-1$.  The two-loop sunset
constant reduces entirely,
$S_{\min}\in\mathbb{Q}[\pi^2,\ln2,\zeta(3),\zeta(5)]$ (depth $\le1$);
the three-loop chain $S^{(3)}=31.5726\ldots$ first introduces a depth-2
period, $\zeta(5,3)$, as its only constant beyond the single-zeta ring
(its irreducibility to single zetas is conjectural).
\end{itemize}
On the finite graph these quantities are $\pi$-free rationals or
algebraic numbers---the transcendentals enter only through the infinite
spectral sums of the continuum projection.  The vertex model's
ground-state self-energy zero is not reproduced on the graph (the
pendant-edge proposition gives
$\Sigma_{\text{graph}}(\text{GS})=2(n_{\max}-1)/n_{\max}\to2$), and the
plaquette transverse propagator's zero holds for every $s$-state (each
$s$-edge is a bridge), so it recovers no ground-state rule.  A
spectral-sum anomalous-moment ratio of $1.084$ divides a coupling-free
sum by the CODATA $\alpha/(2\pi)$; its closeness to $1 + 2/25$ is a
numerical coincidence.

\section{The fine-structure-constant observation}\label{sec:alpha}
%% ========================================================================

The arc's most precise---and most carefully bounded---result is
Paper~2's.  The fine-structure constant approximately satisfies (to
$8.8\times10^{-8}$ relative; an Observation) the depressed cubic
\begin{equation}
\alpha^3-K\alpha+1=0,\qquad
K=\pi\!\left(B+F-\Delta\right)
 =\pi\!\left(42+\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\frac{1}{40}\right),
\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
where the three coefficients are spectral invariants of the Hopf bundle
components:\ $B=42$ is the degeneracy-weighted Casimir trace of the $S^2$
base (with the selection principle $B/N=\dim S^3=3$ proven algebraically
to fix the cutoff $n_{\max}=3$ uniquely), $F=\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$ is half the
spectral zeta of the $S^1$ fiber at $s = 1$, its $k > 0$ modes (equivalently the Fock-degeneracy
Dirichlet series at the packing exponent $d_{\max}=4$~\cite{loutey_paper0}),
and $\Delta=1/40$ is an $S^3$ boundary term equal to the reciprocal of
the single-level Dirac degeneracy $g_3^{\text{Dirac}}=2(n{+}1)(n{+}2)|_{n=3}=40$.
\gvc{k-match-precision}{The physical root gives $\alpha^{-1}=137.036011$, matching CODATA to a
relative error of $8.8\times10^{-8}$ with zero free parameters}; in a
combinatorial search over $1.92\times10^9$ comparable formulas the
formula ranks among the top $\sim\!10$ ($p = 5.2\times10^{-9}$ per
draw), a statistic that does not account for the unregistered search
that produced the formula and does not establish non-accidentality.

The status of Eq.~\eqref{eq:K} must be stated exactly, and it is a
\textbf{hard prohibition} of the framework that it be stated no more
strongly.  \gvc{k-combination-rule}{The combination rule $K=\pi(B+F-\Delta)$ is an
\textbf{Observation}:\ an empirical numerical coincidence with structural

``three-homes'' theorem establishes that $B$, $F$, $\Delta$ live in two

structural-decomposition program eliminated twelve single-mechanism

\section{Bound-state precision QED}\label{sec:bsqed}
%% ========================================================================

Paper~36 turns the Dirac-$S^3$ infrastructure onto Lamb-shift-class
observables, where the answer is known independently and the test is one
of internal consistency rather than prediction.  The hydrogen
$2S_{1/2}$--$2P_{1/2}$ Lamb shift at leading one-loop order, assembled in
the Eides convention with the tabulated Drake--Swainson Bethe logarithms,
is $1052.19$~MHz against the experimental $1057.845$~MHz ($-0.534\%$).
That headline is textbook one-loop arithmetic with literature inputs, and
it omits the one-loop $\alpha(Z\alpha)^5$ term (about $+6.9$~MHz), so the
residual is not attributed.  The GeoVac content is the Bethe-logarithm
machinery (Sturmian basis at $\lambda = Z/n$, acceleration form,
Drake--Swainson asymptotic subtraction, which is Paper~34's thirteenth
catalogued projection~\cite{loutey_paper34}):\ it reproduces the
tabulated $2P$ and $3D$ logarithms to $2.4\%$ and $0.07\%$, while the $2S$
acceleration-form value is a crossing point, not yet converged.  The
leading one-loop assembly transfers to muonic hydrogen under the
rest-mass projection; a muonium comparison is withdrawn because its
theory reference could not be located in the cited source.  The two-loop
sector is the explicit boundary:\ the bare iterated spectral action
reproduces the flat-space two-loop prefactor structure but does not
generate renormalization counterterms, so finite multi-loop predictions
remain external input.

\section{The spectral-action gravity sketch}\label{sec:gravity}
%% ========================================================================

The most speculative paper in the arc, Paper~51, asks whether the
Fock-projected $S^3$ supports a sensible gravity reading at the
Chamseddine--Connes spectral-action level~\cite{chamseddine_connes}.  It
is positioned, correctly and throughout, as an \emph{exploratory,
structural-sketch} program---``with the standard caveats of the
Connes--Chamseddine program''---not as established physics, and it is
unusually candid about what failed.

One result is solid, and its content is Chamseddine and Connes's:\ the
spectral action on the round $S^3$ (and $S^3\times S^1$) is two-term up to
exponentially small corrections---Einstein--Hilbert plus a cosmological
constant, with no higher-curvature power corrections at any order.  Paper~51
gives an alternative algebraic proof through the spectral-zeta identity
$\zeta_{\text{unit}}(-k)=0$ for all $k\ge0$ (via the Bernoulli identity
$B_{2k+1}(3/2)=(2k+1)/4^k$); it is spinor-bundle-specific (the scalar and
transverse-traceless sectors retain the full infinite Seeley--DeWitt
series).  From it follow, at the spectral-action level, a Bekenstein--Hawking
entropy $S_{\text{BH}}=A\Lambda^2/(12\pi)$ (continuum replica
derivation), an effective Newton constant $G_{\text{eff}}=3\pi/\Lambda^2$ (rank-4 Dirac trace),
a formal de~Sitter extremum of the two-term form (the exact action is
monotonic), a proven Fierz--Pauli ``$J$-blindness'' theorem, and
a proven (Layer-2, rate-level) replica-weight-harmless theorem.

The negatives are the point, and they are stated plainly:
\begin{itemize}
\item the per-$t$ ``UV target'' $1/(24\pi t)$ once used to grade the
discrete tip extraction was dimensionally wrong: the continuum cone tip
term is $t$-independent ($1/6$), and the discrete per-$t$ recovery at
the lattice scale fails in both directions against it;
\item the M\"obius factor $\alpha/(2\alpha-1)$, once read as a continuum
$\alpha>1$ ``mechanism,'' is a \emph{finite-lattice-spacing artifact}:\
as the radial mesh refines, the recovery climbs back toward the plain
Sommerfeld--Cheeger value, so there is no exotic mechanism to find;
\item no cutoff function cures the infrared over-count across all
$\Lambda$ (clean negative);
\item the Newton-constant ``factor of~2'' between the action and entropy
routes was an error, not a convention: the action route had been traced
over a rank-2 bundle, and the rank-4 Dirac trace makes the two agree; it
was never a scalar-vs-Dirac cone-coefficient difference (2D Dirac and
scalar cones share the magnitude $(1/12)|1/\alpha-\alpha|$, with
opposite signs);
\item Bisognano--Wichmann wedge entanglement entropy is \emph{not} an
area law---it scales as $\sim2\log n_{\max}$ (area-law fit rejected at
$R^2=0.83$), so the entropy comes from the replica/spectral-action route,
not from wedge entanglement.
\end{itemize}
The overall verdict is that the round-sphere spectral action supplies a
two-term Einstein--Hilbert skeleton (Chamseddine--Connes; an alternative
proof here), the cutoff function remains external calibration data, and
the full discrete-substrate re-derivation of $S_{\text{BH}}$ is an
explicitly open research program.

\section{Honest scope and positioning}
%% ========================================================================

\paragraph{What is robust.}
The load-bearing results are structural and proven.  The Hodge/Wilson
dictionary ($L_0=BB^{\top}$ matter, $L_1=B^{\top}B$ photon) is exact, as
is the machine-precision $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ maximal-torus reduction that
makes Paper~25 the Cartan sector of Paper~30, and Wilson loops on the
scalar graph factorize exactly.  The perturbative period results---the T9
two-term theorem, the $\chi_{-4}$ Dirichlet-$L$ bridge, and the
realized-depth tower with $\zeta(5,3)$ as the first depth-2 period
(conjecturally irreducible)---are theorems (T9, $\chi_{-4}$) and a
Proposition (the realized-depth tower, PSLQ-identified) about the sums as
defined.  The spectral-action two-term exactness on $S^3$ is a theorem
(Chamseddine--Connes; alternative proof in Paper~51).  Each of the three
$\alpha$-ingredients $B$, $F$, $\Delta$ has an identified spectral home.

\paragraph{What is conditional or observational.}
\gvc{k-combination-rule}{The fine-structure combination rule $K=\pi(B+F-\Delta)$ is an
\textbf{Observation}, not a derivation}---the single most important tier
statement in the arc.  The selection-rule census is a statement about a
vertex model, not about the physical QED vertex.  The bound-state QED
headline is standard one-loop arithmetic; the GeoVac content is its Bethe
logarithms.  The Rule-B gauge theory is compatible with compact $U(1)$
with no 3D-versus-4D discrimination.  The entire gravity sector is a
spectral-action-level structural sketch with an open discrete-substrate
program.

\paragraph{What was tried and did not work.}
The arc is bounded by clean negatives.  $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ has no natural
matter-coupled lattice gauge theory---the Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner
obstruction caps the natural gauge reach (a face of the Coulomb/HO
asymmetry).  The scalar graph photon misses seven of the eight selection
rules because it carries no angular quantum numbers.  The vertex model's
ground-state zero, the plaquette ``recovery'' of it, the $\alpha_z$
Furry identity, the anomalous-moment ratio's Parker--Toms match, the
Lamb-shift residual decomposition, and the Rule-B Polyakov-loop
confinement reading are withdrawn or re-scoped.  \gvc{three-homes-negative}{Twelve
single-mechanism derivations of the $\alpha$ combination rule were
eliminated}.  The gravity sector contributes five recorded negatives---the
M\"obius finite-spacing artifact, the failed IR-over-count cure, the
withdrawn per-$t$ tip target, the factor of~2 traced to the spinor rank,
and the non-area-law wedge entropy---each kept on the record so it is
not re-derived.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Read as a whole, the Group~5 arc is a structural map of how far the
spectral geometry of $S^3$ reaches into electromagnetism, the gauge
sector, and gravity, with the boundary between forced structure and free
coupling drawn explicitly at every step.  The natural gauge
constructions, the QED period tower of the spectral sums, and the
two-term round-sphere action are the structural assets;\ the fine-structure constant, the QED
multi-loop constants, and the gravity cutoff are the calibration side.
The discrete and continuous descriptions are dual, with no ontological
priority asserted for either.  The arc is not a derivation of $\alpha$,
not a derivation of the Standard-Model gauge group, and not a theory of
quantum gravity;\ it is a disciplined account of which structures the
framework forces and which numbers it must be told.
