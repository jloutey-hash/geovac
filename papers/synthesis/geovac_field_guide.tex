\begin{abstract}
The Geometric Vacuum (GeoVac) framework began as a packing puzzle that
produced something resembling the periodic table.  Over the course
of the investigation, it acquired a precise structural identity:\ a
discrete spectral triple that is an instance of the Marcolli--van
Suijlekom 2014 graph-network construction at the data level and
extends it via a forced-graph claim and a master Mellin engine,
whose census of $\sthree$ spectral transcendentals sits in cyclotomic
mixed-Tate periods at level $\le 4$, and which separates --- through
theorems and named readings, not preference --- what the underlying
geometry forces from what empirical measurement supplies.  This field guide is the project's
identity statement.  It tells the arc from origin to current frontier
without recapitulating the technical machinery, and points each kind
of reader toward the right entry into the corpus.  As the project's \emph{grand
synthesis} it now sits atop a complete layer of six audience-targeted
group syntheses---one per branch (Groups~1--6), each digesting its
branch in a few pages---and composes them into a single front-to-back
reading that routes each reader to the right branch digest.
Re-centred October 2026:\ the spectral triple is one chapter of the
map, not its spine.  The spine is an accounting.  Compactness forces
discreteness (a mechanism that is not ours, and whose converse is
false), so the compact skeleton is rational and bit-exact;\ every axis
released toward the continuum --- by geometry, by an observer, or by
calibration --- carries an identifiable number class as its price;\ and
the atlas of those prices (\S\ref{sec:atlas}) is the deliverable.  The
one-sentence
reading:\ \emph{GeoVac is the price list of leaving a compact world:\
its label lattice is the rational skeleton that compactness forces, each
released axis carries a named number class as its price, and the atlas
of those prices separates what geometry forces from what measurement
supplies.}
\end{abstract}

\section{How This Started}
\label{sec:origin}
% =====================================================================

A short note on the path that led to the framework, told in the first
person.  The reader can skip it without missing technical content;\ the
arc proper begins in \S\ref{sec:packing}.

In high school I looked at the periodic table and wondered why it had
that shape --- in particular, why the rows didn't grow uniformly but
in a stepped pattern.  I sketched what I thought might be a more
natural picture:\ a circular layout with the first row at the centre
and successive rows fanning outward.  I had no mathematics to back it
up;\ it was a visual hunch.

In college I learned summation notation and the discipline of
inductive proof, and I felt --- for the first time --- that I had
permission to do real mathematics.  I went back to the periodic
table.  The row lengths grew by a recurring $+4$ between successive
pairs.  I built a nested summation that captured the pattern and
arrived at $2n^2$.  I looked up the number.  It was the degeneracy of
the Schr\"odinger principal quantum level.  I felt something between
satisfaction and disappointment:\ the pattern was already known ---
to Schr\"odinger, who had derived it by solving a differential
equation on $\R^3$.  My combinatorial route to the same number was a
curiosity, not a discovery.  I set the question down.

Years later, listening to a guest on Sean Carroll's podcast, I heard
the suggestion that perhaps, instead of quantising gravity, one
should try to quantise spacetime --- a discrete substrate as
foundation, not as a tool for approximating something continuous.
The framing revived the old question.  This time I posed it in polar
coordinates and treated Planck's constant as a unit volume to be
packed.  I wrote down a small set of packing axioms.  The
construction, executed honestly, produced circles whose node counts
reproduced the periodic table.

\begin{quote}
\itshape
This struck me as deeply important.
\end{quote}

I wrote a first paper that I would now describe as a book report:\ a
narrative explanation of how the packing maps to the periodic table,
without any of the spectral or geometric machinery that came later.
That paper became Paper~0~\cite{loutey_paper0} in its mature form.  I
then set out to see how the nodes might relate to quantum-number
counting.  Everything in the rest of this field guide --- the
Fock~1935 identification, the natural-geometry hierarchy, the
spectral-triple rotation, the periods census, the
forced-versus-free seam --- is what happened next.

What follows is the technical arc.

\section{The Packing Puzzle}
\label{sec:packing}
% =====================================================================

The framework's origin is a geometric construction:\ pack
Planck's constant.  Treat $\hbar$ as a unit volume to be filled, and
ask which node arrangements complete the packing without gaps.  In two
dimensions the node counts give $(l, m)$:\ the integer pair indexing
azimuthal and magnetic quantum numbers, before any quantum-mechanical
input.  In three dimensions, an inductive argument over periodic-table
row lengths fixes the principal and spin quantum numbers
$(n, s)$~\cite{loutey_paper0}.  The resulting graph has nodes labeled
by quantum-number tuples $(n, l, m, s)$ and edges encoding spectral
selection rules.

This graph's nodes are exactly the labels of a continuous operator on
the unit three-sphere (the Laplace--Beltrami operator, whose negative
acts on them as the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir;\ the graph's own edge
Laplacian does not converge to it --- see \S\ref{sec:fock}), whose
eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graph_eigs}
\lambda_n = -(n^2 - 1), \qquad n \in \N_{\ge 1},
\end{equation}
with multiplicity $n^2$.  Through Fock's energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$
these become the hydrogen levels $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ --- a map on the
labels, not an overall rescaling of the graph's own spectrum, which is a
grid band structure.  The shape of the periodic table fell out of
the packing.

This was the seed:\ a combinatorial object that, when interrogated,
returned the same numerology as observed atomic structure.  Whether
that was a coincidence or a clue was the question the rest of the
project tried to answer.

\section{Fock 1935:\ Discrete and Continuous Are the Same Thing}
\label{sec:fock}
% =====================================================================

In 1935 Vladimir Fock observed~\cite{fock1935} that the hydrogen atom
on $\R^3$ has a hidden $SO(4)$ symmetry, and that the stereographic
projection $\R^3 \hookrightarrow \sthree$ converts the Schr\"odinger
equation into an angular eigenvalue problem on the unit three-sphere.
What hydrogen really is, from this point of view, is the
Laplace--Beltrami operator on the unit $\sthree$;\ the $1/r$ Coulomb
potential is the coordinate distortion produced by the projection
(specifically, the chordal-distance identity that takes the round
metric to the stereographic one).

The decisive observation for GeoVac is this:\ the nodes of the packed
lattice of \S\ref{sec:packing}, the labels $(n, l, m)$ with $n^2$ per
shell, are \emph{exactly} the labels of the unit-$\sthree$ harmonics at
every cutoff, and on them the continuous $\sthree$ Laplace--Beltrami
operator acts as a diagonal node weight, the $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2 - 1$.
Fock's conformal equivalence between hydrogen and that operator can be
checked symbolically:\ Paper~7 of the GeoVac
corpus~\cite{loutey_paper7} provides $18$ symbolic checks of the chain
(\texttt{tests/test\_fock\_projection.py} and
\texttt{tests/test\_fock\_laplacian.py}).  What the lattice does
\emph{not} do is discretize that operator through its edges:\ the graph
Laplacian $D - A$ never changes $l$, splits into $n_{\max}$ disconnected
blocks, and does not converge to it
(\texttt{tests/test\_graph\_s3\_nonconvergence.py};\ an earlier version
of this guide called the graph Laplacian conformally equivalent to the
$\sthree$ operator, corrected 2026-09-25).  The matched scaling
constant $\kappa = -1/16$ maps the graph Laplacian's extremal eigenvalue
(a grid band edge, $8$) onto the hydrogen ground state;\ its value
\emph{coincides} with a conformal-geometry quantity --- a numerical
observation, not a derivation
(Paper~0~\cite{loutey_paper0}, Paper~7~\cite{loutey_paper7}).

This is the framework's load-bearing identification:
\begin{quote}
\itshape
The hydrogen atom and the continuous unit-$\sthree$
Laplace--Beltrami operator are not two models.
They are the same object in two coordinates (Fock).  The discrete Fock
graph is their shared label lattice.
\end{quote}
The implication is operational:\ where the continuous formulation
requires integration over $\R^3$ with a $1/r$ singularity, the
discrete formulation works on a finite label set with exact
rational structure and selection rules.  Same physics, different cost.

\section{Natural Geometry:\ a Hierarchy}
\label{sec:natural_geometry}
% =====================================================================

Section~\ref{sec:fock}'s identification works because $\sthree$ is the
natural geometry for the hydrogen atom:\ it is the coordinate system in
which the Schr\"odinger equation separates fully into angular
variables.  For other systems --- molecules, multi-electron atoms,
polyatomics --- the natural geometry shifts.  The framework's chemistry
investigation (2024--2026, Phase~2 of the project) systematically
catalogued the natural geometries:

\toprule

The choice of geometry is determined by where separation of variables
occurs;\ in each case the discrete structure is the channel and label
structure of the natural geometry, with the continuous problem reached
through that geometry's own projection, as in \S\ref{sec:fock}.  (An
earlier version said the discrete graph Laplacian reproduces the
continuous problem in each case;\ at Level~1 it does not converge to
the $\sthree$ operator, \S\ref{sec:fock}.)

Phase~2 reached known structural ceilings.  Heavy molecules
($Z \ge 20$ or strong multi-determinant correlation) escape the
framework's accuracy targets through identifiable channels (the
``multi-focal-composition wall'' --- the project's name for a
structural obstruction established by multiple independent negative
results --- documented across six independent diagnostic
investigations).  The framework's most natural quantum-simulation
application --- Pauli-operator counts exactly linear in the qubit
count $Q$ at fixed basis ($N_{\mathrm{Pauli}} = 27.90 \times Q$) for
the core--valence ``composed'' encoding, benchmarked across a $37$-system
library~\cite{loutey_paper14} (the sub-quadratic $O(Q^{1.78})$ $1$-norm is
the atomic, helium, series, not a library property) --- shipped at the end
of Phase~2.  The
absolute Pauli multipliers are convention-sensitive (near parity with
minimal-basis STO-3G, $76\times$ sparser than cc-pVDZ);\ the
load-bearing advantage is the scaling, not any single market-test
ratio.

But a different question began to dominate the conversation around the
project, and that question is where the rest of this guide goes.

\section{The Rotation:\ from Solver to Spectral Triple}
\label{sec:rotation}
% =====================================================================

Phase~3 was planned as quantum simulation.  Instead, the project
rotated to a different question:\ \emph{what is this thing we have
been working with?}  (In the re-centred reading of \S\ref{sec:atlas}
this section is one chapter of the map --- the chapter that names the
observer doors and prices them --- rather than the spine of the story;\
it is kept in full because the prices it establishes are the ones the
atlas's observer rows point to.)

The $\sthree$ label lattice, Fock's conformal equivalence with the
continuous problem, the natural-geometry hierarchy that produces
discrete substrates for every electron configuration --- these are
collectively the data of a Riemannian spectral
geometry.  Made precise, that means an algebra of functions, a
Hilbert space of states, and a Dirac-type operator, all assembled
into a structure that mathematicians call a spectral triple
\cite{connes1994}.

The precise structural claim:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
GeoVac is a discrete spectral triple that is an \emph{instance} of
the Marcolli--van Suijlekom 2014 gauge-network construction
\cite{marcolli_vs2014} at the data level (refined by the
Perez-Sanchez clarification~\cite{perez_sanchez2024, perez_sanchez2025}
that the continuum limit is
Yang--Mills without Higgs in general) and an \emph{extension} of it
at the structural level via the forced-graph claim (Bertrand
$\times$ Fock projection), the master Mellin engine, and the
cosmic-Galois layer $U^*$ (Paper~56).
\end{quote}

Its metric part was checked, not assumed --- and then re-checked, for
the commutative ($S^3$) factor only:\ convergence of the gauge-network /
almost-commutative structure the claim names is \textbf{[OPEN]}.  A
Gromov--Hausdorff convergence theorem on truncated
Camporesi--Higuchi spectral triples~\cite{loutey_paper38} (Paper~38)
gives the quantitative statement for that factor, in van~Suijlekom's state-space
Gromov--Hausdorff framework (adjacent to, but distinct from, the
Latr\'emoli\`ere propinquity~\cite{latremoliere2017}), with the bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:propinquity_rate}
d_{\mathrm{GH}} \le \gamma_{n_{\max}},
\qquad \gamma_{n_{\max}} \sim
\frac{4}{\pi}\,\frac{\log n_{\max}}{n_{\max}}
\xrightarrow{n_{\max} \to \infty} 0,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{n_{\max}}$ is the first moment of the central Fej\'er kernel,
whose constant $4/\pi$ (dual-Coxeter normalisation; $2/\pi$ on the
unit $S^3$) is reported universal across compact connected simple Lie
groups (derived at rank~1, numerically supported, with a fit-sensitive
extraction, at higher ranks;
the rank-uniform proof is a named gap)~\cite{loutey_paper40}.  A tapered
amplitude gives a measured $O(1/n_{\max})$ on SU(2), so $4/\pi$ is the
kernel's constant, not the convergence rate (Paper~38, main theorem).
Paper~38's theorem is unconditional in
van~Suijlekom's state-space framework, via a translation-seminorm
metrization (the kernel condition is proved on the truthful
substrate;\ the dual direction uses an exact-fit spinor lifted state,
and both almost-inverse defects are Fej\'er smoothings at the same
moment).  See Paper~38~\S~1 and the claims register,
\texttt{docs/claims\_register.md}.
The Fej\'er constant reads $4/\pi = \mathrm{Vol}(S^2)/\pi^2$ in the
dual-Coxeter normalisation and $2/\pi = \mathrm{Vol}(S^2)/\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SU}(2))$
on the unit $S^3$ --- read (a reading, not a derivation;\ Paper~38) as
the $k = 0$ volume factor of the master Mellin engine (a quotient of
unit-sphere volumes, not the Hopf fibration's base-to-total ratio), its value
fixed by the metric normalisation, catalogued in
\S\ref{sec:periods} below.

Following Paper~38, the project produced a math.OA arc extending the
framework along the Lorentzian axis (Papers~42 and~44--49;\ the
former Paper~43 is now Paper~42~\S~11)~\cite{loutey_paper42, loutey_paper44,
loutey_paper45, loutey_paper46, loutey_paper47, loutey_paper48,
loutey_paper49}.  The most instructive outcome of that axis is
\emph{negative}:\ the natural device for a ``Lorentzian propinquity''
--- compressing the Krein Dirac to the Krein-positive subspace ---
annihilates the spatial Dirac, so the compressed Lipschitz seminorm
vanishes identically (Paper~45's degeneracy
theorem~\cite{loutey_paper45}, with a bit-exact frozen falsifier).
An earlier convergence claim through that device, together with the
\emph{metric-level} strong-form and bridge convergence claims built on
it (Papers~46, 48, 49), is withdrawn;\ those three and Paper~47 were
archived on 2026-09-14 (\texttt{papers/archive/}, with their surviving
content named in \texttt{docs/retired\_papers.md}), while Paper~45 remains
live and load-bearing.  The papers of record carry
Status notes.  What
survives:\ the operator-system substrate (Paper~44), the elementary
modular-structure results of Paper~42 (including its Krein-carrier
\S~11) --- the finite $2\pi$ period is an automatic closure of a
posited integer-spectrum generator, and its identification with the
Bisognano--Wichmann modular flow is open --- the norm-resolvent
de-compactification arrow (Paper~47), and the cocycle-deficit algebra
of Paper~49 (whose operator-system Lorentzian pre-length-space bridge
stands at the algebra level).  The repaired Lorentzian target --- a Toeplitz temporal
multiplier algebra and a non-compressing Krein-compatible seminorm
--- is stated as an open problem in Paper~45.

\paragraph{Why this rotation mattered.}
The shift from ``how accurately can we solve quantum chemistry?'' to
``what is GeoVac?'' produced the right kind of answers.  Chemistry
accuracy is a sliding scale --- always tighter at more cost.  But
the spectral-triple structural reading is binary:\ either GeoVac IS
the data of a spectral triple, or it isn't.  Theorem-grade closure
is available either way.  The framework's identity is now in the
first category.

\section{Periods:\ Where Transcendentals Come From}
\label{sec:periods}
% =====================================================================

A spectral triple has a heat kernel, and the heat kernel produces
transcendentals.  In GeoVac, those transcendentals are not random.
They sit in named classes, with named mechanisms producing them.

\paragraph{The graph itself is $\pi$-free.}
At what the project calls \emph{Layer 1} --- the bare combinatorial
graph, with integer matrix entries and an algebraic (closed-form,
bounded) eigenvalue spectrum; the pure-integer ladder $n^2-1$ belongs to
the continuum operator, not the graph (Paper~0) --- there are no
transcendentals.  Matrix elements are rationals (or, at most,
algebraic over $\Q$).  This has been checked across the corpus:
verified explicitly on the unit $\sthree$ Coulomb spectrum (Paper~7),
on the Hopf bundle gauge structure (Paper~25), on the Bargmann--Segal
$\sfive$ Hardy lattice (Paper~24), and on the Tier-2 symbolic spin--orbit
block of the qubit Hamiltonians (Paper~14).  The composed library as a whole
is not so certified:\ its frozen-core, Phillips--Kleinman and cross-centre
$V_{ne}$ content is numerical (the project's algebraic registry,
\texttt{docs/algebraic\_registry.md}).

\paragraph{Transcendentals enter at projection.}
Layer~1 by itself does not produce physical observables.  Observables
come from \emph{Layer~2:}\ projections of the discrete substrate onto
continuous manifolds, integration regions, or measurement-coordinate
choices.  Each projection introduces a controlled transcendental
through a controlled mechanism.

The project's census of transcendentals is organized by three named
mechanisms (a census and a reading, not a classification),
collectively the \emph{master Mellin engine}~\cite{loutey_paper18,
loutey_paper32, loutey_paper55}:\ M1, the volume slot ($k = 0$);\ M2,
the Seeley--DeWitt slot ($k = 2$);\ M3, the vertex-parity Hurwitz slot
($k = 1$).  The slot assignments and the period-ring home of each
mechanism are stated once, in Paper~18~\S III.7, with Paper~55 \S\S 3--5
as the census;\ this guide does not restate that table (it was
restated in some two dozen places before the October 2026
single-sourcing sweep, which is how copies drift).  The three
mechanisms are the three sub-cases of a single Mellin transform
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:master_mellin}
\mathcal{M}\!\left[\Tr\bigl(D^k \cdot e^{-tD^2}\bigr)\right](s)
\end{equation}
on the discrete Camporesi--Higuchi $\sthree$ spectral triple, with
operator-order index $k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ read as indexing which
mechanism produces which kind of transcendental (at $k = 1$ the plain
trace vanishes on the symmetric Dirac spectrum, and the slot is read
through the absolute sum and its vertex-parity grading, Paper~18).

\paragraph{The census rests on a conditional theorem.}
Paper~32~\S~VIII~\cite{loutey_paper32} contains a
\emph{case-exhaustion theorem} (\textbf{[CONDITIONAL]}):\ every
appearance of $\pi$ in a chain of the first fifteen Paper~34
projections arises from one of M1, M2, or M3 (argued case by case;\
the converse, that engaging a mechanism forces $\pi$, is withdrawn).  No fourth mechanism has been found despite specific searches.
Paper~55~\cite{loutey_paper55} adds a period-theoretic census:\ every
transcendental in its census ($S^3$ spectral-zeta and heat-trace data;\
not the chemistry seeds, the spectral determinants, or Paper~61's
elliptic period) sits in cyclotomic mixed-Tate periods at level $\le 4$
over $\Z[i, 1/2]$ (Kontsevich--Zagier~\cite{kontsevich_zagier2001},
Brown~\cite{brown2012}, Deligne~\cite{deligne2010},
Glanois~\cite{glanois2015}, Fathizadeh--Marcolli
\cite{fathizadeh_marcolli2016} are the relevant published
classifications;\ each value in the census is placed inside one of
their named period rings).

\paragraph{Beyond the census:\ the chemistry ladder.}  The census
above prices the observer doors.  The chemistry program prices the
geometric doors, and its classes leave the engine's rings
entirely.  At one centre the two-electron classes are exact rationals;\ a second
nucleus releases $l$ and the two-centre repulsion integrals close in
weight one, $\{e^{a}E_1(a), \ln, \gamma_E\}$, with no $\pi$ and no
dilogarithm (Paper~18~\S III, Paper~12, Paper~58);\ a third nucleus
with a shared centre makes the integral an elliptic period, and the
class leaves the polylogarithm tower for the elliptic one (Paper~59,
Paper~61~\cite{loutey_paper59, loutey_paper61});\ a fourth centre adds
nothing new;\ a three-electron chain over two-centre densities with two momentum
integrations lives on a K3 surface whose holomorphic period is a sum of
two products of elliptic periods (a three-scale Bessel moment whose
closed form, given in Paper~59~\cite{loutey_paper59}, follows from an
identity of Bailey~\cite{bailey1936};\
pre-registered 2026-10-13, record in \texttt{docs/predictions/}, home
Paper~59~\cite{loutey_paper59}).  Along a tree of densities, the class is set by the number of
distinct two-centre densities a kernel couples, and a second kernel
sharing a vertex lifts the curve to a surface;\ neither is the number of
nuclei or electrons, and the rule is an observed pattern across the
tested cases.  Rings are recorded separately (Paper~57, J11).  Four linked densities give the first period not built
from elliptic ones (Paper~59~\cite{loutey_paper59}), so the integrals of
a molecular Hamiltonian, which link two, stay elliptic for any molecule.
\S\ref{sec:atlas} indexes these rows
with the observer rows.

\paragraph{What was a zoo became a catalogued program.}
Early in its development the project produced transcendentals through ad-hoc
calculations and was uncertain whether different appearances of $\pi$
or $\zeta(2)$ were ``the same'' or ``different.''  Today the
transcendentals in its census have named motivic homes, read against the
slot of \eqref{eq:master_mellin} that produced them --- a census and a
reading, not a classification:\ the case-exhaustion behind it is
\textbf{[CONDITIONAL]} on the first fifteen projections.  Within that scope
the operational question --- ``what kind of transcendental can appear
from \emph{this} calculation?'' --- has a first answer.

\paragraph{A note on periods.}  A period in the sense of
Kontsevich--Zagier 2001~\cite{kontsevich_zagier2001} is a complex
number whose real and imaginary parts can be expressed as integrals of
algebraic functions over domains defined by polynomial inequalities
with rational coefficients.  $\pi$, $\log 2$, $\zeta(n)$ for $n \ge
2$, Catalan's constant $G = \beta(2)$, the Dirichlet $\beta(s)$ values
at integer $s$ --- all are periods.  The cyclotomic mixed-Tate
sub-class at level $\le 4$ over $\Z[i, 1/2]$ is, conjecturally, a
small and well-understood corner of the full period algebra.  The
identified transcendentals of Paper~55's census sit in this corner;\
whether every GeoVac period does is not established.

\section{The Forced/Free Seam}
\label{sec:forced_free}
% =====================================================================

The master Mellin engine of \S\ref{sec:periods} catalogues what GeoVac
\emph{generates} (a census and a reading).  An equally important question is what GeoVac
\emph{does not} generate --- in particular, the empirical constants of
physics (the fine-structure constant $\alpha$, the Yukawa couplings,
the generation count $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$, the cosmological constant)
that must enter as external inputs.  The framework's most operationally
useful structural discipline is the clean separation between what
geometry forces and what measurement supplies.

\paragraph{What geometry forces (the structural skeleton).}

The following are forced by the framework's construction,
independent of any empirical input, at the tier stated for each item:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The outer manifold is $\sthree$.  Forced by Bertrand's
    theorem~\cite{bertrand1873} (only $1/r$ and harmonic-oscillator
    central potentials produce closed bound orbits, of which $1/r$ is
    the Coulomb case) plus the $SO(4)$ hidden symmetry of the Coulomb
    problem (Paper~7).
  \item The Hopf bundle structure $\sthree \to S^2$ is forced by the
    maximal-torus reduction of $SU(2) = \sthree$
    (Paper~25)~\cite{loutey_paper25}.
  \item The Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac sector is forced by the
    parallelizability of $\sthree$ (Paper~32)~\cite{loutey_paper32}.
  \item The Wilson $SU(2)$ gauge structure is forced by the
    non-abelian generalization of the abelian Hopf structure
    (Paper~30)~\cite{loutey_paper30}.
  \item The Standard Model gauge group $U(1) \times SU(2) \times

    representation (with $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$ imported), and the standard
    real-spectral-triple axioms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What measurement supplies (calibration data).}

The following are all empirical inputs to the framework, falling
outside any structural derivation it offers:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The fine-structure constant $\alpha^{-1} \approx 137.036$.
    Three spectral homes of distinct type for the framework's combination
    $K = \pi(B + F - \Delta)$ have been established (B as Casimir
    trace, $F = \zeta(2)$ as Fock-Dirichlet, $\Delta = 1/40$ as
    Dirac mode degeneracy), but the combination rule itself remains
    a numerical observation, not derived.  Paper~2 is in the
    Observations folder~\cite{loutey_paper2};\ twelve mechanisms
    have been eliminated for a single-principle derivation of $K$.
  \item The Yukawa coupling values.  A 162-cell PSLQ sweep against
    low-coefficient pure-Tate periods returned zero hits.  The Yukawa Dirichlet ring lives
    in Paper~18~\S~IV.6's sixth tier (parameter-tied Dirichlet,
    sharing no generator with M1/M2/M3, an Observation-level reading).
  \item The generation count $N_{\mathrm{gen}} = 3$.  The
    Hopf-tower-to-representation extension was ruled out as a
    shortcut for forcing $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$:\ the ``3 algebra factors'' and the
    ``3 generations'' are different $3$s in the standard SM
    representation.  A deep structural obstruction;\ no known line
    of attack.
  \item The cosmological constant.  The framework's CC formula
    $\Lambda_{\mathrm{cc}} = 6\phi(2)/\phi(1) \cdot \Lambda^2$
    inherits the standard $\sim 10^{120}$ fine-tuning problem
    (Paper~51 G7).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Where the seam is theorem-bound.}

What makes the forced/free separation operationally useful, rather
than rhetorical, is that in three sectors the boundary between the two
categories is controlled by named results (proof-by-argument, each
under stated assumptions;\ the $\alpha$ sector rests only on an Observation, and the
meta-theorem deciding forcedness in general is open, see below):

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Forced-Count Theorem (Paper~32~\S~VIII):\ given the
    algebra selection (an organizing reading plus an adopted axiom),
    the canonical SM representation with $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$ imported,
    and the real-spectral-triple axioms, these inputs fix the
    \emph{dimension} of the $D_F$ moduli space, not the points within
    it.
  \item The Yukawa Non-Selection Theorem (Paper~32~\S~VIII):\ the framework's
    almost-commutative extension admits a Higgs structure but does
    not autonomously select the Yukawa matrix.
  \item The $\eta$-Trivialization Theorem and AC-Factorization
    Theorem (Paper~18~\S~IV.6):\ the combined heat trace on the AC
    extension factorises cleanly into an outer pure-Tate factor and
    an inner Dirichlet factor in the Yukawa variables, with no
    mixing.
\end{itemize}

On an Observation-level reading these three results locate the
seam:\ values are free, relations are forced.  This is the
project's most operationally useful structural reading.

\section{The Atlas:\ the Price List}
\label{sec:atlas}
% =====================================================================

This section is the spine of the re-centred field guide.  Everything
before it supplies one row or another;\ everything after it is a map
of where the rows are recorded.

\paragraph{The organizing observation, with its credit.}
A compact group has a discrete spectrum (Peter--Weyl).  That is the
mathematical content of the $\sthree$ skeleton:\ the compact regime is
where the label lattice is rational and bit-exact.  The physics reading
is not ours --- Paper~18~\S III credits Rovelli's relational postulate
(finite information for a compact phase space) and Liu--Noui's causal
statement of it for loop gravity --- and the converse is false:\ a
confining potential on non-compact space also gives a discrete spectrum
(Maz'ya--Shubin~\cite{mazya_shubin2005}), and the corpus's own
harmonic-oscillator lattice (Paper~24~\cite{loutey_paper24}) is that
counterexample, $\pi$-free and rational at every cutoff on
$\mathbb{R}^3$.  The operator-level statement is the one that holds in
general:\ discrete spectrum with finite multiplicities if and only if
compact resolvent, which is already an axiom of the spectral triple of
\S\ref{sec:rotation}.  What the corpus adds is the \emph{accounting}:\
each axis released from the compact skeleton toward the continuum
carries an identifiable number class as its price, and those prices can
be classified.

\paragraph{Three kinds of door.}
\emph{Geometric doors} release a label of the skeleton:\ a second
nucleus releases $l$ and $m$ survives;\ bending a triatomic releases
$m$.  Their price is set by the geometry alone.  \emph{Observer doors}
are the projections an observer makes to read the skeleton:\ a measure
on the sphere, a heat kernel, a finite time window, an outcome measure.
Their price is a volume slot, a Mellin slot, or an imported measure.
\emph{Calibration doors} are where a number is consumed rather than
generated:\ the value of the fine-structure constant, the width of the
time window.  The verdict vocabulary is the project's internal one
(CLAUDE.md~\S 1):\ forced, free, wall, or observation;\ in the papers it
is a catalogue label, not a claim about what is fundamental.

\paragraph{The ladder, read down the chemistry rows.}
One centre:\ exact rationals for two-electron integrals.  Two centres:\ weight-one
logarithms and $E_1$, with no $\pi$.  Three centres:\ an elliptic
period, and the class leaves the polylogarithm tower.  Four centres:\
nothing new.  A three-electron chain over two-centre densities:\ a K3
whose holomorphic period is a sum of two products of elliptic periods.
Four linked densities:\ the first period not built from elliptic ones.
Along a tree of densities, the class is set by the number of distinct two-centre densities a kernel
couples, and a second kernel sharing a vertex lifts the curve to a
surface, while rings are recorded separately (Paper~57, J11);\ neither count is the number of nuclei or electrons, and both
are an observed pattern across the tested cases rather than a
derivation.  So two statements that sound opposed do not conflict:\ the
four-body explicit-$r_{12}$ bridge reduces, for $s$ leaves and as a
validated reduction rather than a derivation, to three-body kernels and
then to a two-electron integral of geminal-dressed densities (``four
reduces to three''), which changes the cost of the integral and not its value, while a three-electron chain with two kernels
meeting at one electron climbs a rung.

\paragraph{What the atlas does not do.}
It carries no numbers of its own, it does not rank doors, and it does
not say which rung comes next.  Four rows were pre-registered results
(the fourth centre, the chain, four linked densities and the ring of
three), now housed in Paper~59's closing section with guards;\ one wall (the third
nucleus) bars only closed forms of its radial core by factorization,
with accurate evaluation on the far side;\ and one observer row carries an open
identification (a finite real-time window read as a Euclidean circle,
which Paper~35 adopts rather than derives;\ a bare interval keeps a
continuous spectrum).  Among the open questions the ladder leaves are whether
the chain's generic fibre carries content beyond the K3 and elliptic
periods, where a search at one mass triple found no relation in a basis
that omits dilogarithms and products of elliptic periods, whether
a ring of four densities climbs (Paper~59 tests only the ring of
three), whether the correlated triangle climbs above K3 at $\gamma=3/2$,
and whether the real three-dimensional four-density integral reduces to
its one-dimensional family (Paper~57, J10--J11).

\section{What's Open}
\label{sec:open}
% =====================================================================

The project is honest about scope.  The following are named open
questions, in rough order of how much external infrastructure each
would require:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The next rung of the period ladder}.  The
    three-electron chain's holomorphic period is a sum of two products
    of elliptic periods (\S\ref{sec:atlas});\ whether its generic fibre
    carries content beyond the K3 and elliptic periods (iterated
    structure from the Coulomb kernels), and whether a ring of four
    densities climbs, are two of the open questions, with whether the
    correlated triangle climbs at $\gamma=3/2$ and whether the
    three-dimensional four-density integral reduces to its one-dimensional
    family (Paper~57, J10--J11):\ the first has
    a negative search at one mass triple in a basis that omits
    dilogarithms;\ for the second, Paper~59 tests only the ring of three,
    while five linked densities give no new type.  The first is posed in
    Paper~59's closing section and is not guarded.
  \item \textbf{The $\alpha$ observation}.  Paper~2's combination rule
    $K = \pi(B + F - \Delta)$ matches the empirical $\alpha^{-1}$ to
    $4.8\times 10^{-7}$ relative (and the root of the cubic
    $1/\alpha = K - \alpha^2$ to $8.8 \times 10^{-8}$), but is classified as a
    numerical observation, not a derivation.  Twelve mechanisms
    eliminated for a single-principle derivation;\ the project's
    standing internal reading is that $\alpha$ is a projection
    constant, not a derivable number.
  \item \textbf{The Yukawa values}.  A dedicated PSLQ search
    ruled out the Yukawas as low-coefficient pure-Tate periods.  They
    are Class~1 calibration data and live in Paper~18~\S~IV.6's sixth
    tier.  Whether they admit a deeper non-period derivation is open
    and falls outside the master Mellin engine's census.
  \item \textbf{$N_{\mathrm{gen}} = 3$}.  A deep structural
    obstruction;\ no line of attack is known.
  \item \textbf{The cosmological constant problem}.  Standard fine-tuning
    $\sim 10^{120}$ inherited from the Connes--Chamseddine spectral
    action expansion (Paper~51 G7).  No GeoVac-specific resolution
    proposed.
  \item \textbf{The chemistry correlation wall (W1e)}.  Multi-determinant FCI correlation
    in second-row and heavier diatomic binding is below the
    framework's structural accuracy ceiling.  Six independent
    diagnostic investigations identified the obstruction as
    multi-focal-composition rather than a missing single mechanism.
  \item \textbf{The M2/M3 motivic Galois unification (Q5')}.  Both M2 and
    M3 engage the cyclotomic field $\Q(i) = \Q(\zeta_4)$, but via
    structurally independent mechanisms.  Whether a single Tannakian
    symmetry unifies them is an open mathematical research project
    (Paper~55~\S~7.6).  A deep frontier.
  \item \textbf{Deligne--Milne pro-finite reconstruction}.
    Paper~56 closes the cosmic-Galois inclusion $\Phi:\
    U^{*(n_{\max})} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Aut}^\otimes(\omega)$ at
    finite cutoff via the Tannakian dual of an explicit Hopf algebra.
    The converse equality $\mathrm{Aut}^\otimes(\omega) =
    \lim_\leftarrow U^{*(n_{\max})}$ at the pro-finite limit
    (Deligne--Milne 1982 Theorem~2.11) is the named deep
    frontier;\ every intermediate step nonetheless closed readily at
    finite cutoff, suggesting the pro-finite assembly may prove
    similarly tractable once the right transition-map machinery
    is in place.
  \item \textbf{Non-commutative Mondino--S\"amann (Q2')}.  Paper~49
    constructs the OSLPLS category and the strong-form bridge functor
    (Q1$'$) on the enlarged-substrate chirality-flipping generators at the
    categorical and cocycle-algebra level;\ its metric-level
    $\Lambda$-inheritance is descoped by Paper~45's degeneracy theorem, so
    this is a construction rather than a metric convergence (Paper~49 is
    archived; see \texttt{docs/retired\_papers.md}).  The non-commutative
    analog of
    Mondino--S\"amann pre-length-spaces (a non-commutative Lorentzian
    GH program built from spectral-triple data rather than causal
    diamonds) is the remaining substantial NCG-research target.
  \item \textbf{Inhomogeneous extensions}.  The full
    Fathizadeh--Marcolli generic Robertson--Walker spacetime with
    non-trivial scaling factor $a(t)$ requires either a continuum
    substrate or a time-dependent Camporesi--Higuchi spectral triple,
    outside the discrete-substrate principle.  The closest GeoVac
    analog is the discrete-warped-substrate program of
    Paper~51~\S G4-3 (named follow-on).
  \item \textbf{Full Standard Model spectral triple}.  The Connes
    Standard Model on $\sthree$ alone was closed in May~2026 ---
    positive, but structurally thin;\ the genuine cross-manifold
    construction $\sthree \otimes \mathrm{Hardy}(\sfive)$ is blocked
    at the Coulomb-versus-harmonic-oscillator asymmetry of
    Paper~24~\S~V at layer~4 (modular Hamiltonian of the wedge KMS
    state).  Resolution requires NCG-framework extension.
\end{itemize}

What is \emph{not} open --- but is sometimes asked --- is whether
GeoVac is a new foundation for quantum mechanics.  It is not.  GeoVac
is a discretization framework whose discrete substrate (at Level~1, the
exact label set of the unit-$\sthree$ harmonics) is tied by proven
conformal maps to known continuous quantum mechanics at every level where
the equivalence has been tested (not by an operator convergence of the
graph Laplacian, which fails on $\sthree$, \S\ref{sec:fock}).  The math supports both readings;\
the interpretive question is left to the reader.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

The one-sentence reading:\ \emph{GeoVac is the price list of leaving a
compact world:\ its label lattice is the rational skeleton that
compactness forces, each released axis carries a named number class as
its price, and the atlas of those prices separates what geometry forces
from what measurement supplies.}

The story is a journey from a packing puzzle through Fock~1935's
$\sthree$ identification, through the natural-geometry hierarchy and
its chemistry investigation, through a structural reading as a discrete
spectral triple that instances Marcolli--van Suijlekom 2014 at the
data level and extends it via the forced-graph claim and master
Mellin engine, with
a census of its $\sthree$ spectral transcendentals in cyclotomic
mixed-Tate periods at level $\le 4$, and with the forced-versus-free
seam read (an Observation-level reading) off three named results --- to
the atlas of \S\ref{sec:atlas}, where the spectral triple's census and
the chemistry program's period ladder are rows of one table:\ what each
door releases, what survives it, and what number class it costs.  The
earlier one-sentence reading (``a discrete spectral triple whose periods
catalogue the projective content of its $\sthree$ spectral data'')
remains true of the observer rows;\ it was the identity of June 2026,
and it is now one chapter.

The discipline is the separation:\ what geometry forces gets a name,
a theorem, and a paper;\ what measurement supplies is recorded as
calibration data and stays calibration data unless the framework can
produce a derivation, which so far it cannot.

The project began as an amateur geometric puzzle and has acquired the
status of a discrete spectral triple capable of closing
published-open questions in the math.OA literature.  That trajectory
was AI-augmented at every step:\ implementation, sprint memos,
literature collation, paper drafting, and the internal coordination
machinery were all developed collaboratively with Anthropic's Claude,
with PI direction and quality control.

The invitation:\ come read.  The corpus is dense, but the entry points
are now indexed.  Section~\ref{sec:readers_map} above is the
reader's map.
