\begin{abstract}
We exhibit three complementary boundary-side quantities, framed by
standard CFT$_{3}$-on-$\sthree$ calculations, around the GeoVac
spectral triple, each obtained by
a structurally distinct operation --- (A) from the full, untruncated
continuum spectra of the conformally coupled scalar and
Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac operators on the round $\sthree$, (B) from the
wedge KMS state $\rhoW = e^{-\Kalpha}/Z$ built on the truncated triple
from a posited wedge generator, (C) by
comparing that generator with a continuum one: \emph{(A)} the universal F-theorem
coefficient $F_{\sthree}$, the standard zeta-regularised determinant
$-\tfrac{1}{2}\zeta'_{\Delta}(0)$ of the continuum spectrum, is
re-derived in closed Hurwitz form and agrees with the
Klebanov--Pufu--Safdi (2011) values (symbolically for the Dirac, to
61 digits with PSLQ identification for the scalar) for both the conformally coupled
scalar ($F_{s} = \tfrac{\log 2}{8} - \tfrac{3\zeta(3)}{16\pi^{2}}$) and
the free Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac ($F_{D} = \tfrac{\log 2}{4} +
\tfrac{3\zeta(3)}{8\pi^{2}}$) --- an independent re-derivation of known
continuum values, in which the finite cutoff $\nmax$ never enters;
\emph{(B)} the wedge KMS entropy on the truncated triple has the exact
asymptotic $S(\rhoW) = 2\log\nmax + (\coth 1 - \log(2\sinh 1)) +
O(1/\nmax)$, whose slope $2$ is the exponent of the $\sim\nmax^{2}$
degeneracy of the lowest shell of the posited generator $\Kalpha$ (the
slope is independent of the inverse temperature; the constant is the
unit-$\beta$ value, and $\rhoW$ is not the Bisognano--Wichmann-normalised
state, which would be $e^{-\pi\Kalpha}/Z$ since $\Kalpha = 2J_{z}$;\ an
earlier reading of this $2$ as the dimension of the entangling surface
is withdrawn:\ for a CFT$_{3}$ that surface is a circle); \emph{(C)} the framework's wedge generator $\Kalpha =
J_{\mathrm{polar}}$ (a posited rotation generator, not derived from a
state) shares a discrete integer spectrum and closure at rotation angle
$2\pi$ (its adjoint flow closes at $t = \pi$, since $\Kalpha = 2J_{z}$) with the
\emph{Euclidean} rotation generator whose Wick rotation is the
continuum Casini--Huerta--Myers modular Hamiltonian of a hemispherical
cap of the spatial $S^{2}$ (the latter itself generates a boost-like flow with continuous spectrum);\ this
is a structural \emph{analogy} with the Euclidean generator only, and the identification of $\Kalpha$ as
the modular Hamiltonian of a wedge-restricted state is \textbf{[OPEN]}
(2026-09-28;\ see~\cite{paper42}). The
scalar/Dirac free energies decompose orthogonally into their two
transcendental sectors: $F_{D} + 2 F_{s} = \log(2)/2$ isolates the
$\log 2$ sector (a level-2 cyclotomic period, in the ring of the M3
sub-mechanism of the master Mellin engine of~\cite{paper18}), and
$F_{D} - 2 F_{s} = 3\zeta(3)/(4\pi^{2})$ isolates the $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$
sector (an M1$\times$M3 cross-product), following the engine's
period-ring stratification~\cite{paper18};\ no Seeley--DeWitt (M2)
content appears in these values. This is a decomposition of the continuum KPS values, and the
first application of the master Mellin engine's classification to
independently published (non-GeoVac-internal) values.

to~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011} and are not new; the present paper's
contributions are (i) their independent re-derivation from the continuum
spectra via closed Hurwitz forms (with PSLQ confirmation), (ii) the
orthogonal $\log 2$ / $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ dual-basis decomposition of
those continuum values, classified as M3-ring and M1$\times$M3 content
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp}), and (iii) the exact

none of this is holographic. The CHM comparison
(C) is recorded as an analogy only;\ an earlier version claimed a
finite-cutoff identification at GH-convergence rate, which is withdrawn
(2026-09-28): $\Kalpha$ is posited, and a GH distance between spectral
triples does not bound the convergence of one operator to another.
The bulk-side identifications
(Ryu--Takayanagi minimum surfaces, JLMS bulk-modular reconstruction,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The GeoVac spectral triple~\cite{paper7, paper32} is a finite-cutoff
operator-system truncation of the Camporesi--Higuchi spectral
triple~\cite{camporesi_higuchi1996} on the round $\sthree$. Its
modular structure on the hemispheric wedge has been developed across
Papers~42 and~44--49~\cite{paper42, paper44, paper45, paper46,
paper47, paper48, paper49} (the Krein-carrier extension, formerly
Paper~43, is now Paper~42~\S~11): with the posited geometric BW-$\alpha$
generator $\Kalpha = J_{\mathrm{polar}}$ (integer spectrum) and the
Tomita--Takesaki BW-$\gamma$ modular operator of the Gibbs state built
from it, the flow closes, $\sigma_{2\pi}(\Op) = \Op$, bit-exactly ---
automatically, for any integer-spectrum generator, so this is not a
finite-cutoff identification of the four-witness Wick-rotation theorem
(withdrawn 2026-09-28;\ deriving the generator from a
Camporesi--Higuchi wedge state is open, Paper~42). The Lorentzian
\emph{propinquity} extension to signature $(3,1)$ is descoped:\
Paper~45~\cite{paper45} proves a degeneracy theorem (the natural
Lorentzian-propinquity seminorm vanishes identically, closing that
route) and the strong-form construction~\cite{paper46} is
correspondingly descoped on the natural substrate, so a Lorentzian
quantum-metric convergence remains an open target. What survives is
the norm-resolvent non-compact-carrier closure~\cite{paper47} at the
spectral level and Paper~45's signature-agnostic product-carrier rate.
The Krein--Mondino--S\"amann bridge~\cite{paper48} and the
OSLPLS strong-form bridge~\cite{paper49} proposed a synthetic-Lorentzian
geometric interpretation;\ both are descoped and archived (see the
closing section).

The present paper turns from the math.OA structural content of the
modular machinery to its \emph{physics-side observables}. We ask
what the framework's machinery yields on boundary observables
corresponding to standard CFT$_{3}$-on-$\sthree$ calculations, and
whether the resulting structural pattern exhibits any genuinely new
content beyond a numerical match. Two different objects enter, and we
keep them apart. The partition-function side (A) uses the full
continuum spectra of the scalar and Dirac operators --- the spectra
whose truncations form the GeoVac triple, but not the truncations
themselves. The wedge-state side (B, C) lives on the truncated triple
at finite $\nmax$.

\paragraph{Headline results.}
We establish three complementary boundary-side observables and one
structural decomposition theorem:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Partition function re-derivation}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:partition}): the universal F-theorem coefficient,
evaluated as the zeta-regularised determinant of the full continuum
spectrum in closed Hurwitz form, agrees with the
Klebanov--Pufu--Safdi (2011)~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011} values for
both the conformally coupled scalar (to 61 digits, PSLQ-identified) and
the free Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac (symbolically) on the round $\sthree$. This is an independent re-derivation of
known values, not a finite-cutoff result:\ the truncation $\nmax$
never enters.

\item \textbf{Master Mellin engine decomposition}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp}): the scalar and Dirac partition
functions decompose into linear combinations of $\log 2$ and
$\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$, and a dual-basis pair of combinations isolates each
sector orthogonally;\ in the period-ring stratification of the master
Mellin engine~\cite{paper18} the first is an M3-ring (level-2
cyclotomic) period and the second an M1$\times$M3 cross-product. This is a
decomposition of the continuum KPS values, and the first application of
the engine's classification to independently published
(non-GeoVac-internal) values.

\item \textbf{Wedge KMS entropy structural classification}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wedge_kms}): the von Neumann entropy of $\rhoW$ scales
as $S(\rhoW) \sim 2 \log \nmax$;\ the slope $2$ is the exponent of the
$\nmax(\nmax+1) \sim \nmax^{2}$ degeneracy of the lowest shell of the
posited generator $\Kalpha$ \textbf{[MEASURED]} (degeneracy count,
checked against direct diagonalisation) --- a property of the
truncation, not a continuum geometric dimension. The F-coefficient does \emph{not}
appear in $S(\rhoW)$:\ the continuum spectral-zeta side and the
finite-cutoff wedge-state side encode \emph{different} quantities.

\item \textbf{Casini--Huerta--Myers analogy}

rotation is the continuum CHM modular Hamiltonian of a hemispherical
cap $H_{+}$ of the spatial $S^{2}$,
$K_{\mathrm{cont}} = 2\pi \int_{H_{+}} \xi \cdot T^{00}\,
dA$~\cite{casini_huerta_myers2011};\ $K_{\mathrm{cont}}$ itself
generates a boost-like flow with continuous spectrum, so the analogy is
with the Euclidean generator only.
\textbf{[OPEN]}:\ identifying $\Kalpha$ as a modular Hamiltonian

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The GeoVac spectral triple on $\sthree$}

The Fock projection~\cite{fock1935, paper7} maps the
single-particle hydrogen Hamiltonian conformally to the
Laplace--Beltrami operator on $\sthree$ via stereographic projection
in momentum space. The framework's spectra on the unit $\sthree$ are
exact in $\Q$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scalar Laplacian.} Eigenvalues $\lambda_{n}^{2} =
n(n+2)$ with multiplicity $d_{n} = (n+1)^{2}$ for $n = 0, 1, 2,
\dots$. After the conformal mass shift $\xi R_{\mathrm{scalar}} =
\tfrac{1}{8} \cdot 6 = \tfrac{3}{4}$ for the conformally coupled
scalar, the spectrum becomes $(n + \tfrac{1}{2})(n + \tfrac{3}{2})$
with the same multiplicity.

\item \textbf{Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac.}
Eigenvalues $|\lambda_{n}| = n + \tfrac{3}{2}$ with multiplicity
$g_{n}^{\mathrm{Dirac}} = 2(n+1)(n+2)$ for the single (3d) Dirac
fermion (the standard $\sthree$ massless Dirac, whose $F_{D}$ is
re-derived below from the continuum spectrum; in odd dimension there is no Weyl projection),
$4(n+1)(n+2)$ for the doubled four-component Dirac. Both carry the
same Hurwitz-zeta structure under
analytic continuation~\cite{paper22, paper23}.
\end{itemize}

The truncation at level $\nmax$ retains all $n \le \nmax$ shells and
defines the operator system
$\Op_{\nmax} = P_{\nmax} \Acal P_{\nmax}$ in the
Connes--van~Suijlekom framework~\cite{connes_vs2021}. Convergence
$\Op_{\nmax} \to \Op_{\sthree}$ in van~Suijlekom's state-space
Gromov--Hausdorff distance holds at rate $\gamma_{\nmax} \sim (4/\pi) \log(\nmax) /
\nmax$~\cite{paper38}; the rate constant $4/\pi$ is stated in the
corpus's dual-Coxeter normalisation ($\mathrm{Cas}(\mathrm{ad}) =
h^{\vee}$, under which the $SU(2)$ geodesic distance is
the rotation angle) and is convention-dependent, not canonical:\ on the unit
$\sthree$ the same bound reads $2/\pi = \mathrm{Vol}(S^{2})/\mathrm{Vol}(\sthree)$,
the volume quotient that carries its M1 content in the master Mellin
engine~\cite{paper18} (the earlier ``Hopf-base'' label is a misnomer).
The partition-function values of Sec.~\ref{sec:partition} use the
untruncated ($n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$) spectra and do not depend on this
truncation;\ the truncation enters only the wedge-state side
(Secs.~\ref{sec:wedge_kms}--\ref{sec:chm}).

\subsection{Wedge KMS state}

The hemispheric wedge $W$ corresponds, in the truncated full-Dirac
basis, to the half-space with $m_{j} > 0$ along the
wedge-fixing axis. Per~\cite{paper42} Def~5.1, the posited BW-$\alpha$
wedge generator (its identification as a modular Hamiltonian is open) is
\[
\Kalpha = J_{\mathrm{polar}}
\]
where $J_{\mathrm{polar}}$ is the rotation generator preserving the
wedge, with integer eigenvalues $2m_{j} \in \{1, 3, 5, \dots,
2\nmax - 1\}$ and multiplicity $g(2k + 1) = (\nmax - k)(\nmax - k +
1)$ for $k = 0, \dots, \nmax - 1$. The wedge state used throughout is
the unit-$\beta$ Gibbs state of $\Kalpha$ (not the
Bisognano--Wichmann-normalised state;\ see
Remark~\ref{rem:bw_normalisation})
\[
\rhoW = \frac{e^{-\Kalpha}}{Z}, \qquad Z = \Tr_{\Hilb_{W}}\bigl(e^{-\Kalpha}\bigr).
\]
This is the substrate for the wedge-side observables (B) and (C);
observable (A) uses the continuum spectra only.

\section{Partition function: closed-form re-derivation of the KPS values}
\label{sec:partition}

\subsection{Continuum reference}

For the round unit $\sthree$, the free energies of free CFTs computed
via Hurwitz-zeta regularization
are~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011}:
\begin{align}
F_{s}^{\KPS} &= \frac{\log 2}{8} - \frac{3\zeta(3)}{16\pi^{2}}
\approx 0.063807, \label{eq:Fs_KPS}\\
F_{D}^{\KPS} &= \frac{\log 2}{4} + \frac{3\zeta(3)}{8\pi^{2}}
\approx 0.218960, \label{eq:FD_KPS}
\end{align}
for the conformally coupled scalar and the free massless
Dirac fermion (no Weyl projection in odd dimension), respectively. Both values are expressed in terms of the
functional determinant identities $F = -\tfrac{1}{2}\zeta'_{\Delta}(0)$
(scalar) and $F = \zeta'_{|D|}(0)$ (Dirac), with the analytic
continuation of the divergent spectral sum supplied by the standard
zeta-function regularization. The transcendental signature involves
only $\log 2$, $\zeta(3)$, and $\pi^{2}$.

\subsection{Closed Hurwitz forms of the continuum spectral zeta}

The spectral zeta function of the full (untruncated,
$n = 0, 1, \dots, \infty$) continuum spectrum on $\sthree$ admits a
closed-form Hurwitz expansion. No finite cutoff enters:\ the sums run
over all $n$, exactly as in~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011};\ what is
specific here is only the closed Hurwitz form, the half-integer
identity used throughout the framework's QED modules. For the Dirac case, the Dirichlet
series can be written exactly via the half-integer Hurwitz identity
$\zeta_{H}(s, 3/2) = (2^{s} - 1)\zeta_{R}(s) - 2^{s}$:
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(s) = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} \frac{g_{n}^{\mathrm{Dirac}}}{|\lambda_{n}|^{s}}
= 2\bigl(2^{s - 2} - 1\bigr) \zeta_{R}(s - 2) - \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(2^{s} - 1\bigr) \zeta_{R}(s).
\label{eq:DDirac_closed_form}
\end{equation}
This identity is encoded in the production module
\texttt{geovac/qed\_two\_loop.py} and is verified symbolically across
integer $s$.

For the conformally coupled scalar, the spectral zeta admits the
Hurwitz-asymptotic expansion
\begin{equation}
\zeta_{\Delta}(s) = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} \frac{(n + 1)^{2}}{[(n + 1/2)(n + 3/2)]^{s}}
= \sum_{k \ge 0} g_{k}(s)\, \zeta_{R}(2s + 2k - 2),
\label{eq:zeta_scalar_expansion}
\end{equation}
with $g_{k}(s) = \tbinom{s + k - 1}{k} / 4^{k}$ the
binomial-like coefficient.

\subsection{Closed-form match: scalar}
\label{subsec:scalar_match}

Differentiating~(\ref{eq:zeta_scalar_expansion}) at $s = 0$ and using
$g_{k}(0) = \delta_{k,0}$, $g_{k}'(0) = 1/(k \cdot 4^{k})$ for $k
\ge 1$, plus the standard identities $\zeta_{R}'(-2) = -\zeta(3)/(4
\pi^{2})$, $\zeta_{R}(0) = -\tfrac{1}{2}$, $\zeta_{R}(2k - 2)$
expressible in $\pi^{2k - 2} \cdot \Q$ for $k \ge 2$:
\begin{equation}
\zeta'_{\Delta}(0) = 2\zeta'_{R}(-2) + \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{\zeta_{R}(2k - 2)}{k \cdot 4^{k}}.
\label{eq:zeta_prime_scalar}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form scalar partition function (numerical + PSLQ)]
\label{thm:scalar_bitexact}
\textbf{[NUMERICAL, 61 digits + PSLQ;\ value prior art~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011}]}
The zeta-derivative of the continuum conformal-scalar
spectrum~(\ref{eq:zeta_scalar_expansion}) satisfies
\begin{equation}
F_{s} := -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\zeta'_{\Delta}(0) = \frac{\log 2}{8} - \frac{3\zeta(3)}{16\pi^{2}} = F_{s}^{\KPS},
\end{equation}
in agreement with~(\ref{eq:Fs_KPS}) to 61 digits, with the closed form
identified by PSLQ.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The asymptotic Hurwitz expansion~(\ref{eq:zeta_prime_scalar})
converges geometrically in $k$ at rate $1/4$ per term;
truncation at $k_{\max} = 100$ gives 61+ matching digits at
$\mathrm{dps} = 200$ when compared to $-2 F_{s}^{\KPS}$ via
\texttt{mpmath}. PSLQ at coefficient bound $10^{6}$ and tolerance
$10^{-40}$ finds the integer relation $[8, 2, -3]$:
\[
8 \cdot \zeta'_{\Delta}(0) + 2 \log 2 - 3 \cdot \frac{\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}} = 0,
\]
which is exactly the claimed identity. See driver
\texttt{debug/ads\_track\_a\_scalar\_partition\_function.py} and JSON
\texttt{debug/data/ads\_track\_a\_scalar\_partition\_function.json}
for full computation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed-form match: Dirac}
\label{subsec:dirac_match}

Differentiating~(\ref{eq:DDirac_closed_form}) symbolically at $s = 0$,
using the same Riemann-zeta identities at $s = -2, 0$:
\begin{align}
D'_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(0)
&= \tfrac{1}{2}\log 2 \cdot \zeta_{R}(-2) + \bigl(-\tfrac{3}{2}\bigr) \zeta_{R}'(-2)
- \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log 2 \cdot \zeta_{R}(0) + 0\bigr] \\
&= 0 - \tfrac{3}{2} \cdot \bigl(-\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{2}}\bigr)
- \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \bigl(-\tfrac{\log 2}{2}\bigr)
= \frac{3\zeta(3)}{8\pi^{2}} + \frac{\log 2}{4}.
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form Dirac partition function (symbolic)]
\label{thm:dirac_bitexact}
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC;\ value prior art~\cite{klebanov_pufu_safdi2011}]}
The zeta-derivative of the continuum Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac
spectrum~(\ref{eq:DDirac_closed_form}) satisfies, as an exact
symbolic identity,
\begin{equation}
F_{D} := D'_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(0) = \frac{\log 2}{4} + \frac{3\zeta(3)}{8\pi^{2}} = F_{D}^{\KPS},
\end{equation}
in bit-exact agreement with~(\ref{eq:FD_KPS}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct symbolic computation; \texttt{sympy.simplify(D'(0) - F\_D)}
returns $0$. See driver
\texttt{debug/ads\_track\_a\_dirac\_partition\_function.py}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Master Mellin engine decomposition}

The master Mellin engine of~\cite{paper18}~\S III.7 classifies the
transcendental signatures of GeoVac observables via the three
sub-mechanisms
\[
\mathcal{M}[\Tr(D^{k} \cdot e^{-t D^{2}})], \qquad k \in \{0, 1, 2\},
\]
labeled M1 ($k = 0$), M3 ($k = 1$), and M2 ($k = 2$). We follow the
period-ring stratification of~\cite{paper18}~\S III.7: M1 (measure and
volume factors) lies in $\Q[\pi, \pi^{-1}]$;\ M2 (Seeley--DeWitt
heat-kernel coefficients) lies in the pure-Tate ring
$\bigoplus_{k} \pi^{2k} \cdot \Q$, with no $\log 2$ and no $\zeta(3)$;\
M3 (half-integer and quarter-integer Hurwitz / vertex-parity sums) lies
in the cyclotomic mixed-Tate periods $\mathcal{MT}(\Z[i, 1/2])$ at level
$\le 4$, which contain the odd zeta values and the level-2 cyclotomic
period $\log 2$.

Theorems~\ref{thm:scalar_bitexact} and~\ref{thm:dirac_bitexact}
exhibit $F_{s}$ and $F_{D}$ as linear combinations of $\log 2$ and
$\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$. In the Dirac computation, $\log 2$ enters through
the $2^{s}$ factor of the half-integer Hurwitz identity
$\zeta_{H}(s, 3/2) = (2^{s} - 1)\zeta_{R}(s) - 2^{s}$
(eq.~\ref{eq:DDirac_closed_form});\ it is a level-2 cyclotomic period
and so lies in M3's ring. $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ enters through
$\zeta_{R}'(-2) = -\zeta(3)/(4\pi^{2})$;\ $\zeta(3)$ lies in M3's ring
and $1/\pi^{2}$ in M1's, so it is an M1$\times$M3 cross-product (the

\begin{theorem}[Scalar/Dirac $\log 2$ / $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ orthogonal decomposition]
\label{thm:mellin_decomp}
\textbf{[SYMBOLIC]} The continuum free energies $F_{s}$ and $F_{D}$
project orthogonally onto the $\log 2$ sector (an M3-ring, level-2
cyclotomic period) and the $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ sector (an M1$\times$M3

\begin{align}
F_{D} + 2\,F_{s} &= \frac{\log 2}{2}
\qquad\text{(isolates the $\log 2$ sector; all $\zeta(3)$ cancels),}
\label{eq:log2_isolation}\\
F_{D} - 2\,F_{s} &= \frac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{2}}
\qquad\text{(isolates the $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ sector; all $\log 2$ cancels).}
\label{eq:zeta3_isolation}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting~(\ref{eq:Fs_KPS}) and~(\ref{eq:FD_KPS}):
\[
F_{D} + 2 F_{s} = \bigl(\tfrac{\log 2}{4} + \tfrac{3\zeta(3)}{8\pi^{2}}\bigr)
+ 2 \bigl(\tfrac{\log 2}{8} - \tfrac{3\zeta(3)}{16\pi^{2}}\bigr)
= \tfrac{\log 2}{4} + \tfrac{\log 2}{4} = \tfrac{\log 2}{2},

and similarly $F_{D} - 2 F_{s} = 3 \zeta(3)/(4\pi^{2})$. The two
combinations span a $2$-dimensional space; the projection
coefficients $(1, +2)$ and $(1, -2)$ act as a dual basis for the
$\log 2$ / $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ decomposition on the (scalar, Dirac) plane.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mechanism: degeneracy log on the lowest $\Kalpha$ shell}

The $\Kalpha$ spectrum is bounded above by $2\nmax - 1$ at finite
cutoff. In $\rhoW$ (unit $\beta$), the per-state weight at
$\Kalpha = 1$ (the lowest shell) is $e^{-1}/Z$, while at $\Kalpha = 3$
it drops to $e^{-3}/Z$, a factor of $e^{-2} \approx 0.135$ suppression
per shell. The partition function is therefore dominated by the
lowest shell of multiplicity $n_{\mathrm{eq}} = \nmax(\nmax + 1)
\sim \nmax^{2}$:
\[
Z \approx n_{\mathrm{eq}} \cdot e^{-1} \approx 0.368\, \nmax^{2},
\]
giving effective probability $p_{\mathrm{eq}} \approx 1/n_{\mathrm{eq}}$
per lowest-shell state, and hence
\[
S(\rhoW) \approx -n_{\mathrm{eq}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{eq}} \log p_{\mathrm{eq}}
= \log n_{\mathrm{eq}} \approx 2 \log \nmax.
\]
The two asymptotic limits in $s$-deformation $\rho(s) = e^{-s
\Kalpha}/Z$, $s \to 0^{+}$ (maximally mixed on $\Hilb_{W}$, giving
$S \to \log \dim \Hilb_{W}$) and $s \to \infty$ (maximally mixed on
the lowest shell, giving $S \to \log n_{\mathrm{eq}}$), are
saturated bit-exactly~\cite{debug:bh_phase0}.

\section{Casini--Huerta--Myers analogy}
\label{sec:chm}

\subsection{Continuum CHM hemisphere modular Hamiltonian}

For a CFT$_{3}$ the Lorentzian spatial slice is a two-sphere, and the
Casini--Huerta--Myers construction~\cite{casini_huerta_myers2011} (valid
for any CFT, not only free ones) gives the modular Hamiltonian of the
vacuum restricted to a hemispherical cap $H_{+} \subset S^{2}$ as
\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{cont}} = 2\pi \int_{H_{+}} \xi(x) \cdot T^{00}(x)\, dA(x),
\label{eq:K_chm}
\end{equation}
where $\xi$ is the conformal Killing vector field preserving the causal
development of the cap (vanishing on the entangling surface
$\partial H_{+} = S^{1}$, the boundary circle of the cap), and $T^{00}$
is the local energy density. The CHM map sends the cap's causal
development conformally to $\R \times H^{2}$, carrying the restricted
vacuum to a thermal state at $\beta = 2\pi$ (in units of the curvature
radius)~\cite{casini_huerta_myers2011, bisognano_wichmann1976}. In the
Lorentzian theory $\xi$ generates a \emph{boost-like} conformal flow.
That $K_{\mathrm{cont}}$ has continuous spectrum and that the cap
algebra is of type~III are not statements of CHM or of
Bisognano--Wichmann;\ they are standard facts of algebraic quantum field
theory (under standard conditions local algebras are type~III$_{1}$
factors~\cite{buchholz_dantoni_fredenhagen1987}, and for a type~III$_{1}$
factor Connes' invariant $S(\mathcal{M})$, the intersection of the
modular spectra, is the whole half-line $[0, \infty)$). The
Bisognano--Wichmann $2\pi$ is a KMS parameter, not a periodicity of the
real-time flow. Only the \emph{Euclidean} continuation of this flow has
discrete spectrum and a periodic flow:\ the Euclidean section is
(conformally) $\sthree$, and the continued flow is the rotation of
$\sthree$ that fixes a great circle (the Euclidean image of the
entangling circle), closing at angle $2\pi$;\ the KMS $\beta = 2\pi$ is
exactly that Euclidean periodicity. The comparison
below is with this Euclidean rotation generator, not with
$K_{\mathrm{cont}}$ itself.

= \{1, 3, 5, \dots, 2\nmax - 1\},

\begin{remark}[Normalisation of $\Kalpha$ and of $\rhoW$]
\label{rem:bw_normalisation}
Since $\mathrm{spec}(\Kalpha) = \{2m_{j}\}$, $\Kalpha = 2J_{z}$, where
$J_{z}$ generates rotations by angle $\theta$ about the wedge axis
($e^{-i\theta J_{z}}$). Three consequences. (i) The adjoint flow

of odd integers are even. The $2\pi$ closure above holds but is twice
the minimal period;\ the unitary $e^{it\Kalpha}$ itself first returns

rotation-angle parameter $\theta = 2t$ the adjoint flow closes at
$\theta = 2\pi$, which is the Euclidean periodicity matched in
Observation~\ref{thm:chm_identification}. (ii) A state normalised as in
Bisognano--Wichmann, with KMS parameter $2\pi$ in the rotation angle,

$\rhoW = e^{-\Kalpha}/Z$;\ $\rhoW$ is the unit-$\beta$ Gibbs state of
$\Kalpha$ (inverse temperature $2$ in rotation-angle units). (iii) For
$\rho_{b} = e^{-b\Kalpha}/Z$ one has $Z \simeq \nmax^{2}/(2\sinh b)$ and
$\langle\Kalpha\rangle \to \coth b$, so
$S(\rho_{b}) = 2\log\nmax + b\coth b - \log(2\sinh b) + O(1/\nmax)$:
the slope-$2$ headline is independent of $b$, while the constant

and is \emph{not} a Bisognano--Wichmann-normalised quantity (at
$b = \pi$ the constant would be $\pi\coth\pi - \log(2\sinh\pi) =
0.0136\ldots$).
\end{remark}

\section{Extension to $S^{5}$: structural refinement of the dual-basis decomposition}
\label{sec:s5_extension}

The closed-form Hurwitz evaluation of Sec.~\ref{sec:partition} extends
to the continuum spectra on round $S^{5}$. The Camporesi--Higuchi spectra on
$S^{5}$ are spectrally analogous to $S^{3}$: the conformally coupled

$(2n + 4)(n + 1)(n + 2)(n + 3)/24$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, and the
Weyl Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac has $|\lambda_{n}| = n + 5/2$ with
multiplicity $(n + 1)(n + 2)(n + 3)(n + 4)/12$. The same
zeta-regularised evaluation of these untruncated spectra produces partition function

S$^{3}$ ring by one $\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}$ generator.

\subsection{Closed forms on $S^{5}$ (PSLQ-identified)}

\begin{theorem}[Conformally coupled scalar on $S^{5}$]
\label{thm:scalar_S5}
\textbf{[NUMERICAL, 200 dps + PSLQ; value prior art]}
The zeta-derivative of the continuum conformal-scalar spectrum on
round unit $S^{5}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
F_{s}^{S^{5}} := -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\zeta'_{\Delta_\text{conf}}(0)^{S^{5}}
= -\frac{\log 2}{128} - \frac{\zeta(3)}{128\pi^{2}} + \frac{15\,\zeta(5)}{256\pi^{4}}
\approx -5.74 \times 10^{-3}.
\label{eq:Fs_S5}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The Hurwitz expansion
\begin{equation}
\zeta_{S^{5}}^\text{conf}(s) = \frac{1}{12}\sum_{k \ge 0} g_{k}(s) [\zeta_{R}(2s + 2k - 4) - \zeta_{R}(2s + 2k - 2)]
\end{equation}
with $g_{k}(s) = \binom{s + k - 1}{k}/4^{k}$ follows from the
re-indexed multiplicity structure $v^{2}(v^{2} - 1)/12$ at $v = n + 2$.
At $s = 0$, only $g_{0}(0) = 1$ survives, giving
$\zeta_{S^{5}}^\text{conf}(0) = 0$; the derivative
\begin{equation}
\zeta'_{S^{5}}(0) = \frac{1}{6}[\zeta_{R}'(-4) - \zeta_{R}'(-2)]
+ \frac{1}{12}\sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{\zeta_{R}(2k - 4) - \zeta_{R}(2k - 2)}{k \cdot 4^{k}}
\end{equation}
converges geometrically in $k$. PSLQ at 200~dps and $k_{\max} = 100$
finds the integer relation $[32, -2, -2, 15]$ (applied to
$4\,\zeta'_{S^{5}}(0)$), identifying
\begin{equation}
\zeta'_{S^{5}}(0) = \frac{\log 2}{64} + \frac{\zeta(3)}{64\pi^{2}}
- \frac{15\,\zeta(5)}{128\pi^{4}}.
\end{equation}
The standard identities $\zeta_{R}'(-2) = -\zeta(3)/(4\pi^{2})$ and
$\zeta_{R}'(-4) = +3\zeta(5)/(4\pi^{4})$ deliver the $\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}$
contribution; the $\log 2$ piece arises, as on $\sthree$, from resumming
the Hurwitz-asymptotic tail (an earlier version attributed it to
$\zeta_{R}'(0) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)$, which would also bring in
$\log\pi$;\ withdrawn 2026-09-29).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weyl Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac on $S^{5}$]
\label{thm:dirac_S5}
The zeta-derivative of the continuum Weyl Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac
spectrum on round unit $S^{5}$ satisfies, as an exact symbolic identity,
\begin{equation}
D'_\text{Dirac}(0)^{S^{5}}
= -\frac{3\log 2}{128} - \frac{5\,\zeta(3)}{128\pi^{2}} - \frac{15\,\zeta(5)}{256\pi^{4}}
\approx -0.02163.
\label{eq:DD_S5}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The Dirichlet series
\begin{equation}
D_\text{Dirac}(s)^{S^{5}} = \frac{1}{12} \left[\zeta_{H}(s - 4, 5/2) - \tfrac{5}{2}\zeta_{H}(s - 2, 5/2) + \tfrac{9}{16}\zeta_{H}(s, 5/2)\right]
\label{eq:DD_S5_closed}
\end{equation}
follows from re-indexing $u = n + 5/2$ in the multiplicity
$(n + 1)(n + 2)(n + 3)(n + 4)/12 = (u^{2} - 9/4)(u^{2} - 1/4)/12$. The
half-integer Hurwitz function $\zeta_{H}(s, 5/2)$ has closed form
$(2^{s} - 1)\zeta_{R}(s) - 2^{s} - (2/3)^{s}$. Differentiating each
shift symbolically at $s = 0$ and combining via the multiplicity
coefficients $(1, -5/2, 9/16)$ delivers the claimed result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Log 3 cancellation theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$\log 3$ cancellation on $S^{5}$]
\label{thm:log3_cancellation}
The $(2/3)^{s}$ terms in $\zeta_{H}(s - a, 5/2)$ for
$a \in \{0, 2, 4\}$ contribute, at $s = 0$, factors of
$\log(2/3) = \log 2 - \log 3$ scaled by $(2/3)^{-a} = (3/2)^{a}$. The
coefficient of $\log 3$ in $D'_\text{Dirac}(0)^{S^{5}}$ evaluates to
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{12}\left[\frac{81}{16} - \frac{5}{2}\cdot\frac{9}{4} + \frac{9}{16}\cdot 1\right]
= \frac{1}{12}\left[\frac{81 - 90 + 9}{16}\right] = 0
\end{equation}
exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The structural combination $(1, -5/2, 9/16)$ encoded in the
multiplicity polynomial $(u^{2} - 9/4)(u^{2} - 1/4) = u^{4} - (5/2) u^{2} + 9/16$
aligns the three shift operators $\{\zeta_{H}(s, 5/2),
\zeta_{H}(s-2, 5/2), \zeta_{H}(s-4, 5/2)\}$ such that all $\log 3$
contributions cancel exactly. The $S^{5}$ values stay in the M-engine ring $\{\log 2, \zeta(3)/\pi^{2},
\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}\}$, even though the naive Hurwitz expansion suggests
a contribution from $\log 3$ --- a level-3 cyclotomic period, which
would lie outside M3's level-$\le 4$ ring
$\mathcal{MT}(\Z[i, 1/2])$ in the stratification of~\cite{paper18}. The
same mechanism extends the ring statement from $S^{3}$ to $S^{5}$:\
$\log 2$ in M3's ring, and $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$, $\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}$ as
M1$\times$M3 cross-products.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Dual-basis projection: structural-specificity to $S^{3}$}

Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp} of Sec.~\ref{sec:partition} establishes
that on $S^{3}$, the (scalar, Dirac) pair admits a dual-basis
projection onto its two sectors: $F_{D} + 2 F_{s} = \log(2)/2$ (the
$\log 2$ sector only), $F_{D} - 2 F_{s} = 3\zeta(3)/(4\pi^{2})$ (the
$\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ sector only). On $S^{5}$,
the analogous statement \emph{fails}.

\begin{proposition}[Dual-basis non-extension to $S^{5}$]
\label{prop:dual_basis_S5}
The Paper~50 Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp} dual-basis projection
does not extend to $S^{5}$. The M-engine ring on $S^{5}$ is
three-dimensional ($\log 2$, $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$, $\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}$),
but the (scalar, Dirac) plane is two-dimensional. The system
$a F_{s}^{S^{5}} + b F_{D}^{S^{5}} = c \cdot (\text{single ring generator})$
is over-determined: imposing isolation of one generator requires three
linear conditions on two parameters $(a, b)$, which has no nontrivial
solution.

Explicitly, attempting to isolate $\log 2$:
\begin{align}
\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}\text{ coefficient} = 0 \;&\Longrightarrow\; a + 5b = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a = -5b,\\
\zeta(5)/\pi^{4}\text{ coefficient} = 0 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 15a - 15b = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a = b,
\end{align}
which are inconsistent unless $b = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Continuation of the $F$-coefficient ladder through $S^{11}$; dual-basis non-extension]
\label{rem:general_d_conjecture}
Two statements must be distinguished here: the \emph{single-species}
in-ring closure of $F$ (which continues to all odd $d$ tested), and the
\emph{dual-basis projection} of Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp} (which is
special to $S^{3}$). The structural-specificity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp} concerns only the second.

\textbf{Single-species in-ring closure (continues through $S^{11}$).}
For each free species individually, $F = -\tfrac{1}{2}\zeta'_{\Delta}(0)$
on round $S^{d}$ ($d$ odd) lies in the ring
\[
\mathcal{R}_{d} = \{\log 2\} \cup
\{\zeta(2k+1)/\pi^{2k} : k = 1, \dots, (d-1)/2\}
\]
of dimension $(d-1)/2 + 1$ (top atom $\zeta(d)/\pi^{d-1}$). This is
verified (symbolically or by PSLQ identification) at $S^{3}$, $S^{5}$
(Theorems~\ref{thm:scalar_S5},~\ref{thm:dirac_S5}), and --- by direct
high-precision computation (Door~1 graduation, 2026-06-03; $260$ dps,
frozen basis, reconstruction error $\lesssim 10^{-261}$, decoy-controlled)
--- at $S^{7}$, $S^{9}$, and $S^{11}$. The conformally coupled scalar on
$S^{11}$, for instance, is
\begin{align}
\zeta'_{\mathrm{sc}, S^{11}}(0) =\;
&-\tfrac{7}{131072}\log 2
 -\tfrac{3897}{45875200}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}
 -\tfrac{485}{8257536}\tfrac{\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}} \notag\\
&+\tfrac{609}{1310720}\tfrac{\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}}
 +\tfrac{425}{262144}\tfrac{\zeta(9)}{\pi^{8}}
 +\tfrac{1023}{524288}\tfrac{\zeta(11)}{\pi^{10}},
\end{align}
and the free Weyl Camporesi--Higuchi Dirac value is symbolic-exact in the
same ring. A decoy test assigns Catalan $G$, $\log 3$, and unscaled
$\zeta(3)$ coefficient exactly zero, confirming a clean signature:\
$\log 2$ (M3's ring, level~2) and the M1$\times$M3 cross-products
$\zeta(2k+1)/\pi^{2k}$.

\emph{Erratum.} An earlier version of this remark reported $S^{7}$ as a
structural non-match (``no PSLQ relation in the tested bases''). That was a
\emph{false negative} from an under-resolved search. The genuine $S^{7}$
scalar relation
\[
\zeta'_{\mathrm{sc}, S^{7}}(0) =
-\tfrac{1}{512}\log 2
-\tfrac{41}{15360}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}
+\tfrac{5}{1024}\tfrac{\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}}
+\tfrac{63}{2048}\tfrac{\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}}
\]
has integer coefficients of order $10^{4}$, but locking it requires the
value to $\gtrsim 200$ digits; a $30$-digit value run against
$\mathrm{maxcoeff} = 10^{16}$ sits below the resolution threshold and
returns spurious non-detection. Verified independently at $200$ dps
(reconstruction error $\approx 2.4 \times 10^{-202}$) and reproduced by the
Door~1 driver at $260$ dps. The ladder therefore \emph{closes} (these are
closed-form evaluations of continuum spectral zeta functions): every
odd rung $S^{3}, \dots, S^{11}$ has a rational-coefficient closed form in
$\mathcal{R}_{d}$, with no transcendental outside $\mathcal{R}_{d}$.

\textbf{Dual-basis projection (special to $S^{3}$, unaffected).} Isolating
a single ring generator using only the $(\text{scalar}, \text{Dirac})$ pair
requires $\dim \mathcal{R}_{d} = 2$, which holds at $S^{3}$ alone. For
$d \ge 5$ the projection is over-determined
(Prop.~\ref{prop:dual_basis_S5}); the related cross-species ladder
recursion
$\mathrm{scalar} - m \cdot \mathrm{Dirac} = c_{d} \cdot \mathrm{Dirac}_{S^{d-2}}$
likewise closes at $S^{5}$ only, with the top-atom ratio
$\mathrm{scalar}_{\mathrm{top}}/\mathrm{Dirac}_{\mathrm{top}} =
2^{-(d-5)/2}$ hitting the required value ($m = 1$) at $d = 5$ alone
(Door~1 graduation). Thus the $F$-coefficients are in-ring at every odd
$d$, but the two-species dual basis that \emph{spans} the ring is special
to $S^{3}$. Supplying the missing higher-spin free species ---
$(d-1)/2 - 1$ of them at $S^{d}$ --- to span $\mathcal{R}_{d}$ remains the
natural continuation, and is the actual structural-specificity content of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Other free-field CFTs.} The scalar and Dirac cases
exhibit the orthogonal $\log 2$ / $\zeta(3)/\pi^{2}$ decomposition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mellin_decomp}. Does the same projection structure
extend to higher-spin partition functions on $\sthree$, in

The Maxwell partition function on round $\sthree$ is the natural

\item \textbf{State-space-GH-rate convergence statement.} The
spectral triples converge at the state-space Gromov--Hausdorff level
(Paper~38);\ Observation~\ref{thm:chm_identification} does not inherit
an operator-level rate from this (withdrawn 2026-09-28). Nor does
Sec.~\ref{sec:partition} define or use a finite-cutoff free energy

$F^{\KPS}$, with a rate $|F_{\nmax} - F^{\KPS}| \le C \cdot

Berezin-reconstruction framework of Paper~38; this is open.

\item \textbf{Bulk-side identification (Prop.~\ref{prop:bulk_blocked}).}
The JLMS bulk-modular Hamiltonian identification, the RT
entanglement-entropy formula, and the AdS$_{4}$ dual geometry
remain open. Importing AdS$_{4}$ / H$^{4}$ infrastructure is the
named major structural extension that would reach these; it is
beyond sprint scale.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Holographic Weyl anomaly $\leftrightarrow$ Connes--Chamseddine spectral action: both live in M2.}
A structural classification shared with the AdS/CFT literature that the framework
obtains directly --- without additional computation --- is the M2-sector
placement of the holographic Weyl anomaly and the
Connes--Chamseddine spectral action.

Henningson and Skenderis~\cite{henningson_skenderis1998} derived the Weyl
anomaly of CFT$_{d}$ on a manifold $M$ via the bulk AdS$_{d+1}$
gravitational action regularized at a finite radial cutoff. For even
$d$, the result reproduces the boundary central charges
$a_{\text{CFT}}, c_{\text{CFT}}$ as specific combinations of bulk
gravitational coefficients. The bulk integral is computed via the
Riemannian curvature data of AdS, evaluated through a heat-kernel
expansion in the AdS-radius cutoff $\epsilon$.

The Connes--Chamseddine spectral action~\cite{chamseddine_connes1997}
$\mathrm{Tr}\,f(D/\Lambda)$, on any almost-commutative spectral triple,
has heat-kernel expansion coefficients given by Seeley--DeWitt
coefficients $a_{k}(D^{2})$. For the GeoVac spectral triple on round
$\sthree$, the Seeley--DeWitt coefficients of $\DCH^{2}$ are exact,
with no higher coefficients (Paper~28~\cite{paper28},
Sprint MR-B closed form, two-term-exact theorem);\ they carry an
overall $\sqrt{\pi}$ from the heat-trace prefactor
$\mathrm{Vol}(\sthree)(4\pi t)^{-3/2}$, and once that standard
normalisation is divided out they lie in the pure-Tate M2 ring
$\bigoplus_{k}\pi^{2k}\cdot\Q$ of the master Mellin engine
(Paper~18~\cite{paper18} \S III.7). The spectral action
therefore lives in the M2 sector.

\textbf{Structural classification.} Both the Henningson--Skenderis
holographic Weyl anomaly and the Connes--Chamseddine spectral action
sit in the master Mellin engine's M2 sub-mechanism (Seeley--DeWitt
heat-kernel ring $\bigoplus_{k}\pi^{2k}\cdot\Q$, after the standard
$\sqrt{\pi}$ heat-trace normalisation is divided out). This is a
shared heat-kernel \emph{classification}, not a computed identity: both
are heat-kernel-trace observables ($\mathcal{M}[\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-tD^{2}}]$
at operator order $k = 2$), the HS quantity computed on the bulk-gravity
side and the CC quantity on the finite spectral triple. The framework
does not compute the HS holographic anomaly; the content is the
taxonomic placement of both in the M2 ring. The framework's
discrete-cutoff realization (Paper~38~\cite{paper38} state-space GH
convergence at the displayed rate $(4/\pi)\log n/n$, in the
dual-Coxeter convention;\ $2/\pi$ on the unit $\sthree$) is the
operator-system-level home of the
CC side.

This is the framework's most direct contact point with established
holographic computations in the M2 sector and is a corollary of the
master Mellin engine case-exhaustion theorem (Paper~32 \S VIII), not
a new computation. The substantive content is the explicit
identification of HS's bulk computation as an M2-sector observable,
placed in the same heat-kernel ring as the framework's spectral action
on $\sthree$ --- a structural classification, not a reproduction of the
holographic computation.

\paragraph{Relation to the TT-bar cut-off AdS picture.}
Hartman, Kruthoff, Shaghoulian, and Tajdini~\cite{hartman_kruthoff_shaghoulian_tajdini2019}
showed that sphere partition functions of TT-bar-deformed large-$N$
holographic CFT$_d$ in $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ non-perturbatively
match bulk computations of AdS$_{d+1}$ at finite radial cut-off. A
parallel with the framework's spectral cutoff $\nmax$ is tempting, but
the two cut-off structures are \emph{categorically distinct}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{TT-bar (Hartman et al.~2019):} a \emph{deformation of the
action}. The TT-bar operator $\mathcal{O}_{T\bar T}(\mu)$ modifies the
field theory's UV at finite $\mu$; the deformed CFT has a different
energy spectrum and a different action. The bulk dual is AdS at finite
radial cutoff $r_c$ related to $\mu$ via a (dimension-dependent)
explicit formula.

\item \textbf{GeoVac $\nmax$ (this paper):} a \emph{truncation of the
spectrum}, not the action. The framework's discrete spectral triple at
$\nmax$ keeps the underlying free CFT and its action intact, but
restricts the mode sum to $n \le \nmax$. The F values of
Sec.~\ref{sec:partition}
(Theorems~\ref{thm:scalar_bitexact},~\ref{thm:dirac_bitexact}) are
computed from the untruncated spectrum, not as a limit of these
truncations; the intermediate $\nmax$ values are not a TT-bar-deformed
CFT but genuine spectral truncations of the original free CFT.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{At the partition-function-value level,} the two regularizations
might still be related, but this paper constructs no finite-cutoff
$Z_{\nmax}$ (a sharp mode truncation of $\log Z$ diverges with $\nmax$
and would need renormalisation). Whether a suitably renormalised
$Z_{\nmax}^{\text{GeoVac}}(R)$ equals $Z_{\text{TT-bar}}(\mu(\nmax, R),
R)$ for some $\mu(\nmax, R)$ is an open question. A positive answer would identify the framework's
spectral cutoff with a specific TT-bar-deformation strength; a negative
answer would crystallize that the two cutoffs are structurally
incompatible at the partition-function level.

\paragraph{Not holographic.}
\label{para:category_iii_position}
The framework contains no bulk:\ its $\sthree$ is the Fock-projection
image of the Coulomb spectrum (Paper~7~\cite{paper7}), not the conformal
boundary or future-infinity boundary of any four-dimensional geometry,
and there is no holographic dictionary mapping bulk fields to boundary
operators. It is therefore not holographic, and it is neither an
instance nor a refutation of AdS/CFT or of dS/CFT~\cite{strominger2001,
maldacena2002} (in whose Euclidean form the CFT$_{3}$-on-$\sthree$
partition function is the boundary datum). The F values of
Sec.~\ref{sec:partition} are the standard zeta-regularised determinants
of the continuum operators, evaluated here in closed Hurwitz form.
Separately, Paper~38~\cite{paper38} shows that the truncated triples
converge to that continuum spectral triple in state-space
Gromov--Hausdorff distance;\ that convergence is a statement about the
triples and does not produce the F values. An earlier version of this
paragraph proposed a third, non-holographic ``correspondence'' category
with state-space GH convergence as its mechanism and credited the
discrete triple with the boundary data;\ that reading is withdrawn
(2026-09-29), since the F values never pass through the truncation.

\paragraph{Areas yet to be explored.}
Beyond the immediate catalogue targets, several adjacent directions
extend the framework's reach into established physics:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Holographic central charges} ($a$- and $c$-anomalies in
even $d$). Henningson-Skenderis~\cite{henningson_skenderis1998} and
follow-ups give closed forms for the holographic dual of free CFT
central charges. The framework's spectral-action machinery
(Paper~28~\cite{paper28}) potentially provides a closed-form
evaluation; the connection to Paper~24's Bargmann-Segal $S^{5}$ is
unexplored.

\item \textbf{Entanglement-entropy universal terms.} The continuum
Casini-Huerta-Myers~\cite{casini_huerta_myers2011} entanglement
entropy of a ball region has a universal subleading constant equal
to $-F$. Connecting this to the framework's wedge KMS structure
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wedge_kms}) and to the master Mellin engine
decomposition would strengthen the F-as-engine-signature reading.

\item \textbf{Higher-rank Lie groups beyond $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.} The
Paper~40 unified GH-convergence theorem~\cite{paper40} extends the
state-space GH convergence machinery to all simple compact Lie groups. A natural
catalogue extension is CFT-on-$G$ partition functions for various
$G$ (heat-kernel zeta on $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, etc.)
where the framework's GH-rate constant $4/\pi$ is universal but the
specific transcendental ring may differ.

\item \textbf{Spectral-action coefficients.} The
Connes-Chamseddine~\cite{chamseddine_connes1997} spectral action
$\mathrm{Tr}\,f(D/\Lambda)$ has heat-kernel expansion coefficients
that classify the gravitational + matter content of an
almost-commutative spectral triple. These coefficients are exactly
the master Mellin engine M2 sector. The catalogue could include
M-engine readings of standard spectral-action calculations on
$\sthree$, $S^{5}$, and higher.

\item \textbf{Lorentzian-side observables.} The catalogue here is
boundary-side Riemannian. The Lorentzian papers (Paper~42; Papers~46--49,
now archived or descoped) posited a wedge modular Hamiltonian (its
identification remains open) and studied Pythagorean orthogonality and
twin-paradox-as-quantum-information structures. Cataloguing
Lorentzian-side CFT observables (thermal partition functions on
$\sthree \times \mathbb{R}_{t}$, Rindler-wedge entropies, etc.) is a
parallel direction.
\end{itemize}

Each catalogue entry either (i) classifies independently published
continuum values under the master Mellin engine, (ii) extends a structural cancellation
theorem (like log~3 cancellation on $S^{5}$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:log3_cancellation}), or (iii) identifies a wall
where the simple ring fails (like the squashed-$\sthree$ deformed-Hurwitz
case, not in the MR-B Jacobi-$\vartheta_{2}$ closed form). Each outcome is paper-worthy
in its own right; the cumulative catalogue would constitute a
structural classification of free-field CFT-on-sphere partition
functions via the master Mellin engine.
