# GeoVac: Pauli Counts Exactly Linear in Qubit Number for Molecular Quantum Simulation

I built a framework that produces qubit Hamiltonians whose Pauli count is exactly linear in the qubit count across molecules, with 51x-317x fewer Pauli terms than Gaussian-basis Jordan-Wigner encodings at matched qubit count (H2O, n_max = 1-3; identity included on both sides). The library covers **37 systems** (35 composed molecules plus He and H2) across three periodic-table rows, including multi-center systems (CO, N2, F2) and transition-metal hydrides (ScH, TiH).

The concrete result: a lithium hydride Hamiltonian on 30 qubits with 838 Pauli terms, where a comparable Gaussian cc-pVDZ encoding produces 63,519 (76x). Across the 35 composed molecules the Pauli count is **exactly linear in the qubit count**, N_Pauli = 27.90 x Q for main-group molecules (30.03 x Q for the d-block hydrides) at fixed basis, compared to Q^3.9-4.3 system-size scaling for Gaussian Jordan-Wigner. Against minimal-basis STO-3G, composed LiH is near parity (838 vs 907 raw Pauli terms, and 3.0x MORE than the qubit-reduced 276; 1-norm 34.0 vs 34.3 Ha) -- the decisive wins are the exact Q-linearity and the reduction against correlating bases, not the raw count. (All figures under the exact global-M_L rule, 2026-08-29; the earlier 334 / Q^2.5 / 11.11 x Q figures were a retired sign error.)

The sparsity is structural, not approximate. GeoVac assigns each electron group in a molecule -- core pairs, bond pairs, lone pairs -- its own natural coordinate system (hyperspherical or prolate spheroidal), then couples them through screened Coulomb integrals. Angular momentum selection rules enforced by the Gaunt integrals produce exact zeros in the electron repulsion integrals, giving block-diagonal two-electron terms with no cross-block entries. This structure survives Jordan-Wigner encoding and is compatible with all downstream optimizations.

For near-term quantum computing, this matters because the measurement budget in VQE scales with the number of Pauli terms. Fewer terms means proportionally fewer measurements to estimate the energy. The 1-norm (which controls shot noise and Trotter error) is near STO-3G's for LiH (34.0 vs 34.3 Ha); for the atomic series it grows as Q^1.78.

I want to be upfront about limitations. GeoVac's classical accuracy is below production quantum chemistry -- 5% equilibrium bond length error for LiH, 12% for BeH2, 19.4% for water, compared to sub-0.1% for Gaussian methods. The accuracy is best suited for single-point quantum simulation at fixed geometries (0.20% energy error for LiH at n_max=3), not geometry optimization. The Pauli advantage is against raw Jordan-Wigner -- density fitting and tensor hypercontraction use block-encoding representations that bypass the Pauli picture entirely, so the comparison is specifically relevant to VQE and other variational near-term methods. For atoms, the 2D variational solver reaches 0.022% for He (variational), and 0.004% with a Schwartz cusp extrapolation (non-variational, marginally below exact); zero free parameters.

The Hamiltonians are available as a standalone Python package (not on PyPI; installs from the GitHub repository, see the README) with export to OpenFermion, Qiskit, and PennyLane. The underlying theory is documented in a paper series on GitHub with DOI-stamped Zenodo releases.
