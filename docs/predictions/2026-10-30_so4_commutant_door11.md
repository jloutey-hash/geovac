**Commit status:** committed on its own, before any computation it scores.
**Rests on:** `docs/atlas.md` door 11 (added columns, v5.21.25); Paper 58 `thm:abelian_residue` (the m rule); Paper 25

**Guardrail check (Papers 8-9):** not in scope. Nothing here encodes a molecule in a single-centre basis; the

## Why

The atlas's door-11 cell says SO(4) breaks to the SO(2) of rotations about the bond axis, and leaves OPEN whether
the U(1) that survives is the one Paper 25's Wilson structure is built on. SO(4) has six generators, the rotations
L and the Runge-Lenz components A. The Hopf fibre is generated by L_z + A_z, a torus of one SU(2) factor; the
rotation about the bond axis is generated by L_z alone, a torus that mixes both factors. These are different
subgroups. The question has a sharp form: which directions X in so(4) satisfy [V_B, X] = 0, where V_B is the
second nucleus's potential.

## Method (fixed before scoring)

- Basis: hydrogenic (n, l, m), Z_orb = 1, n ≤ 3 (14 states), complex Y_lm with Condon-Shortley phases, the same
  convention as the cross-centre builder's Gaunt coefficients (checked: the builder's z angular factor matches ours
  to 1e-16). Because every generator is block-diagonal in n, the commutator in the truncated basis is the exact
  restriction of the full one; the cutoff costs nothing.
- Generators: L from the ladder algebra; A from Pauli's shell identity P_n r P_n = -(3n/2) P_n A P_n (Z = 1), the
  dipole matrix built from the builder's own radial polynomials. Checks passed: [L,L] = iL, [L,A] = iA, [A,A] = iL
  and L² + A² = n² - 1 all to 2e-14.
- Operator scored: V_B = compute_cross_center_vne(Z_orb = 1, states, Z_nuc = 1, R = 2 bohr, L_max = 6). Also
  R = 4 and R = 1, reported.
- Commutant: the null space of X ↦ [V_B, X] on span of the six generators, by SVD of the 196 × 6 matrix of
  vectorized commutators. Relative singular values are divided by ‖V_B‖ · max‖X_a‖. A direction counts as
  surviving if its relative singular value is below 1e-10, and as broken if above 1e-3.
- Driver: `debug/so4_commutant.py` (`controls` mode run; `score` mode not run).

## What I know before comparing (disclosed)

- **Controls (measured, non-scored; `python debug/so4_commutant.py controls`):**
  - An SO(4)-invariant operator (H itself, diagonal −1/2n²): commutant dimension 6, all six relative singular
    values 0 to machine precision. This sets the "zero" level: below 1e-10 is zero.
  - A central operator (the finite-nucleus correction of door 24, uniform sphere R = 0.05): commutant dimension
    exactly 3, the null vectors are L_x, L_y, L_z to 1e-3 in every component, and the three broken directions have
    relative singular values 0.166 each; the smallest relative commutator among the A's is 0.62. This sets the
    "broken" level: 1e-3 is two orders below the control's broken values.
- No door-11 commutant has been computed. The m rule (Paper 58) already implies [V_B, L_z] = 0, because the builder
  is m-diagonal; so SY1's content is the other five directions, and SY2's is one specific combination.
- What group theory says without computing: the centralizer of V_B must contain L_z. Whether any A direction or
  any mixture survives is what the computation decides. I expect none, because V_B couples l within a shell with
  coefficients fixed by Gaunt and radial integrals that do not follow the SO(4) pattern of A.

## Predictions

**SY0 (controls; measured, PASS).** Dimension 6 for the invariant operator; dimension 3 with null space {L_x, L_y, L_z}
for the central one.

**SY1 (one survivor).** The commutant of V_B at R = 2 is one-dimensional: exactly one relative singular value below
1e-10, the other five above 1e-3, and the null vector has |c_Lz| > 0.999.
- Falsifier: a second direction below 1e-10, or a null vector with weight on any A or on L_x, L_y above 0.03.
- Probability 0.85.

**SY2 (the Hopf U(1) does not survive).** ‖[V_B, L_z + A_z]‖ / ‖V_B‖ ≥ 1e-3, so Paper 25's fibre generator is broken.
- Falsifier: the ratio below 1e-3.
- Probability 0.9. The bound is the control's broken level with two orders of margin (the control gave 0.62 for the
  smallest A commutator). If SY1 holds, SY2 follows, since L_z + A_z is not a multiple of L_z; it is listed on its
  own because it is the cell's question.

**SY3 (reported, not gated).** The commutant dimension and its null vector are the same at R = 4 and R = 1.
- Reason: which directions commute is set by the m rule and the l-coupling pattern, not by the distance.
- Probability 0.9.

**Gate.** GO if SY0 passes and SY1 and SY2 both hold. BORDERLINE if one misses. STOP if both miss.

## What a GO means and does not mean

- Means: releasing the bond axis keeps one direction of the host symmetry, the rotation about the axis, and that
  direction is not the Hopf fibre. The atlas's door-11 cell then reads "the surviving U(1) is L_z, with fixed points
  on the axis; the Hopf U(1), free on S³, is broken", and the Wilson column records that Paper 25's identification
  of the gauge phase with the fibre does not transfer to a second nucleus.
- Does not mean: anything about a Wilson theory on the two-centre graph. A U(1) Wilson action can be written on any
  graph; what is tested is which geometric subgroup remains, not whether a gauge theory exists.

## Caps

- Z_orb = Z_nuc = 1 and one radial family (hydrogenic). The pattern is a symmetry statement and should not depend
  on these, but only R = 1, 2, 4 at Z = 1 are run.
- n ≤ 3. Exact restriction for the commutator (generators are block-diagonal in n), so no cutoff dependence is
  claimed or expected.
