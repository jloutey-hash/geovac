## Object

Slater functions, AB's two geometries: B at the origin, C at (0, 0, D1), A at D2 (sin g, 0, cos g), x = cos g,
sin g >= 0.

    DX = (1s_A 1s_A | 1s_B 3d_xz,C),      3d_xz,C    = N_xz x_C z_C e^{-zC rC},            (m = 1, in the ABC plane)
    DD = (1s_A 1s_A | 1s_B 3d_x2-y2,C),   3d_x2-y2,C = N_x2y2 (x_C^2 - y_C^2) e^{-zC rC},  (m = 2)
    N_xz = sqrt(15/(4 pi)) R_n,  N_x2y2 = sqrt(15/(16 pi)) R_n,  R_n = sqrt((2 zC)^7 / 720).

3d_yz and 3d_xy at C give zero against a 1s_A pair (odd under y -> -y): R2 gives exactly 0, R1 1.1e-17 and
-1.5e-18 (A1, x = 1/2). Cases A1 (D1 = 9/5, D2 = 13/5, zB = 13/10, zC = 1, zA = 9/10) and A2 (3/2, 12/5, 1, 6/5, 4/5).

**Reduction (structure derived; coefficients fixed in Phase 2 and checked by AX0 (ii) and P1).** About B,
x_C = x, y_C = y, z_C = r t - D1. So the DX density is AQ's m = 1 density with e^{-zC s} replaced by
e^{-zC s}(r t - D1), whose Legendre coefficients are r (T d)_k - D1 d_k (AD's multiply-by-t operator T on AB's
d_k). The DD density is r^2 sin^2(theta) cos(2 phi) e^{-zB r - zC s}: only the m = 2 term of the addition theorem
survives, and its multipole follows from AB's d_k by applying sqrt(1 - t^2) P_l^m = (P_{l+1}^{m+1} - P_{l-1}^{m+1})
/ (2l + 1) (no Condon-Shortley phase) twice. Hence

    DX = sum_{L >= 1} G_L P_L^1(x),   DD = sum_{L >= 2} K_L P_L^2(x),

both against AB's kernel (NA - Y + zA dY, the 1s potential of A), unchanged.

- **P3 (decay).** In AQ's instrument, beta_eff(220) lies in the toy band T +- 0.17, with one sign for L >= 20:
  **DX (P_L^1): A1 [-5.35, -5.01], A2 [-5.31, -4.97]; DD (P_L^2): A1 [-6.43, -6.08], A2 [-6.38, -6.03].**
  Reason (derived before computing, AD's mechanism): a degree-2 harmonic h at C gives a non-analytic part
  |a|^7 h(a), a = A - C = (D2 sin g, 0, D2 x - D1). At x*, |a|^2 = 0, so a_x^2 = -a_z^2 and
  h_xz = a_x a_z = +-i a_z^2, h_x2-y2 = a_x^2 = -a_z^2, both nonzero exactly when a_z(x*) = (D2^2 - D1^2)/(2 D1)
  != 0, i.e. D1 != D2, equivalently x* != 1, where the branch point would meet the endpoint (true in both
  cases). In fact h_x2-y2 = a_x^2 = D2^2 (1 - x^2) is exactly the P_L^2 prefactor, leaving AB's own |a|^7, and DX
  leaves (D2 x - D1)|a|^7: given AD, P3 is near-structural, and its live content is the basis shift. So both keep the 7/2 branch; the prefactors sqrt(1-x^2) and 1-x^2 are
  analytic at x*, and each order of the associated basis costs one power of L (T1, T2). What P3 tests: that the
  coefficient does not cancel. If it cancelled, the 9/2 markers sit near -6.26/-6.20 (DX) and -7.34/-7.27 (DD).
  **Disclosed limit:** P3 cannot tell a d factor from AQ's p factor for DX (same basis, same branch); an engine
  that dropped z_C would pass P3. P1 catches that. Probability 0.85.
