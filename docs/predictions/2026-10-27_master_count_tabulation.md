**Commit status:** committed on its own, before any computation in this file.

## The question

Paper 59's one-mass slice is a candidate for a Boys-function analogue. How many master integrals does that family
have, and in how many scaled variables? The brief's gate is GO if there are at most 4 masters in at most 2 scaled
variables. The count is made by critical points (Euler characteristic), not by deriving any operator.

## Where the "4" comes from (checked against Paper 59 before registering)

- **The object.** Paper 59 `sec:reduction`, eq:laplace:

    N(D) = c1^{-1/2} Int_1^inf e^{-Dx} dx / sqrt((x^2-1)(rho x^2 + 1 - rho)),   rho = c2/c1,   L = sqrt(c1) N.

  It is the slice N(D) = M(D, 0) of the two-offset fibre M (eq:besselmoment), meaning the second density's offset
  D2 is zero.
- **The operator.** eq:pf is a fourth-order operator in D at fixed rho, marked [SYMBOLIC PROOF; MEASURED]. Its
  characteristic roots are the four branch points +-1, +-i omega, with omega^2 = (1-rho)/rho.
- **Why the rank is 4.** `sec:obstruction`, the paragraph "In principle":
  - The slice is the Fourier-Laplace transform of the rank-one connection with flat section Q^{-1/2}.
  - Its generic rank is the total drop: one unit at each of the four branch points, none at the regular point
    infinity.
  - The operator is irreducible over C(D).
- **The rho direction stays inside.** eq:inmodule says the modulus operator applied to L closes inside the span of
  L, L', L'', L''' (MEASURED, held-out residual below 1e-25). So the joint system in (D, rho) also has rank 4.
- **Where the memo's 4 comes from.** It is the order of eq:pf. It applies to the D-module generated by the
  unit-index integrand. It does not automatically cover a family that includes the index shifts which Slater
  orbitals bring. That gap is what MC2 tests.
- **Aside, not scored.** The memo says "the kite family has 18 masters". The Lee-Pomeransky count for the kite at
  P2 is 22 (`debug/data/lit_scan_euler_kite_p2.log`). The two numbers count different things: 18 is likely an IBP
  count after symmetry or sector identification, and 22 counts all critical points. Control C1 uses 22.

## What I know before computing (disclosed)

Everything below is hand reasoning done while writing this record. No script was written or run, and no cost
probe was made.

**The family.** Put in index shifts, with general masses:

    I(n1, n2) = Int_0^inf dk e^{-D Delta1} Delta1^{-n1} Delta2^{-n2},   Delta_i = sqrt(c_i k^2 + m_i^2).

With x = Delta1/m1 this becomes, by hand algebra,

    I = m1^{1-n1} m2^{-n2} c1^{-1/2} Int_1^inf e^{-Dhat x} x^{1-n1} (x^2-1)^{-1/2} (sigma x^2 + 1 - sigma)^{-n2/2} dx,

with Dhat = D m1 and sigma = c2 m1^2 / (c1 m2^2). At m = 1 and n1 = n2 = 1 this is eq:laplace.

**Scaled variables.** The parameters are (c1, c2, m1, m2, D). Two scalings, of k and of the mass, plus the
prefactor, leave the pair (Dhat, sigma).
- Hand argument that this is minimal: the ratio of the irregular exponents at D = infinity, i omega : 1, is
  invariant under rescaling D and depends on sigma. So sigma cannot be removed, and D cannot be removed either,
  because the exponents scale with it.

**The count in one variable with an exponential twist.**
- Take U = P^1 minus the removed finite points, with the twist e^{-Dhat x}. The twist has a double pole at
  infinity, which gives irregularity 1. Then dim H^1 = -chi(U) + 1 = (number of removed finite points).
- The same number is the count of zeros of omega = d(-Dhat x + sum nu_i log g_i): the number of zeros of a
  meromorphic 1-form on P^1 is its number of poles minus 2.
- The count does not depend on the exponents, as long as each point that should be removed actually is removed.
  A point is removed if its exponent is non-integer, or if the family contains a pole there.
- Applied to the slice:
  - **n1 = 1.** Removed points: +-1 and the two roots of sigma x^2 + 1 - sigma. That gives **4**.
  - **n1 >= 2.** The factor x^{1-n1} puts a pole at x = 0. That point is unramified, and the residue of the class
    there is Q(0)^{-1/2}, which is not zero for sigma != 1. So it is a new master, giving **5**. The new master is
    the D-antiderivative: I(2,1)(D) is the integral of L from D to infinity, in hatted variables.
  - **Different powers of Delta2.** If n2 is odd, the quartic factor has a half-integer exponent and monodromy -1.
    If n2 is even, the exponent is an integer and the monodromy is trivial. These are two different local
    systems. Each has the same count, 4 or 5, but neither family lies in the span of the other.
- **Which shifts production needs** (my hand bookkeeping, not checked against code). Paper 59's eq:reduced
  applies two zeta-derivatives to each density. On the slice that gives n1 in {1, ..., 5}. For the second density
  the D2 factor kills the even powers, which leaves n2 in {1, 3, 5}, all odd, so one local system. With n1 >= 2
  present, the production slice family has 5 masters, not 4.
- **Mellin in D.** Integrating D^{s-1} I(D) over D gives Gamma(s) times the integral of
  x^{-s+1-n1}(x^2-1)^{-1/2}(...)^{-n2/2} dx. This is a pure generalized Euler integral, the setting of
  Agostini-Fevola-Sattelberger-Telen. There x = 0 is always removed and n1 is only a shift in s. Its count is
  |chi(C* minus {+-1, two roots})| = **4** for every n1. So the extra master is a D-module effect, an
  antiderivative, and it disappears in the Mellin (difference-module) picture.
- **Degenerate loci.**
  - sigma = 1: the two roots merge at x = 0, giving 3. This is the genus-zero two-centre case.
  - sigma = 0: the quartic factor is 1, giving 2, the K0, K1 pair.
  - Dhat = 0: no twist, generic exponents, chi(P^1 minus 5 points) = -3, giving 3.
  - +-1 and +-i omega never collide otherwise.
- **The two-offset fibre** (D2 != 0, unit indices, no j0 factor). This is outside the slice and is used only to
  bound what a GO licenses.
  - The family lives on the genus-1 curve x^2 = c1 k^2 + 1, w^2 = c2 k^2 + 1.
  - Quotient by k -> -k, which is a conic in (x, w). The quotient has:
    - 4 branch points, (x, w) = (+-1, +-1), from k = 0;
    - 2 points at infinity where f = -D1 x - D2 w has simple poles, each irregular of rank 1;
    - no singularity at x = 0 or w = 0 for unit indices.
  - Count: omega has 4 simple poles (branch points) and 2 double poles (irregular points), 8 in total, so it has
    8 - 2 = **6** zeros. Equivalently -chi(P^1 minus 6 points) + 2 = 4 + 2 = 6.
  - The scaled variables are (D1 m1, D2 m2, sigma) = 3. The j0 smearing of eq:reduced adds the variable b on top.
  - At D2 = 0, the extra symmetry w -> -w cuts this to the 4 of the slice. That is a consistency check of the
    bookkeeping, not a computation.

## Model and methods (what phase 2 computes)

Generic exponents nu = (3/7, 5/11, 2/13, 7/17, 4/19); s = 9/23 for Lee-Pomeransky. Counts are taken in exact
rational arithmetic (sympy) and repeated modulo p0 = 2^61 - 1. Random rational parameters come from
`random.Random(20261027)`, drawn before anything runs, and are printed to the log.

- **Route X (exponential twist, one variable; the D-module count, which is what matters for tabulation in D).**
  - Form omega = -Dhat + sum nu_i g_i'/g_i over the removed points of the family member.
  - Clear denominators. Count the distinct roots of the numerator that are not removed points: the degree of the
    squarefree part after removing gcds with each g_i.
  - Run it twice: with generic nu, and with the actual exponents (-1/2 on x-1, x+1 and the quartic; 1 - n1 on x;
    -n2/2 on the quartic).
- **Route M (Mellin dual, Agostini-Fevola-Sattelberger-Telen generalized Euler integral on C*).** The same count,
  with a generic exponent on x and no twist.
- **Route LP (controls only).** The Lee-Pomeransky count of critical points of sum nu_i log x_i - s log G, with
  G = U + F. The counting function is copied from `lit_scan_euler_kite.py`: a Groebner basis with saturation by
  t x1...xn G, counting standard monomials.
- **Route F (MC6).**
  - Parametrize the conic c2(x^2-1) = c1(w^2-1) through (1,1): w - 1 = t(x - 1).
  - Count zeros of omega_f = d(-D1 x(t) - D2 w(t)) + sum over the 4 branch points of nu_i dlog of their factors.
    In this chart one branch point sits at t = infinity.
  - Choose c2/c1 a rational square, so the points at infinity are rational. Otherwise the choice is generic.
- **MC3 numerics.** Use mpmath quadrature at 30 digits on eq:laplace's form of I(1,1), at 3 random parameter
  tuples, and compare with the scaled form.

## Predictions

**MC0 (positive controls; run FIRST; if any fails, MC1-MC6 are void).**
- C1: the kite at P2 = (1, 3/2, 7/3, 8/3), m5 = 0, z = -37/101, using `lit_scan_euler_kite.py` unchanged at
  p = 32003, gives **22**. This reproduces the log.
- C2: the one-loop bubble with unequal masses and generic p^2 gives **3**: the bubble plus two tadpoles.
- C3: the two-loop sunrise with three unequal masses and generic p^2 gives **7**: four sunrise masters plus three
  tadpole products.
- C4 (exponential twist, textbook ranks):
  - e^{-Dx} x^nu gives **1** (Gamma).
  - e^{-Dx}(x-1)^a (x+1)^b gives **2** (modified Bessel, order 2). The same holds at a = b = -1/2.
  - e^{-zx} x^{a-1}(1+x)^{b-a-1} gives **2** (Kummer U).
- C5 (Mellin route): the Euler integral x^a (1-x)^b (1-zx)^c gives **2** (Gauss 2F1).
- Probability that all pass: 0.85. C3 is the most likely to fail on cost or saturation; see the caps.

**MC1 (unit-index slice equals the rank of eq:pf).**
- At 5 random points (Dhat, sigma), Route X gives **4**, with generic nu and with the actual exponents.
- Route M also gives **4**.
- Falsifier: any count other than 4.
- Probability 0.95.

**MC2 (index shifts up to +2, n1, n2 in {1, 2, 3}, at 3 random points).**
- (a) n1 = 1, any n2: **4**.
- (b) n1 in {2, 3}, any n2: **5**. The extra master is the x = 0 pole, the D-antiderivative.
- (c) Route M gives **4** for all nine (n1, n2).
- What "generic" means here, registered now:
  - Route X's count is independent of the exponents, as long as the same set of points is removed. Half-integer
    exponents (D = 3) on +-1 and on the quartic are never integers, so they are never resonant.
  - The only exponent that switches anything is the x-factor's 1 - n1. If it is 0, x = 0 is not a point. If it is
    a negative integer, x = 0 is a removed pole.
  - So "stable under index shifts" is predicted to fail in Route X between n1 = 1 and n1 >= 2, and to hold within
    each of those classes and in Route M.
- Falsifiers:
  - (a) any count other than 4;
  - (b) a count of 4, meaning the antiderivative reduces, against the residue argument, or any count other than 5;
  - (c) any count other than 4.
- Probabilities: (a) 0.95; (b) 0.85; (c) 0.9.
- MC2b, reported with MC2 and scored the same way: the production index set n1 in {1..5}, n2 in {1, 3, 5} gives
  **4** at n1 = 1 and **5** at n1 >= 2.

**MC3 (scaled variables = 2).**
- At 3 random tuples (c1, c2, m1, m2, D), I(1,1) equals the prefactor m2^{-1} c1^{-1/2} times the scaled integral
  in (Dhat, sigma), to at least 1e-25.
- Minimality holds by the exponent-ratio argument above. That argument is a hand check and is not re-computed.
- Falsifier: a mismatch above 1e-25.
- Probability 0.97. This is bookkeeping, a CHECK.

**MC4 (where the count drops).**
- Route X at n1 = n2 = 1: **4** at 20 random sigma (Dhat fixed) and at 20 random Dhat (sigma fixed).
- **3** at sigma = 1, **2** at sigma = 0, **3** at Dhat = 0.
- Falsifier: a drop at any random point, or a different value at the three special points.
- Probability 0.85.

**MC5 (the gate; refined from the brief, and fixed now).** Two families:

Outcomes:

- **BORDERLINE:** F_D has at most 4, F_prod has exactly 5, and the extra master is the antiderivative (MC2(b)

## Novelty status

- **MC1: KNOWN.** Paper 59 already gives rank 4 (eq:pf; the Fourier-Laplace paragraph citing Katz 1990). Masters
  as Euler characteristic: Bitoun-Bogner-Klausen-Panzer, arXiv:1712.09215; Agostini-Fevola-Sattelberger-Telen,
  arXiv:2208.08967. A hit is a check of the method.
- **MC2, MC2b, MC4, MC6: PROBABLY KNOWN.** These are instances of the standard formula dim = removed points plus
  irregularity for exponentially twisted cohomology on curves, and of Fourier-Laplace rank (Katz; Malgrange). No
  search was made for these specific families.
- **MC3: KNOWN** (elementary).
- **Consequence:** nothing in this record can score as a FINDING. Its value is the feasibility verdict.

## Controls

- MC0 is run first, and its verdicts are printed before any slice count.
- **Negative controls inside MC4:** sigma = 1 and sigma = 0 must drop. A counter that returns 4 everywhere fails
  them.
- **Cross-route control:** for every slice member, Route X and Route M are both run. They must agree at n1 = 1.
- Every count at a random point is repeated modulo p0. Exact and modular counts must agree.

## What GO, or BORDERLINE, would and would not license

- **It would license** only this: tabulation is not ruled out for the slice. A few master functions of 2 scaled
  variables, with the remaining members reached by IBP and D-recurrences, is a Boys-like shape.
- **It would not license:**
  - Any speed claim. The bar is Chen-Silberman-Segal-Greif, arXiv:2610.12608: about 4e4 four-centre STO ERIs/s end
    to end at 1.7e-9, on a V100. The timing test against that bar, with CPU normalization, is a separate later
    step with its own record.
  - Anything about the physical three-centre ERI. That needs the two-offset fibre (MC6: predicted 6 masters in 3
    variables, plus b from j0), the outer (s, t) Feynman integrals and the zeta-derivatives. The slice is one
    slice.
  - Anything about the kite or Y families (22 critical points, about 4 variables). These remain out of reach of a
    dense table, per the memo.
  - Stability or accuracy of the recurrences. Boys-type downward or upward recursions can be unstable, and that is
    untested.

## Risks

- Route X's count assumes the zeros of omega do not land on removed points at the random parameters. Repeating
  modulo p0 and at several points guards against an accidental coincidence.
- MC2b's index set comes from hand bookkeeping of the zeta-derivatives. If the production code shows n2 even
  terms on the slice, a second local system enters: two tables, 4 or 5 each. That would move MC5 to STOP under
  the rule above. This is stated now so it cannot be read either way after the fact.
