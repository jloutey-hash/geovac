## Why

Each shell's n² states can be grouped by the rotation group L (fixed l, collapse m: what Paper 25's base graph
does, a stand-in since 2026-10-31) or by the chiral SU(2) factors J± = (L ± A)/2 whose torus L_z + A_z = 2J+_z is
the Hopf fibre. The PI's principle, "use the geometry the quantum numbers suggest", asks which base-graph
quantities depend on the grouping. This record fixes a Hopf recipe and separates comparisons forced by SO(4)
(verified as controls) from open ones (predicted).

## What Paper 25 computes from its base graph

Q1 sectors (n, l); Q2 weights 2l+1; Q3 inter-sector edge weights (intra-sector edges dropped); Q4 the base
Laplacian's spectrum, characteristic polynomial and zero modes; Q5 B = Σ (2l+1) l(l+1) = 42 at n ≤ 3; Q6 F, which
uses only the shell degeneracy n². Δ = 1/40 and the full graph's L₁ spectrum are not base-graph quantities.

## Method (fixed before scoring)

- **Graph and generators.** Nodes (n, l, m), n ≤ n_max, the 0/1 adjacency of `GeometricLattice(max_n)`; L and A from
  the SY guard's construction, rebuilt on n_max shells (driver env `HG_NMAX`). n_max = 3 (14 nodes, 13 edges) and
  n_max = 4 (30 nodes, 34 edges). **Scope choice (PI-accepted): both.**
- **Hopf groups.** In shell n, group (n, μ) is the eigenspace of J−_z = (L_z − A_z)/2 with eigenvalue μ ∈ {−j..j},
  j = (n−1)/2: it keeps the J− label and collapses the fibre label m+, as the rotation recipe keeps l and collapses
  m. Weight = n. Grouping by m+ instead gives identical results ((−1)^l swaps J+ and J− and commutes with the
  l-preserving adjacency; F3), so this is not a choice.
- **Edge rule (PI-accepted: the occupation rule).** Occupation p_{n,μ}(n,l,m) = ⟨n,l,m|P_{n,μ}|n,l,m⟩ =
  |⟨j, m−μ; j, μ | l, m⟩|², exact rational; M = node × group occupation matrix (0/1 for the rotation grouping).
  Base adjacency W = off-diagonal part of Mᵀ A M; base Laplacian diag(W1) − W. Exact, convention-free, and equal to
  Paper 25's rule when M is 0/1 (C2, C3).
- **Alternative rule (reported only, HG5):** W[g,g′] = ‖P_g A P_g′‖²_F; carries one relative sign convention.
- **The "Hopf B" (PI-accepted reading): Tr J+² over the shells**, the chiral group's own Casimir summed with weights,
  the like-for-like analogue of B = Σ (2l+1) l(l+1). Σ_g Tr(P_g L²) = B is reported beside it. Both are forced.
- **Driver:** `debug/hopf_grouping_controls.py` (`forced`, `controls` run at n_max = 3 and 4; `score` written, not run).

- F1 per shell Tr J+² = Tr J−² = ½ Tr L² (n = 2: 3 vs 6; 3: 18 vs 36; 4: 60 vs 120). Totals: **Hopf B = 21 against
  B = 42** at n ≤ 3 and **81 against 162** at n ≤ 4. Symbolic identity Σ_{l<n}(2l+1)l(l+1) = n²(n²−1)/2, residual 0.
- F4 Tr(P_g L²) = n(n²−1)/2 on every group: summed this way B survives, but uniform per group, not resolved into l(l+1).
- F2 exact CG² occupations agree with numerical J−_z eigenprojectors to 8.9e-16. F5 μ → −μ with m → −m symmetry exact.
- F6/F8 Hopf weights 1 | 2,2 | 3,3,3 (| 4,4,4,4) against rotation 1 | 1,3 | 1,3,5 (| 1,3,5,7); both sum to n² per

## Predictions

**HG0 (controls; measured, PASS).** As above.

**HG1 (derived before scoring; a failure means a bug and voids the run), at n_max = 3 and 4.**
(a) The Hopf base graph is connected: exactly one zero mode (rotation: 3 and 4). Derivation: every entry of Mᵀ A M is
≥ 0; the radial s-edges join consecutive shells' s nodes, which by F7 occupy every group of their shell, so every
group of shell n links to every group of shell n+1.
(b) By F5 the characteristic polynomial factors into a mirror-even and a mirror-odd part: x · p₃ · p₂ at n_max = 3,
x · p₅ · p₄ at n_max = 4 (the even part holds the zero mode).
(c) Inter-group weight above the radial count: > 5 (n_max 3), > 14 (n_max 4); all radial edges cross shells, and
at least one angular edge has positive cross weight ((2,1,−1) lies wholly in μ = −½, (2,1,0) is split ½/½).

**HG2 (open: the fibre gets thinner).** The inter-group weight exceeds half the total: > 13/2 at n_max = 3 and > 17
at n_max = 4 (rotation: 5 of 13 and 14 of 34). Reason: an angular edge steps m at fixed l; at fixed μ = m− that is a
step in m+, so it stays inside a group only through a diagonal overlap that is generically below 1.
- Falsifier: the bound fails at either n_max. Probability 0.8.

**HG3 (open, gated at n_max = 3: the integer spectrum is a property of the rotation stand-in).** At n_max = 3 at least
one of p₃, p₂ does not split over ℚ (rotation's spectrum there is all integers).
- Falsifier: both split into rational linear factors. Probability 0.75.
- Reported at n_max = 4: the factorization, against rotation's x⁴(x−10)(x−9)(x−3)(x−2)(x²−4x+2).

**HG4 (reported).** λ_max against rotation's 6 (n_max 3) and 10 (n_max 4); expected smaller.

**HG5 (reported).** The alternative rule's W, spectrum and zero modes, with its convention stated.

## Decision

- **VOID:** HG0 or HG1 fails at either n_max (a bug; fixed and re-run under a new record).
- **GO:** HG2 holds at both n_max and HG3 holds.
- **BORDERLINE:** exactly one of {HG2 at n_max 3, HG2 at n_max 4, HG3} fails.
- **STOP:** two or more fail.

## Reading if GO, stated in advance

On the same labels, Paper 25's base-graph quantities split three ways.
- **Grouping-blind:** F, the total edge weight, and the number of groups per shell (n under both).
- **Grouping-dependent, forced:** the weight profile; the Casimir sum (Hopf B = half of B; B itself survives only
  as a uniform per-group trace); the zero-mode count (one against one per l-block).
- **Grouping-dependent, measured:** how much of the graph lies inside the fibre, and (at n_max = 3) whether the
  spectrum is rational.

Paper 25's three zero modes are the l-blocks of the rotation grouping; the Hopf grouping on the same labels joins
them. No Paper 2 number changes, and the K combination rule stays an Observation whatever this shows.

**Caps:** two cutoffs; one graph; one radial family for A; the occupation rule is one reasonable way to squash
blended states, not a theorem that it is the Hopf quotient.
