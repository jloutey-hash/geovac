# Pre-registered prediction: C2b as the operator of a two-Bessel integral with a geminal line (CF1-CF4)

**Registered:** 2026-10-22, before any computation in this file (PI-directed: "I also want to do the
feynman period one", after the EC result).
**Prompted by:** `2026-10-22_c2b_identification.md` (EC1-EC3 held: C2b is the period of the rational
elliptic surface with j = J1). The PI asked whether C2b is a Feynman period. Paper 59 already ties the
tree ladder to the banana ladder (`sec:four_density`), and C2b's genuine singular points
t = |1 - gamma|, 1 + gamma are the zeros of the Kallen function lambda_K(t^2, 1, gamma^2).
**Extends:** `2026-10-22_c2b_identification.md`, `2026-10-22_correlated_triangle_g32.md`,
`2026-10-17_ring_extensions.md` (QG).
**Commit status:** committed on its own, before any computation in this file.

## The question

Take H(t) = int_0^oo K0(t x) K0(x) exp(-gamma x) dx at gamma = 3/2: the moment of two density lines,
of scales t and 1, with one geminal line. Is C2b equivalent to the operator of H? And do the other
pieces of Y's annihilator follow the same reading, one piece for each pair of lines leaving the apex?

## What I know before computing (disclosed)

- C2b and its data: as in the EC record. Its curve has j = J1 = P^3 / (x (4x-1)^2 (4x-25)^2),
  P = 16x^2 + 152x + 25, x = t^2; fibres I1*, I2, I2, I1 at x = 0, 1/4, 25/4, oo; six I2 over t.
- Y's other pieces at gamma = 3/2 (record g32): the K3 piece F has F_3's local data twisted by t,
  genuine singular points t = 0, +-2, oo. The four order-one pieces are algebraic: R2's rational
  solution; exponent -5/2 at t = 2; exponent -7/2 at t = 3; t^5 (t^2-9)^(-9/2) times a sextic in t^2.
- Recalled, not checked against the source: Gradshteyn-Ryzhik 6.672.6 at nu = 0,
  int_0^oo K0(a x) K0(b x) cos(c x) dx = (pi^2 / (4 sqrt(a b))) P_(-1/2)((a^2 + b^2 + c^2) / (2 a b)).
  A sine companion with Q_(-1/2) is expected, not recalled.
- Recalled: int_0^oo K0(t x) exp(-c x) dx = arccos(c/t) / sqrt(t^2 - c^2).
- **Derived by hand while drafting, nothing computed:**
  - (i) Suppose H solves the Legendre equation of degree -1/2 in z(t) = (t^2 + 1 - gamma^2) / (2t),
    gauged by t^(-1/2). This is the formula above with c = i gamma, since exp(-gamma x) =
    cosh(gamma x) - sinh(gamma x), and P_(-1/2) and Q_(-1/2) solve the same equation. Then its curve is
    Legendre's with lambda = (1 - z)/2 = (gamma^2 - (t-1)^2) / (4t), and j = 64 (z^2 + 3)^3 / (1 - z^2)^2.
    At gamma = 3/2, z^2 = (4x-5)^2 / (64x), z^2 + 3 = P / (64x) and 1 - z^2 = -(4x-1)(4x-25) / (64x).
    So j = P^3 / (x (4x-1)^2 (4x-25)^2) = J1. The first step after the commit checks this algebra
    symbolically.
  - (ii) lambda (1 - lambda) = -lambda_K(t^2, 1, gamma^2) / (16 t^2). So the modulus is fixed by the
    Kallen function of the two density scales and the geminal.
  - (iii) Fibre count. Legendre's family has I2 at lambda = 0, 1 and I2* at oo. The map z(t) has
    degree two and is unramified over z = +-1 and oo. The pullback has I2 over t = +-(1 +- gamma) and
    I2* over t = 0, oo; the twist by t^(1/2) turns the two I2* into I2. That gives six I2 over t, as
    for C2b.
  - (iv) A momentum reading. On a line, K0(a|x|) is the Fourier transform of pi / sqrt(k^2 + a^2). So
    the cosine integral is a one-dimensional convolution, proportional to
    int dk / sqrt((k^2 + a^2)((k - c)^2 + b^2)). Its curve y^2 = (k^2 + a^2)((k - c)^2 + b^2) has the
    shape of Paper 59's three-centre curve y^2 = (c1 k^2 + 1)(c2 k^2 + 1), now with an external
    momentum c. At c = i gamma the geminal enters as an imaginary momentum. This is a reading, not
    a computation.
  - (v) The pair of geminal lines gives int K0(t x) exp(-2 gamma x) dx, singular at t = 2 gamma = 3
    with a square-root branch, where two of Y's order-one pieces sit.
- So (i) settles the j-invariant by hand, if the recalled formula and its continuation to imaginary
  c hold. Two things are not settled. First, that H obeys that operator at all t > 0, including
  t < gamma - 1 = 1/2, where the cosh and sinh parts diverge on their own. Second, the twist: the same
  j fixes the curve only up to a quadratic twist, and the EC map needed the factor x^(1/2).

## Model

H(t) = int_0^oo K0(t x) K0(x) exp(-gamma x) dx, gamma = 3/2, which converges for every t > 0. Route 1:
mpmath quadrature at 40 or more digits. Route 2: the Mellin route of the slice scripts. Use
M[K0(t x)](s) = t^(-s) 2^(s-2) Gamma(s/2)^2 and the Mellin recurrence of g(x) = K0(x) exp(-gamma x),
from g's order-two equation in x. That gives an operator L_H in t. L_Leg is the Legendre operator of
degree -1/2 pulled back by z(t) and gauged by t^(-1/2), with polynomial coefficients in t. The map
search is the EC Stage E linear algebra over Q, in t, with C2b pulled back by x = t^2.

## Predictions

**CF1 (H is the gauged Legendre function).** L_Leg annihilates H. The residual is at most 1e-30 of the
largest term at 40 digits, at four values of t in (0.2, 4), one of them below 1/2. A change of one
part in 200 in one coefficient of L_Leg gives a residual above 1e-6. The minimal annihilator of H
from route 2 has order two.
- Falsifier: a residual above 1e-25 at any point, or H needs order three or a source term.

**CF2 (main: C2b is H's operator).** C2b, pulled back to t, is equivalent to L_Leg by an exact map of
order at most one, rational in t.
- Falsifier: no such map in a box at least the bound set by the local exponents, while the same
  call finds the EC map, used as a control.
- Confidence: high, given (i). The open point is the twist.

**CF3 (pairs of lines; evidence, not scored).** The operator of E(t) = int K0(t x) exp(-3x) dx has
composition factors equivalent, by a rational gauge, to the two order-one pieces of L9 singular at
t = 3. For order-one pieces with algebraic solutions this reduces to matching the square-root class
at t = 3, so it has little power to discriminate. It is recorded as evidence only. The pair of
density lines, F_3 = int K0(t x) K0(x)^2 dx at t = 2, is already the disclosed K3 piece.

**CF4 (a second gamma: the test that the reading is a rule).** At gamma = 2, Y's annihilator has an
order-two piece equivalent to L_Leg at gamma = 2, where z = (t^2 - 3)/(2t) and
j = 64 (z^2 + 3)^3 / (1 - z^2)^2. Its genuine singular points are t = 1 and 3, where z = +-1. This
needs a new run of the record-g32 series at gamma = 2.
- Falsifier: no order-two piece of Y at gamma = 2, or one not equivalent to L_Leg there.
- Feasibility rule as in record g32. A STOP is reported as a STOP and is not scored.

## Novelty status

- The closed forms for the cosine and exponential integrals: KNOWN (Gradshteyn-Ryzhik). They are
  verified against the source at scoring.
- CF1: CHECK if it holds; the continuation to imaginary c is expected to be classical.
- CF2, CF4: NOT FOUND. No source is known to identify a piece of a correlated molecular integral
  with this Legendre family. A hit is a **FINDING**. The search is made at scoring.

## Controls

- Positive: at gamma = 0, H is F_2 = int K0(t x) K0(x) dx and z = (t^2 + 1)/(2t). L_Leg must then
  annihilate F_2, and it must be equivalent to Paper 59's Legendre operator of F_2.
- Positive: the map search finds the EC map from the EC source operator to C2b.
- Negative: the 1/200 perturbation of CF1. Without the twist t^(-1/2), L_Leg must fail to annihilate H.

## Caps stated in advance

One geminal shape, the exponential. The equivalence is over Q(t), not Q(x). It identifies the
operator class of C2b. It does not say that Y's component along C2b is H itself. The Feynman reading
is a dictionary, as in Paper 59: K0 is the two-dimensional propagator in position space, and
exp(-gamma |x|) is the one-dimensional one. No claim is made that Y is a Feynman integral. CF1 and
CF2 are at gamma = 3/2 only; CF4 adds gamma = 2 if feasible.

## Disposition

Appended 2026-10-22, after scoring. Scripts `debug/c2b_feyn_{A,B_map,C_diag,D_formula}.py`; data and
logs `debug/data/c2b_feyn_*`.

**Hand algebra.** Checked symbolically: j = 64 (z^2+3)^3/(1-z^2)^2 equals J1 at gamma = 3/2, and
lambda (1 - lambda) = -lambda_K/(16 t^2).

**CF1: FAILED.** L_Leg does not annihilate H. The relative residual is 0.14, 0.26, 0.56 and 0.13 at
t = 0.3, 0.8, 1.7, 3.2, at 50 digits. The gamma = 0 control passes: L_Leg annihilates F_2 to 2e-51.
The no-twist control fails, as it should. Route 2 gives an operator L5 of order five that L_Leg
divides exactly on the right, L5 = Q3 o L_Leg. So H lies in a module of order five that contains
L_Leg's solutions, and it is not one of them. Cause: the registration took exp(-gamma x) and
assumed the sinh part is a Legendre function too. It is not. The cosh part
C(t) = int K0(t x) K0(x) cosh(gamma x) dx is: L_Leg annihilates it to 5e-41 at 40 digits at
t = 0.8, 1.7, 3.2. The sinh part leaves 0.09 to 0.62. Class: **MISS** (the wrong function).

**CF2: HELD.** Twisted by t = x^(1/2), L_Leg in x is the EC source operator coefficient for
coefficient, up to the scalar -1. The map search in x found nullity 1 at E = ((4x-1)(4x-25))^5 (4x+5),
deg N0 = 22, with nothing in the smaller boxes. The map is exact, of order one, and equals the EC map
up to scale, which also meets the control. The search was over Q(x), stronger than the registered
Q(t). Class: **FINDING**, by the registration's novelty rule. Two web searches at scoring found no
source that places a piece of a correlated molecular integral on this Legendre family.

**Closed form.** The continued formula holds: C(t) = (pi^2/(4 sqrt t)) P_(-1/2)(z) to 2e-16 at
15 digits at t = 0.8, 1.7, 3.2, with z in (-1, 1) at the first two. At real c, the recalled
Gradshteyn-Ryzhik form agrees only to 1e-3. That looks like a quadrature limit, from the oscillation
and the log at x = 0, but it is not settled. The book entry was not checked against the source.

**Reading.** C2b is the operator of C(t), the cosine transform of the two density lines K0(t x) K0(x),
taken at imaginary momentum i gamma. In momentum form that is item (iv): the geminal enters as an
imaginary external momentum on the curve y^2 = (k^2 + t^2)((k - i gamma)^2 + 1). The modulus is
lambda = (gamma^2 - (t-1)^2)/(4t).

**CF3: evidence, as registered.** int K0(t x) exp(-3x) dx = arccos(3/t)/sqrt(t^2 - 9) has the square-root
class sqrt(t^2 - 9) at t = 3, as do L9's two order-one pieces there. This has little power to
discriminate, as stated. Note from CF1: the right line function for the pair of geminals may also be
cosh, not exp. That was not tested.

**CF4: NOT RUN.** It needs a new run of the record-g32 series at gamma = 2.

**Deviations.** The CF2 search was over Q(x), not Q(t). The real-c check of the formula ran at
15 digits after a 30-digit run timed out.

**Correction to the closed-form paragraph (appended 2026-10-22).** The 1e-3 gap at real c came from the
oscillatory quadrature routine, not from the formula. The integrand decays like exp(-(a+b)x), so plain
quadrature applies. With it, at 30 digits, the recalled form holds to 2e-31 at (a, b, c) = (1, 1, 1/2),
(0.7, 1.3, 2) and (2, 1, 0.3) (`debug/data/c2b_feyn_D2_realc.log`). The book entry itself was still not
checked against the source.
