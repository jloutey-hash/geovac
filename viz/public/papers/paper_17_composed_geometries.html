Composed Natural Geometries for Core-Valence Diatomics: LiH and BeH^+ from Natural-Geometry Solvers

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 17 · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286726 · PDF

Abstract

The natural geometry hierarchy assigns each separable quantum system a coordinate system where Coulomb singularities become boundary conditions: $S^3$ for atoms (Paper 7), prolate spheroids for H$_2^+$ and H$_2$ (Papers 11, 12), hyperspherical for He (Paper 13), with molecule-frame hyperspherical coordinates as the structural two-electron treatment of H$_2$ that composition builds on (Paper 15). Systems with core electrons resist a single natural geometry because core and valence electrons occupy different length scales. We introduce composed natural geometries—fiber bundles where each electron group inhabits its own natural coordinate system, connected by screening functions and Phillips–Kleinman pseudopotentials built from the core wavefunction plus one empirical atomic input (the neutral atom's first ionization energy), with the barrier-width formula chosen among four candidates by comparison with the LiH equilibrium bond length. For LiH ($Z_A=3$, $Z_B=1$, 4 electrons): the Li$^+$ $1s^2$ core is solved via Level 3 hyperspherical (Paper 13), yielding $Z_{\rm eff}(r)$ and pseudopotential parameters; the two valence electrons are solved via Level 4 molecule-frame hyperspherical (Paper 15) with $Z_{\rm eff}$ injection; the potential energy surface is scanned via a $\rho$-collapse adiabatic method that reduces per-$R$-point cost to seconds. Results: $R_{\rm eq} = 3.22$ bohr (6.9% error vs. experiment 3.015, improved from 17% in LCAO), $\omega_e = 1465$ cm$^{-1}$ (4.2% error vs. 1406). The $l$-dependent PK variant reduces the $R_{\rm eq}$ error further, to 5.3%—the figure quoted as this paper's headline elsewhere in the corpus; 6.9% is the plain channel-blind PK. Total wall time is ${\sim}4$ minutes per molecule on commodity hardware. The same formula transfers to BeH$^+$ ($Z_A=4$) with no per-molecule tuning, giving a bound ion whose $R_{\rm eq}$ is 30% long.
