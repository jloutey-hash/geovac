Full Configuration Interaction for Heteronuclear Diatomics in Concatenated Atom-Centred Bases: LiH as a Benchmark

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper fci · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286736 · PDF

Abstract

We present a Topological LCAO architecture for heteronuclear full configuration interaction (FCI) within the GeoVac discrete graph framework. Two independent atom-centered hydrogenic lattices are concatenated into a bipartite molecular graph via Mulliken cross-nuclear attraction on the diagonal, Slater–Condon two-electron repulsion integrals with exact $R^k$ radial integrals and Gaunt coefficients, and STO-overlap bridges on the off-diagonal; the resulting one- and two-electron Hamiltonian is diagonalized in the full Slater determinant space (4 845 determinants for LiH at $n_{\max}=2$, where the results below are obtained; the solver also handles the 367 290 determinants of $n_{\max}=3$). For LiH the balanced Hamiltonian produces a monotonically attractive potential energy surface with no equilibrium geometry; at $n_{\max}=2$ the curve still lies 0.22 Ha below the separated-atom energy at $R=8$ bohr, so it does not dissociate to the atoms and no binding energy is extracted. The absence of a minimum is traced to the $R$-independence of the intra-atom one-electron block: unlike continuous quantum mechanics, each atom's hydrogenic diagonal $-Z^2/(2n^2)$ is fixed at construction and does not respond to inter-nuclear compression; the large-$R$ offset is not diagnosed here. A Fock-weighted kinetic correction, whose form is motivated by the energy-shell constraint $p_0^2 = Z^2/n^2$ of Paper 7 but whose overall scale $\lambda$ is a single fitted parameter (retained as a labeled qualitative diagnostic, not a zero-parameter result), partially restores the equilibrium by concentrating orthogonalization repulsion on compact core orbitals, giving $R_{\mathrm{eq}}\approx 3.7$ bohr at $\lambda=0.1$ ($n_{\max}=2$) without repairing the large-$R$ limit. A 29-version diagnostic arc (v0.9.8–v0.9.37) systematically tested and eliminated every competing hypothesis: basis set superposition error as a geometric driver, integral incompleteness, orbital exponent relaxation, the single-$n$, shared-$p_0$ SO(4) Sturmian bond sphere approximation, and Hamiltonian imbalance between cross-nuclear and electron repulsion orbital subspaces. In particular, Wigner $D$-matrix selection rules $D^{2}_{10,10}(\gamma)=1$ and $D^{2}_{00,10}(\gamma)=0$ are proven as structural results. The excitation-driven Direct CI solver achieves $O(N_{\mathrm{SD}}× N_{\mathrm{connected}})$ scaling, preserving the $O(V)$ character of the underlying graph Hamiltonian.
