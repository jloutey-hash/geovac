The Periodic Table Recast as S_N Representation Theory on S^(3N-1)

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 16 · Quantum Computing · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286783 · PDF

Abstract

The non-relativistic $N$-electron Schrödinger equation, after separation of the hyperradius, reduces to angular dynamics on the hypersphere $S^{3N-1}$. The angular kinetic energy is the $\mathrm{SO}(3N)$ Casimir operator with eigenvalue $\mufree = \nu(\nu + 3N - 2)/2$. Fermionic antisymmetry restricts the spatial wavefunction to a specific $S_N$ irreducible representation—the conjugate of the spin Young diagram $[n_\uparrow, n_\downarrow]$, a diagram with two columns of heights $n_\uparrow$ and $n_\downarrow$. The minimum angular quantum number compatible with it is the Pauli filling count $\nu_{\min} = E(n_\uparrow) + E(n_\downarrow)$, where $E(m)$ sums the $m$ lowest one-particle polynomial degrees and degree $d$ holds $(d+1)(d+2)/2$ polynomials. We prove this for all $N$ and $S$ from Schur–Weyl duality (the minimal-quanta statement for non-interacting particles in an oscillator is prior art, Theorem 1 of Rittenhouse et al., whose proof they credit to Cavagnero (1986); it holds for any non-interacting $N$ (it is applied there to balanced spin populations); what is new here is the $S_N$/Schur–Weyl proof route and the atomic tabulation) and corroborate it by an exact character count for all spins through $N = 12$ and by brute force for $N = 3, 4$. It depends on the spin, equals $N-2$ exactly when both columns lie in the first two oscillator shells ($1 \le n_\downarrow \le n_\uparrow \le 4$; at lowest spin, for $2 \le N \le 8$), and grows as $N^{4/3}$; the ground-state orbital angular momentum and parity can raise it further (lithium's ${}^2S$ needs $\nu = 2$, not 1). The $S_N$ irrep depends only on $N$ and $S$, so it cannot by itself separate the chemical groups (noble gases and alkaline earths share an irrep, and every doublet shares the alkali shape); together with the shell occupancy, an empirical configuration input, it yields a classification of atoms into five types—A (trivial), B (noble gas), C (alkali), D (alkaline earth), E (open shell)—that recasts the periodic table's group structure in representation-theoretic language. The periodic law corresponds to the repetition of the type sequence $\mathrm{C} \to \mathrm{D} \to \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{B}$ within each period. This classification maps onto, rather than predicts, the known periodic table structure; its value lies in providing a unified group-theoretic framework for a pattern that is usually described empirically. The angular quantities ($\nu$, $\mufree$) are integers defined for every $N$, with nothing special at $Z = 1/α \approx 137$; we read the Dirac instability as a metric rather than a topological effect (an interpretive observation, not derived here).
