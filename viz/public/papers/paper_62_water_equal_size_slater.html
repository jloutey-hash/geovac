Water at Twenty-Four Functions: Slater-Type Bases Set on the Free Atom Against cc-pVDZ, With the Qubit Cost

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 62 · Quantum Chemistry · PDF

Abstract

Central result. At a fixed basis size of twenty-four functions, the size of cc-pVDZ on water, we ask how much of the Gaussian basis's correlated energy a one-centre Slater-type design recovers when its exponents are set on the free atoms, and what that costs in qubit resources. [MEASURED] A Slater double-zeta-plus-polarization basis built by cc-pVDZ's own design rule sits $40.7 \mathrm{mHa}$ below cc-pVDZ at frozen-core CCSD(T), of which $28.8 \mathrm{mHa}$ is self-consistent field. [MEASURED, pre-registered] cc-pVDZ is built for systematic convergence, not for the lowest energy at its size; of three published Gaussian bases of the same size, ANO-R1 is $23.6 \mathrm{mHa}$ below cc-pVDZ, and the Slater DZP's margin over it is $17.1 \mathrm{mHa}$. [MEASURED, negative] Every exponential kit with fixed or shared exponents loses: the hydrogenic $Z/n$ rule by $196 \mathrm{mHa}$, a single Coulomb–Sturmian scale per atom by more than one hartree, and a two-scale Sturmian kit by $130 \mathrm{mHa}$; the hydrogenic loss is the fixed radial node structure, not the decay rate. [MEASURED, pre-registered] Releasing the oxygen exponents one at a time, every value set by the free oxygen atom's radial Hartree–Fock energy with nothing tuned on water, closes that gap: with five oxygen exponents the water energy lands $3.1 \mathrm{mHa}$ below cc-pVDZ, and $7.0 \mathrm{mHa}$ below with hydrogen exponents set on H$_2$. The split of the $2p$ from the $3p$ exponent carries $0.900$ of the water gain, as it carries $0.902$ of the atom's; the water gain is $0.516$ of the atom's. Both were predicted before the water energies were computed. The one non-monotone rung is diagnosed: its correlation loss transfers from the atom one to one and is mostly a $3d$ exponent the atomic objective never sees, while its Hartree–Fock gain is two opposite pieces that cancel on water. [MEASURED] The Jordan–Wigner Pauli count is the same for every twenty-four-function basis, $107 382$ terms on 46 frozen-core qubits, because in canonical orbitals without degenerate pairs it is fixed by the orbital count and the $C_{2v}$ pattern; across this paper's own kits the 1-norm rises with accuracy, from 405 to 706 Ha (the one-scale Sturmian aside), but not across Gaussian families: ANO-R1 is more accurate than cc-pVDZ at 625 Ha. Caps. One geometry and one method per molecule; the ladder and the native kits are water only. The water-tuned Slater basis stays $34 \mathrm{mHa}$ below the best atom-set rung, and $21.7$ mHa below once the $3d$ is also set on the atom's correlated energy (pre-registered; water then lands $19.0$ mHa below cc-pVDZ, still $4.7 \mathrm{mHa}$ above ANO-R1). [MEASURED, pre-registered] At matched accuracy the Gaussian cost depends on the family. In the cc-pV family aug-cc-pVDZ at 41 functions stays $8.0 \mathrm{mHa}$ short of the Slater DZP while already costing $9.2×$ the Pauli terms. In the ANO family a 35-function truncation of ANO-R2 is $6.2 \mathrm{mHa}$ below it, at $4.7×$ the Pauli terms and $2.3×$ the 1-norm on 68 qubits against 46 (registered as falling short; the prediction missed). The measured matched-accuracy cost is therefore at most $4.7×$ the terms; ANO-R1 at 24 functions falls short, and the crossing between 24 and 35 functions was not located. [MEASURED, pre-registered] The same rule on hydrogen fluoride, a second molecule, repeats both cc-pV findings: the Slater DZP is $59.5 \mathrm{mHa}$ below cc-pVDZ at nineteen functions with the same Pauli count, and aug-cc-pVDZ is still short at $9.3×$ the terms; the ANO family was not run there. [MEASURED, pre-registered; STOP] A ring of six hydrogens, with no core, does not: the Slater DZP is only $3.5$ mHa below cc-pVDZ at thirty functions, and one more Gaussian polarization shell at 48 functions already beats it, so the cc-pV matched-accuracy margin is a property of these two molecules, not of exponential functions. Nothing here is a GeoVac lattice structure: the bases are tuned exponential functions evaluated through Gaussian fits, and the angular sparsity of the one-centre framework does not enter.
