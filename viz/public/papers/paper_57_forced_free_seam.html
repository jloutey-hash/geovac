Forced and Free Content in the GeoVac Framework: An Observations Paper

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 57 · Mathematical Foundations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286768 · PDF

Abstract

The GeoVac framework produces a wide class of structural facts (the $(n,l,m)$ label set and its $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ Casimir spectrum, $\sphere{3}$ outer manifold, master Mellin engine, inner-algebra factor count, the $D_F$ moduli dimension $\dim \Mcal(\DF) = 32$ at one generation (matter-sector projection rank $16$; the factor count and this dimension hold relative to the Hopf-tower organizing reading, the adopted Upgrade B axiom and the imported CCM representation), the two-term spectral action on $\sphere{3}$ (Chamseddine–Connes), and many more), admits the gauge group $U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3)$ under an organizing reading plus an adopted axiom (Paper 32; not a derivation), and consumes specific numerical content as external input (Yukawa values, generation count, calibration cutoff moments, atomic-physics screening exponents, multi-loop QED counterterms, etc.). We catalogue both sides of this forced/free seam across the corpus — 43 forced entries (the gauge-group entries forced relative to the adopted Upgrade B axiom), 5 Observations ($\kappa$, matched not forced; the magnetic-moment ratio of Paper 28's vertex model, an unnormalized coincidence; two released-axis results that lack a derivation, the decompactification front and the four-body $r_{12}$ bridge; and the rung rule, a pattern across the tested instances), 1 admitted-not-forced entry, 27 calibration entries and 3 walls, spanning foundations, gauge structure, periods, gravity, QED, the Standard Model inner factor, multi-focal-composition walls, chemistry / quantum computing scaling, foundational calibration and the released axes of the chemistry program: a second nucleus, the decompactification front, a third and a fourth centre, bending, a three-electron chain, the four-body $r_{12}$ bridge, four linked densities and a ring of three densities, and, from the label set itself, a spin grown large, the hydrogen continuum, a finite nucleus and its magnetization, and the Kustaanheimo–Stiefel lift, each recorded with the number class that enters when the axis is released, where one is assigned — weight-one logarithms at two centres, an elliptic period at three, a K3 holomorphic period for the chain, a period not built from elliptic ones for four linked densities, and, at the fibre, an elliptic period again for a Coulomb ring of three, with dilogarithms at one centre; $π^{1/2}$ and $e^{-x^2}$, only as limits, for the spin; and one exponential of $π$ for the continuum; while a finite nucleus consumes moments of its charge as matching coefficients. We identify a cross-domain unity pattern (the same wall appears in seven settings, all carrying the same multi-focal-composition signature, the two nuclear ones sharing the textbook matching mechanism of nonrelativistic QED) and test five candidate principles for what discriminates the two sides: multi-focal depth, period-content classifiability, dimensional character, compactness inheritance, and packing-reachability. The fifth candidate (packing-reachability: an entry is forced iff a witness structural derivation exists from $\{$Paper 0 + standard real-spectral-triple axioms + Hopf-tower truncation + Bertrand + Upgrade B + CCM canonical SM representation$\}$) consolidates the forced/free boundary rather than independently discriminating it: its 98.3% agreement with the F/C status (59/60, on the 2026-06-08 labelling in which $\kappa$ and the anomalous-moment ratio were counted forced) is an internal-consistency check confirming the catalogue applies its conventions uniformly — the packing-reachable tag is the F/C label by construction — not an independent accuracy. The independent discriminator signal is carried by multi-focal depth, period class, and the two-family decomposition. The earlier-named two-family decomposition (multi-focal composition vs. inner-factor input data) is preserved as the structural failure-mode story beneath the principle: both families share the same surface verdict (packing-unreachability) but exhibit different structural reasons for it. This paper consolidates what was previously scattered across CLAUDE.md \S 1.7 working hypotheses, dead-end records, and individual sprint memos.
