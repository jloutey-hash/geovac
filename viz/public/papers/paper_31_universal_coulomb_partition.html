The Universal/Coulomb Partition of the GeoVac Spectral Triple What belongs to angular momentum, what belongs to -Z/r, and why the difference matters

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 31 · Mathematical Foundations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286757 · PDF

Abstract

The GeoVac framework [ref] is a discrete spectral triple in the sense of Connes [ref] and the graph-spectral-triple lineage of Marcolli and van Suijlekom [ref]: an algebra $\mathcal{A}$ of functions on the Fock-projected discrete graph, a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ of scalar and spinor states, and a Dirac operator $D$ inherited from the Camporesi–Higuchi spectrum on $S^3$. This paper identifies a clean structural decomposition of the framework's results into two disjoint sectors: • A universal sector, depending only on $\mathcal{A}$ and the angular structure of the basis. The angular sparsity theorem of Paper 22 [ref] — the angular ERI density is potential-independent (global-$M_L$ Coulomb $D=6.06%$ at $l_{\max}=3$, the density the composed pipeline realizes; the stricter pair-diagonal restriction gives $D_{\mathrm{pd}}=1.44%$), bit-identical across Coulomb, harmonic oscillator, Woods–Saxon, square well, and Yukawa potentials — is the canonical example. • A potential-specific sector, depending on the choice of $D$. The Fock projection rigidity theorem of Paper 23 [ref] ($S^3$ projection unique to $-Z/r$ by $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ symmetry) and the Bargmann–Segal rigidity theorem of Paper 24 [ref] (the 3D isotropic harmonic oscillator is the unique central potential whose spectrum arises from the Euler operator on $H^2(S^5)$ restricted to symmetric $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ irreps; its converse half rests on an imported result Paper 24 flags as unverified) jointly establish the partition's sharpness, the second subject to that caveat. The two rigidity theorems sit on opposite sides of an operator-order divide: Coulomb uses a second-order Riemannian Laplacian, the HO a first-order complex Euler operator. The divide does not decide where $π$ enters: both label sets are $π$-free, and on both sides $π$ enters through a measure and through spectral sums at even argument;\ in the Kustaanheimo–Stiefel frame the Coulomb problem is itself a constrained oscillator with a linear projection (Paper 24). The $S^5$ gauge-structure result of Paper 24 [ref] supplies the standing Coulomb/HO discriminator, Wilson gauge with natural matter coupling, decided by representation theory (on $S^3 = \mathrm{SU}(2)$ matter and links share a representation, on $S^5$ matter coupling needs a Clebsch–Gordan intertwiner). Calibration $π$, listed here as a second discriminator until 2026-10-19, is withdrawn. (A third, the spectrum-computing role of $L_0$, [retracted 2026-09-26: coulomb-ho-layer1-and-three-discriminators] was listed here until 2026-09-25; it is withdrawn, because the graph Laplacian on $S^3$ does not converge to the $S^3$ Laplacian and is spectrally inert there as on $S^5$.) We frame the partition in spectral-triple language and identify which features of GeoVac transfer to non-Coulomb systems for free, which require system-specific rederivation, and what this implies for nuclear, oscillator, and gauge-theoretic extensions of the framework.
