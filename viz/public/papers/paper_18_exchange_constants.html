Spectral-Geometric Exchange Constants: Projecting Discrete Spectra onto Continuous Manifolds

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 18 · Mathematical Foundations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286744 · PDF

Abstract

Compact Lie groups have discrete unitary representations (Peter–Weyl theorem); the GeoVac label set exploits this discreteness directly. Transcendental conversion factors appear when these discrete representations are projected onto non-compact coordinate systems used for measurement. We classify these factors by the type of compactness mismatch they mediate—six exchange-constant tiers (intrinsic, conformal/calibration, embedding, algebraic-implicit, composition, inner-factor input data) plus the algebraic (no-exchange) case, seven categories in all, spanning the GeoVac hierarchy through Level 5 plus the almost-commutative inner factor—and identify them with Weyl–Selberg exchange constants of spectral geometry. The falsifiable claim: the transcendental content of any GeoVac observable is determined entirely by the type of projection linking the compact discrete description to the non-compact coordinate system (this strong, observable-indexed form is under strain from the H$_2$ dissociation energy and is not reconciled with the body's weaker, route-indexed Claim 4; see Sec. (ref)). Beyond the basis-level catalog, three theorems classify the spectral content of operators on round $S^3$: (Theorem 1) for the Camporesi–Higuchi $D$ against $D^2$, operator order is the primary discriminant of transcendental class (not for every second-order operator: $\nabla^*\nabla$ fails)—the squared Dirac operator $D^2$ produces $π^{\mathrm{even}}$ exclusively at every integer $s$, while the first-order Dirac operator $D$ produces odd-zeta content $ζ_R(2k{+}1)$ at odd $s$ (conjecturally outside $\mathbb{Q}(π)$;\ Apéry proved only that $ζ_R(3)$ is irrational); (Theorem 2) the full classification is three-axis, adding bundle type (scalar vs. spinor, modulating coefficients only) and diagram topology (free vs. parity-restricted, introducing Dirichlet $L$-values $G = \beta(2)$, $\beta(4)$ through the quarter-integer Hurwitz shift); (Theorem 3) the three components of the fine-structure-constant combination $K = π(B + F - \Delta)$ of Paper 2 [ref] have spectral homes of distinct type (finite Casimir trace, infinite Dirichlet series at the packing exponent, Dirac mode degeneracy on the spinor bundle);\ $B$ and $F$ share the (second-order, scalar) cell of the three-axis grid and differ by finite versus infinite summation, and $\Delta$ sits in the (first-order, spinor) cell. The combination rule—$K$ matches $α^{-1}$ to $4.8×10^{-7}$ relative, and $α$ approximately satisfies the cubic $α^3 - Kα + 1 = 0$ to $8.8×10^{-8}$—remains a numerical Observation without first-principles derivation. A complementary Observation (structural incommensurability) catalogues the meta-pattern: GeoVac observables that look unitary at the surface ($K$ itself; the two-loop Dirac spectral sum on $S^3$; zero structure of the GeoVac zeta) resolve under structural analysis into objects of different type (not always different cells of the three-axis grid:\ $K$'s $B$ and $F$ share one). No further unification is currently visible. The taxonomy is read (a census and a reading, not a classification) in terms of Mellin transforms of heat kernels: the free heat kernel produces $π^{\mathrm{even}}$ in its integer-$s$ spectral-zeta values (for $\Delta_{\mathrm{LB}}$ and $D^2$), restricting the spectrum to one parity class produces $L$-content, and operator order determines whether the Mellin integrand samples squared or raw eigenvalues. The taxonomy is $R$-independent, persisting in the flat-space limit via Weyl's law.
