The Fine Structure Constant as a Spectral Coincidence on the Three-Sphere

Josh Loutey · GeoVac synthesis document · QED and Gauge Theory · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286802 · PDF

Abstract

We record a numerical observation. The number $K = π(42 + π^2/6 - 1/40) = 137.036064$ lies within $4.8×10^{-7}$ (relative) of the inverse fine structure constant $α^{-1}$, and the smallest positive root of the cubic $α^3 - Kα + 1 = 0$ lies within $8.8×10^{-8}$ of $α$ (CODATA 2018). The three ingredients are spectral data of the unit three-sphere $S^3$: $B = 42$ is the degeneracy-weighted trace of the $SO(3)$ Casimir $l(l+1)$ over the scalar $S^3$ harmonics of the first three shells, the cutoff being the only one at which $B/N = \dim S^3 = 3$; $F = ζ(2) = π^2/6$ is the Dirichlet series of the shell degeneracy $n^2$ at $s = 4$; and $\Delta = 1/40$ is the reciprocal of a Dirac degeneracy on $S^3$. The combination rule $K = π(B + F - \Delta)$ is an Observation, not a derivation: its weights, signs, prefactor and the cubic are chosen, and we list which steps are computed and which are chosen. In a search over $N = 1.92×10^9$ formulas built from ingredients of the same kind, ten match $α$ more closely ($p = 5.2×10^{-9}$ per formula), so the expected chance count is $Np \approx 10$: within that search the formula is not unusual. The search was run after the formula was found and does not correct for that. Twelve mechanisms proposed to generate all three ingredients from one spectral construction were tested and eliminated; we tabulate them.
