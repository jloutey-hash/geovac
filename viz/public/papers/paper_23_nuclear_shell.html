Nuclear Shell Model Hamiltonians on the Hyperspherical Lattice

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 23 · Quantum Computing · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286787 · PDF

Abstract

We present structurally sparse qubit Hamiltonians for the nuclear shell model, constructed by applying the GeoVac angular-sparsity framework to the harmonic oscillator (HO) single-particle basis with a Mayer–Jensen spin–orbit term. The HO shell closures $2, 8, 20, 40, 70, 112$ follow from state counting on the oscillator labels, and the physical magic numbers $2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126$ are recovered with a spin–orbit strength $v_{ls}/\hbar\omega \approx 0.17$ and a Nilsson $\ell(\ell+1)$ correction $d_{\ell\ell}/\hbar\omega \approx 0.02$. We build a two-species qubit encoding for the deuteron ($1p+1n$, $16$ qubits, \gvq{p23_deuteron_pauli}{688} non-identity Pauli terms, $1$-norm $\approx 384$ MeV) using the Minnesota nucleon–nucleon potential and Moshinsky–Talmi brackets, and extend the architecture to helium-4 ($2p+2n$, $16$ qubits, \gvq{p23_he4_pauli}{904} Pauli terms) with intra-species antisymmetrization and proton–proton Coulomb repulsion;\ each encoded Hamiltonian reproduces its full-CI spectrum in the physical particle-number sector to $2× 10^{-12}$ MeV. He-4 has \gvq{p23_he4_deuteron_pauli_ratio}{1.31}$×$ the deuteron's Pauli terms, the added proton–proton and neutron–neutron channels; its $12.25×$ larger Hilbert space costs no terms, because at fixed qubit count the Jordan–Wigner term set does not depend on the particle number. A composed nuclear–electronic encoding ($26$ qubits), a deuteron nuclear block coupled to a hydrogen-like electron through a proton-spin hyperfine term, exercises the multi-block architecture and quantifies the $\sim 10^{13}$ coefficient ratio between nuclear, electronic, and hyperfine scales. We state and apply the Fock projection rigidity theorem: the $S^3$ conformal projection that makes the one-electron Coulomb Hamiltonian equivalent to the $S^3$ Laplace–Beltrami operator (whose harmonics the GeoVac lattice's nodes label) requires the hydrogenic spectrum, and does not extend to HO or Woods–Saxon systems. A finite nucleus breaks it in a controlled way: the labels survive, $n$ is released from being a whole number, and the $s$-wave quantum defect $-(2/5)(ZR)^2$ is the same for every $n$ and continues across threshold, moments of the nuclear charge being consumed as the matching coefficients of an effective theory; a finite magnetization is priced the same way, the Zemach moment first, with a logarithm set by the charge alone; in the Dirac equation near and past $Zα = 1$ the radius becomes compulsory (Sec. (ref)). For nuclear Hamiltonians, the GeoVac contribution is therefore the angular machinery and qubit encoding, not the conformal projection. All nuclear physics inputs (Minnesota strengths and ranges, used without the potential's space-exchange mixture; spin–orbit strength, $\hbar\omega$) follow the standard shell-model literature; the quantitative binding energies at $N_{\mathrm{shells}}=2$ are far from experiment and should be read as encoding-validation benchmarks, not as a nuclear-structure calculation.
