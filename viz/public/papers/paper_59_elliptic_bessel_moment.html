The Three-Center Electron-Repulsion Integral Is an Elliptic Bessel Moment: Genus One at the Third Center in Momentum-Space Slater Theory

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 59 · Quantum Chemistry · PDF

Abstract

Two-center electron-repulsion integrals over Slater-type orbitals close in elementary functions plus the exponential integral, the logarithm, and Euler's $\gamma$—a genus-zero transcendence class—which is why the two-center problem has been solved since Roothaan and Ruedenberg. The genuine three-center integral has resisted a closed form for seventy years, and the standard verdict is that it is simply hard: reduce it to an infinite series or a one-dimensional quadrature and be done. We give it a structural reading instead. Evaluating the two-electron three-center integral $(XY|XZ)$ over $1s$ orbitals in momentum space—the Fock projection under which Coulomb–Sturmians become hyperspherical harmonics, the setting native to the Averys' generalized-Sturmian program—the three centers enter as three phases rather than three foci, so the coordinate obstruction (prolate spheroidal coordinates admit exactly two foci) dissolves. [MEASURED] The resulting momentum integral reproduces the exact integral (agreement $1.9×10^{-14}$ against a closed-form Gaussian reference; $4.3×10^{-7}$ against an independent Slater evaluation), and its angular part collapses to a single spherical Bessel kernel. [MEASURED / classical] What remains—the radial content—is a two-scale Bessel moment whose period is a complete elliptic integral (verified to 31 digits), living on the genus-one curve $y^{2}=(c_{1}k^{2}+1)(c_{2}k^{2}+1)$; it degenerates to the genus-zero two-center case exactly on the diagonal $c_{1}=c_{2}$. [SYMBOLIC + MEASURED] The one-mass slice is the Laplace transform of the elliptic differential—an elliptic deformation of the Bessel $K_{0}$, reducing to $K_{0}(D)$ when one scale decouples ($\rho\to0$) and to the complete elliptic integral $K$ at zero separation—and satisfies an explicit fourth-order Picard–Fuchs equation with polynomial coefficients (residual $\sim\!10^{-24}$), whose characteristic exponents are the four branch points of the curve. [OBSERVATION] This places a molecular integral inside the modern theory of Bessel moments and two-mass elliptic Feynman integrals; a targeted literature search finds that connection unmade in either field. [OPEN] We do not give the finite closed form, and we show the natural route to it is obstructed: the elliptic-dilogarithm (unequal-mass sunrise) mechanism does not engage, because the modulus Picard–Fuchs source is not an elementary subtopology but lies in the integral's own Gauss–Manin $D$-module—the branch-point boundary that would supply the sunrise's tadpole vanishes identically. [OBSERVATION] The object is therefore an apparently unclaimed elliptic transcendent one level above the elliptic-dilogarithm tower, a period of a rank-four irregular connection. [SYMBOLIC + MEASURED] That connection is irreducible—it is the Fourier–Laplace transform of an irreducible connection, and its monodromy admits no invariant coordinate subspace—so no closed form arises through factorization; [OPEN] whether the irreducible object admits a transcendental closed form we leave open. The message is not a solution but a relocation: the third center does not make the radial integral messier within the two-center class, it raises the transcendence one genus, past the reach of the sunrise's elliptic-dilogarithm mechanism.
