Linked Electron Densities Climb the Banana Ladder: Period Classes of Molecular Integrals over Exponential-Type Orbitals

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 63 · Mathematical Foundations · PDF

Abstract

Paper 59 read the radial core of the three-center electron-repulsion integral over $1s$ Slater orbitals as an elliptic Bessel moment: two two-center densities coupled by a Coulomb kernel build a genus-one curve. This paper asks two questions that result leaves open. How does the class climb when an integral links more densities? And how far does the reading reach past $1s$ orbitals? [SYMBOLIC PROOF + PRIOR ART] The second is answered first, in part, by a dictionary. Every exponential-type orbital on a center is a polynomial differential operator in its exponent and its center coordinate, with coefficients rational in the exponent, applied to the $1s$ Yukawa function on that center; at the fibre, for $s$-type densities, every density weight is a polynomial in $c$, $c^{-1}$ and the Euler operators $c \partial_c$ applied to the $1s$ weight. So every multi-density integral with rational kernels is that operator applied to the $1s$ integral of the same density graph and lies in that integral's $D$-module. For a tree of $s$-type densities on its one-scale slice the holomorphic period is a differential operator applied to the $1s$ one, so it sits at or below the $1s$ rung, and for $n\le8$, when it is not identically zero, on it. [MEASURED] In every case computed, all with $s$-type densities, a general-orbital integral sits on that rung. [SYMBOLIC + MEASURED] On the graph the rung is read off the holomorphic period. Any tree of $n$ linked two-center densities has the same period, a Bessel moment in $n$ scales;\ its one-scale family is annihilated by an operator of order $n$, which for $n=2$ to $5$ is the Hadamard product of the Legendre operator with the operator of the equal-mass banana integral with $n-1$ propagators: $n=2$ is Legendre's $K$, $n=3$ Clausen's elliptic square (a K3 surface), $n=4$ entry \#58 of the Calabi–Yau tables, the first period not built from elliptic ones, and $n=5$ brings no new type. The real three-dimensional star and path of four densities reduce onto that operator with no K3 piece. A fourth distinct center adds no genus [MEASURED]. [SYMBOLIC + MEASURED] A Coulomb ring of three densities does not climb: its loop integrates out by the three-dimensional uniqueness relation and it sits on the elliptic rung, below the chain of three; the correlated triangle of explicitly correlated methods, with exponential geminals on two edges, sits with the chain on the K3 rung at $\gamma=1$, $3/2$ and $2$, its geminal acting as an imaginary external momentum, and its operators follow from integration by parts on the kite and the tetrahedron. A Gaussian geminal leaves the ladder for exponential periods. [OBSERVATION] Read as a Feynman graph, every instance fits one pattern: the class is set by the shape of the density graph and the masses on its lines. We state it as a bounded conjecture. Trees sit at rung $n$; the rung names an operator and not a finite set of numbers, since on every slice tested the three-electron fibre is a K3 function integrated once and at generic scales it has no relation to the K3 and elliptic periods in any basis tried; rings are ordered by uniqueness, not by count; and the integrated value sits at or below its fibre. The caps are stated: the three-dimensional reduction is shown through four densities, the dictionary's rung bound only for $s$-type trees on the one-scale slice, the only ring tested is the triangle, and the generic fibre's content is evidence. [PRIOR ART] The operators are known in the mathematics of Bessel moments and banana integrals; the Averys' momentum-space treatment of these same integrals re-expands every two-center density in a one-center basis and reaches the genus-zero class only through an infinite, non-sparse series. What is new is the route from molecular integrals to the ladder, the dictionary that places every orbital's integral in the $D$-module of its graph's $1s$ integral, and the prospective records in which most rungs were registered before they were computed.
