Entanglement Structure of the Angular Momentum Eigenbasis: Correlation in the Electron Repulsion and Basis-Intrinsic Sparsity

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 26 · Precision Observations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286828 · PDF

Abstract

We characterize the entanglement structure of multi-electron atoms in the angular momentum eigenbasis—the basis whose orbitals are products of hydrogenic radial functions and spherical harmonics, labeled by quantum numbers $(n, l, m)$. Four computational results are presented. First, the location of correlation: at the fixed exponent $k=Z$ the one-body Hamiltonian of a He-like ion is exactly diagonal in this basis, so the off-diagonal electron repulsion carries all of the correlation energy and all of the entanglement, and the entanglement entropy scales as $S \sim Z^{-2.52}$ across He-like ions. (An earlier version assigned $10$–$39%$ of the correlation energy to a ``graph Laplacian'' [retracted 2026-09-25: he-graph-native-hop-headline] off-diagonal one-body term; that term is not part of the ion's Hamiltonian and the attribution is withdrawn, Sec. (ref). Added to the Hamiltonian as a model perturbation it does illustrate the one-body entanglement-inertness theorem of Paper 27: it moves the model energy by $10$–$39%$ of the correlation energy while its layer-attributed [retracted 2026-09-25: he-graph-native-hop-headline] share of the entanglement is $<0.2%$, although adding it changes $S$ at $Z=2$ by $7%$.) Second, basis-intrinsic sparsity: for systems with spherical symmetry, the angular momentum eigenbasis gives sparse electron repulsion integrals (ERIs), and a generic unitary rotation of the orbital indices, no matter how small, fills the ERI tensor from $17%$ to $100%$ density in a rapid but graded transition at the identity. The sparse bases are not isolated: a common rotation of the coordinate frame mixes the $m$ labels and leaves every ERI unchanged. The nonzero ERI count is $Z$-independent, confirming that sparsity is purely geometric. Third, entanglement network topology: orbital mutual information maps reveal a hub migration pattern—$1s \to 2s \to 2p$—that tracks the partially filled shell across the first row of the periodic table (for $Z = 5$–$9$ the ground states are degenerate multiplets and the hub shares depend on the member; for $Z = 7$–$9$, where members were sampled, the values and even the presence of a nonzero valence network do too). Fourth, core-valence decoupling: the core-valence mutual information falls to $\approx 4×10^{-3}$ by $Z = 4$, decays monotonically through $Z = 6$, and reaches the $\approx 10^{-15}$ floating-point noise floor for $Z \geq 7$ — an exact core-closure property of this basis (the $1s$ occupation is exactly $2$), so it constrains the basis rather than justifying the composed fiber bundle factorization used in the GeoVac molecular framework. In the composed molecular architecture, per-block entanglement is $R$-independent by construction, with a bond/core entropy ratio of about $40×$ (block Hamiltonians without the Phillips–Kleinman term; about $57×$ with it). These results establish that the angular momentum eigenbasis isolates correlation and entanglement in the two-body $V_{ee}$, and that the Gaunt sparsity of the GeoVac qubit Hamiltonians — whose Pauli count is exactly linear in the qubit count at fixed basis — is a direct consequence of this basis choice.
