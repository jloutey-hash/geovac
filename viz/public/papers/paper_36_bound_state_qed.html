The Leading One-Loop Hydrogen Lamb Shift: Assembly, a Convention Erratum, and Coulomb-Sturmian Bethe Logarithms

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 36 · Precision Observations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286819 · PDF

Abstract

We assemble the hydrogen $2S_{1/2}$–$2P_{1/2}$ Lamb shift at leading one-loop order, $α(Zα)^4$, from the standard expressions reviewed by Eides, Grotch and Shelyuto, with the electron mass in place of the reduced mass and the Bethe logarithms tabulated by Drake and Swainson. The result is 1052.19 MHz, $5.65$ MHz ($0.534%$) below the Lundeen–Pipkin measurement, 1057.845(9) MHz, and $5.64$ MHz below the 2019 measurement of Bezginov et al., 1057.8298(32) MHz. This is textbook arithmetic, and the residual is not a measure of missing multi-loop physics: the self-energy part of the one-loop $α(Zα)^5$ term alone, with coefficient $A_{50} = 4π(139/128 - \tfrac12\ln 2) \approx 9.29$, adds about $+6.9$ MHz, more than the residual. Adding the review's other tabulated terms down to the kHz level gives $1057.824$ MHz, $0.020$ MHz below Lundeen–Pipkin and $0.005$ MHz below Bezginov et al. The paper records three things. First, an erratum: an earlier assembly used the self-energy constant $38/45$ in place of $10/9$. The difference is exactly the Uehling coefficient $4/15$, which had been subtracted inside the self-energy while the Uehling shift was also added separately; restoring $10/9$ moves the Lamb shift by $+27.13$ MHz. Second, Bethe logarithms computed from scratch in a Coulomb-Sturmian pseudostate basis at exponent $Z/n$: $\ln k_0(2P)$ and $\ln k_0(3D)$ reproduce the tabulated values to $2.4%$ and $0.07%$ with 40 basis functions ($2P$: $1.24%$ with 55), while the $2S$ acceleration-form value is not converged ($-0.92%$ at 40 functions is a crossing; $+2.98%$ at 50). Third, a negative result: a model two-loop self-energy sum on the Dirac spectrum of the three-sphere grows without bound with its cutoff and yields no finite two-loop coefficient without renormalization counterterms, which it does not contain. Evaluating hydrogen Bethe logarithms through the dynamical symmetry of the Coulomb problem is prior art (Lieber, 1968; Huff, 1969).
