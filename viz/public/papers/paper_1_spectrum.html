The Geometric Atom: the Hydrogen State Space as a Label Lattice

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 1 · Mathematical Foundations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286750 · PDF

Abstract

We study a discrete labelling of the hydrogen atom's bound-state space. The states $|n,\ell,m\rangle$ are indexed by a label lattice, drawn as a paraboloid (a visualization in which the depth coordinate $-1/n^2$ is placed by hand, not an output), on which the $SU(2)× SU(1,1)$ ladder operators act as sparse matrices. This single-particle model separates two kinds of content: (i) Algebraic exactness: [SYMBOLIC PROOF] \gvc{p1-ladder-exact}{Transition operators $T_\pm, L_\pm$ reproduce the Rydberg quantum numbers exactly from operator eigenvalues (a two-line commutator identity, checked in exact arithmetic): the $SU(1,1)$ element $N = -2[T_+, T_-]$ returns $n$ on the interior shells $n < n_{\max}$ (not on the truncated top shell), and $L^2$ returns $\ell(\ell+1)$ on every shell}; [PRIOR ART] on every shell, including the top one, the $SO(4)$ Casimir $\mathbf{L}^2 + \mathbf{A}^2 = n^2 - 1$ ($\mathbf{A}$ the energy-normalized Runge–Lenz vector) is exact on the labels (Fock [ref]). The energies $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ are an imposed map from $n$ (Fock's energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$, with $p_0 = Z/n$ fixed by Fock's kernel eigenvalue, Paper 7), not an output of the ladder operators; once Fock's kernel fixes the function, on the labels they are $E_n = -1/(2(C+1))$ of the Casimir $C = n^2-1$ (i.e. $H = -\tfrac12(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$ at $Z = 1$). (ii) Geometric artifacts: [MEASURED] A node-amplitude proxy for the $s/p$ gap is nonzero at finite lattice cutoff ($37%$ at $n_{\max}=5$, oscillating through $15.6%$ at $8$ and $1.7%$ at $10$ to $0.39%$ at $30$ on the binary production lattice; see the \S III QA note). [INTERNAL THEOREM] Its size tracks the residue of $n_{\max}$ modulo $3$ — exactly closed form on $n_{\max} \equiv 0$, and generically larger off it (the off-/on-branch ratio grows asymptotically as $n_{\max}/(\sqrt{3} π)$) — so the quoted endpoint sits on a favourable branch and the sequence is not evidence of convergence. The cutoff sweep from $n_{\max}=5$ to 30 shows this is a discretization artifact—a residue-controlled truncation effect (the $l$-blocks are disconnected and carry no degeneracy to lift)—that oscillates and reads $0.39%$ at $n_{\max}=30$ on the $n_{\max}\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ branch ($1.65%$ and $2.58%$ at $28$ and $29$): a proxy value, not a recovery of the Coulomb degeneracy, which belongs to the continuum operator (the graph's $l$-blocks are disconnected, so the graph has no $s/p$ degeneracy of its own). (iii) Geometric phase structure: [SYMBOLIC PROOF] The Berry phase vanishes identically for the real-valued transition operators; on the optional topological-weight lattice ($w = 1/(n_1 n_2)$) the edge-weight log-holonomy $\Theta(n) = -2\ln((n{+}1)/n) \sim n^{-1}$ measures the variation (a discrete gradient) of that weight function (on the binary production lattice and the CG-magnitude construction $\Theta \equiv 0$). This model describes only the electron's state space: it contains no electromagnetic coupling, and we find no trace of the fine structure constant $α$ in it (a companion paper [ref] records a separate numerical Observation about $α$, not a coupling mechanism).
