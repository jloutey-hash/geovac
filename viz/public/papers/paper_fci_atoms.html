Full configuration interaction in a hydrogenic basis on the S^3 label set: Multi-electron atoms via relational lattice indexing

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper fci · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286734 · PDF

Abstract

We implement full configuration interaction (FCI) for multi-electron atoms in a hydrogenic basis on the $S^3$ label set $(n,l,m)$: the physical Hamiltonian is the diagonal hydrogenic one-body term plus two-electron integrals assembled by Slater–Condon rules from exact $R^k$ radial integrals and Gaunt angular coupling coefficients. For helium, the ground-state energy with the physical Hamiltonian converges monotonically to $-2.8476 Ha$ (1.93% error versus exact) at $n_{\max}=5$, above the Hartree–Fock limit: the fixed-exponent hydrogenic bound-state basis omits the continuum. For lithium, the energy converges to $-7.4007 Ha$ (1.03% error) at $n_{\max}=5$. Against strong baselines the comparison is unfavourable: the helium energy stays above the Hartree–Fock limit, which correlation-consistent Gaussian bases pass already at cc-pVDZ; only the one-function STO-3G basis is bettered. For beryllium (4 electrons), the solver achieves $E = -14.563 Ha$ (0.71% error) at $n_{\max}=4$ with $487{,}635$ determinants—the first four-electron calculation in this framework. For lithium hydride (4 electrons, first heteronuclear molecule), the $R$-independence of each atom's one-electron block makes the potential-energy surface monotonically attractive with no equilibrium geometry, and it does not return to the separated-atom energy at large $R$, so no binding energy is extracted. An excitation-driven direct CI algorithm following Knowles and Handy [ref] replaces the $O(N_{SD}^2)$ pairwise determinant loop with $O(N_{SD} × N_{connected})$ assembly, achieving a 45$×$ speedup for lithium. The Hamiltonian is assembled via a relational lattice index that maps Slater determinants to sparse matrix entries without evaluating continuous four-center integrals, achieving $>$99% sparsity at the largest basis sizes tested. These results establish hydrogenic-basis FCI on the $S^3$ label set as a working, if basis-limited, route for few-electron systems.
