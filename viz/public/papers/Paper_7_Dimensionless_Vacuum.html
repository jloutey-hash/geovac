The Dimensionless Vacuum: Recovering the Schrödinger Equation on a Scale-Invariant Label Lattice

Josh Loutey · GeoVac synthesis document · Mathematical Foundations · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286774 · PDF

Abstract

Fock showed in 1935 that stereographic projection turns the momentum-space hydrogen Schrödinger equation into a rotation-invariant integral equation on the unit three-sphere $S^3$, whose kernel $1/|\omega - \omega'|^2$ is zonal and diagonal on the hyperspherical harmonics (the eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator $\Delta_{S^3}$, eigenvalue $-(n^2-1)$). [PRIOR ART] On those harmonics the kernel acts as $2π^2(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1/2}$ (eigenvalue $2π^2/n$), so the bound-state Hamiltonian is a function of $\Delta_{S^3}$, $H = -\tfrac{Z^2}{2}(1-\Delta_{S^3})^{-1}$, i.e. $E_n = -Z^2/(2(C+1))$ with $C = n^2-1$ the $SO(4)$ Casimir: in this sense Fock's projection makes $\Delta_{S^3}$ equivalent to the Schrödinger problem. (The kernel is the restriction to $S^3$ of the Green's function of the $ℝ^4$ Laplacian, not the Green's function of $\Delta_{S^3}$.) [MEASURED] We report what the GeoVac discrete graph Laplacian is measured to do through $n_{\max} = 30$—[INTERNAL THEOREM] its top eigenvalue saturates the bipartite bound $\lambda_{\max} \to 2 d_{\max} = 8$ at the closed-form rate $(C_{\mathrm{sat}} + o(1))/n_{\max}^2$ (Paper 0 \S VI; $C_{\mathrm{sat}}$ a saturation constant, not the Casimir $C$), proved rather than sampled—but this saturation is the band edge of two-dimensional grid graphs (twice the maximum degree) and carries no $S^3$ information. [WALL] \gvc{graph-s3-wall}{The edge Laplacian $L = D - A$ does not converge to the $S^3$ Laplace–Beltrami operator: its edges never change $l$, so it splits into $n_{\max}$ disconnected $l$-blocks, and its kernel is $n_{\max}$-dimensional at every cutoff while $\Delta_{S^3}$ has a single zero mode (tests/test_graph_s3_nonconvergence.py)}. What survives is exact at every cutoff: the node set $(n, l, m)$, $n^2$ per shell, is the Gel'fand–Tsetlin label set of the Peter–Weyl truncation of $L^2(S^3)$, on which $-\Delta_{S^3} = \mathbf{L}^2 + \mathbf{A}^2$ acts as the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2 - 1$ (a node weight, not an edge Laplacian; standard $SO(4)$ representation theory). The $S^3$ equivalence itself is carried by Fock's continuum projection, whose chain we audit by symbolic computation (its non-definitional step, the kernel eigenvalue, is tested for $n \le 4$; general $n$ is Fock's). [PRIOR ART] The Laplace–Beltrami operator on the round unit $S^3$ has pure integer eigenvalues $\lambda_n = -(n^2 - 1)$ with no intrinsic physical scale (standard; checked symbolically for $n \le 4$). [SYMBOLIC PROOF] The Rydberg energy levels $E_n = -Z^2/(2n^2)$ enter only through the energy-shell constraint $p_0^2 = -2E$, which acts as a ``stereographic focal length'' mapping the unit sphere to flat $ℝ^3$ momentum coordinates; the quantization $p_0 = Z/n$ itself is forced by the eigenvalue $2π^2/n = \mathrm{Vol}(S^3)/n$ of Fock's $S^3$ Coulomb kernel on the degree-$(n-1)$ harmonics. [PRIOR ART] That eigenvalue holds for every $n$ (Fock 1935; the Funk–Hecke theorem); we verify it in exact arithmetic for $n \le 4$ and every $l < n$ (one real harmonic per $l$) without inputting $E_n$ (tests/test_paper7_fock_kernel.py). [SYMBOLIC PROOF + MEASURED] At positive energy the projection lands on the hyperboloid $H^3$: continuing $n \to iZ/k$ releases only the cap $l \le n-1$, and every continuum density tested is the continued bound one times the single factor $1/(1-e^{-2π Z/k})$, the ratio of the two normalizations (Section (ref), tests/test_paper7_hydrogen_continuum_door.py); the factor is standard quantum defect theory [ref]. At $E = 0$, the third shape, the Runge–Lenz algebra contracts to the Euclidean group and both sides meet one $π$-free threshold function. [SYMBOLIC PROOF] The projection geometry is audited by a suite of 18 symbolic checks (ten of them limiting-case, definitional or non-discriminating) covering the stereographic map, the conformal Laplacian and its $l = 0$ spectrum, and the (definitional) eigenvalue-to-energy bookkeeping.
