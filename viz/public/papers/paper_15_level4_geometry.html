The Level 4 Natural Geometry: Two-Center Two-Electron Molecules in Molecule-Frame Hyperspherical Coordinates

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 15 · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286722 · PDF

Abstract

The natural geometry hierarchy assigns each separable quantum system a coordinate system in which Coulomb singularities become boundary conditions and the problem becomes sparse and discrete: $S^3$ for hydrogen (Paper 7; there the discrete object is the exact label set of the $S^3$ harmonics, not an edge-Laplacian discretization, which does not converge), prolate spheroidal for H$_2^+$ (Paper 11), and hyperspherical for helium (Paper 13). The two-center, two-electron molecule H$_2$ combines all five Coulomb singularities; its leading geometry is prolate spheroidal (Paper 12). As the structural $N$-electron generalization, we introduce molecule-frame hyperspherical coordinates $(R_e,α,\theta_1,\theta_2,\Phi)$ parameterized by the dimensionless ratio $\rho = R/(2R_e)$, where $R$ is the internuclear distance and $R_e$ the electronic hyperradius. The electron–electron cusp appears at $α=π/4$, $\theta_{12}=0$, independent of molecular geometry, where in prolate spheroidal coordinates the same locus is not a coordinate surface. That is a structural difference in where the cusp sits, and—see below—it does not translate into an accuracy advantage. The molecular charge function depends on $(R,R_e)$ only through $\rho$, so it can be cached on a one-dimensional $\rho$ grid; the angular eigenvalues themselves depend on both $\rho$ and $R_e$. A coupled-channel expansion in body-frame partial waves $(l_1,m_1,l_2,m_2)$ with $l_1+l_2$ even (gerade symmetry) and $m_1+m_2=0$, solved with a two-dimensional $(R_e,α)$ solver that avoids the adiabatic approximation, recovers 96.0% of the exact dissociation energy $D_e$ at $l_{\max}=6$ with $\sigma$ and $π$ channels (61 channels) on a hyperradial grid with inner wall $R_{e,\min} = 0.3$ bohr; the Schwartz cusp tail at this truncation is $0.03$ mHa ($0.02$ percentage points). That wall is not converged: at $l_{\max}=4$ moving it to $0.05$ bohr raises the recovered $D_e$ from $94.1%$ to $96.9$–$97.3%$, depending on the hyperradial grid. The $96.0%$ is not compared against Paper 12: Paper 12's $\sigma$-only $92.4%$ lacks the $π$ channels included here, and its $|m| \le 1$ result ($99.1%$) differs from the ones here in truncation, cusp treatment and solver class alike, so no coordinate-system advantage is claimed in either direction. The framework is extended to heteronuclear diatomics (HeH$^+$, $Z_A=2$, $Z_B=1$) via a charge-center origin shift and removal of the gerade constraint; the adiabatic solver gives 93.1% of the exact $D_e$ at $l_{\max}=4$ ($\sigma{+}π$, 57 channels), a non-variational over-binding: the 2D treatment at the same truncation and inner wall recovers only ${\sim}5%$, the absolute energy error ($2.4%$) being comparable to the small $0.075$ Ha binding. The solver has a double-adiabatic coupled-channel structure—nuclear coordinates as outer slow variable, hyperradius as inner slow variable, angular channels as fast variables—generalizing the single-adiabatic expansion of Paper 13.
