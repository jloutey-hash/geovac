The Molecular Fock Projection: Diatomic Molecules as Prolate Spheroidal Lattices

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 11 · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286714 · PDF

Abstract

Fock's 1935 stereographic projection maps the hydrogen atom onto the unit three-sphere $S^3$, where the Schrödinger equation becomes a pure Laplace–Beltrami eigenvalue problem with no potential term. The GeoVac program builds a graph on the $S^3$ label set $(n,l,m)$, on which $-\Delta_{S^3}$ is the diagonal $SO(4)$ Casimir $n^2-1$ at every cutoff, from which the hydrogenic energies follow through Fock's energy shell $p_0^2 = -2E$ (Papers 0, 1, 7);\ the graph Laplacian itself is not a discretization of the $S^3$ operator and does not converge to it, and its extremal eigenvalue matches the hydrogen ground state only by construction through the scale $\kappa = -1/16$ (Paper 7). For molecules, however, $S^3$ is not the natural geometry: the single-$p_0$ momentum scale cannot encode the $R$-dependent bonding physics in that single-$n$ shared-$p_0$ approximation (Paper 8–9, negative theorem; the genuine cross-$n$ Coulomb–Sturmian basis escapes it, and a validated two-centre Coulomb–Sturmian H$_2$ CI binds, Paper 60). We resolve this by identifying the natural geometry principle: every separable quantum system has a natural lattice—the label set and channel structure of the coordinates in which separation occurs, within which the separated equations are then solved. For one-center systems that geometry is $S^3$ (where the GeoVac lattice is the label set, not a convergent discretization of the Laplace–Beltrami operator, Paper 7); for two-center systems it is the prolate spheroid. We discretize the separated prolate spheroidal equations for H$_2^+$ using a spectral angular solver (Legendre basis) and two radial solvers—a self-adjoint finite-difference (FD) scheme and a spectral Laguerre basis—with Brent root-finding in the separation parameter $c^2 = -R^2 E_{\mathrm{elec}}/2$ to enforce self-consistency. For $\sigma$ states ($m = 0$), the spectral Laguerre matrix elements can be computed algebraically from three-term recurrence relations, eliminating numerical quadrature entirely (the default path uses Gauss–Laguerre quadrature, which agrees to machine precision). For $π$ and $\delta$ states ($m \neq 0$), an associated Laguerre basis absorbs the centrifugal $1/x$ singularity via partial-fraction decomposition and Stieltjes integral recurrence, reducing the non-algebraic content to a single transcendental seed value $e^a E_1(a)$. The method has zero free parameters: all inputs are nuclear charges, internuclear distance, and basis/grid resolution. The separation constant is also an exact label. In any product (Laguerre $×$ Legendre) basis, the matrices of $H$ and of the Erikson–Hill operator $\Lambda$ are Atkinson operator determinants, so $[H,\Lambda]=0$ holds at every truncation (measured to $10^{-14}$). $\Lambda$ runs from $L^2$ in the united atom to the Runge–Lenz $A_z$ (parabolic) label in the separated atoms. It does not transfer to a LiH orbital set that is not tensor-closed. Results for H$_2^+$ with the spectral Laguerre radial solver ($n_{\mathrm{basis}} = 20$): total energy at $R = 2.0$ bohr $E = -0.602634214494936$ Ha, reproducing Peek's value $-0.6026342144949$ Ha at that distance [ref] to $3.6×10^{-14}$ Ha—below the last digit to which that reference is quoted, so the residual is reference-limited rather than a property of the method ([MEASURED]). Equilibrium bond length $R_{\mathrm{eq}} = 1.9973$ bohr ($0.004%$ above the exact $1.99719$ bohr), and correct dissociation to H + p at $R \to \infty$. The spectral solver achieves a $250×$ dimension reduction and a $270×$ speedup over the FD solver, whose 1.01% error was a discretization artifact. This establishes the prolate spheroidal lattice as the molecular analog of Fock's atomic $S^3$ projection.
