The operator-algebra arc of the GeoVac framework: a reading guide for spectral triples, state-space Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, and the Lorentzian question

Josh Loutey · GeoVac synthesis document · Synthesis / Reading Guides · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286856 · PDF

Abstract

We provide a unified reading of the fourteen papers that make up the operator-algebra arc of the GeoVac framework (Papers 29, 32, 38, 39, 40, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50). The arc constructs an explicit almost-commutative spectral triple $(\AGV, \HGV, \DGV)$ on the Fock-projected unit three-sphere with a Camporesi–Higuchi spinor Dirac operator, places it in the Marcolli–van Suijlekom gauge-network lineage, and establishes four load-bearing results. First, the underlying truncated graphs are Ramanujan at small sizes — a finite-size graph-RH Observation (two of four families cross the irregular Ramanujan bound at modest sizes, through real eigenvalues, Paper 29 \S7), with integer-algebraic Ihara zeros (Paper 29). Second, \gvc{wh1-p38-gh-convergence}{the Connes–van Suijlekom truncations $\Tcal_{n_max}$ converge to the continuum spectral triple $\Tcal_{S³}$ in the van Suijlekom state-space Gromov–Hausdorff sense — for the commutative ($S^3$) factor on the true Camporesi–Higuchi window; convergence of the almost-commutative extension is [OPEN]} — \gvc{p38-rate-constant}{with the Fejér-kernel bound $d_{\mathrm{GH}}(\Tcal_{n_max}, \Tcal_{S³}) \le \gamma_{n_max}$, $\gamma_{n_max} \sim (4/π)\logn_max/n_max$, and a measured $O(1/n_max)$ under a tapered amplitude (Paper 38, main theorem; $4/π$ in the dual-Coxeter normalisation of the $SU(2)$ metric, $2/π$ on the unit $S^3$)}. Third, Paper 40 extends the convergence to compact connected semisimple Lie groups (conditionally for general $G$); whether the Fejér-moment constant is the same for every simple group is [OPEN], since its rank-2 extractions used a normalisation that does not reproduce the $SU(2)$ anchor. Fourth, the natural route to a Lorentzian convergence theorem on truncated $SU(2)×U(1)_{t}$ Krein spectral triples — compressing to the Krein-positive subspace — is closed by a degeneracy theorem (Paper 45): the compression annihilates the spatial Dirac and the restricted Lipschitz seminorm vanishes identically. An earlier convergence claim through this device, together with the strong-form and bridge extensions built on it (Papers 46, 48, 49), is withdrawn; the Lorentzian analog of the convergence question Connes and van Suijlekom [ref] deferred to ``elsewhere'' (answered on the Riemannian side for scalar compact groups by Gaudillot-Estrada and van Suijlekom, and for the spinor triple's commutative factor by Paper 38) remains open, now with a sharpened specification. Paper 47's de-compactification limit $T \to \infty$ survives at the norm-resolvent / spectral level. A master-theorem sketch (\S(ref)) subsumes Papers 38, 39, and 40 as special cases: \gvc{p40-general-g-conditional}{for arbitrary $k \ge 1$ and compact connected simple Lie groups $G_{1}, \ldots, G_{k}$, the k-fold tensor product converges (state-space GH) with per-factor Fejér-moment bounds, leading constant open beyond $SU(2)$ in Paper 40 (proved only for $SU(2)$ and $SU(2)×SU(2)$, on the lifted-state route with leading constant $1$; the general-$G$ $k = 1$ case is conditional on the per-group spin-window decomposition; the joint Lipschitz constant $C_3^{(k)} = 1$, by compression, belongs to the abandoned approximation-pair route)}. \gvc{m1-gh-rate-reading}{The Fejér-moment constant $4/π$ (Paper 38) is read — convention-dependently (dual-Coxeter normalisation) and as a reading, not a derivation — as the $M_{1}$ ($k = 0$ volume-slot) signature of the master Mellin engine, whose case-exhaustion (Paper 32 \S VIII) is [CONDITIONAL]}; the reading concerns the kernel's constant, not the GH rate; the $1/π^{2}$ of the Lorentzian HS-orthogonality (Paper 42 \S 11.7) is not shown to be the same signature — it is the square of the posited normalisation $\beta = 2π/\kappa_g$. Further papers (48, 49, 50, 53) extend the arc; Papers 48–50 are recorded in \S(ref)–\S(ref), and Paper 53 is summarized at the end of this abstract (Paper 52 is archived); metric-level claims among them descoped by Paper 45's degeneracy theorem are reported here in corrected form. Nothing in the account below is retracted by that move, and each of the four retains content that is not refuted—Lemma 3.2's degeneracy diagnosis, the norm-resolvent arrow, the bridge's categorical design, and the cocycle-deficit algebra respectively. Paper 45 itself remains live and load-bearing. See docs/retired_papers.md. [ARCHIVED 2026-09-28] Paper 43 has likewise been archived: both of its headline readings were withdrawn (the finite-cutoff period closure is automatic, and its $1/π^{2}$ is the posited $\beta = 2π/\kappa_g$ squared), and its surviving content — the Krein carrier, the $(4,6)$ axiom audit, the Krein-carrier period closure, the six-witness statement by construction, and the chirality-parity HS-orthogonality — now lives in Paper 42 \S 11, to which every Paper 43 reference below is repointed. Paper 50 re-derives the Klebanov–Pufu–Safdi sphere F-theorem values in closed Hurwitz form from the full continuum spectra (the finite truncation never enters, so this is a re-derivation of known continuum values, not a finite-cutoff result); an earlier positioning paper that read the truncation convergence as a non-holographic ``correspondence'' producing these values is archived (2026-09-29), its content superseded by a short not-holographic remark in Paper 50 (the framework has no bulk); and Paper 53 proves state-space convergence of the spectral truncations of the flat plane $ℝ^{2}$ (a positive squared-window reconstruction, rate $\gamma_{1}\Lambda^{-1/2}$; the four compression/lift facts and the GH bound on the named state classes [INTERNAL THEOREM], their canonicity open), leaving the disk-with-cone open: the finite-disk Markov–Ces\`aro reconstruction map is not positive; the plane Bochner–Riesz lift is not positive either (its kernel is negative somewhere, which is why the squared window is used); and the cone has no translation group. Paper 48 constructs Krein-pointed proper quantum metric spaces and a bridge functor to the Mondino–Sämann synthetic-Lorentzian pre-length spaces; the metric-level inheritance is descoped and the bridge survives as a conditional design. Paper 49 builds an OSLPLS (operator-system Lorentzian pre-length space) category and reads off-orbit super-additivity as the operator-algebraic dual of the twin paradox, with the deficit quantified by Datta's max-divergence chain inequality; the $\Lambda$-inheritance is descoped while the cocycle-deficit algebra survives. Paper 50 evaluates the standard zeta-regularised CFT-on-sphere free energies on $S³$ and $S⁵$ from the continuum spectra in closed Hurwitz form (re-deriving known values), exhibiting an orthogonal $\log 2$ / $ζ(3)/π^{2}$ decomposition of the continuum (scalar, Dirac) values (an $M_{3}$-ring period and an $M_{1}× M_{3}$ cross-product in the master Mellin engine's period-ring stratification) and a log 3 cancellation theorem on $S⁵$. Because these values are computed from the continuum spectra, they are not evidence from the truncated triple; the framework's substrate-level scale-freedom—packing diameter $d_{0}$ cancels out, scales enter only through the Fock focal length $p_{0}$—parallels the scale-invariance of conformal field theory, but does not explain these closures. This synthesis gathers the statements, sketches the load-bearing proofs, and exhibits the structural relationships between the papers; no new theorems are introduced.
