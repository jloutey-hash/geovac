Algebraic Two-Electron Integrals on the Prolate Spheroidal Lattice

Josh Loutey · GeoVac Paper 12 · Quantum Chemistry · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21286718 · PDF

Abstract

Paper 11 established the prolate spheroidal lattice as the natural geometry for diatomic molecules, solving H$_2^+$ to machine precision (spectral Laguerre solver) with zero free parameters. The extension to two electrons requires electron-electron repulsion integrals $\langle\phi_i|1/r_{12}|\phi_j\rangle$ over the six-dimensional two-particle configuration space. The standard approach—numerical quadrature on a grid in prolate spheroidal coordinates—converges slowly due to the Coulomb singularity at $r_{12} = 0$ and saturates at $\sim$80% of the exact H$_2$ dissociation energy $D_e$ as the basis grows. We replace numerical integration with algebraic evaluation using the Neumann expansion of $1/r_{12}$ in prolate spheroidal coordinates, which decomposes each six-dimensional integral into a finite sum of products of one-dimensional auxiliary integrals computable by recurrence relations. The resulting two-electron integrals are exact within the Neumann truncation order $L$, with no six-dimensional quadrature and no fitted parameters. The one step historically left to numerical integration—the log-singular second-kind Legendre moment $B_l$—also has a closed form (Sec. (ref)): the only transcendental content is the exponential integral $E_1$, the Euler–Mascheroni constant $\gamma$ and $\ln$, so in the $\sigma$ sector the reduction is fully quadrature-free. (The $|m| \ge 1$ channels keep the same kernel; generalising the same $A_l$, $B_l$, $X_l$ recurrences to associated Legendre functions (Sec. (ref)) gives a moment-recurrence engine that is stable in the $\delta$ ($|m| = 2$) sector where the differentiation route diverges, and—with the same closed-form seeds—is likewise fully quadrature-free.) A systematic convergence study on H$_2$ at $R = 1.4011$ bohr demonstrates that the Neumann algebraic $V_{ee}$ yields 92.2% of the exact $D_e$ with 27 basis functions, compared to 79.6% for numerical $V_{ee}$ at the same basis size—a uniform 12–20 percentage point improvement across all basis sizes from 1 to 72 functions. The Neumann result plateaus at 92.4%, confirming that $V_{ee}$ integration error has been eliminated. [MEASURED] The remaining 7.6% gap is a basis-space limit, and we identify which one. The basis used here is $\varphi$-independent, so it spans only $m_1 = m_2 = 0$ configurations; but a $^1\Sigma_g^+$ state constrains the total $M = m_1 + m_2$ to zero, not each $m_i$ separately, so the $π^2$, $\delta^2$, \ldots configurations—every one of them $^1\Sigma_g^+$—are absent by construction. Restoring them in the same basis with the same Neumann kernel gives $99.1%$ of $D_e$ at $|m| \le 1$ (stability envelope $99.0$–$99.1%$; Sec. (ref)), a gain of $11.6$ mHa that no growth along the $\sigma$ axis reproduces (near-tripling the $\sigma$ basis is worth $0.34$ mHa). [MEASURED] That $99.1%$ is where the monomial radial basis $\xi^j$ stalls—a Hankel moment problem whose overlap matrix is past double precision, not a structural ceiling. Re-basing the same span onto an orthogonal-polynomial family is an exact change of basis that removes the linear dependence, and the energy then climbs monotonically and variationally to $\gvq{p12_rebased_de_pct_aopt}{99.81}%$ of $D_e$ ($\gvq{p12_rebased_err_mha_aopt}{0.32}$ mHa, inside chemical accuracy) at a larger truncation and the variationally optimal exponent (Sec. (ref)). [MEASURED] An explicit-$r_{12}$ (James–Coolidge) extension of the algebraic engine, in which every $r_{12}$ matrix element reduces to the same $A K_0 - B K_1$ Neumann kernels as $V_{ee}$ (no quadrature), reaches $\gvq{p12_r12_err_mha}{0.053}$ mHa above the exact value—a sixfold reduction of the CI residual—confirming constructively that ordinary integer powers of $r_{12}$, not non-analytic terms, close the gap (Sec. (ref)). The same reduction—on one centre the classical Hylleraas-CI treatment of many-electron correlated integrals—extends without a resolution of identity to a heteronuclear diatomic (HeH$^+$) and through the complete many-body matrix elements of a two-center four-electron explicit-$r_{12}$ CI:\ the genuinely four-body Coulomb bridge and the two-center three-body triangle are both reduced RI-free—the latter through a low-rank expansion of its non-separable two-point kernel contracted mode-by-mode with the general-$m$ prolate Neumann Coulomb—so the entire $2×2$ CI energy assembles deterministically; for the cusp-correct geminal $f = r e^{-\gamma r}$ its correlation energy matches a same-geminal variational Monte-Carlo reference to sub-mHa, and the energy lies above the exact value (variational) (Sec. (ref)).
