## 3. Avery
1. **J.E. Avery, "Fast Electron Repulsion Integrals for Molecular Coulomb Sturmians", Adv. Quantum Chem. 67 (2013)** [V, KU portal]: ERIs via expansion of *densities* in 2k-Sturmians + hyperspherical harmonics, ~40 ns/integral. Orbital ERIs only; no geminals.
2. **J.E. Avery & J.S. Avery, "4-Center STO Interelectron Repulsion Integrals With Coulomb Sturmians", Adv. Quantum Chem. 76 (2018)** [S, publisher page 403].
3. **J.S. Avery & J.E. Avery, *Generalized Sturmians and Atomic Spectra* (World Scientific, 2006)** and J.S. Avery, *Hyperspherical Harmonics and Generalized Sturmians* [S/M]: many-electron generalized Sturmians, correlation via configuration mixing, not explicit r_ij.
No Avery work found touching explicit r_ij / geminal integrals. Relevance: his density-expansion idea (expand a one-electron *density* in an auxiliary Sturmian set, then do the Coulomb analytically) is structurally the same move as the corpus's "dressed density -> Neumann Coulomb" step; he is a natural person to ask whether the *dressed* (geminal-convolved) density stays inside a 2k-Sturmian expansion.

## 5. Framing for the PI (one paragraph)
"The way we evaluate the many-electron explicitly correlated terms is not new in principle: expanding the r_ij factors across a bridging electron with an addition theorem, or integrating out one electron to get a dressed density, is how atomic Hylleraas-CI has handled its three- and four-electron integrals since Calais-Lowdin and Perkins, and King, Ruiz and Sims-Hagstrom have the four-electron case worked out exactly. It is also known that with one r_ij per term nothing is ever worse than four-electron, whatever N is. What we have not found anywhere is the same thing carried to two centres: Slater-type geminals between prolate-spheroidal densities, with the Coulomb done by the two-centre Neumann expansion, inside a four-electron diatomic energy (LiH) on a many-determinant reference, with no RI and no numerical 3N-dimensional integration. The literature's answer for 4-electron diatomics is Gaussian geminals, or F12 with RI. So: the method is the classical one-centre Hylleraas reduction, and the extension to two centres looks new as far as we can tell."

## 6. Questions for Avery
1. Is he aware of any exact (non-RI, non-Gaussian) evaluation of three- or four-electron explicitly correlated integrals over *two-centre* Slater/Sturmian densities? (Our search found none; his group knows the ETO molecular-integral community -- Fernandez Rico, Ruiz, Harris -- best.)
2. Does a geminal-convolved density (rho * e^{-gamma r}) stay finitely expandable in his 2k-Sturmian density basis? If so his fast-ERI machinery would evaluate our bridge term directly, multi-centre included.
3. Has the Sturmian / hyperspherical community attempted Hy-CI for molecules beyond H2, and where did it stop (the Ruiz-Sims-Padhy 2021 review lists molecular Hy-CI for H2 only)?
4. Would the Shibuya-Wulfman / Coulomb-Sturmian route offer a three-centre version of the Neumann step (our polyatomic wall)?
