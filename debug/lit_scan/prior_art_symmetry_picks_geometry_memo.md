## What we can build on

1. **The established theorem is the Miller school's.** Separable coordinates correspond to maximal commuting sets of second-order symmetry operators in the enveloping algebra. Subgroup-type coordinates correspond to subgroup chains, and the separation constants are the chain labels. Two consequences:
   - The PI's chemistry principle, read as "surviving labels pick the geometry", is **anticipated** for Levels 1-2 (Boyer-Kalnins-Miller; Kalnins-Miller-Winternitz O(4)/hydrogen 1976).
   - The precise form is **"the surviving commuting quadratic operators pick the geometry", not "the surviving subgroup picks the geometry"**. The two differ in two ways:
     - Non-subgroup systems exist (ellipsoidal, prolate, oblate, Lamé on S³).
     - Miller-Patera-Winternitz report that the converse fails: some commuting pairs give no separable coordinates.
2. **H₂⁺ is the bridge case, and it EXTENDS-OURS.** Coulson-Joseph's K is quadratic and is not a Lie generator. When a second nucleus is added, the Lie survivor is only L_z, which matches the atlas door-11 finding (record SY). Yet the problem still separates, in prolate spheroidal coordinates, through K, which interpolates between L² (united atom) and the Runge-Lenz component (separated atoms). The atlas symmetry-survivor column therefore needs a **second-order (hidden) survivor** entry next to the Lie survivor. Without it, door 11 reads as "geometry lost" when it is in fact "geometry changed".
3. **The S³ classification table.** Olevskii / Schöbel-Veselov / Dawson-Dullin give 6 systems. Spherical is SO(4) ⊃ SO(3) ⊃ SO(2). Cylindrical (toric) is SO(2)×SO(2), the Hopf/chiral grouping of record HG. Parabolic (Stark) is the hydrogen image of a non-spherical chain. This table is the menu for the "third grouping" study proposed in the memory note.
4. **Dynamical symmetry (Iachello school, Leviatan review)** is the established version of "chain gives basis gives geometry", including chain mixing under breaking and partial dynamical symmetry. It is the right language for intermediate cases such as the atlas doors where some labels survive.
5. **Gauge side.** Michel's critical-orbit theorem (extended to gauge theories by Gaeta-Morando) is the analogue of the separation theorem: the orbit-space geometry fixes the admissible residual subgroups independently of the potential. Li 1974 and Slansky 1981 supply the pattern and branching tables. Kugo-Yamatsu warn that the survivors are often special rather than regular subgroups.
