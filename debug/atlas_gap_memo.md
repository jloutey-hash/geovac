## Door A1: a uniform electric field (Stark)

**Evidence (computed here).**
- *Linear Stark, exact.* `debug/atlas_gap_linear_stark.py`: z in the (l, m) basis with exact radial integrals and Gaunt coefficients, shells n = 2, 3, 4, every m. All eigenvalues equal (3/2) n (n1 - n2), with n1 + n2 = n - |m| - 1 (9 of 9 blocks). The n = 2 shell splits 4 -> 1 + 2 + 1 (+-3F, 0 doubly), the same pattern as door 11's guard. Paper 11's separated-atom limit explains it: a distant second nucleus acts as a Stark field at leading order.
- *Ground-state Rayleigh-Schroedinger series, exact.* `debug/atlas_gap_field_series.py`: psi_k = e^{-r} sum c_ij r^i P_j(cos theta). L = H0 + 1/2 is triangular on r^i P_j, so each order is solved in exact rationals (intermediate normalization). Result:
  E = -1/2 - (9/4)F^2 - (3555/64)F^4 - (2512779/512)F^6 - (13012777803/16384)F^8 - ..., odd orders zero; computed to F^120. These are the textbook values (Silverstone 1978 tabulates such coefficients to 17th order; we opened only the abstract, so the match to his table is not checked digit by digit).
- *Large order and pi.* If the width is Gamma = (4/F) e^{-2/(3F)} (Oppenheimer's formula), the Cauchy dispersion relation in g = F^2 predicts a_k -> -(6/pi)(3/2)^{2k}(2k)! for the F^{2k} coefficient. `debug/atlas_gap_richardson.py` (exact coefficients to k = 60, Richardson orders 2-8): the ratio a_k / [-(6/pi)(3/2)^{2k}(2k)!] extrapolates to 1 - 8e-11. That is an independent check of both the Oppenheimer prefactor and the action 2/3. pi enters only through the dispersion integral's 1/pi, an M1-type measure factor. The exponent's action 2/3 is rational.

**What is released, what survives.** For any F != 0 the potential is unbounded below along the field. The resolvent is no longer compact, the spectrum is the whole real line, and the bound states become resonances. So n is released, and with it l. What survives exactly is m (rotation about the field axis) and the parabolic separation constant: the problem separates in parabolic coordinates, which makes it integrable. The first-order labels (n1, n2, m) survive as resonance labels. Inside SO(4) the first-order survivor is the torus generated by L_z and A_z.

**Price.** Two pieces.
- Per order: rational coefficients. The series is FORCED and pi-free, below the period ladder's first rung.
- Non-perturbative: the series diverges factorially. Its content is an exponentially small width e^{-2/(3F)} with a rational action, plus 1/pi in the large-order law. The Borel sum is lateral (it has an imaginary part). This is a trans-series price, not a period: e^{-2/(3F)} at rational F is an exponential of an algebraic number. It sits in the same family as door 23's e^{-2 pi eta} and door 12's e^a E_1(a), a sideways move off the period ladder. It is not a climb.

The field is consumed as input, the projection chain is the parabolic separation, and the class is named (rational per order, exponential of a rational action non-perturbatively). Falsifier: a non-rational per-order coefficient, or a large-order constant other than 6/pi (fails the Richardson check).

## Door A2: a uniform magnetic field (Zeeman, with the diamagnetic term)

**Evidence (computed here).**
- *Linear term.* (B/2)(L_z + 2S_z) commutes with H0, L^2 and L_z, so it shifts each level by a rational B(m + 2m_s)/2. This is a label shift, with no computation needed. Inside SO(4) it keeps the torus <L_z, A_z>.
- *Diamagnetic series, exact.* Same solver, V = (beta/8) r^2 sin^2 theta with beta = B^2 (the linear term vanishes for m = 0):
  E = -1/2 + (1/4)beta - (53/192)beta^2 + (5581/4608)beta^3 - (21577397/2211840)beta^4 + ..., computed to beta^60. The series alternates.
- *Large order.* The ratio a_k / [(-1)^{k+1} pi^{-2k} Gamma(2k+3/2)] Richardson-extrapolates to 1.82925940468, against (4/pi)^{5/2} = 1.82925940492 (relative 1e-10). This is the form Avron (1981) derived. We opened only that paper's abstract, so the constant's attribution rests on our fit plus the paper's stated subject. Here pi sits in the growth rate: the Borel singularity lies at B = +-i pi. The non-perturbative scale is therefore e^{-pi/|B|}, reached only for imaginary B. At real B the series is Borel summable (Avron–Herbst–Simon 1981, per the bibliographic record; not opened).

**What is released, what survives.** The linear term releases nothing. The diamagnetic term breaks the n-degeneracy and the Runge-Lenz symmetry and mixes l (with Delta l = 2, at fixed parity). It does not release the discreteness of the bound spectrum: below the first Landau threshold the levels remain bound, because the term confines in the plane. m and z-parity survive exactly. At high field the classical problem is non-integrable (the "hydrogen in a magnetic field" chaos literature: Friedrich–Wintgen, Phys. Rep. 1989; Hasegawa–Robnik–Wunner 1989, cited from memory and not opened). Within an n-manifold at first order an approximate extra integral is reported (Solovev 1981; Herrick 1982, also cited from memory and not opened).

**Price.** Per order, rationals, FORCED and pi-free. Non-perturbatively, pi enters the instanton action itself, but only off the physical axis; the physical sum is Borel summable with no width. At high field the number class of a finite-B eigenvalue is unknown. Non-integrability suggests there is no closed form, but that is evidence, not a wall.

**Proposed row.**

| 29 | A uniform magnetic field on hydrogen (Zeeman; diamagnetic term $\tfrac18 B^2\rho^2$) | linear term: nothing (a rational shift $B(m+2m_s)/2$); diamagnetic term: the $n$-degeneracy and the Runge–Lenz symmetry, $l$ mixed by $\Delta l = 2$; bound levels stay discrete below the Landau threshold | $m$ and $z$-parity exactly; at first order in a shell, an approximate extra integral (prior art, not checked here) | per order, exact rationals ($\tfrac14, -\tfrac{53}{192}, \tfrac{5581}{4608}, \dots$ in $B^2$); the series alternates and is Borel summable, with large order $(-1)^{k+1}(4/\pi)^{5/2}\pi^{-2k}\Gamma(2k+\tfrac32)$: $\pi$ in the action, off the physical axis only; a finite-$B$ eigenvalue's class OPEN | FREE (the field $B$, a Layer-2 input, Paper 34); FORCED (the linear shift, rational coefficients); OPEN (high field) | none | Paper 34 `sec:layer2_boundary` (Landau spectrum HO-class) | owed: guard rebuilding $-53/192$ and the large-order constant (data `debug/data/atlas_gap_richardson.json`) | Zeeman 1896; Avron 1981; Avron–Herbst–Simon 1981; Friedrich–Wintgen 1989 | $SO(4) \to$ the torus $\langle L_z, A_z\rangle$ (linear), $\to SO(2)_m \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (diamagnetic) | as door 11 |

**Verdict.** FREE for the field. The high-field regime is OPEN, not a WALL, because no obstruction is proved. Falsifier: a non-rational coefficient, or a Richardson constant away from (4/pi)^{5/2}.

## Door B: quotients of the sphere, S^3/Gamma (lens spaces)

**Evidence (computed here).** `debug/atlas_gap_lens.py` covers L(p,1) = S^3/Z_p with (z1, z2) -> (w z1, w z2), w = e^{2 pi i/p}. This is left multiplication by a unit quaternion, so Z_p sits in SU(2)_L. The eigenspace of eigenvalue n^2 - 1 is V_j (x) V_j with j = (n-1)/2. The multiplicities were computed by three routes: weight counting, an exact character average, and the Molien/Ikeda generating function. All three agree for n <= 8:

| p | m_n, n = 1..8 | generating function sum_n m_n t^{n-1} |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1, 0, 9, 0, 25, 0, 49, 0 | (1 + 6t^2 + t^4)/(1 - t^2)^3 |
| 3 | 1, 0, 3, 8, 5, 12, 21, 16 | (1+t)(1 - 2t + 5t^2 - 2t^3 + t^4) / ((1-t)^3 (1+t+t^2)^2) |
| 5 | 1, 0, 3, 0, 5, 12, 7, 16 | (1+t)(1 - 2t + 5t^2 - 8t^3 + 13t^4 - 8t^5 + 5t^6 - 2t^7 + t^8) / ((1-t)^3 (1+t+t^2+t^3+t^4)^2) |

The closed form is m_n = n * #{k in {-(n-1), -(n-3), ..., n-1} : p | k}. The generating function is Ikeda–Yamamoto's F_L(z) = ((1-z^2)/q) sum_l prod_j 1/((1 - xi^{l s_j} z)(1 - xi^{-l s_j} z)). We read the formula in Lauret–Miatello–Rossetti, arXiv:1904.01146, which attributes it to Ikeda–Yamamoto, Osaka J. Math. 16 (1979) 447, Thm 3.2. Our formula is that one with s_1 = s_2 = 1.

*A spectral sum.* `debug/atlas_gap_lens_F.py` computes the lens analogue of Paper 2's F = sum n^2/n^4 = zeta(2), namely F_p = sum m_n/n^4, exactly. For odd p, c(n) = n/p + e(n) with e periodic mod 2p, and e turns out to be **odd**. So the even part, which would carry zeta(3) or L(3, chi_5), vanishes identically, and only cotangent derivatives remain:
- F_2 = pi^2/8
- F_3 = pi^2/18 + 8 sqrt(3) pi^3/729 (the sqrt(3) pi^3 term is L(3, chi_{-3}))
- F_5 = pi^2/30 + pi^3 * (an algebraic number in the real cyclotomic field of level 20)/6250

Each agrees with direct Hurwitz-zeta evaluation to 1e-60. *Eta-type sums.* The cotangent sum behind the signature eta invariant of L(p,1), sum_k cot^2(pi k/p) = (p-1)(p-2)/3, holds exactly for p = 2..11. It is rational. The attribution to Atiyah–Patodi–Singer II (1975) is from memory, not opened.

**Is there a physical door?** No new physics; this is a symmetry-sector selection. A finite group acts freely on S^3 = SU(2) only if no element has a fixed point. Every element of the rotation group (conjugation q -> g q g^{-1}, generated by L) fixes q = +-1. So a nontrivial free Gamma cannot lie inside the spatial rotations. It must act through the chiral factors J^+- = (L +- A)/2, that is, through the Runge-Lenz vector. That is a dynamical symmetry, not a point map of position space. For L(p,1) the quotient keeps, shell by shell, the states with 2 J^+_z = L_z + A_z ≡ 0 (mod p), i.e. m + (n1 - n2) ≡ 0 (mod p) in parabolic labels (sign convention for n1 - n2 not fixed here). For p = 2 this gives "odd n only", as the table shows. L_z + A_z is the Hopf fibre generator (atlas door 11 cell; door 25), so L(p,1) amounts to coarsening the Hopf U(1) to Z_p. That last sentence is a reading, not checked against Paper 24. The hydrogen Hamiltonian commutes with this Gamma, but nothing physical imposes the sector. Point-group quotients (Gamma inside the rotations) do correspond to physical symmetry sectors, but they have fixed points: they give orbifolds, not lens spaces, and their price is the ordinary rational branching of door 15.

**Proposed row.**

| 30 | A quotient of the sphere, $S^3/\Gamma$ (lens spaces $L(p,q)$, spherical space forms) | nothing physical: a symmetry sector is kept; a free $\Gamma$ must act through the Runge–Lenz vector, so it is not a point map of space (rotations fix $q = \pm1$) | the eigenvalues $n^2-1$; multiplicities cut to the $\Gamma$-invariant harmonics (for $L(p,1)$: $L_z + A_z \equiv 0 \bmod p$) | multiplicities integers with a rational generating function (Molien; Ikeda–Yamamoto); spectral sums in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}[\pi]$ (e.g. $\sum m_n/n^4 = \pi^2/18 + 8\sqrt3\,\pi^3/729$ at $p=3$, $\zeta(3)$ cancelling); eta-type cotangent sums rational | FORCED (the multiplicities and the generating function, as mathematics); not a physical door | none | none (mathematical; nearest: Paper 24 `sec:ks_frame`, the Hopf fibre) | owed: guard rebuilding the three-route multiplicity table and $F_3$ (data `debug/data/atlas_gap_lens*.json`) | Molien 1897; Ikeda–Yamamoto 1979; Ikeda 1980; Lauret–Miatello–Rossetti 2019 | $SO(4) \to$ the centralizer of $\Gamma$ ($U(1)_{J^+} \times SU(2)_{J^-}$ for $L(p,1)$, $p>2$); rotations intact only for $p = 2$ | n/a |

**Verdict.** FORCED as mathematics, but a mathematical row, not a door out of the skeleton. The PM may prefer to put it in the "What this is not" section rather than the table. Compared with door 5 (pi^{2k} * Q on S^3), the coefficient field widens to a cyclotomic field of level about p, and odd powers of pi appear, multiplied by algebraic numbers. That is a sideways move, not a climb. Falsifier: a lens-space spectral sum at an integer argument whose value is not in Q-bar[pi] or Q-linear combinations of Dirichlet L-values (mixed Tate over the cyclotomic field).

## Conclusion: does any of these make the class climb?

No.
- **External fields** keep the per-order price rational, which is lower on the ladder than even two centres. Their extra content is non-perturbative: an exponentially small width e^{-2/(3F)} with a rational action (Stark), and a pi in the Borel action off the physical axis (diamagnetic). That is a sideways step into trans-series, the same family as door 23's e^{-2 pi eta}. It is not a new period. The one thing these doors add that the atlas lacks is a price measured in the divergence of a series rather than in a closed form. All of it is classical prior art (Oppenheimer, Silverstone, Benassi–Grecchi–Harrell–Simon, Avron). What we did ourselves: exact rational coefficients to high order, and a Richardson check of both large-order constants to 1e-10.
- **Lens quotients** keep integers and rational generating functions. Their spectral sums stay in Q-bar[pi]: the coefficient field widens to cyclotomic level p, and the zeta(3) and L(3, chi_5) pieces cancel exactly in the F analogue. Again sideways, and classical (Molien, Ikeda–Yamamoto). Our only small additions are the cancellation in F_p and the observation that a free quotient must act through the Runge-Lenz vector, so it is not a physical door.
- **Torus and squashed ring** price nothing. They measure how fast a label dies (integer orders by charge counting), not a number class.

So these three moves support the atlas's reading. Geometry alone, meaning more centres, fields, quotients or deformations, does not climb past the elliptic rung (door 13). The climbs in the atlas (K3 at door 17, the Calabi–Yau-type operator at door 20) come from linking densities with kernels, that is from electrons, not from changing the geometry. A caveat: the high-field diamagnetic eigenvalue's class is OPEN, so "no climb" is established here only for the perturbative and Borel-sum content.
