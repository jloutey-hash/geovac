## Verdicts per comparison target

**vs. Double factorization / THC (arbitrary-basis, rank-compressed second quantization).** These are today's strongest fault-tolerant baselines and they are not really playing the same game as Paper 60's headline result: DF/THC report λ as a function of orbital/basis size N (directly tied to qubit count) for real many-electron molecules at production scale (FeMoco, water, N2), with λ already pushed to the tens-to-hundreds range through classical rank compression. Paper 60's sublinear result (‖M‖₁ ~ K^0.84) is instead a function of **configuration count K** in a single-atom CI expansion — a different resource axis that the paper itself flags as not yet translated into a qubit-count statement. Where Paper 60 does state an N-electron, basis-size result for the interacting problem (H2 CI, Sec. V.B–V.C), it reports **λ ~ n_orb^2.2**, a polynomial growth in the same ballpark as *naive*, uncompressed second quantization — i.e., roughly what DF/THC were invented to beat, not an improvement on them. Paper 60 loses this comparison outright on its own molecular numbers, and the paper says so explicitly ("the block-encoding cost scales polynomially... not a sublinear matrix"). The atomic-only sublinear win has no molecular-scale, many-electron analogue yet, so there is no head-to-head win over DF/THC to claim.

**vs. First-quantized plane-wave methods (Babbush 2018, Su et al. 2021, and the "sublinear to the continuum" 2019 line).** This is Paper 60's own chosen precedent for the "compute don't load" lever, and the comparison is honest but lopsided in maturity and scope. Both approaches generate structure on the device from labels rather than loading a classically-precomputed tensor — plane-wave methods generate momentum-space phases, Paper 60 generates Gaunt/3j/6j angular coefficients. But plane-wave first quantization has full circuits, constant factors, and resource estimates for real molecules and materials at large N (up to millions of plane waves), demonstrated λ and Toffoli reductions of ~1000× over naive approaches, and a qubit-count advantage (O(η log N)) that specifically targets the *large-basis* regime. Paper 60's result lives at the opposite end of the scale: a single helium atom with K≤164 configurations and an H2+/H2 toy molecule at ~16–31 qubits. Paper 60 does not compete with plane-wave first quantization on any axis except conceptual kinship; it is a much smaller, much less mature instance of the same idea, not a rival result. Ties only in spirit ("compute don't load"), loses decisively on demonstrated scale, generality, and hard resource numbers.

**vs. Sparse/low-rank second-quantized qubitization (Berry et al. 2019).** Same story as DF/THC: a mature, general, many-electron, arbitrary-basis method with explicit T-complexity O(N^{3/2}λ) and published λ values for real systems. Paper 60 offers no comparable many-electron, many-orbital λ number that beats this; its own honest molecular result (λ ~ n_orb^2.2) does not engage with rank/sparsity compression at all. Loses on demonstrated generality and scale; not a head-to-head contest since Paper 60 never attempts the same benchmark systems.

**vs. VQE/NISQ second-quantized methods.** Different cost model entirely (gate-depth/shot-count vs. fault-tolerant query count), and Paper 60 is explicit that this particular paper is a fault-tolerant/qubitization resource analysis, not a NISQ proposal (the GeoVac project positions for NISQ elsewhere). At the *scale* Paper 60 actually reaches (He, H2+, H2 — a handful of qubits), ordinary VQE second-quantized methods have been running on real hardware for a decade with directly comparable or smaller qubit counts (e.g., minimal-basis LiH at 10 qubits/276 Pauli terms). On maturity, VQE decisively wins at this scale: it is hardware-demonstrated, Paper 60 is resource-estimate/simulation-only. Not a fair apples-to-apples cost comparison (different metrics), but on "has anyone actually run something this size," VQE wins.

**vs. Quantum generalized-eigenvalue-problem algorithms (Liang 2022, Baek 2023, Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al. 2025/26).** These are not competing cost results but the toolkit Paper 60 itself must borrow from for the molecular case (QSVT-based S^{-1/2} whitening, per Liang 2022, is exactly the machinery used in Paper 60's own H2+ resource model, Sec. IV.A). None of the three combines a Sturmian/hyperspherical basis with an isoenergetic (fix-E, solve-for-scale) inversion; the closest, Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al., runs the *forward* direction (scan a candidate eigenvalue and detect a singularity/minimum in the singular-value spectrum), which is a genuinely different mechanism from fixing the energy algebraically and reading off eigenvalues as scale parameters. Paper 60's description of this paper as running "the forward direction... which is not the isoenergetic advantage" is accurate. No head-to-head win/loss applies; these are enabling primitives, not rivals.

## Novelty check

**Supported, with the caveats the paper itself already states.** I found no prior quantum algorithm combining (i) a Coulomb-Sturmian/hyperspherical basis, (ii) a quantum eigenvalue routine (qubitization/QPE or otherwise), and (iii) an isoenergetic reformulation (fix the energy, solve for a scale parameter, and read the energy off the eigenvalues directly). Specific negative checks:

- Coulomb-Sturmian bases in the quantum-computing literature: only the classical Herbst–Avery–Dreuw Hartree–Fock convergence study (arXiv:1811.05777, Phys. Rev. A 99, 012512, 2019) turned up — a classical basis-set paper, no quantum algorithm.
- Hyperspherical-harmonics + quantum computing: hits are classical nuclear/atomic few-body methods (e.g. arXiv:1909.00783) and generic "quantum computing architectures use angular momentum entanglement" boilerplate — nothing that builds a Sturmian/hyperspherical block-encoding.
- "Sublinear scaling" quantum chemistry literature already exists (Babbush/Berry/McClean/Neven 2019, arXiv:1807.09802, "sublinear scaling in basis size") but this is a **different sublinearity claim** — Toffoli gate complexity sublinear in the number of plane-wave orbitals N, not LCU-λ sublinear in CI configuration count K for a Sturmian secular matrix. Worth flagging in the paper (or at least in internal notes) so a reader doesn't conflate the two "sublinear" results — they are unrelated mechanisms on different resource axes.
- The three cited quantum generalized-eigenvalue papers (Liang 2022, Baek 2023, Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al. 2025/26) are all confirmed as real, on-topic references; none pre-empts the isoenergetic-inversion idea. The novelty claim as scoped ("no existing quantum algorithm combining a Sturmian basis, a quantum eigenvalue routine, and the isoenergetic inversion") holds up under this search.

**Citation-accuracy flag (secondary finding, not the main task but worth surfacing):** the bibliography entry for Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al. gives the title as "Quantum algorithm for the generalized eigenvalue problem via a singularity test." The actual arXiv title (confirmed on arxiv.org/abs/2506.13534, v1 and current) is **"Quantum algorithm for solving generalized eigenvalue problems with application to the Schrödinger equation."** The paper's *prose description* of the mechanism ("scans a candidate eigenvalue as a singularity test") is an accurate paraphrase of the actual method, but the bibitem's title field does not match the real title and should be corrected. Author initials in the bibitem ("K. Pliś" and "A. Żak") also do not match the confirmed arXiv author list (Szymon Pliś; Emil Zak) — worth a fix-on-sight per the project's citation-hygiene discipline. All other checked references (Babbush 2018, Su 2021, Berry 2019 qubitization-of-arbitrary-basis is not directly cited but its sibling low/rank/sparse line is representative of the field, Low & Chuang 2019, Liang 2022, Baek 2023, Gilyén et al. 2019, Childs–Kothari–Somma 2017, Herbst–Avery–Dreuw 2019) resolved to real papers with matching arXiv IDs and content as characterized in Paper 60.

## The sharpest honest sentence

Paper 60 identifies a genuine and apparently novel niche — an atomic secular matrix that is metric-free and whose LCU 1-norm is sublinear in configuration count — but that result lives on a different resource axis (configurations, single-center, one atom) than the axis (orbitals/qubits, many-electron, real molecules) on which double factorization, tensor hypercontraction, and first-quantized plane-wave methods already report mature, hardware-relevant λ and Toffoli numbers, and on the one place Paper 60 does measure the many-electron molecular axis directly, it reports ordinary polynomial scaling (λ ~ n_orb^2.2) with no advantage over — and no attempt to out-compress — those established baselines.
