## The contribution, in one sentence

The label-generated angular sparsity that underwrites GeoVac's qubit-resource advantage is an **atomic-sector artifact** — it survives only because every prior builder declines to form a genuine two-center orbital product; the moment the bond is actually evaluated the Gaunt *l*-selection rule fails and only the axial *m*-rule survives (forced by the *abelian residue* theorem: the two centers' rotation groups intersect in the axial *U(1)*, whose character group is ℤ, so the one surviving cross-center rule is additive *m*-conservation) — from which it follows that the W1e binding wall was a **Hamiltonian-specification** defect, not a correlation defect, demonstrated by NaH binding on genuine end-to-end integrals with *fewer* correlation degrees of freedom than the runs that failed.

That is a genuine, non-trivial, corpus-own thesis, and it is findable in the abstract. The difficulty is not that the thesis is missing — it is that the paper hangs four further strong-but-separable results off it (a 60-digit quadrature-free diatomic + a closed-form H₂ PES; the (AA|BB) Lindemann–Weierstrass decidability + weight-one filtration; the continuous decompactification front; the cost-conservation re-readings), and the spine has to be reconstructed by the reader through a lot of connective tissue.

- **The (AA|BB) decidability upgrade is elegant and genuinely a *decision*, not a measurement.** Closing the direct-Coulomb radial integral in weight-one, π-free, dilogarithm-free elementary form and then proving all 195 permitted entries nonzero by Lindemann–Weierstrass separation — with the categorical line drawn honestly at where E₁/γ block the cross classes — is a clean transcendence-theory result, and the "both natural readings are wrong (not escalating, but weight-one ≠ decidable)" passage is the right nuance.
