# Prior-art scan: quantum algorithm for isoenergetic (generalized) Sturmian method

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Scope:** literature/novelty scan only (web search + fetch). No GeoVac code touched. Feeds a scoping decision on whether "block-encode a potential-weighted secular matrix at fixed E, estimate its spectrum via QPE/qubitization, wrapped in an outer classical root-search over E" is worth pursuing as a novel quantum algorithm.

---

## Q1 — Quantum algorithms on Coulomb-Sturmian / hyperspherical-harmonic / generalized-Sturmian bases?

**No hit.** Coulomb-Sturmian and generalized-Sturmian/hyperspherical-harmonic bases are well developed classically (Avery's own book series; Herbst, Avery & Dreuw, *Quantum chemistry with Coulomb Sturmians: construction and convergence of Coulomb Sturmian basis sets at Hartree-Fock level*, Phys. Rev. A **99**, 012512 (2019), arXiv:1811.05777 — note James Emil Avery is a co-author, same lineage as the generalized-Sturmian method itself). None of this work touches quantum computing; it is entirely classical HF-level basis-set construction.

No paper was found building a VQE/QPE/qubitization/block-encoding algorithm directly on a Coulomb-Sturmian, hyperspherical-harmonic, or generalized-Sturmian basis for electronic structure. Quantum-computing electronic-structure work uses Gaussian/plane-wave/finite-difference/tight-binding bases (e.g. the "Fast Quantum Simulation of Electronic Structure by Spectral Amplification" line, non-local-pseudopotential first-quantization work) — Sturmian-type bases are absent from that literature. This is a genuine, clean gap.

## Q2 — Quantum algorithms for generalized eigenvalue problems Hc = ESc; does S (or S^-1/2) conditioning reappear? Any isoenergetic/inverse (fix E, solve for a coupling) results?

Active area, several distinct lines:

1. **Fault-tolerant block-encoding, explicit κ(S) cost.** Liang, Shen, Li, Fei, *Quantum algorithms for the generalized eigenvalue problem*, Quantum Inf. Process. **21**, 23 (2022), arXiv:2112.02554. Given α_A-block-encodings of A, B, solves Ax=λBx in time Õ(κ_E(α_A+ρα_B)κ_B/ε). **κ_B (= condition number of the metric matrix) is a direct multiplicative cost factor** — the conditioning problem does NOT go away on a quantum computer here.

2. **ODE/Fourier-spectral route.** Shao & Liu, *Quantum algorithms for the polynomial eigenvalue problems* / published as *Solving generalized eigenvalue problems by ordinary differential equations on a quantum computer*, Proc. R. Soc. A **478**, 20210797 (2022), arXiv:2010.15027. Requires B invertible, B⁻¹A diagonalizable with real spectrum; maps to an ODE solved via block-encoding + QPE.

3. **Near-term variational GEVP solvers** (no complexity/conditioning analysis, NISQ-oriented): Rayleigh-quotient-with-metric-B loss functions, e.g. arXiv:2507.04783 (non-Hermitian case) and the finite-element-method variational GEVP paper (Sato, Watanabe, Raymond, Kondo et al.).

4. **Closest structural match — outer parameter scan + inner QSVE, S handled implicitly.** Rajchel-Mieldzioć, Pliś & Żak, *Quantum algorithm for solving generalized eigenvalue problems with application to the Schrödinger equation*, Phys. Rev. Research (2025/26), arXiv:2506.13534. Solves Hψ=ESψ (pseudospectral collocation, distributed-Gaussian basis) by defining a residue operator M(α)=B″+V·B−αB and locating α where the minimum singular value of the block-encoded M(α) hits zero, via QPE + amplitude amplification. **Explicitly avoids S⁻¹ / κ(S) entirely** — sidesteps the classical numerical instability they quantify (κ(S) reaching 10¹⁶–10²⁰ for 30–40 Gaussians). Combined cost Õ(√(NK)) (N = basis size, K = energy-grid points), because the outer grid scan and inner estimation are fused into a single joint amplitude-amplification search rather than a naive K× multiplier.
   - **Important structural difference from isoenergetic Sturmian:** here the scanned parameter α *is* the candidate eigenvalue E itself (a singularity/null-space test), not a fixed-E genuine eigen-decomposition in a secondary coupling variable. It does not exploit the isoenergetic reformulation's actual selling point (a well-posed, non-singular GEVP at fixed E).

5. **S-conditioning reappears as measurement/statistical error in the near-term regime too.** The non-orthogonal quantum eigensolver (NOQE) family for chemistry — Baek, Hait, Shee, Leimkuhler, Huggins, Stetina, Head-Gordon & Whaley, *Say NO to Optimization: A Non-Orthogonal Quantum Eigensolver*, PRX Quantum **4**, 030307 (2023), arXiv:2205.09039; plus follow-ons CANOE (arXiv:2603.13188) and *Improved Measurement Cost Scaling in the Nonorthogonal Quantum Eigensolver* (arXiv:2608.12830) — solves exactly Hc=Sc-type GEVPs from non-orthogonal ansatz states and shows that an ill-conditioned S converts small per-entry measurement error into much larger eigenvalue error; recent (2026) work shows the sensitivity is controlled by κ of the *retained* (thresholded) overlap block, giving linear- rather than worse-than-linear measurement scaling.

**No hit** on a genuinely isoenergetic/inverse quantum algorithm (fix E as an external classical parameter, solve a proper — not singularity-search — eigenvalue problem for a coupling/scale parameter at that fixed E). The closest analog (#4) still scans the eigenvalue itself.

## Q3 — Inner eigenvalue-estimation + outer classical root-search over a Hamiltonian parameter: known cost multipliers / obstructions?

- The only paper found that actually implements and costs this shape is Rajchel-Mieldzioć/Pliś/Żak (arXiv:2506.13534, above). Their headline result is that the **naive nested-loop cost (K outer iterations × full inner QPE cost) is avoidable**: by phrasing "is α an eigenvalue?" as a joint singular-value/marked-item search over the combined (N, K) space, amplitude amplification gives Õ(√(NK)) instead of Õ(K)·(inner cost) — a genuine quadratic speedup on the outer loop itself, not just on the inner one.
- This joint speedup is **not generic** — it required their specific formulation (locating minima in a singular-value landscape = Grover-style search over a discretized grid). A root-search that instead needs classical bisection/Newton iteration on a *derived quantity* (e.g. "find E such that eigenvalue β_k(E) of a fixed-E GEVP equals 1") does not obviously reduce to the same marked-item-search structure, so whether the √(NK) trick transfers to an isoenergetic-Sturmian outer loop is an open question, not a known result.
- General QPE root-finding exists in a different, unrelated form: companion-matrix-based polynomial root finding via QPE (arXiv:1510.04452) — a different problem class (finding all roots of a fixed polynomial), not eigenvalue-in-a-parameterized-Hamiltonian.
- The broader "quantum eigenvalue processing" framework (Low & Su, arXiv:2401.06240, SIAM J. Comput. 2024) gives QSVT-style tools (QEVT/QEVE) for extracting/transforming eigenvalues of non-normal block-encoded operators at Heisenberg-limited precision — a possible building block for the inner step, but it does not address the outer-loop-over-a-Hamiltonian-parameter question at all.
- **No documented killer/no-go obstruction** for the inner-QPE + outer-classical-root-search architecture in general. The realistic cost risks are (i) the S-conditioning-reappears issue from Q2 if the fixed-E secular matrix is not kept genuinely well-conditioned by construction (isoenergetic Sturmian's whole classical selling point — shared turning-point behavior — needs to be shown to keep the block-encoded metric well-conditioned; nobody has checked this on a quantum encoding), and (ii) absent a joint-search trick like #4's, a plain K-outer-iterations × inner-QPE-cost multiplier is the fallback, which is a real but not fatal overhead (linear in the number of bisection/Newton steps to root-find E, each paying full QPE precision cost).

## Verdict

**(a) Novel or done?** The specific combination — isoenergetic (fixed-E, solve-for-coupling) generalized Sturmian method, on a Coulomb-Sturmian/hyperspherical-harmonic basis, block-encoded and spectrum-estimated via QPE/qubitization, wrapped in an outer classical root search over E — **has not been done**. Every individual ingredient has close-but-distinct prior art (quantum GEVP solvers exist; one paper does outer-scan-over-eigenvalue + inner QSVE for Hψ=ESψ; Sturmian/hyperspherical bases are well developed classically), but no paper combines them, and no paper exploits the isoenergetic reformulation's structural advantage (a genuine, better-conditioned GEVP at fixed E rather than a singularity search over E) on quantum hardware. This appears to be a real, well-scoped gap — worth flagging as novel-if-pursued, not "already done."

**(b) Known killer obstruction?** None found that rules it out categorically. The two live risk factors documented elsewhere in the literature are (i) metric/overlap conditioning κ(S) reappearing as either a direct multiplicative Õ(κ_B) cost (fault-tolerant block-encoding route) or as amplified statistical error (near-term/NOQE route) unless the block-encoded S is kept well-conditioned by construction — which is exactly the "well-conditioned, potential-weighted" qualifier already built into the proposed scoping, so this is a design constraint to honor, not a wall; and (ii) the outer root-search-over-E multiplier is a plain K× unless a problem-specific joint amplitude-amplification trick (as in arXiv:2506.13534) can be constructed, which is unproven for the isoenergetic-eigenvalue-in-β (rather than singularity-in-E) formulation.
