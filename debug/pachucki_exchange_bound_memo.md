# Pachucki's ODE and the order of the unequal-exponent 1s exchange operator

Date 2026-11-17. Derivation memo; nothing committed, no paper or record edited.
Scripts: `debug/pachucki_bound_core.py` (function algebra + exact ansatz solver),
`debug/pachucki_bound_1s.py` (1s closed form, rank, numerics),
`debug/pachucki_bound_operator.py` (minimal operator, comparison with the committed mod-p
operators), `debug/pachucki_bound_lgt0.py` (2p/3d families with m = 0).
Data: `debug/data/pachucki_bound_1s_{1_2,1_3_2}.json`, `pachucki_bound_operator_{1_2,1_3_2}.json`,
`pachucki_bound_{2psA,2ps2ps}_1_2.json`.

## Verdict

Yes. Pachucki's differential equation gives a **derived** closed form of the 1s exchange
integral at unequal exponents, and from it the order. At (a,b) = (1,2) and (1,3/2):

* K(R) lies in the Q(R)-span of 11 functions, closed under d/dR; two coordinate ties cut the
  module generated by K to **dimension 9 exactly** (rank over Q(R, LC), with LC = ln((a+b)/(b-a))
  kept as an indeterminate; it cannot drop at LC = ln 3 or ln 5 since those are transcendental).
* The minimal operator built from that closed form has order 9, coefficient degrees
  [14, 15, 16, ..., 16] (degree 16), no dependence on LC, and **is identical, coefficient by
  coefficient up to one scalar, to the committed guessed operator modulo 67108859 and
  33554393, at both pairs** (`exch_run_1_2_*_EXS.json`, `exch_run_1_3_2_*_EXB.json`).
* So "order <= 9" is derived, not guessed. "Order = 9" additionally needs that the 9 basis
  functions below are linearly independent over C(R) (standard Kolchin-Ostrowski-type
  statement for exponential integrals with distinct exponents; not re-proved here). The
  committed scan independently excluded orders 1-8 at degree <= 60.

The equal-exponent bound 4 is not reached by this route (p^2 in Pachucki's Eq. 21 is 0/0 at
a = b); there it is the classical Sugiura/Ruedenberg closed form (span of e^{-2p},
e^{-2p} ln p, E1(2p), e^{2p}E1(4p), closed under d/dR) - prior art, already in Paper 58.

## 1. The map (step 1)

Pachucki 2009 Eq. (1): f(r) = int d3r1/4pi d3r2/4pi e^{-u3 r1A - u2 r1B - w2 r2A - w3 r2B}
r /(r1A r1B r2A r2B r12). Since d_{u3}[e^{-u3 r1A}/r1A] = -e^{-u3 r1A}, four derivatives (sign +)
remove the four electron-nucleus denominators. With normalised 1s orbitals (a^3 b^3/pi^2) and
the two 4pi measures,

    K(R) = (1s_A^a 1s_B^b | 1s_A^a 1s_B^b) = (16 a^3 b^3 / R) * d_u3 d_u2 d_w2 d_w3 f |_theta0,
    theta0: u3 = w2 = a,  u2 = w3 = b                (Pachucki's Sec. VII exchange point).

Numerical check (mpmath, 60 digits; the only quadrature is the 1-D tail integral defining f0
below): K(1,2; R=1.4) = 0.20632030202807079004021890670600820962904556376581, against
`exchange_hp` (dps 80, committed in `exch_numeric_1_2_EXS.json`)
0.206320302028070790040218906706008209629045563765807893...: agreement to all 50 printed
digits. Also R = 3.015 at (1,2), and R = 1.4, 3.015 at (1,3/2) (reference
`exch_numeric_1_3_2_EXB.json`): 50 digits each.

## 2. The derivation (step 2)

Pachucki 2009 Eq. (29): r f'' + f' - p^2 r f + F = 0, with p^2 from Eq. (21) and F from
Eqs. (23,24,31-35) (I read the equations from the PDF; transcription checked two ways: Eq. (63)'s
F^{(-1)} is exactly the antiderivative of Eq. (31) at theta0 in the exact algebra, and the
end-to-end 50-digit agreement above).

At theta0, p^2 = 0 to **second** order: its numerator is (u2^2-u3^2+w2^2-w3^2)(u2^2 w2^2 - u3^2 w3^2),
both factors vanish at theta0, the denominator (b^2-a^2)(a^2-b^2) does not (a != b). So the
first derivatives of p^2 vanish; the nonzero d_U p^2 (|U| >= 2) are rationals (e.g. at (1,2):
d_{w2 w3} p^2 = 40/9, d_{u2 w3} p^2 = 32/9, d_{u2 w2 w3} p^2 = 160/27).

Differentiate (29) by a multi-index S and set theta = theta0:

    (r f_S')' = -F_S + r * sum_{0 != U <= S} binom(S,U) (d_U p^2) f_{S-U}.        (*)

f_S decays exponentially as r -> oo (so does the integrand); the kernel of (r d)' is {1, ln r},
which does not decay. Hence **f_S is the unique decaying solution of (*)**. No boundary
condition at r = 0 and no analyticity argument in theta is needed.

Function space. F_S (any S) at theta0 lies in the Q[r, 1/r]-span of 10 explicit functions
(LC = ln|...| of Eqs. 34-35 appears as a constant):

    e^{-(a+b)r}, e^{-2ar}, e^{-2br}, E1((a+b)r), e^{2ar}E1(2(a+b)r), e^{2br}E1(2(a+b)r),
    e^{-2ar}Ei((a-b)r), e^{-2ar}Ei(2(a-b)r), e^{-2br}Ei((b-a)r), e^{-2br}Ei(2(b-a)r).

Add f0 = f(theta0) = int_r^oo F^{(-1)}(s)/s ds (Pachucki Eq. 62) with the exact rule
r f0' = -F^{(-1)} (Eq. 63, explicit). The set of 11 is closed under d/dr, and every member
decays. Solving (*) by an ansatz sum x_{key,k} r^k B_key (k in [-6, 6] for 1s, [-14, 14] for
l > 0) is linear algebra over Q(LC); a solution, if it exists, is the decaying solution, hence
f_S. **All 15 systems (S a nonempty subset of {u3,u2,w2,w3}) are solvable in this span, with no
free ansatz columns** (so the span carries no kernel element). f0 is the only non-elementary
function; the mechanism that keeps it alone is the identity
L0(r^2 f0 /4) = r f0 - r F^{(-1)} - r^2 F/4 (L0 = d r d), so the coupling terms r f_T never
create a new iterated integral.

Naive count, for comparison. Without closing the system, (*) only says f_S lies in the
preimage under L0 of (source module + r * modules of lower f_T): each level adds the two
kernel solutions {1, ln r} lifted by r-multiplication (r^2, r^2 ln r, r^4, ...), and the source
module has rank 10, so the a-priori D-module bound from (*) alone is 10 + 2 per level of the
tower (top, |T| = 2 and |T| = 0 levels feed f_1234), i.e. 16 or more, and it ignores that the
four ln-type kernels might cancel. It does not reach 9; the exact solve does. Even the
"explicit + f0" count is 11; the last two units come from the ties of Sec. 3.

## 3. From 11 to 9, and the measured data (step 3)

The Q(R)-span of K, K', ..., K^(10) in the 11 coordinates has rank 9. The two right-null
vectors of the coordinate matrix are supported on {e^{-2aR}Ei((a-b)R), e^{-2aR}Ei(2(a-b)R)} and
on the b-twin, with ratio 1:2. So these Ei terms occur only in the combinations

    phi_a = e^{-2aR}[Ei(2(a-b)R) - 2 Ei((a-b)R)],   phi_b = e^{-2bR}[Ei(2(b-a)R) - 2 Ei((b-a)R)],

which are exactly the brackets of Pachucki's F^{(-1)} (Eq. 63). K's module is the Q(R)-span of

    f0, E1((a+b)R), e^{-(a+b)R}, e^{-2aR}, phi_a, e^{-2bR}, phi_b, e^{2aR}E1(2(a+b)R), e^{2bR}E1(2(a+b)R)

(nine; closed: phi_a' = -2a phi_a + (e^{-2bR} - 2e^{-(a+b)R})/R).

(i) Rates at infinity. Each generator carries one formal rate: f0 and E1((a+b)R) give 0 (their
free constants), then -(a+b); -2a twice (e^{-2aR}, phi_a); -2b twice; 2a and 2b (the free
e^{2aR}, e^{2bR} of the two E1 terms). This is the measured multiset exactly, including 0 twice.
The derived operator's characteristic polynomial at infinity is
x^2 (x-2a)(x-2b)(x+2a)^2(x+(a+b))(x+2b)^2 at both pairs. The arguments (a-b)R do occur, but
only inside phi_a, phi_b with prefactor rates -2a, -2b: that is why no rate +-(a-b) appears.
(ii) R = 0. The derived operator's indicial roots are {-1 (double), 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9} at both
pairs, as measured; the log onset R^9 ln R and its coefficient follow because the operator is
identical to the committed one and the closed form equals K (Sec. 1).
(iii) The operator itself: equal to the committed one modulo both primes, every coefficient, at
both pairs (`pachucki_bound_operator_*.json`, keys `equal_mod_*`).

## 4. l > 0 (step 4)

For orbital products that are polynomials in the distances (m = 0 on the axis:
z_A = (r_A^2 + R^2 - r_B^2)/2R), the integral is a Q(R)-combination of higher exponent
derivatives d^alpha f, alpha in N^4, and (*) holds for every multi-index. Run at (1,2):

* (2p_sigma_A 1s_B | 2p_sigma_A 1s_B), exponents (1,2): 144 multi-indices (up to d^3 in one
  exponent per electron); every f_alpha solved in the same 11-function span, no free ansatz
  columns; the module generated by the integral has rank 9. Same span, same bound 9, matching
  the measured order 9 (record EP). Cost: 1169 s for the symbolic exponent derivatives,
  35 s for the solves (`debug/data/pachucki_bound_2psA.log`).
* The 50-digit numeric cross-check against `exchange_hp` for this case did NOT finish (the
  hydrogenic tau-sum at tau <= 16, 24 overran the 28-minute cap, exit 124), so the 2p closed form
  itself is unchecked numerically; the map is the 1s map plus z_A = (r_A^2 + R^2 - r_B^2)/2R,
  and the rank does not depend on the normalisation.
* (2p_sigma_A 2p_sigma_B | ...) and 3d_0: not run (the derivative step alone would exceed the
  budget; 2p-2p needs d^3 on all four exponents). Expected to land in the same span, since
  exponent derivatives at theta0 cannot create new exponential rates, but not verified.

So for the m = 0 families the argument is the same and carries the bound: further exponent
derivatives and R-polynomial prefactors change only the Laurent coefficients, never the
11 keys, so <= 11 holds in general and the measured ties give 9. That general "<= 11" step
(that every L0-solve closes in the span) is verified case by case here, not proved as a lemma.

The 2p_{+-1} (and 3d_{+-2}) cases are not of this form: the azimuthal product
x1 x2 + y1 y2 brings in r12^2, i.e. Pachucki's f_n with n = 2 (his w1-direction equation,
Eqs. 42, 48, 64). Pachucki states that all such integrals reduce to f, f', Ei and exponentials;
granted that, the same 11-function span applies, but I did not transcribe Eq. (48)/(54) and
did not verify those families; they stay on the mod-p operators.

## 5. Derived vs still resting on mod p (step 5)

Derived (exact, given Pachucki's Eq. 29 and F, prior art; transcription validated):
* closed form of K at (1,2) and (1,3/2) in the 9-function span, hence order <= 9;
* the order-9 operator itself, its LC-independence, R = 0 exponents and rates at infinity;
* the coincidence of that operator with the guessed one modulo two primes per pair - which
  retroactively certifies the guessed operators.

Still not derived:
* order >= 9 needs linear independence of the nine functions over C(R) (standard; not
  re-proved), or the scan's exclusion of orders 1-8 (degree box 60);
* general a < b: only the two rational pairs were computed (the construction is uniform in
  (a,b) - same keys, same ties - but the general statement is not a symbolic computation here);
* a = b: not reachable through Eq. (21); the bound 4 rests on the classical closed form;
* m != 0 families: see Sec. 4.

second-order equation for the two-centre master integral~\cite{pachucki2009} four times in the

an equation whose unique decaying solution lies in the span, over $\mathbb{Q}(R)$, of nine
