# Door 24 Wilson-gauge cell: which SU(2) survives a finite nucleus (2026-10-31)

**Status: diagnostic, not pre-registered.** Driver `debug/door24_wilson_cell.py`, log
`debug/data/door24_wilson_cell.log`. Nothing under papers/, tests/, geovac/, docs/ was edited.

## Verdict: DECIDED

The finite nucleus leaves the diagonal SO(3) (generated by L) standing. That is not the SU(2) that the
"S³ = SU(2)" reading of Paper 30 picks out. Both chiral SU(2) factors are broken. Paper 30's Wilson action can
still be written on the label graph, because its link group never acted on the labels. What does not carry over
is the identification of the link group with the host's own SU(2). This has the same shape as door 11, but here it
comes from its own computation and from the group structure of SO(4). It is not borrowed from door 11.

## 1. Paper 30's SU(2), stated exactly

- **What Paper 30 defines.** Paper 30 (`papers/group5_qed_gauge/paper_30_su2_wilson.tex`, §§III–IV) puts link
  variables U_e ∈ SU(2) in the fundamental 2×2 representation (quaternion form, eq. su2_quaternion). Gauge
  transformations are independent at each node: U_{u→v} ↦ g_u U g_v^†. The code
  (`geovac/su2_wilson_gauge.py`, `enumerate_oriented_edges(adjacency)`) takes only an adjacency matrix. The group
  acts on an internal C² at each node, **not on the (n,l,m) labels**. Paper 30 never names a subgroup of SO(4).
  The words "SO(4)", "left", "right" and "fibre" do not occur in that sense. Its only link to the host is the
  motivating sentence "as manifolds, S³ = SU(2): the underlying manifold … is the gauge group".
- **What that sentence fixes, under the atlas's rule** (gauge groups attach to manifolds). Read as a geometric
  statement, "the manifold is the group" means SU(2) acting on S³ as it acts on itself, which is free and
  transitive. Inside SO(4) = (SU(2)₊ × SU(2)₋)/Z₂ the only subgroups that do this are the two chiral factors, left
  and right multiplication. On the labels they are generated by J^± = (L ± A)/2. The diagonal SU(2), {(h,h)}, acts
  by conjugation and fixes ±1, so it does not qualify.
- **Which chiral factor.** Paper 30's maximal-torus proposition makes Paper 25 the Cartan sector of its SU(2).
  Paper 25's conclusion (the door-11 SY record) identifies the Hopf fibre generator as L_z + A_z = 2J⁺_z, which is
  a torus of su(2)₊. So the subgroup consistent with both papers is **SU(2)₊, with generators J⁺_a = (L_a + A_a)/2,
  acting on each shell n as n copies of spin (n−1)/2** (the shell is the (j,j) irrep, j = (n−1)/2). The mirror
  convention (L_z − A_z, the opposite Hopf fibration) gives SU(2)₋. The door-24 result below is the same for both.

This identification is a reading, not something Paper 30 states. It is the only reading under which "Paper 30's
reading transfers" has content, because Paper 30's own construction attaches to no subgroup.

## 2. Door 24's survivor, stated exactly

The correction V = V_sphere + Z/r (uniform sphere, R = 0.05, Z = 1) is the operator in
`tests/test_atlas_symmetry_survivors.py`. It is central, so it is diagonal in (l, m) and mixes n. Its commutant in
so(4) has **dimension 3 and is span{L_x, L_y, L_z}**. That is the diagonal SO(3), L = J⁺ + J⁻, acting on S³ by
conjugation. The commutant was first measured as the non-scored SY0 control and is already guarded by
`tests/test_atlas_so4_commutant.py::test_sy_central_control_keeps_exactly_the_rotations` (n ≤ 3). It is
reproduced here at n ≤ 4.

## 3. Computation (hydrogenic Z = 1, n ≤ 4, 30 states)

The generators are block-diagonal in n, so the truncation is exact for every commutator.

The survivor fixes the s state in every shell. This is the label-level trace of conjugation fixing the points ±1,
which under Fock's map are p = 0 and p = ∞, the points the nucleus singles out. The chiral factors fix nothing
beyond n = 1, which is the trace of acting freely.

**Why the outcome was certain in advance.** Once the commutant is span(L) (already guarded), the intersection
with su(2)_± is span(L) ∩ span(J^±). That intersection is {0}, because each L_a = J⁺_a + J⁻_a has a nonzero
component in the other factor. The table checks that piece of linear algebra numerically. It does not discover
anything. The isotropic sizes (0.716, 0.358) are reported only and are not gated. The 0.358 = 0.716/2 equality
follows from [V, L] = 0, which gives [V, J^±] = ±[V, A]/2.

## 4. Proposed atlas cell (door 24, Wilson column)

> the surviving SO(3) is the rotation group L, acting on S³ = SU(2) by conjugation, with fixed points (it fixes
> the s state of every shell); both chiral SU(2) factors, J^± = (L ± A)/2, the left and right multiplications that
> make S³ a group, are broken (‖[V, J^±]‖/‖V‖ = 0.36; commutant of V in so(4) = span L). Paper 30's
> identification of the link group with the host's SU(2) does not transfer to a finite nucleus; a Wilson action
> can still be written on the surviving label graph, whose link group never acted on the labels.

## 5. Proposed guard

Extend `tests/test_atlas_so4_commutant.py`, reusing its `generators()` and `door24`-style central operator.
Write it as a separate piece of work, per the guard rule.

- `test_door24_chiral_factors_broken`: J^± close su(2) and commute with each other. The commutant of the central
  V in span(J⁺) and in span(J⁻) is 0. ‖[V, J^±_z]‖/‖V‖ > 1e-3.
- `test_door24_survivor_has_fixed_points`: L has one invariant vector per shell for n ≤ 3, and J⁺ has none for
  n ≥ 2.
- Wrong answers it rejects:
  - "the survivor is a chiral SU(2)", in which case some J^± would commute;
  - "SU(2)₋ survives under the mirror convention";
  - "the SO(3) survivor acts freely like the group itself", in which case the invariant count would be 0;
  - "the J's are mislabelled", in which case the closure check would fail.
- Fire-test: replace V with an SO(4)-invariant operator (diag −1/2n²). Then the J⁺ commutant becomes 3 and the
  guard must fail.
