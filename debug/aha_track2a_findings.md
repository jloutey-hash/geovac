## 1. What s↔t actually does — the hypothesis' ρ-leg is **wrong**, and the correction is more interesting

P59 `sec:modular` + `sec:obstruction`: `c₁ = s(1−s)`, `c₂ = t(1−t)`, `ρ = c₂/c₁`, and (eq:lambda_rho, reproduced here to 10⁻⁵¹) `λ(τ(ρ)) = 1 − ρ`.

**Therefore s↔t ⟺ c₁↔c₂ ⟺ ρ ↦ 1/ρ** — *not* ρ ↦ 1−ρ.

Pushed to the modulus:

```
 ρ ↦ 1/ρ    ⟺    λ ↦ λ/(λ−1)    ⟺    the coset T·Γ(2),  T : τ ↦ τ+1
```

(verified symbolically, and at theta level: `λ(τ+1) = λ/(λ−1)` to 10⁻³⁸.)

This is the transposition of S₃ = PSL₂(ℤ)/Γ̄(2) that **fixes the cusp λ=0** and swaps λ = 1 ↔ ∞. It is *not* the S-coset λ ↦ 1−λ (τ ↦ −1/τ), which is what the hypothesis assumed and whose fixed point is τ = i / ρ = 1/2.

**Is ρ ↦ 1−ρ physical at all?** No. Enumerating the full symmetry group of the Feynman square (the dihedral group generated by s↔t, s↦1−s, t↦1−t — all 8 elements), the induced action on the modulus is **exactly {id, ρ↦1/ρ}** and nothing else (s↦1−s and t↦1−t act trivially because c = s(1−s) is s↦1−s-invariant). So the involution the hypothesis assumed does not occur in the physics; the one that does is the other transposition.

This also independently re-derives the audit's §3.2 deflation from the group side: **ρ = 1/2 is not a fixed point of any physical symmetry.** It is one member of a 2-element s↔t *orbit* {ρ = 1/2, ρ = 2} (both have j = 1728). The physics *visits* τ = i twice; it never *pins* it.

## 2. The elliptic lift is real, and it names a group

The coset T·Γ(2) contains parabolic elements (T itself) **and elliptic ones**. Explicitly

```
 M = [[1, −1], [2, −1]],   det M = 1,   tr M = 0  ⟹  M² = −I,
 M ≡ T (mod 2)  ⟹  M ∈ T·Γ(2),
 M fixes τ* = (1+i)/2,  the root of 2τ² − 2τ + 1 = 0,  disc = −4.
```

Verified numerically: `λ(M·τ) = λ(τ)/(λ(τ)−1)` at three generic τ (err ≲ 10⁻⁵⁰); `M·τ* = τ*`; `λ(τ*) = 2` exactly (10⁻³⁰); `j(τ*) = 1728` (10⁻²⁵), so τ* ~_{SL₂(ℤ)} i and **E_{τ*} has CM by ℤ[i]**.

The elliptic-fixed-point ⟹ CM statement is the classical one and worth citing precisely: a fixed point of an elliptic element of a subgroup of SL₂(ℤ) is a root of an *integral* quadratic `cτ² + (d−a)τ − b = 0` whose discriminant `(a+d)² − 4 = tr² − 4 < 0`, hence imaginary quadratic, hence a CM point (Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, VII.1; Diamond–Shurman §2.3). For PSL₂(ℤ) the only possibilities are order 2 (τ ~ i, disc −4, ℤ[i], j = 1728) and order 3 (τ ~ ζ₆, disc −3, ℤ[ζ₃], j = 0) — verified: `j(ζ₆) = 0`.

**Group identification.** `⟨Γ(2), M⟩ = Γ₀(2)`: M has c = 2 ≡ 0 (mod 2); Γ(2) ◁ Γ₀(2); [PSL:Γ(2)] = 6 and the coset image has order 2 ⟹ index 3 = [PSL:Γ₀(2)]. (Not Γ⁰(2): T has b = 1 odd. Not Γ_θ: T mod 2 is neither I nor S.) Γ₀(2) has index 3, 2 cusps, ν₃ = 0, and — Riemann–Hurwitz check `g = 1 + 3/12 − ν₂/4 − 0 − 2/2 = 0` forces **ν₂ = 1** — exactly one elliptic point, of order 2, at τ* = (1+i)/2.

So the structural claim of the candidate mechanism is **confirmed at the group level**:

> the physical ℤ₂ (density exchange) is realised on the family by an element of order **4** in SL₂(ℤ) squaring to **−I**, whose fixed point has CM by **ℤ[i]** — with the kernel {±I} of SL₂(ℤ) → PSL₂(ℤ) playing precisely the role the −1 of SU(2) → SO(3) plays on the P56 side.

## 7. What this pass *does* buy: the four failed candidates get a common ancestor

The audit's candidates 1–4 each half-worked and each failed for a different-looking reason. This pass identifies why they rhyme. In each case the operative statement is:

> **an order-2 symmetry realised *projectively* on a rank-2 ℚ-object, through a double cover with central −1, forcing J² = −I hence ℚ[J] = ℚ(i).**

| audit candidate | the double cover it saw | verdict there |

| **2a (this pass)** | **SL₂(ℤ) → PSL₂(ℤ)** (kernel ±I) vs **SU(2) → SO(3)** | **the ancestor pattern, still not a map** |

And the ancestor comes with its own hard deflation, which is the honest bottom line:

> **D1 (measured).** PSL₂(ℤ) ≅ ℤ₂ ∗ ℤ₃, so every elliptic element has order 2 or 3; order 2 ⟹ fixed point ~ i ⟹ disc −4 ⟹ ℤ[i] (j = 1728), order 3 ⟹ ~ ζ₆ ⟹ disc −3 ⟹ ℤ[ζ₃] (j = 0). Verified: all three transposition fixed points of X(2) (λ = 2, 1/2, −1) have j = 1728 exactly, and j(ζ₆) = 0.
>
> Hence "a ℤ₂ symmetry of a modular family of elliptic curves pins a ℤ[i] CM point" is a **theorem about PSL₂(ℤ)'s torsion**, true of *any* such family. It carries about one bit (order 2 vs order 3), not a mechanism. The same one-bit structure is what makes the P56 side ℚ(i): (−1)^{2j} = −1 vs +1.

Two independent ℤ₂-realisations landing on ℚ(i) is therefore about as surprising as two coin flips agreeing — which is the sharpest statement of **CONVERGENT** the corpus has yet had, and strictly stronger than the audit's "isomorphic invariants on unrelated ℚ²'s".

**Also worth recording (D4).** The two order-2 symmetries are not even of the same *kind*: route (a)'s is **antiunitary** (Kramers time reversal on a compact-group rep); route (b)'s is **ℚ-linear** (a Hodge-structure automorphism). A natural comparison map would have to reconcile that too.
