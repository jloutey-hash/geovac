UNIFICATION CHECK -- hybrid vs exchange: is it the SAME boundary law?
====================================================================================================

  Exchange class (this track):  boundary log = ln kappa,
      kappa = prod_j lambda_j^{q_j},  q_j = Stokes charge at lambda_j,
      all four singularities in ONE sector, charges (-1,+1,+1,-1).

  Hybrid class (AHA T3):  boundary log = ln Lambda, and the log sits in the
      LEADING sector, which is E_1-FREE -- i.e. in a sector with NO Borel
      singularity of its own.  So the two laws cannot be literally the same
      statement.  What CAN be compared is the weaker, representation-free
      claim:  boundary log in the multiplicative group generated by the
      Borel singularity positions, with exponents in {0, +-1}.

  U1  hybrid boundary log lies in the multiplicative group generated by
      its Borel singularity positions                               : 7/7
  U1b the extracted Lambda agrees with AHA-T3's published cross-ratio
      (up to inversion), reproduced here by a completely different route
      (log-content vector of the closed form)                        : 7/7
      NOTE: the exponent vector is canonical only when the lambda_j are
      multiplicatively INDEPENDENT (e.g. {5/2,11/2,13/2,19/2}, where the
      brute force returns exactly (+1,-1,-1,+1) = the T3 cross-ratio).
      When they are dependent (4 = 2^2) many exponent vectors give the
      same Lambda, so 'the exponent pattern' is not well defined there.

  U2  BUT the exponent PATTERN is not the exchange class's charge vector.
      Hybrid: (+1,-1,-1,+1) on {a_c-a_d, a_c+a_d, mu-a_d, mu+a_d} -- pairs
      the singularities across the two DIVERGENT sectors by the sign of a_d.
      Exchange: (-1,+1,+1,-1) on {0, 2a, 2b, 2A} -- all four in ONE sector,
      exponents = the Stokes charges themselves.

  VERDICT: the two classes share the WEAK law (boundary log is a {0,+-1}
  monomial in the Borel singularity positions -- so the boundary period is
  skeleton-forced in both) but NOT a single exponent rule.  The exchange
  class's stronger statement (exponent = Stokes charge) is specific to its
  single-sector geometry, where the log-carrying term and the singularities
  live in the same sector.
